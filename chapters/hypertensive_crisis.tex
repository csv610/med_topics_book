\chapter{Hypertensive Crisis}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
A hypertensive crisis is a severe, sudden elevation in blood pressure, generally above 180/120 mmHg. It is divided into two categories: hypertensive urgency, where blood pressure is very high but there is no evidence of organ damage, and hypertensive emergency, where very high blood pressure is causing damage to organs such as the brain, heart, kidneys, or eyes. A hypertensive emergency is life-threatening and requires immediate hospital treatment with intravenous medication. Hypertensive urgency is managed with oral medication and close follow-up.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Hypertensive crises are uncommon but serious events, accounting for a small proportion of emergency department visits. Hypertensive emergencies are more common in people with poorly controlled chronic hypertension, in men, in older adults, and in people who have stopped their blood pressure medicines. The most dangerous complications, including stroke and aortic dissection, can be fatal without urgent treatment. With prompt care, outcomes are much better, though the underlying organ damage may be lasting.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Missed medication:} stopping blood pressure medicines is a common trigger
    \item \textbf{Secondary causes:} kidney disease, hormone disorders, and pregnancy conditions such as pre-eclampsia
    \item \textbf{Drugs:} stimulants, cocaine, and some herbal products
    \item \textbf{Pathophysiology:} sudden severe pressure damages the walls of small arteries, causing bleeding, swelling, and reduced blood supply to organs
    \item \textbf{Organ damage:} stroke, brain swelling, heart attack, heart failure, kidney injury, and retinal bleeding
    \item \textbf{Aortic dissection:} tearing of the aorta wall, a catastrophic complication
    \item \textbf{Urgency vs emergency:} the distinction is the presence of organ damage
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Blood pressure above 180/120 mmHg
    \item Hypertensive urgency: very high pressure without symptoms
    \item Headache, blurred vision, and nosebleeds can occur
    \item Hypertensive emergency symptoms: severe headache, chest pain, breathlessness, confusion, and seizures
    \item Visual disturbance and ringing in the ears
    \item Weakness or numbness suggesting stroke
    \item Blood in the urine from kidney damage
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item White-coat spikes in the clinic, which settle with rest
    \item Anxiety with transient pressure rises
    \item The presence or absence of organ damage determines the category
    \item Secondary causes are identified by investigation
    \item Any very high reading with symptoms is treated as an emergency until proven otherwise
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
A single very high reading should be rechecked after rest; if it remains very high, medical assessment is needed. Any symptoms with very high pressure are an emergency.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Blood pressure above 180/120 with headache, blurred vision, chest pain, or confusion
    \item Shortness of breath or swelling of the legs with high pressure
    \item Sudden weakness, facial drooping, or difficulty speaking
    \item Seizures or loss of consciousness
    \item Severe back or chest pain (possible aortic dissection)
    \item Any very high reading in pregnancy
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Blood pressure measurement:} repeated readings, both arms
    \item \textbf{History:} medicines, missed doses, drugs, and symptoms
    \item \textbf{ECG:} for heart strain and heart attack
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} kidney function, electrolytes, and cardiac markers
    \item \textbf{Urine tests:} for protein and blood
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} CT of the brain or chest where stroke or dissection is suspected
    \item \textbf{Eye examination:} for retinal changes
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hypertensive urgency:} oral blood pressure medicines and close follow-up; pressure is lowered gradually
    \item \textbf{Hypertensive emergency:} hospital admission with intravenous medication in intensive care
    \item \textbf{Controlled lowering:} pressure is reduced carefully to avoid depriving organs of blood
    \item \textbf{Treatment of the cause:} pre-eclampsia, hormone disorders, and drug-related causes
    \item \textbf{Investigation:} for secondary causes in resistant cases
    \item \textbf{Medication plan:} a reliable regimen to prevent recurrence
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} regular review until blood pressure is controlled
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Stroke and brain swelling
    \item Heart attack and heart failure
    \item Aortic dissection
    \item Acute kidney injury
    \item Vision loss from retinal damage
    \item Permanent disability or death in severe emergencies
    \item Recurrence without ongoing blood pressure control
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The outlook depends on how quickly treatment begins and which organs are affected. With immediate treatment, most people survive hypertensive emergencies, though organ damage may leave lasting effects. Hypertensive urgency resolves with medication and follow-up. The most important factor in long-term outcomes is sustained blood pressure control afterwards, which prevents recurrence and reduces cardiovascular risk.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Take blood pressure medicines exactly as prescribed and never stop suddenly
    \item Keep blood pressure medicines in stock
    \item Monitor blood pressure at home if advised
    \item Attend regular reviews and dose adjustments
    \item Maintain healthy lifestyle habits
    \item Report new symptoms promptly
    \item Carry a current medicines list
    \item In pregnancy, attend antenatal care for blood pressure checks
\end{itemize}
