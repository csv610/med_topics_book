\section*{Definition and Overview}
CHD (coronary heart disease) concerns a body system or anatomical region. Understanding the normal structure and function of body systems is fundamental to clinical medicine, as disease processes disrupt normal anatomy and physiology, and treatments aim to restore or compensate for that disruption.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Diseases of major body systems represent a leading cause of morbidity and mortality. Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death globally. Respiratory diseases (COPD, asthma, pneumonia) are a major burden. Musculoskeletal conditions are the leading cause of disability worldwide. The prevalence of most system-based diseases increases with age, reflecting cumulative exposure to risk factors and degenerative processes.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Degenerative:} age-related wear and tear (e.g.\ osteoarthritis, atherosclerosis, degenerative disc disease)
    \item \textbf{Inflammatory/autoimmune:} immune-mediated damage (e.g.\ rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease)
    \item \textbf{Infectious:} pathogen invasion of the system
    \item \textbf{Neoplastic:} benign or malignant tumours arising from the system's tissues
    \item \textbf{Genetic/congenital:} inherited disorders or developmental anomalies
    \item \textbf{Traumatic:} injury to the system's structures
    \item \textbf{Vascular:} impaired blood supply causing ischaemia or infarction
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Pain, swelling, or tenderness in the affected region
    \item Loss of normal movement, strength, or organ function
    \item Changes in appearance: redness, deformity, lumps, or colour change
    \item Numbness, paraesthesia, or altered sensation
    \item Symptoms specific to the organ or system involved
    \item Systemic symptoms: fever, weight loss, fatigue if inflammation or malignancy is present
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Inflammatory vs.\ infective vs.\ neoplastic processes
    \item Acute vs.\ chronic presentations
    \item Primary vs.\ referred symptoms
    \item Structural vs.\ functional disorder
    \item Localised vs.\ systemic disease
    \item Other conditions affecting the same anatomical region
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor if symptoms are new, severe, persistent, or interfering with daily life.

\textbf{Call 000 for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chest pain, difficulty breathing, or sudden weakness
    \item Loss of consciousness, fitting, or sudden severe pain
    \item Heavy bleeding or major injury
    \item Signs of stroke (FAST: Face, Arms, Speech, Time)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History and examination:} focused on the relevant system, with full systemic review
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} organ-specific markers (e.g.\ cardiac enzymes, liver function, renal function, inflammatory markers)
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} X-ray, ultrasound, CT, MRI, or fluoroscopy to visualise structures
    \item \textbf{Functional tests:} ECG, spirometry, nerve conduction, or manometry as appropriate
    \item \textbf{Endoscopy:} direct visualisation of hollow organs (e.g.\ bronchoscopy, colonoscopy, cystoscopy)
    \item \textbf{Biopsy:} tissue sampling for histopathological diagnosis
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Conservative:} lifestyle modification, physiotherapy, and self-management
    \item \textbf{Pharmacotherapy:} medicines specific to the condition (e.g.\ antihypertensives, bronchodilators, anti-inflammatories)
    \item \textbf{Interventional:} injections, nerve blocks, or minimally invasive procedures
    \item \textbf{Surgical:} definitive correction or reconstruction when conservative measures fail
    \item \textbf{Rehabilitation:} structured programs to restore function and prevent recurrence
    \item \textbf{Ongoing monitoring:} for chronic conditions requiring long-term management
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Progressive loss of function in the affected system
    \item Secondary effects on other body systems
    \item Chronic pain or disability
    \item Treatment-related complications (medication side effects, surgical complications)
    \item Psychological impact of chronic disease
    \item Reduced quality of life and independence
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Outcomes depend on the specific condition, its severity, the timeliness of diagnosis, and response to treatment. Many system-based diseases are chronic and require lifelong management, but good control can prevent complications and maintain function. Early intervention and adherence to treatment generally improve prognosis. Rehabilitation plays a key role in recovery from acute events and in managing chronic conditions.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Stay physically active; maintain strength and flexibility
    \item Eat a balanced, nutrient-rich diet
    \item Maintain a healthy weight
    \item Avoid smoking and limit alcohol
    \item Use protective equipment at work and during sport
    \item Keep up to date with screening and check-ups
    \item Manage existing health conditions and follow treatment plans
\end{itemize}
