\chapter{Chest Tightness and Discomfort}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Chest tightness and discomfort is a sensation of pressure, constriction, heaviness, or band-like squeezing around the chest that is not necessarily the same as sharp pain. It can arise from the airways, the heart, the stomach and oesophagus, the muscles of the chest wall, or from anxiety. Because it can signal serious conditions such as asthma, angina, or a heart attack, persistent or unexplained chest tightness always deserves careful assessment. Understanding the character of the symptom and its triggers is the key to identifying the underlying cause.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Chest tightness is a very common complaint in both general practice and emergency departments. It features prominently in asthma, which affects roughly 1 in 10 children and about 1 in 12 adults in many developed countries. Among older adults and people with risk factors such as smoking, high blood pressure, or diabetes, chest tightness may indicate narrowing of the coronary arteries. Anxiety-related chest tightness is especially common in younger adults and can recur over many years. The wide range of possible causes means that the symptom carries different significance in different people.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Airways:} asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease narrow the airways, making it difficult to breathe out and producing a sensation of tightness
    \item \textbf{Heart:} angina arises when narrowed coronary arteries cannot deliver enough blood to the heart muscle during exertion
    \item \textbf{Stomach and oesophagus:} acid reflux and spasm of the oesophagus cause a burning or squeezing feeling behind the breastbone
    \item \textbf{Anxiety:} hyperventilation and muscle tension during panic can produce chest tightness and a sense of breathlessness
    \item \textbf{Infection:} bronchitis or pneumonia may cause a tight, uncomfortable chest with cough and fever
    \item \textbf{Chest wall:} muscular strain or costochondritis can mimic tightness, particularly after exercise or a fall
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A feeling of pressure, squeezing, or constriction around the chest
    \item Tightness accompanied by wheezing, cough, or breathlessness, which points to the airways
    \item Tightness brought on by exertion and relieved by rest, which suggests angina
    \item Burning tightness after meals or when lying flat, which suggests reflux
    \item Tightness with a racing heart, tingling hands, or dizziness in anxious people
    \item Worsening of symptoms with deep breathing, coughing, or pressing on the chest wall
    \item Tightness that wakes the person from sleep, which is a warning feature for asthma or cardiac causes
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)
    \item Angina and heart attack
    \item Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease and oesophageal spasm
    \item Panic disorder and generalised anxiety
    \item Pneumonia, bronchitis, or pleurisy
    \item Costochondritis and chest wall muscle strain
    \item Anaemia or an overactive thyroid, both of which can cause a sensation of chest pressure
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor if chest tightness is new, recurring, wakes you at night, or interferes with everyday activities. Anyone with known asthma or angina should be reviewed if their symptoms become harder to control or require more reliever medicine.

\medskip
\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chest tightness with sweating, nausea, or pain spreading to the arm, neck, or jaw
    \item Severe difficulty breathing, gasping, or an inability to speak in full sentences
    \item Chest tightness that does not settle with the person's usual reliever inhaler
    \item Collapse, confusion, or loss of consciousness
    \item Sudden severe tightness after exertion, injury, or during pregnancy
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History:} when the tightness occurs, what triggers it, and what relieves it
    \item \textbf{Physical examination:} listening to the chest, and checking oxygen levels and breathing effort
    \item \textbf{Lung function testing:} spirometry to measure airflow into and out of the lungs
    \item \textbf{Peak flow monitoring:} a hand-held device used at home to track airway narrowing
    \item \textbf{ECG and cardiac tests:} to check for angina, a heart attack, or abnormal rhythms
    \item \textbf{Chest X-ray:} to exclude pneumonia, fluid around the lungs, or other problems
    \item \textbf{Trial of treatment:} relief of tightness with an inhaler or acid-suppressing medicine helps confirm the cause
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} haemoglobin, thyroid function, and inflammatory markers where appropriate
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Asthma and COPD:} reliever and preventer inhalers, together with a written action plan
    \item \textbf{Angina:} medicines to relax the coronary arteries and reduce the workload of the heart
    \item \textbf{Reflux:} acid-reducing medication and lifestyle measures such as avoiding large late meals
    \item \textbf{Anxiety:} breathing exercises, psychological therapy, and, where appropriate, medication
    \item \textbf{Chest wall causes:} rest, simple pain relief, and gentle stretching
    \item \textbf{Smoking cessation:} important for nearly every cause of chest tightness
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} review to confirm the diagnosis and adjust treatment
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A heart attack or worsening angina if cardiac tightness is dismissed
    \item A severe asthma attack requiring hospital admission and intensive treatment
    \item Recurrent panic attacks and avoidance of normal activities
    \item Chronic reflux leading to oesophagitis or narrowing of the oesophagus
    \item Anxiety and distress from not knowing the cause of the symptoms
    \item Pneumonia if a chest infection progresses untreated
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
With an accurate diagnosis, most causes of chest tightness can be managed effectively. Asthma and angina are chronic conditions, but symptoms are usually well controlled with the right treatment, and most people continue to lead active lives. Reflux-related and anxiety-related tightness often improves substantially with treatment. Anyone whose symptoms change, worsen, or fail to respond to usual treatment should be reassessed promptly, since the underlying cause can change over time and new problems may develop.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Take preventer asthma medication daily as prescribed, even when feeling well
    \item Avoid known asthma triggers, including smoke, pollen, and cold air
    \item Stop smoking and avoid second-hand smoke
    \item Eat smaller, earlier meals and avoid foods that trigger reflux
    \item Manage stress and learn breathing or relaxation techniques
    \item Exercise regularly, warming up gradually and stopping if tightness develops
    \item Attend regular reviews with a doctor and keep an asthma or angina action plan up to date
\end{itemize}
