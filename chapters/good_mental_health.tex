\chapter{Good Mental Health}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Good mental health is more than the absence of mental illness. It is a state of wellbeing in which a person can realise their potential, cope with the normal stresses of life, work productively, and contribute to their community. Good mental health involves emotional wellbeing (feeling and managing emotions), psychological wellbeing (thinking clearly and feeling good about oneself and life), and social wellbeing (connecting with others and having satisfying relationships). Like physical health, mental health exists on a spectrum and fluctuates over time, and it can be strengthened with habits and support. Protecting and promoting mental health benefits every aspect of life, from work and relationships to physical health.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Mental health problems are common. Around 1 in 5 Australians experiences a mental health condition in any given year, and nearly half will experience one in their lifetime. Anxiety, depression, and substance use disorders are the most common. Mental health is shaped by many factors, and certain groups -- including young people, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people, and people experiencing disadvantage -- carry a higher burden. Despite the prevalence of mental illness, most Australians report good or very good mental wellbeing most of the time, and mental health promotion is now recognised as a public health priority alongside the treatment of illness.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Good mental health is determined by a complex mix of biological, psychological, and social factors, the same factors that, when disrupted, contribute to mental illness.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Biological factors:} genetics, brain chemistry, physical health, sleep, and hormones
    \item \textbf{Psychological factors:} coping skills, self-esteem, resilience, thinking styles, and the ability to regulate emotions
    \item \textbf{Social factors:} supportive relationships, community connections, secure housing, meaningful work, and financial security
    \item \textbf{Life experiences:} childhood experiences, trauma, loss, and major life transitions
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle:} physical activity, nutrition, sleep, and the use of alcohol and other drugs
    \item \textbf{Protective factors:} the presence of supports and skills that buffer against stress
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Signs of good mental health include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item The ability to experience and express a full range of emotions
    \item Feeling generally positive and satisfied with life
    \item Managing stress and setbacks without being overwhelmed
    \item Maintaining satisfying relationships and connecting with others
    \item Being productive and engaged at work, study, or in daily activities
    \item Having a sense of purpose and meaning
    \item Good sleep, appetite, and energy
    \item Recovering from difficulties and adapting to change (resilience)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Recognising when wellbeing dips into illness matters for seeking help early.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Depression: persistent low mood, loss of interest, and changes in sleep or appetite lasting more than two weeks
    \item Anxiety disorders: excessive worry or fear that is hard to control and interferes with daily life
    \item Stress: a normal response to pressure that resolves when the pressure passes
    \item Burnout: exhaustion and reduced function related to prolonged work or caregiving stress
    \item Grief: sadness after a loss, which follows its own course
    \item Substance use and its effects on mood and function
    \item Physical illness, including thyroid disorders and chronic pain, which can affect mental wellbeing
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek help from a GP if low mood, anxiety, or other symptoms persist for more than two weeks, are severe, or affect work, study, or relationships. A GP can assess, provide treatment, and create a mental health care plan for subsidised psychology sessions.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Thoughts of harming yourself or ending your life, or plans to do so
    \item Hearing voices, severe paranoia, or loss of touch with reality
    \item Any mental health crisis where you or someone else is at risk
    \item Severe symptoms such as an inability to care for yourself
\end{itemize}
For immediate support in a non-emergency, Lifeline (13 11 14), Beyond Blue (1300 22 4636), and Kids Helpline (1800 55 1800) are available around the clock.

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Mental health is assessed through conversation and questionnaires rather than laboratory tests.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clinical interview:} discussion of mood, thoughts, behaviour, sleep, appetite, and life circumstances
    \item \textbf{Standardised questionnaires:} tools such as the PHQ-9 and GAD-7 screen for depression and anxiety
    \item \textbf{Risk assessment:} asking about self-harm and suicidal thoughts is a routine part of good care
    \item \textbf{Physical assessment:} a GP may check for physical causes such as thyroid disease, anaemia, or vitamin deficiency
    \item \textbf{Review of alcohol and drug use:} substance use commonly affects mental health
    \item \textbf{Ongoing review:} regular check-ins track progress and adjust support
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Good mental health is maintained and strengthened through everyday habits and, when needed, professional support.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Physical activity:} regular exercise is one of the most effective ways to improve mood and reduce anxiety
    \item \textbf{Sleep:} prioritising consistent, sufficient, good-quality sleep
    \item \textbf{Healthy eating:} a balanced diet supports brain health and mood
    \item \textbf{Connection:} nurturing relationships with family, friends, and community
    \item \textbf{Stress management:} mindfulness, relaxation, and time for enjoyable activities
    \item \textbf{Meaning and purpose:} engaging in activities that matter to you, including work, volunteering, or hobbies
    \item \textbf{Limiting alcohol and drugs:} reducing use that worsens mood
    \item \textbf{Seeking help early:} talking to a GP or counsellor before problems escalate
    \item \textbf{Professional treatment:} psychological therapies and, when appropriate, medication for mental health conditions
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
When mental health is neglected, the consequences can be significant.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Development or worsening of depression, anxiety, and other mental health conditions
    \item Effects on physical health, including increased risk of heart disease and a shorter life expectancy
    \item Impaired work performance, unemployment, and financial strain
    \item Relationship breakdown and social isolation
    \item Substance misuse used as a way of coping
    \item Reduced quality of life and wellbeing
    \item In severe cases, self-harm and suicide
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Mental health is not fixed; it can be improved at any stage of life. People who adopt protective habits, build supportive connections, and seek help early recover well and maintain good mental health over the long term. Most mental health conditions respond to treatment, and many people recover fully. Investing in mental health produces benefits across all areas of life, and even small changes -- regular activity, better sleep, and stronger connections -- can measurably improve mood and wellbeing.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Exercise regularly, aiming for at least 30 minutes of moderate activity most days
    \item Protect your sleep with a consistent routine and a calm bedtime environment
    \item Eat a varied, balanced diet and stay hydrated
    \item Stay connected: schedule time with people who matter to you
    \item Practise mindfulness, deep breathing, or relaxation daily
    \item Limit alcohol and avoid recreational drugs
    \item Take breaks and set boundaries between work and rest
    \item Spend time in nature and do activities you enjoy
    \item Seek help early from your GP or a counsellor when you notice a change
    \item Reach out to support services when you need to talk
\end{itemize}
