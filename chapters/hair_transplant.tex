\chapter{Hair Transplant}
\index{Hair Transplant}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
A hair transplant is a surgical procedure that moves hair follicles from one part of the scalp, usually the back or sides where hair is genetically resistant to balding, to areas of thinning or baldness. It is used to treat male and female pattern hair loss, and sometimes to restore hair after burns, injury, or scarring. Modern techniques, particularly follicular unit extraction (FUE) and follicular unit transplantation (FUT), produce natural-looking results. The transplanted hair falls out within weeks and then regrows permanently over several months. A hair transplant does not prevent ongoing hair loss in other areas, so many people also use medicines such as minoxidil or finasteride.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Hair loss affects the majority of men and a large proportion of women, and hair transplantation is one of the most common cosmetic surgical procedures worldwide. In many countries, thousands of hair transplants are performed each year, and the number has grown with improvements in technique. Candidates are typically men in their 20s to 50s with pattern hair loss, and increasingly women with thinning hair. The best results are in people with adequate donor hair and realistic expectations. Demand for the procedure continues to grow.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Hair transplantation works by moving genetically stable hair follicles to areas of loss.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pattern hair loss:} dihydrotestosterone (DHT) causes hair follicles in the crown and front of the scalp to shrink (miniaturise) and eventually stop producing hair
    \item \textbf{Donor dominance:} follicles from the back and sides of the scalp are genetically resistant to DHT and continue to grow after being transplanted
    \item \textbf{Follicular unit:} hair grows in natural clusters (follicular units) of one to four hairs, which are transplanted intact for natural results
    \item \textbf{FUE:} individual follicular units are extracted directly from the donor area with tiny punches
    \item \textbf{FUT (strip):} a strip of scalp is removed and dissected into grafts
    \item \textbf{Graffting:} grafts are placed into tiny incisions in the recipient area, where they establish a blood supply and grow
    \item \textbf{Timeline:} transplanted hair sheds at 2--6 weeks and begins regrowing at 3--6 months, with full results at 12--18 months
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Candidates for hair transplant surgery have specific features.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Male or female pattern hair loss that is stabilised or being managed
    \item Adequate donor hair density at the back and sides of the scalp
    \item Hair loss that has plateaued, or realistic expectations that ongoing loss will be managed
    \item Good general health and suitability for the procedure
    \item In women, confirmation that the hair loss is not due to another cause
    \item Desire for a permanent solution to thinning or baldness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Before transplant, other causes of hair loss should be excluded or treated.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Telogen effluvium (temporary shedding), which usually resolves without surgery
    \item Alopecia areata, which is autoimmune and not suited to transplantation in active disease
    \item Thyroid disease and iron deficiency, which should be treated first
    \item Scarring alopecia, which requires different management
    \item Medication-related hair loss
    \item Traction alopecia from hairstyles
    \item Body dysmorphic disorder, in which expectations are unrealistic and surgery is not appropriate
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
People considering a hair transplant should first see their GP or a dermatologist to confirm the cause of hair loss and discuss medical treatments, which are often the first step. Surgery should be performed by a qualified, experienced surgeon.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item This is an elective procedure; however, after surgery seek urgent care for heavy bleeding, severe swelling, or signs of infection such as spreading redness and fever
    \item Sudden chest pain or breathing difficulty, which may indicate a rare complication
    \item Severe allergic reaction to medicines used during the procedure
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Assessment before surgery is thorough.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hair loss assessment:} the pattern and cause of hair loss are confirmed
    \item \textbf{Donor assessment:} density and quality of donor hair are evaluated
    \item \textbf{Medical history:} health conditions, medicines, and bleeding risk
    \item \textbf{Discussion of expectations:} the surgeon explains what is achievable, the timeline, and the limitations
    \item \textbf{Planning:} the number of grafts and the design of the hairline are planned
    \item \textbf{Photography:} baseline photographs document the starting point
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
The procedure and its follow-up are managed in stages.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{FUE:} thousands of follicular units are extracted and implanted in a single session, which can take many hours
    \item \textbf{FUT:} a strip is removed and dissected; this is less common now
    \item \textbf{Anaesthesia:} local anaesthetic, sometimes with sedation
    \item \textbf{Recovery:} scabs and redness settle within one to two weeks
    \item \textbf{Post-operative care:} instructions on washing, activity, and avoiding trauma to grafts
    \item \textbf{Combined treatment:} continuing minoxidil or finasteride to prevent loss of non-transplanted hair
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} review visits and photography track growth
    \item \textbf{Second sessions:} some people have further transplants as hair loss progresses
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Bleeding and bruising
    \item Infection, which is uncommon
    \item Swelling of the forehead and eyes
    \item Numbness or altered sensation in the donor or recipient areas
    \item Poor graft survival or unnatural results in unskilled hands
    \item Scarring, including visible scars in the donor area with FUT
    \item Ongoing hair loss in non-transplanted areas if medical treatment is not continued
    \item The risk of an unsatisfactory result when expectations were unrealistic
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
A hair transplant performed by an experienced surgeon produces permanent, natural-looking hair in the transplanted areas, with results that most people are very satisfied with. Transplanted hair continues to grow for life, but the procedure does not stop pattern hair loss elsewhere, so ongoing medical treatment is important. The full cosmetic result develops over 12--18 months. Most people who have a transplant with appropriate expectations are pleased with the outcome and the improvement in confidence it brings.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Start with medical treatment, such as minoxidil or finasteride, which may slow or stop loss
    \item Choose a qualified surgeon with experience in hair transplantation
    \item Discuss realistic expectations and the timeline for results
    \item Follow post-operative instructions carefully, including washing and activity restrictions
    \item Protect the scalp from sun and injury after surgery
    \item Continue medical treatment to protect non-transplanted hair
    \item Attend follow-up appointments
    \item Be patient, as regrowth takes months
    \item Do not smoke, as smoking impairs healing
\end{itemize}
