\chapter{Polyarteritis nodosa}
\index{Polyarteritis nodosa}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; seek specialist advice for individual care.}

\section*{What it is}
Polyarteritis nodosa (PAN) is a rare form of vasculitis in which inflammation damages medium-sized arteries. It can reduce blood flow to the skin, muscles, nerves, kidneys, gastrointestinal tract, or other organs. It is not the same as inflammation of the smallest blood vessels or atherosclerosis.

\section*{Symptoms}
Symptoms vary and may include fever, fatigue, weight loss, muscle or joint pain, tender skin nodules or ulcers, nerve pain or weakness, abdominal pain after eating, high blood pressure, testicular pain, or kidney problems. Symptoms can progress quickly and have many other possible causes.

\section*{Diagnosis}
Assessment may include blood and urine tests, tests for infections such as hepatitis B or C, imaging of affected arteries, nerve or muscle tests, and sometimes biopsy. Diagnosis and treatment should be led by a specialist because immunosuppression can be harmful if the cause is an infection or another condition.

\section*{Treatment and follow-up}
Treatment depends on severity and organ involvement and may include glucocorticoids, other immunosuppressive medicines, treatment of an associated infection, blood-pressure control, wound care, and rehabilitation. Monitoring is needed for disease activity, organ injury, infection, and medicine adverse effects.

\section*{When to seek urgent care}
Seek emergency help for severe abdominal pain, black or bloody stools, chest pain, breathlessness, sudden weakness or numbness, severe headache, confusion, loss of vision, very high blood pressure symptoms, or rapidly worsening illness.
