\chapter{Cataracts}
\index{Cataracts}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
A cataract is a clouding of the lens of the eye, the normally clear structure that focuses light onto the retina. When the lens becomes cloudy, light is scattered and blocked, producing blurred, hazy, or faded vision. Cataracts usually develop gradually with age and most commonly affect both eyes, although one eye is often worse than the other.

Cataracts are not a growth and not a film over the eye; they are changes within the lens itself. Vision loss from cataract is treatable: surgery to remove the cloudy lens and replace it with an artificial implant is highly effective. Because the condition progresses slowly, many people live comfortably with early cataracts for years before treatment is needed.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Cataract is the leading cause of blindness worldwide and one of the most common causes of visual impairment, accounting for around half of all cases of blindness globally. The risk increases steadily with age: about one in three people over 65 has some cataract, and the majority of people over 80 have significant lens clouding.

Age-related cataract is by far the most common type, but cataracts also occur in children and younger adults, usually due to inherited conditions, diabetes, trauma, steroid use, or intrauterine infection. Globally, the burden falls most heavily on low- and middle-income countries, where access to surgery is limited and untreated cataract remains a major cause of preventable blindness.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The lens is made of water and proteins arranged to keep it transparent. Cataract forms when these proteins clump together or change structure, causing the lens to lose its clarity:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Ageing:} the most important factor; protein changes accumulate over decades
    \item \textbf{Ultraviolet light exposure:} long-term sun exposure is linked to earlier cataract formation
    \item \textbf{Diabetes:} high blood sugar promotes lens swelling and protein change
    \item \textbf{Smoking and alcohol:} both are associated with an increased risk
    \item \textbf{Corticosteroid use:} prolonged steroid treatment, especially of the eye or in high doses
    \item \textbf{Trauma:} injury to the eye, including penetrating injury or blunt force
    \item \textbf{Inherited and congenital causes:} genetic conditions and infections during pregnancy, such as rubella
    \item \textbf{Other eye conditions and surgery:} inflammation inside the eye or previous eye operations
\end{itemize}

Whatever the cause, the result is increasing light scatter and reduced light transmission through the lens, with progressive loss of visual clarity.

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Gradually worsening, usually painless blurring of vision
    \item Glare, halos, or starbursts around lights, especially at night
    \item Difficulty seeing in dim light or in bright sunlight
    \item Colours that look faded, yellowed, or less vivid
    \item Frequent changes to glasses prescriptions that give only partial improvement
    \item Trouble reading, driving, or recognising faces
    \item Occasional double vision in one eye
    \item In mature cataracts, the pupil may look grey or white
\end{itemize}

Symptoms develop over months to years and may be noticed first when driving at night or when changing light levels.

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Refractive error:} short-sightedness or long-sightedness that improves with glasses
    \item \textbf{Glaucoma:} damage to the optic nerve, often without early symptoms but with visual field loss
    \item \textbf{Macular degeneration:} loss of central vision due to retinal ageing, more common in older people
    \item \textbf{Diabetic retinopathy:} retinal damage from diabetes that reduces vision
    \item \textbf{Corneal disease:} clouding, scarring, or swelling of the front surface of the eye
    \item \textbf{Retinal detachment or vitreous disease:} floaters, flashes, or a curtain-like loss of vision
\end{itemize}

Cataract frequently coexists with several of these, and an eye examination by an optometrist or ophthalmologist is needed to determine how much each contributes to reduced vision.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See an optometrist or ophthalmologist if you notice blurred vision, glare, halos, or difficulty with reading, driving, or daily activities. Regular eye examinations are recommended after the age of 60, even without symptoms, and earlier if you have diabetes or a family history of eye disease.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:} sudden or rapid loss of vision, severe eye pain, or flashes of light with a curtain-like loss of part of the field of vision, which may indicate retinal detachment or another eye emergency.

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History:} the pattern of symptoms, their effect on daily life, and risk factors such as diabetes, steroids, trauma, and family history
    \item \textbf{Visual acuity testing:} measurement of reading and distance vision with and without glasses
    \item \textbf{Slit-lamp examination:} magnification of the front of the eye to see the type, position, and severity of the cataract
    \item \textbf{Dilated retinal examination:} inspection of the retina and optic nerve through the widened pupil
    \item \textbf{Glare testing:} assessment of how much light scattering affects vision
    \item \textbf{General health review:} blood glucose or other tests where an underlying cause is suspected
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Observation:} early cataracts that do not affect daily life can simply be monitored
    \item \textbf{Optical aids:} new glasses, brighter lighting, and anti-glare lenses can help in the early stages
    \item \textbf{Cataract surgery:} removal of the cloudy lens with implantation of a clear artificial lens, the only treatment that cures the cataract itself
    \item \textbf{Treatment of underlying causes:} good diabetic control and avoiding or reducing steroids where possible
    \item \textbf{Timing of surgery:} performed when symptoms interfere with daily activities, work, or driving, or when the cataract prevents treatment of other eye problems
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Progressive visual loss leading to difficulty reading, driving, and performing daily tasks
    \item Increased risk of falls and fractures in older people because of poor vision
    \item Reduced quality of life, social isolation, and, in some people, low mood
    \item Advanced or neglected cataracts may cause acute glaucoma in rare cases
    \item Untreated cataract is a major cause of preventable blindness worldwide
    \item Post-operative complications such as posterior capsule opacification, which is treatable with laser
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Cataracts are progressive, and vision usually continues to worsen slowly without treatment. However, outcomes are excellent with surgery, which restores useful vision in the vast majority of people and is one of the safest and most effective operations performed. The final visual result depends partly on the health of the rest of the eye, so coexisting conditions such as macular degeneration or glaucoma limit the best achievable vision. For people who are not ready for or suitable for surgery, visual aids and good lighting can maintain function in the meantime.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Protect the eyes from ultraviolet light with sunglasses and a hat
    \item Do not smoke, and limit alcohol intake
    \item Control diabetes and other chronic conditions carefully
    \item Eat a diet rich in fruit and vegetables, including antioxidants such as vitamin C
    \item Have regular eye examinations after the age of 60, or earlier with risk factors
    \item Use brighter lighting and anti-glare glasses if early symptoms develop
    \item Report sudden vision changes to an eye care professional without delay
\end{itemize}
