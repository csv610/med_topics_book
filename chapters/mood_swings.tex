\chapter{Mood swings}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Mood swings refer to rapid, noticeable shifts in an individual's emotional state, characterised by transitions between feelings of happiness, irritability, sadness, or anxiety. While minor fluctuations in mood are a normal part of the human experience, frequent, extreme, or unpredictable mood swings can disrupt daily functioning and strain personal relationships. Mood swings can occur as a physiological response to stress, hormonal changes (such as during puberty, pregnancy, or menopause), sleep deprivation, or poor nutrition. However, they can also serve as a key clinical sign of underlying mental health conditions, including bipolar disorder, borderline personality disorder, or major depressive disorder.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Mood swings are an extremely common phenomenon, experienced at some point by nearly all individuals. They are particularly prevalent during phases of significant hormonal transition: up to 75--80\% of menstruating individuals experience mild premenstrual mood shifts, and approximately 20--50\% report moderate Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) or severe Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder (PMDD). During menopause, up to 70\% of women report mood changes alongside vasomotor symptoms. In the psychiatric context, mood swings are a core diagnostic feature of borderline personality disorder (which affects 1--2\% of the general population) and bipolar disorder. In young people, mood swings are frequently reported, with studies indicating that emotional lability peaks during early-to-mid adolescence before stabilising in adulthood.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The physiological and psychological mechanisms underlying mood swings involve hormonal, neurological, and environmental triggers.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hormonal fluctuations:} Rapid changes in levels of oestrogen, progesterone, testosterone, and thyroid hormones directly affect neurotransmitter pathways in the brain that regulate mood.
    \item \textbf{Chronic stress and cortisol:} Elevated cortisol levels from ongoing psychological or physical stress impair the prefrontal cortex's ability to regulate emotional responses from the amygdala.
    \item \textbf{Sleep deprivation:} Inadequate or disrupted sleep impairs emotional regulation, leading to heightened reactivity, irritability, and rapid mood transitions.
    \item \textbf{Neurotransmitter imbalance:} Transient alterations in the synthesis, release, or receptor binding of serotonin, dopamine, and noradrenaline.
    \item \textbf{Substance use and withdrawal:} Active consumption or withdrawal from alcohol, caffeine, nicotine, or illicit drugs (such as stimulants) causes rapid changes in brain chemistry and emotional stability.
    \item \textbf{Nutritional factors:} Rapid spikes and crashes in blood glucose levels caused by irregular eating patterns or high-sugar diets can induce irritability and lethargy.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
The presentation of mood swings varies based on the trigger, but is characterised by shifts in affect, energy, and behaviour.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rapid transitions in affect:} Suddenly shifting from a state of calm or happiness to irritability, anger, anxiety, or sadness within a short period.
    \item \textbf{Emotional hyperreactivity:} Overreacting to minor setbacks or everyday stressors with intense crying spells, outbursts of anger, or panic.
    \item \textbf{Fluctuations in energy and motivation:} Experiencing bursts of high energy followed by sudden periods of extreme fatigue, lethargy, or apathy.
    \item \textbf{Physical concomitants:} Accompanying symptoms such as headaches, muscle tension, palpitations, or digestive discomfort during emotional spikes.
    \item \textbf{Interpersonal conflicts:} Increased frequency of arguments or misunderstandings with family, friends, or colleagues due to unpredictable emotional responses.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
To manage mood swings effectively, it is essential to distinguish benign physiological fluctuations from clinical psychiatric and medical disorders.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder (PMDD):} Severe, disabling mood swings occurring strictly during the luteal phase of the menstrual cycle and resolving with the onset of menses.
    \item \textbf{Bipolar disorder:} Characterised by distinct, prolonged periods of mania or hypomania lasting days or weeks, rather than hours-long shifts.
    \item \textbf{Borderline personality disorder:} Characterised by a pervasive pattern of instability in interpersonal relationships and self-image, with mood shifts typically lasting a few hours.
    \item \textbf{Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD):} Presents with emotional dysregulation, impulsivity, and short-lived mood spikes, often triggered by frustration or rejection.
    \item \textbf{Thyroid disease:} Hypothyroidism or hyperthyroidism, which causes systemic metabolic changes that manifest as depression or hyperactive anxiety.
    \item \textbf{Substance abuse disorders:} Where mood changes are directly linked to drug intoxication or withdrawal cycles.
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Individuals should seek medical advice if their mood swings are frequent, extreme, cause significant distress, or interfere with their ability to work or maintain relationships.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Thoughts of suicide, self-harm, or harming others.
    \item Severe, uncontrollable agitation or violent outbursts.
    \item Loss of contact with reality, such as hearing voices or holding beliefs not grounded in reality (psychosis).
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
A thorough diagnostic assessment aims to identify any underlying physical or psychological conditions contributing to emotional instability.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clinical interview and mood mapping:} Recording the timing, triggers, and duration of mood changes, often using a daily mood diary over several weeks.
    \item \textbf{Hormonal and endocrine screening:} Testing thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), free T3 and T4, and sometimes reproductive hormones (FSH, LH, progesterone) if menopause or PMS is suspected.
    \item \textbf{Blood glucose testing:} Fasting blood glucose or HbA1c to rule out hypoglycaemic episodes or diabetes-related mood changes.
    \item \textbf{Psychiatric screening scales:} Utilising validated questionnaires for depression, anxiety, ADHD, or personality disorders to identify underlying psychiatric pathology.
    \item \textbf{Medication and substance review:} Detailed assessment of prescription medications, over-the-counter drugs, alcohol, and illicit substance use.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Treatment is tailored to the primary cause of the mood swings, focusing on lifestyle interventions, psychological strategies, and medical therapies.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle modifications:} Prioritising sleep hygiene, eating regular balanced meals with complex carbohydrates, and reducing intake of stimulants like caffeine and nicotine.
    \item \textbf{Psychotherapy:} Dialectical Behaviour Therapy (DBT) is the gold standard for emotional dysregulation; Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) helps identify and reframe negative thought patterns.
    \item \textbf{Stress management techniques:} Practising mindfulness, meditation, deep breathing exercises, or yoga to regulate the autonomic nervous system.
    \item \textbf{Hormonal therapies:} Combined oral contraceptive pills, progesterone therapy, or hormone replacement therapy (HRT) for menopausal or menstrual-related mood swings.
    \item \textbf{Pharmacological agents:} Low-dose antidepressants (SSRIs) for PMDD or depression, or mood stabilisers if a bipolar spectrum disorder is diagnosed.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
Chronic, unmanaged mood swings can lead to significant impairments in physical and mental well-being.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Relationship breakdown:} Strain and conflict with partners, family members, and friends due to emotional unpredictability.
    \item \textbf{Occupational instability:} Poor performance, frequent absences, or loss of employment resulting from emotional outbursts or low-energy phases.
    \item \textbf{Development of chronic psychiatric disorders:} Progression of untreated emotional dysregulation into major depression, severe anxiety, or substance abuse.
    \item \textbf{Self-destructive behaviours:} Engaging in impulsive activities, such as reckless spending, substance abuse, or self-harm, during periods of emotional distress.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The prognosis for individuals experiencing mood swings is highly favourable, especially when the underlying cause is identified and addressed early. Physiological mood swings related to developmental stages (like adolescence) or transient life stressors usually resolve spontaneously over time. Those linked to menstrual cycles or menopause respond well to targeted hormonal or lifestyle interventions. For mood swings arising from psychiatric conditions, active engagement in psychotherapy (such as DBT) and adherence to medical treatments can lead to substantial, long-term stabilisation of affect and a return to normal functioning.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
Self-care strategies are vital for maintaining emotional equilibrium and preventing triggers.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Keep a mood and symptom journal:} Track daily mood, sleep quality, diet, and menstrual cycle to identify personal patterns and triggers.
    \item \textbf{Engage in regular aerobic exercise:} Exercise releases endorphins, which act as natural mood lifters and stabilisers.
    \item \textbf{Establish healthy boundaries:} Learn to say no to excessive work or social commitments to prevent burnout and stress-induced emotional changes.
    \item \textbf{Avoid alcohol and drug consumption:} These substances destabilise brain chemistry and worsen emotional highs and lows.
    \item \textbf{Implement relaxation practices:} Dedicate time each day to activities that promote relaxation, such as reading, listening to music, or spending time in nature.
\end{itemize}
