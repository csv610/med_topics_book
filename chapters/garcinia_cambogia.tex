\chapter{Garcinia Cambogia}
\index{Garcinia Cambogia}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Garcinia cambogia is a tropical fruit, also called Malabar tamarind, whose rind contains a substance called hydroxycitric acid (HCA). Extracts of the rind are sold as dietary supplements, promoted for weight loss and appetite control. The theory is that HCA blocks an enzyme involved in fat production and increases serotonin, which may reduce appetite. However, the evidence that garcinia cambogia causes meaningful weight loss is weak, and the quality of supplements varies. It is not a substitute for diet and exercise, it can interact with medicines, and it has been linked to rare but serious liver injury. This chapter explains what garcinia cambogia is, what the evidence shows, and the risks of taking it.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Garcinia cambogia is one of the most widely promoted weight-loss supplements, with sales rising dramatically after media endorsement. Surveys suggest that a significant minority of people trying to lose weight have used herbal and over-the-counter supplements, including garcinia cambogia. Despite its popularity, clinical trials show that any weight loss achieved is small and of questionable clinical significance. Reports to regulators have linked garcinia cambogia-containing products to rare cases of liver injury, and the supplement is not recommended by local medical and dietetic bodies.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hydroxycitric acid (HCA):} the active substance in the fruit rind, proposed to inhibit the enzyme ATP-citrate lyase, which is involved in making fat from carbohydrate
    \item \textbf{Proposed effects:} less fat production, reduced appetite through raised serotonin, and modestly lower food intake
    \item \textbf{The evidence:} clinical trials show small, inconsistent weight loss, typically less than 1 kg over several weeks, and many trials are small or poorly conducted
    \item \textbf{Absorption:} HCA is poorly absorbed, and little reaches the bloodstream in active form
    \item \textbf{Metabolic effect:} even if the enzyme is inhibited, the body has multiple pathways for fat synthesis, so the practical effect is minimal
    \item \textbf{Liver injury:} rare but serious cases of liver damage have been reported with some products, possibly from the extract or contaminants
    \item \textbf{Interactions:} supplements can interact with diabetes medicines, anticoagulants, and other drugs
    \item \textbf{Quality:} supplement content varies between brands and batches, and products may contain undeclared or harmful ingredients
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The intended effect: modest, if any, weight loss, which is not reliably reproducible
    \item No difference in energy or wellbeing compared with placebo in most people
    \item Possible side effects: digestive upset, headache, and nausea
    \item Rare serious effects: jaundice, dark urine, and other signs of liver injury
    \item Interactions with medicines, especially for diabetes, blood pressure, and blood clotting
    \item The cost of the supplement without the promised benefit
    \item The absence of any effect on the lifestyle changes that actually produce lasting weight loss
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Considerations when assessing garcinia cambogia use include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Whether the supplement adds anything to diet and exercise, which are the effective treatments for overweight
    \item The possibility of interactions with prescribed medicines
    \item The risk of liver injury, which is rare but serious
    \item The quality and labelling of the specific product
    \item Underlying causes of weight gain that should be assessed, including thyroid disease and medicines
    \item The distinction between short-term weight loss and sustained health improvement
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Anyone taking garcinia cambogia, or considering it, should discuss it with a doctor, especially if they have medical conditions or take medicines. Anyone taking it who develops jaundice, dark urine, abdominal pain, or unusual fatigue should stop it and seek medical care immediately.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Signs of liver injury: jaundice, dark urine, severe abdominal pain, or vomiting
    \item Severe allergic reactions
    \item Any emergency symptoms
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Medical review:} assessment of weight, health conditions, and medicines
    \item \textbf{Liver function tests:} for anyone with symptoms suggesting liver injury while taking the supplement
    \item \textbf{Assessment of interactions:} review of all medicines and supplements
    \item \textbf{Evidence review:} the doctor can summarise the trial evidence showing minimal benefit
    \item \textbf{Referral:} to a dietitian for evidence-based weight management
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Stop the supplement:} there is no established benefit that justifies continued use
    \item \textbf{Evidence-based weight management:} a structured program of dietary change, physical activity, and behavioural support, which produces lasting results
    \item \textbf{Dietitian input:} individualised dietary advice
    \item \textbf{Treat underlying conditions:} thyroid disease, diabetes, and other conditions that affect weight
    \item \textbf{Medication review:} adjust medicines that cause weight gain where possible
    \item \textbf{Monitor liver function:} for people who have had symptoms, until tests normalise
    \item \textbf{Report adverse effects:} to the Therapeutic Goods Administration to help monitor supplement safety
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Rare but serious liver injury, including cases requiring transplantation
    \item Interactions with diabetes, blood-thinning, and other medicines
    \item Financial cost without meaningful benefit
    \item Delay in effective weight management while relying on an ineffective product
    \item Side effects including digestive symptoms and headache
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The outlook for weight loss depends on the approach, not the supplement. People who stop garcinia cambogia and adopt structured diet and exercise programs achieve sustainable weight loss and health improvement, while supplements alone produce at best trivial, temporary results. Most people who have taken garcinia cambogia experience no harm, but the small risk of serious liver injury, combined with the lack of benefit, means the sensible course is to avoid it. Evidence-based weight management remains the reliable path.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Discuss any weight-loss supplement with a doctor before taking it
    \item Be sceptical of products promising rapid weight loss; if it sounds too good to be true, it usually is
    \item Base weight management on diet, exercise, and behaviour change
    \item See a dietitian for an individualised plan
    \item Stop the supplement and seek urgent care for jaundice, dark urine, or abdominal pain
    \item Tell the doctor about all supplements when medicines are prescribed
    \item Check product information and report adverse effects to the TGA
\end{itemize}
