\chapter{Human Growth Hormone}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Human growth hormone (GH) is a hormone produced by the pituitary gland that drives growth in childhood, maintains muscle and bone in adulthood, and influences metabolism throughout life. Growth hormone deficiency in children causes slow growth and short stature, and in adults it causes changes in body composition, energy, and wellbeing. Excess growth hormone causes gigantism in children and acromegaly in adults. Growth hormone is available as a medication, given by injection, to treat deficiency and a few other conditions. Its use requires specialist assessment, and it is misused by some athletes for performance enhancement.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Growth hormone deficiency is uncommon, affecting around 1 in 4,000--10,000 children, and it is one of several causes of short stature. In adults, deficiency is usually acquired from pituitary disease or its treatment, including after brain surgery or radiotherapy. Acromegaly affects around 1 in 100,000 people. Growth hormone treatment in Australia is subsidised for approved indications, including childhood deficiency, and its use for other purposes is uncommon. Use as a performance-enhancing drug occurs but is discouraged and often involves unregulated products.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Production:} growth hormone is made by the anterior pituitary and acts through insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1)
    \item \textbf{Deficiency in children:} congenital or acquired pituitary problems cause slow growth
    \item \textbf{Deficiency in adults:} pituitary tumours, surgery, radiotherapy, and head injury
    \item \textbf{Excess:} pituitary adenomas secrete too much GH, causing gigantism before growth plates close and acromegaly after
    \item \textbf{Pathophysiology:} GH drives growth at the growth plates, builds muscle, and mobilises fat; deficiency slows growth and alters body composition
    \item \textbf{Action:} most effects are mediated by IGF-1 produced in the liver
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Childhood deficiency: short stature, slow growth, delayed puberty, and a chubby appearance
    \item Adult deficiency: fatigue, reduced muscle mass, increased body fat, poor bone density, and reduced quality of life
    \item Acromegaly: enlarged hands and feet, facial changes, sweating, headaches, and joint pain
    \item Gigantism: excessive height in childhood
    \item Treatment with GH: improved growth and body composition in deficiency
    \item Symptoms depend on whether levels are too low or too high
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Short stature has many causes, including familial short stature, constitutional delay, and other medical conditions
    \item Growth hormone deficiency is confirmed by stimulation tests
    \item Acromegaly is distinguished from gigantism by growth plate status
    \item IGF-1 and glucose suppression tests diagnose excess
    \item Specialist endocrine assessment is required for diagnosis
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Children with growth below the expected range should be assessed by a paediatrician or endocrinologist. Adults with pituitary disease and symptoms of deficiency or excess should have endocrine review.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe headache with visual disturbance (possible pituitary emergency)
    \item Sudden vision loss
    \item Collapse or confusion
    \item Any life-threatening emergency
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Growth measurement:} serial height and growth velocity in children
    \item \textbf{IGF-1 blood test:} first-line screening for both deficiency and excess
    \item \textbf{Growth hormone stimulation tests:} confirm deficiency
    \item \textbf{Glucose tolerance test:} confirms excess, which fails to suppress GH
    \item \textbf{MRI of the pituitary:} for tumours and structural causes
    \item \textbf{Bone age X-ray:} in children
    \item \textbf{Other pituitary tests:} for coexisting hormone deficiencies
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Deficiency in children:} daily growth hormone injections until growth is complete
    \item \textbf{Deficiency in adults:} low-dose GH replacement, titrated by IGF-1 levels
    \item \textbf{Excess:} surgical removal of the pituitary adenoma, with medication and radiotherapy as needed
    \item \textbf{Monitoring:} IGF-1 levels, growth, and side effects are followed
    \item \textbf{Specialist care:} endocrinologists manage all phases of treatment
    \item \textbf{Other pituitary hormones:} replaced as needed
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle:} exercise and nutrition support bone and muscle health
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Untreated deficiency: short stature in children, reduced wellbeing in adults
    \item Untreated acromegaly: heart disease, diabetes, arthritis, and reduced life expectancy
    \item Treatment side effects: fluid retention, joint pain, and raised blood sugar
    \item Pituitary surgery complications
    \item Misuse of growth hormone: unregulated products, side effects, and no proven performance benefit
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
With timely treatment, children with growth hormone deficiency achieve normal or near-normal adult height, and adults feel better with replacement. Acromegaly is controlled effectively with surgery and medical therapy, and life expectancy returns toward normal with early treatment. Growth hormone treatment is safe when prescribed and monitored by specialists. Overall outcomes for growth hormone disorders are excellent with modern endocrine care.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Ensure children have growth measured at health checks
    \item Seek early assessment for short stature or slow growth
    \item Attend specialist endocrine follow-up
    \item Take growth hormone injections exactly as prescribed
    \item Do not use unregulated growth hormone products
    \item Maintain healthy diet and exercise
    \item Monitor for symptoms of pituitary problems and report them
\end{itemize}
