\chapter{Heater Safety}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Heater safety covers the precautions needed to use home heating safely and avoid the serious hazards associated with heaters: fire, burns, carbon monoxide poisoning, and electric shock. Heaters come in many forms, including electric, gas, wood, and open-flame types, each with specific risks and safety rules. In Australia, house fires and injuries are more common in winter, and many are caused by heaters used carelessly, such as clothes drying on them, being left on overnight, or being placed too close to flammable materials. Simple, consistent safety habits prevent most heater-related injuries and deaths.

\section*{Epidemiology}
House fires kill around 60 people and injure hundreds more in Australia each year, and winter is the peak season. Heating equipment is involved in a substantial proportion of winter house fires, with common causes including curtains or bedding touching heaters, clothes left to dry on them, and faulty or poorly maintained appliances. Carbon monoxide poisoning is less common in Australia than in colder countries but occurs with unflued gas heaters in poorly ventilated rooms. Older adults, young children, and people with disabilities are overrepresented among fire deaths.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Combustion:} gas, wood, and kerosene heaters burn fuel and produce heat, but also carbon monoxide and other gases if ventilation is poor
    \item \textbf{Fire:} heat ignites nearby flammable materials such as curtains, bedding, clothing, and paper
    \item \textbf{Burns:} contact with hot surfaces, particularly in children and older people with reduced mobility
    \item \textbf{Electrical faults:} damaged cords, overloaded power boards, and old or faulty appliances cause fires and electric shock
    \item \textbf{Carbon monoxide poisoning:} incomplete combustion produces this odourless, colourless gas that binds to blood haemoglobin and starves the body of oxygen
    \item \textbf{Faulty installation:} incorrect flue fitting or venting of gas and wood heaters
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Fire-related injuries: burns, smoke inhalation, and blast injuries
    \item Burns vary from minor redness to deep, life-threatening injuries
    \item Smoke inhalation causes coughing, breathlessness, and carbon monoxide poisoning
    \item Carbon monoxide poisoning causes headache, dizziness, nausea, confusion, and collapse; it is often mistaken for flu
    \item Electric shock causes burns, tingling, muscle spasm, and cardiac arrest in severe cases
    \item Children may have burns on hands, face, or chest from touching or falling onto heaters
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Carbon monoxide poisoning mimics flu, gastroenteritis, migraine, and heart disease, and is missed unless it is considered
    \item Smoke inhalation symptoms resemble asthma and pneumonia
    \item Burns must be assessed for depth, size, and airway involvement
    \item Unexplained headaches or nausea in winter should prompt consideration of unflued gas heaters
    \item Fires may involve pre-existing conditions such as epilepsy or substance use, which need separate management
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor for any significant burn, persistent headache or nausea in a home with gas heating, or any symptom suggesting carbon monoxide exposure. All electrical injuries need medical assessment.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Any house fire, however small; get everyone out and call the fire brigade
    \item Burns involving the face, hands, feet, or genitals, burns larger than a palm, or burns with blisters
    \item Smoke inhalation with coughing, breathlessness, or confusion
    \item Suspected carbon monoxide poisoning: multiple people unwell with headache and nausea in a closed space
    \item Electric shock with collapse, chest pain, or irregular heartbeat
    \item A child with any burn or suspected inhalation injury
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Fire investigation:} after a fire, the cause is investigated to prevent recurrence
    \item \textbf{Burn assessment:} depth, size, and location determine treatment and whether hospital care is needed
    \item \textbf{Carbon monoxide testing:} blood carboxyhaemoglobin levels confirm exposure
    \item \textbf{Appliance inspection:} licensed gas fitters and electricians check heaters, flues, and ventilation
    \item \textbf{Smoke alarm checks:} testing alarms after any incident
    \item \textbf{Medical evaluation:} assessment of smoke inhalation and any injuries
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Fire response:} get everyone out, stay out, and call 000
    \item \textbf{Burn first aid:} cool burns under running water for 20 minutes, remove jewellery and clothing, cover with a clean dressing, and seek medical care
    \item \textbf{Carbon monoxide poisoning:} move to fresh air, turn off the heater, open windows, and seek urgent medical care; high-dose oxygen is the treatment
    \item \textbf{Heater maintenance:} have heaters serviced by qualified technicians and flues checked annually
    \item \textbf{Safe placement:} keep heaters clear of curtains, bedding, and clothing, and stable on level surfaces
    \item \textbf{Electrical safety:} replace damaged cords, use approved power boards, and never run cords under rugs
    \item \textbf{Installation:} use licensed gas fitters and electricians for installation and repairs
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Burns and scarring, including severe disfiguring and disabling injuries
    \item Smoke inhalation injury to the airways and lungs
    \item Carbon monoxide poisoning can cause lasting neurological damage or death
    \item House fires cause property loss, displacement, and psychological trauma
    \item Death, particularly among older adults and children
    \item Electric shock can cause cardiac arrest and neurological injury
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Heater-related injuries are almost entirely preventable, and when safety rules are followed, heating is safe in virtually all homes. Minor burns heal with simple care, while major burns require specialist treatment and can leave lasting effects. Carbon monoxide poisoning is fully reversible when treated early with oxygen. Following the basic precautions of clearance, maintenance, ventilation, and smoke alarms protects families from the worst outcomes of winter heating.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Keep a one-metre clearance around all heaters and never dry clothes on them
    \item Install working smoke alarms on every level and test them monthly
    \item Use electric blankets and heaters according to the manufacturer's instructions
    \item Have gas heaters and wood stoves serviced annually by a licensed professional
    \item Ventilate rooms with unflued gas heaters and never sleep with them running
    \item Install a carbon monoxide alarm where gas or wood heating is used
    \item Use electrical appliances safely and replace damaged cords
    \item Supervise children around heaters and fit safety guards or barriers
\end{itemize}
