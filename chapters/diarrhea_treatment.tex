\chapter{Diarrhoea Treatment}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Diarrhoea treatment focuses on preventing and treating dehydration, which is the main danger of diarrhoea, while the underlying cause resolves. For most people, treatment at home with fluids, rest, and a light diet is all that is needed. Oral rehydration solution is the cornerstone of treatment because it replaces both water and the salts lost in the stool. Medicines have a limited role, and antibiotics are only used for specific infections. Severe dehydration requires hospital treatment with intravenous fluids. This chapter explains how to treat diarrhoea at home and when medical care is needed.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Most people with diarrhoea are managed at home, and only a minority need medical care or hospital admission. Oral rehydration therapy has dramatically reduced deaths from diarrhoea worldwide. In Australia, dehydration from gastroenteritis is a common reason for children to present to emergency departments, and a small proportion need hospital admission for intravenous fluids. Older adults in residential care are at particular risk of dehydration and complications.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Fluid loss:} diarrhoea and vomiting remove water and electrolytes from the body
    \item \textbf{Dehydration:} if losses exceed intake, the blood volume falls and organs are deprived of oxygen
    \item \textbf{Oral rehydration solution:} contains water, glucose, and salts in the right balance; glucose helps the gut absorb water and salts
    \item \textbf{Electrolyte disturbance:} sodium and potassium imbalances can cause weakness, confusion, and heart rhythm problems
    \item \textbf{Underlying cause:} most diarrhoea is infectious and self-limiting, so treatment targets the effects rather than the cause
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Mild dehydration: thirst, dry mouth, and darker urine
    \item Moderate dehydration: reduced urine output, dizziness, and fatigue
    \item Severe dehydration: confusion, sunken eyes, rapid heart rate, low blood pressure, and reduced skin elasticity
    \item Response to treatment: improved hydration and less frequent stools over days
    \item Warning signs needing medical care: blood in the stool, high fever, severe abdominal pain, or inability to drink
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Self-limiting infectious diarrhoea versus diarrhoea needing specific treatment
    \item Mild versus moderate versus severe dehydration, which determines the treatment setting
    \item Oral rehydration versus intravenous fluid therapy
    \item Diarrhoea requiring antibiotics versus diarrhoea where antibiotics are not indicated
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek medical care if there is blood in the stool, high fever, severe pain, persistent vomiting, or signs of dehydration, or if the person is a baby, young child, older adult, or has a chronic illness.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Confusion, drowsiness, fainting, or inability to wake
    \item No urine output for many hours
    \item A baby who is listless, floppy, or has a sunken fontanelle
    \item Vomiting that prevents any fluids being kept down
    \item Signs of shock: rapid heart rate, cold skin, or low blood pressure
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clinical assessment:} degree of dehydration is judged from symptoms and signs
    \item \textbf{Stool tests:} may be needed for persistent or severe diarrhoea, blood in the stool, or outbreaks
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} electrolytes and kidney function in significant dehydration
    \item \textbf{Monitoring:} weight, urine output, and hydration signs during treatment
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Oral rehydration solution:} available from pharmacies; given in small frequent sips for children and adults
    \item \textbf{For babies:} continue breastfeeding or formula, with extra oral rehydration solution as advised
    \item \textbf{Resume eating:} bland foods such as toast, rice, bananas, and soup as soon as tolerated
    \item \textbf{Avoid:} sugary drinks, soft drinks, and sports drinks, which can worsen diarrhoea
    \item \textbf{Zinc supplementation:} recommended for children in some settings to shorten episodes
    \item \textbf{Antidiarrhoeal medicines:} generally avoided in infectious diarrhoea; used cautiously in non-infectious cases
    \item \textbf{Probiotics:} may help some cases of gastroenteritis
    \item \textbf{Intravenous fluids:} for severe dehydration or when oral fluids cannot be tolerated
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe dehydration and shock
    \item Electrolyte imbalance causing seizures or heart rhythm problems
    \item Acute kidney injury
    \item Malnutrition and growth faltering in children
    \item Prolonged illness from incorrect treatment
    \item Complications of intravenous therapy, which are uncommon
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Diarrhoea treatment is highly effective, and the vast majority of people recover fully within a few days. Oral rehydration solution prevents complications when started early, and severe dehydration responds rapidly to intravenous fluids. With appropriate treatment, death and serious complications from diarrhoea are very rare in Australia. Most people can manage diarrhoea safely at home with good fluids and a light diet.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Keep oral rehydration solution at home for illness
    \item Start fluids early, before dehydration develops
    \item Give fluids in small, frequent amounts if vomiting occurs
    \item Continue normal feeding as soon as possible
    \item Wash hands and keep surfaces clean to prevent spread
    \item See a doctor early for babies, older adults, and people with chronic illness
    \item Follow medical advice for any specific treatment
\end{itemize}
