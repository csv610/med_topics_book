\chapter{Eyes}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
The eyes are the organs of sight, and they work with the brain to turn light into the images we see. Each eye has a lens that focuses light, a pupil that controls how much light enters, and a retina that converts light into nerve signals, which travel to the brain along the optic nerve. Vision is one of the senses people value most, and protecting the eyes through regular checks, sun protection, and safety practices matters at every age. This chapter provides an overview of how the eyes work and the basics of eye health.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Vision problems are among the most common health issues, affecting people of all ages. Refractive error affects a large proportion of the population, eye disease such as cataract, glaucoma, and macular degeneration becomes common with age, and eye injuries occur at work, in sport, and at home. A significant proportion of vision loss is avoidable with regular eye examinations, protection, and prompt treatment.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The eye works through a series of steps.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The front of the eye:} the cornea and lens focus light onto the retina
    \item \textbf{The pupil:} the iris adjusts the pupil to control light entry
    \item \textbf{The retina:} light-sensitive cells convert light into nerve signals
    \item \textbf{The optic nerve:} carries the signals to the brain
    \item \textbf{The brain:} interprets the signals as images
    \item \textbf{Accommodation:} the lens changes shape to focus at different distances
    \item \textbf{Vision problems:} blur occurs when the eye's shape prevents focusing; disease damages the structures of the eye
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
The signs of eye problems are important to know.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Blurred, distorted, or reduced vision
    \item Eye pain, redness, itching, or discharge
    \item Floaters, flashes, or a shadow in the vision
    \item Difficulty seeing at night
    \item Headaches and eye strain
    \item In children: squinting, an eye turning, or poor school performance
    \item The gradual, painless vision loss of age-related eye disease
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Eye symptoms have many causes, and assessment distinguishes them.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Refractive error, needing glasses
    \item Dry eye, allergy, and infection
    \item Glaucoma, cataract, and macular degeneration
    \item Migraine and neurological causes
    \item Emergencies such as retinal detachment and acute glaucoma
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Everyone should have regular eye examinations. Any new, sudden, or worrying eye symptom requires assessment, and some symptoms are emergencies.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden loss of vision
    \item Sudden severe eye pain
    \item A red eye with severe pain and nausea
    \item Sudden floaters with flashes or a shadow in the vision
    \item A chemical injury or penetrating injury to the eye
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Eye health is assessed with tests and examinations.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Visual acuity:} the eye chart
    \item \textbf{Refraction:} the glasses prescription
    \item \textbf{Slit-lamp examination:} the front of the eye
    \item \textbf{Pressure and fields:} for glaucoma
    \item \textbf{Dilated examination:} the retina and optic nerve
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} OCT and retinal photography
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Good eye health is maintained with prevention and treatment.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Glasses and contacts:} correct refractive error
    \item \textbf{Protection:} sunglasses, safety glasses, and sports goggles
    \item \textbf{Regular checks:} detect disease early
    \item \textbf{Treatment:} drops, laser, and surgery for eye disease
    \item \textbf{Managing health:} blood sugar, blood pressure, and weight
    \item \textbf{Prompt care:} for symptoms and injuries
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Vision loss from untreated eye disease
    \item Eye injuries from work, sport, and household hazards
    \item Falls and reduced independence from poor vision
    \item The impact of vision loss on quality of life
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Most eye problems are treatable, and most vision loss is preventable with early detection. Glasses correct refractive error, cataract surgery restores sight, and treatments slow or halt glaucoma, macular degeneration, and diabetic retinopathy. Regular checks and protection give the best outcomes.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Have regular eye examinations at every age
    \item Wear sunglasses that block UV light
    \item Use protective eyewear for work, sport, and DIY
    \item Follow the 20-20-20 rule for screens
    \item Manage diabetes, blood pressure, and weight
    \item Do not smoke
    \item Seek prompt care for any eye symptom
\end{itemize}
