\chapter{Coffee (see caffeine)}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Coffee is one of the most widely consumed pharmacologically active beverages globally, prepared from the roasted seeds of \textit{Coffea} species. Its primary active constituent is caffeine (1,3,7-trimethylxanthine), a natural central nervous system stimulant. Beyond caffeine, coffee contains a complex mixture of bioactive compounds, including chlorogenic acids, diterpenes (such as kahweol and cafestol), and melanoidins, which exhibit antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. Clinically, moderate coffee intake is associated with enhanced cognitive function, alertness, and metabolic benefits, whereas excessive consumption can lead to acute toxicity, sleep disturbances, cardiovascular stimulation, and physical dependence.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Coffee is consumed daily by billions of people worldwide. In Australia, approximately 75\% of adults consume coffee regularly, with a significant proportion consuming multiple cups daily. The average daily intake of caffeine among regular consumers is approximately 150 to 200 mg. Caffeine consumption patterns vary by age and occupation, with peak consumption occurring in adults aged 30 to 50 years. Mild caffeine toxicity is common, particularly among young adults and individuals using energy drinks alongside coffee, while severe, life-threatening caffeine toxicity remains rare and is typically associated with pure caffeine powders or tablets rather than standard beverage consumption.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The physiological and psychological effects of coffee are primarily driven by caffeine and its metabolites, though other constituents also play minor roles.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Adenosine receptor antagonism:} Caffeine competitively blocks adenosine $A_{1}$ and $A_{2A}$ receptors in the brain, preventing adenosine from binding and inducing drowsiness, thereby increasing alertness and arousal.
    \item \textbf{Dopaminergic and noradrenergic enhancement:} By blocking adenosine receptors, caffeine indirectly enhances dopaminergic transmission and increases the release of noradrenaline, improving mood, concentration, and psychomotor performance.
    \item \textbf{Phosphodiesterase inhibition:} At high concentrations, caffeine inhibits phosphodiesterase enzymes, leading to increased cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) levels, which promotes bronchodilation and cardiac contractility.
    \item \textbf{Calcium release:} Caffeine stimulates the release of calcium from the sarcoplasmic reticulum in cardiac and skeletal muscle, which can enhance muscle contraction and trigger tachyarrhythmias at toxic doses.
    \item \textbf{Metabolic breakdown:} Caffeine is rapidly absorbed in the gastrointestinal tract and metabolised in the liver by the cytochrome P450 1A2 (CYP1A2) enzyme pathway into paraxanthine, theobromine, and theophylline, which undergo further renal excretion.
    \item \textbf{Diterpene effects:} Unfiltered diterpenes in coffee (such as cafestol and kahweol) can elevate serum low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol by downregulating the hepatic LDL receptor pathway.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
The clinical presentation of coffee and caffeine consumption ranges from physiological arousal to acute toxicity or withdrawal.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mild to moderate consumption effects:} Increased alertness, improved concentration, transient elevations in blood pressure, mild diuresis, and enhanced bowel motility.
    \item \textbf{Acute caffeine toxicity (caffeinism):} Restlessness, severe anxiety, psychomotor agitation, insomnia, facial flushing, muscle twitching, gastrointestinal distress, and cardiac palpitations.
    \item \textbf{Severe toxicity features:} Severe cardiac arrhythmias (e.g.\ supraventricular tachycardia, atrial fibrillation, or ventricular fibrillation), metabolic acidosis, hypokalaemia, rhabdomyolysis, seizures, and altered mental status.
    \item \textbf{Caffeine withdrawal symptoms:} Headache (characteristically throbbing and bilateral), marked fatigue, drowsiness, dysphoric mood, irritability, difficulty concentrating, and mild flu-like symptoms.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
When evaluating symptoms related to excessive coffee intake or withdrawal, other conditions must be ruled out:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Primary anxiety disorders:} Panic disorder and generalised anxiety disorder, which present with similar palpitations, tremors, and hyperarousal.
    \item \textbf{Thyroid disease:} Hyperthyroidism or thyrotoxicosis, which causes chronic tachycardia, fine tremors, heat intolerance, and anxiety.
    \item \textbf{Pheochromocytoma:} A catecholamine-secreting tumour that causes paroxysmal hypertension, palpitations, headache, and diaphoresis.
    \item \textbf{Sympathomimetic toxicity:} Intoxication from amphetamines, cocaine, or over-the-counter decongestants (e.g.\ pseudoephedrine), which presents with severe hypertension, tachycardia, and dilated pupils.
    \item \textbf{Migraine or tension-type headache:} Must be distinguished from caffeine withdrawal headache, although withdrawal can also trigger a primary migraine episode.
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Individuals experiencing persistent insomnia, chronic gastrointestinal irritation, or worsening anxiety associated with coffee consumption should consult a general practitioner for an individualised reduction plan.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden, severe chest pain or pressure.
    \item Rapid, irregular, or pounding heart palpitations accompanied by dizziness, lightheadedness, or fainting.
    \item New-onset seizures or convulsions.
    \item Severe muscle rigidity, spasms, or signs of heatstroke.
    \item Difficulty breathing or acute shortness of breath.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Diagnosis of caffeine-related issues is primarily clinical, based on a thorough dietary history and physical examination, supplemented by targeted investigations in acute overdose settings.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dietary and supplement history:} Detailed estimation of daily caffeine intake from coffee, tea, energy drinks, pre-workout supplements, and medications.
    \item \textbf{Electrocardiogram (ECG):} Performed to assess for sinus tachycardia, supraventricular tachycardia, atrial fibrillation, or ventricular arrhythmias.
    \item \textbf{Serum electrolytes:} Monitored to detect hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, and hyperglycaemia, which are common in acute toxicity.
    \item \textbf{Serum caffeine level:} Measured in cases of suspected severe toxicity, although not routinely available in emergency settings.
    \item \textbf{Creatine kinase (CK):} Assessed if rhabdomyolysis is suspected due to prolonged agitation or seizures.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management of caffeine-related concerns ranges from lifestyle modifications to supportive care in cases of acute toxicity.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Gradual cessation or reduction:} To avoid severe withdrawal headaches, individuals should reduce daily coffee intake slowly over several weeks rather than stopping abruptly.
    \item \textbf{Dietary moderation:} Restricting caffeine intake to less than 400 mg per day (approximately 3 to 4 standard espresso cups) for healthy, non-pregnant adults.
    \item \textbf{Special populations limit:} Restricting intake to less than 200 mg per day during pregnancy and lactation due to prolonged clearance times.
    \item \textbf{Supportive therapy for mild toxicity:} Reassurance, oral rehydration, and avoidance of further stimulants.
    \item \textbf{Intravenous fluids:} Administered in acute toxicity to maintain renal perfusion, correct hypokalaemia, and prevent rhabdomyolysis-induced kidney injury.
    \item \textbf{Pharmacological control of agitation:} Benzodiazepines (e.g.\ diazepam) to manage severe agitation, tremors, and seizures.
    \item \textbf{Antiarrhythmic therapy:} Beta-blockers or other antiarrhythmics for unstable tachyarrhythmias, administered under intensive cardiac monitoring.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cardiovascular strain:} Chronic hypertension, cardiac arrhythmias, and increased risk of cardiovascular events in susceptible individuals.
    \item \textbf{Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD):} Relaxation of the lower oesophageal sphincter leading to acid reflux and oesophagitis.
    \item \textbf{Sleep disturbances:} Chronic insomnia, disrupted sleep architecture, and daytime fatigue.
    \item \textbf{Obstetric complications:} Increased risk of low birth weight, foetal growth restriction, or miscarriage with high maternal intake.
    \item \textbf{Osteoporosis risk:} High intake may increase urinary calcium excretion, potentially compromising bone mineral density in individuals with inadequate calcium intake.
    \item \textbf{Severe metabolic derangement:} Life-threatening hypokalaemia and rhabdomyolysis following acute massive overdose.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The prognosis for individuals with coffee-related symptoms is excellent. Most mild side effects and withdrawal symptoms resolve completely within 2 to 7 days of reducing intake. With appropriate lifestyle changes, chronic sleep or gastrointestinal issues typically improve rapidly. In cases of acute toxicity, prompt medical management and supportive care yield full recovery without long-term sequelae, provided that severe arrhythmias or ischaemic events are avoided.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Monitor daily intake:} Keep track of all caffeine sources, including espresso, instant coffee, energy drinks, and over-the-counter pain relievers.
    \item \textbf{Establish a caffeine curfew:} Avoid consuming coffee or other caffeinated beverages within 6 to 8 hours of bedtime to preserve sleep quality.
    \item \textbf{Choose filtered coffee:} Use paper filters (e.g.\ drip or pour-over methods) to trap cholesterol-raising diterpenes, rather than relying solely on cafetiere or boiled methods.
    \item \textbf{Utilise decaffeinated alternatives:} Substitute regular coffee with decaffeinated varieties or herbal teas to reduce daily stimulant intake.
    \item \textbf{Maintain adequate hydration:} Balance coffee consumption with plenty of water throughout the day to prevent dehydration.
\end{itemize}
