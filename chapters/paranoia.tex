\chapter{Paranoia}
\index{Paranoia}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. Distressing or unusual beliefs deserve respectful, confidential assessment.}

\section*{What it is}
Paranoia describes persistent or intense fears that other people intend harm, are watching, deceiving, or targeting someone. Suspicious thoughts can occur for many reasons and do not by themselves establish a diagnosis. They may occur with severe stress, trauma, sleep deprivation, depression, psychosis, substance use, medication effects, neurological illness, or another medical condition.

\section*{What it may feel like}
A person may feel unsafe, misinterpret ordinary events as threatening, withdraw from others, or find it hard to consider alternative explanations. Hallucinations, disorganised thinking, severe mood changes, poor sleep, or difficulty caring for basic needs can occur with some underlying conditions.

\section*{Assessment}
A healthcare professional may ask about symptoms, sleep, mood, medicines, alcohol and other substances, physical health, and safety. Assessment may include a physical examination, blood tests, medication review, or referral to mental-health services. Share the exact experiences and any recent changes; seeking help is not an admission that fears are “made up.”

\section*{Support and treatment}
Treatment depends on the cause and may include psychological therapy, support with sleep and stress, treatment of a medical condition, or prescribed medicines. Do not stop psychiatric or other prescribed medicines suddenly. A trusted person can help with appointments if the individual agrees. Avoid alcohol and recreational drugs, which can intensify suspicious thoughts.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Get urgent help if someone may harm themselves or another person, cannot care for basic needs, is severely confused or agitated, has not slept for several nights, or is hearing commands to act. If possible, stay calm, reduce stimulation, avoid arguing about the belief, and contact local emergency or crisis services.
