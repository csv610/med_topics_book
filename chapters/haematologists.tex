\chapter{Haematologists}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
A haematologist is a medical specialist who diagnoses and treats diseases of the blood, bone marrow, and lymphatic system. Haematologists manage conditions such as anaemia, bleeding and clotting disorders, leukaemia, lymphoma, myeloma, and other blood cancers, as well as disorders of red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets. They interpret blood tests, perform and interpret bone marrow examinations, and treat patients with medicines, chemotherapy, and procedures such as plasma exchange. Haematology is closely linked to oncology, and many haematologists treat blood cancers in partnership with other specialists. People are usually referred to a haematologist by their GP when blood tests reveal an abnormality or a blood condition is suspected.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Blood disorders are common. Anaemia affects around 8--10\% of the population at any time and is even more common in certain groups. Blood cancers account for around 10\% of all cancers in Australia, with lymphoma, leukaemia, and myeloma being the main types, affecting thousands of people each year. Clotting disorders such as venous thromboembolism and bleeding disorders such as haemophilia and von Willebrand disease require specialist haematology care. Haematologists also manage the haematological effects of chemotherapy and other treatments, and coordinate care with cancer services.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
This chapter concerns the role of a medical specialist. The conditions a haematologist manages include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Red cell disorders:} anaemia (iron, B12, and folate deficiency), haemoglobinopathies such as sickle cell disease and thalassaemia, and haemolytic anaemias
    \item \textbf{White cell disorders:} leukaemia (acute and chronic), myelodysplastic syndromes, and myeloproliferative neoplasms
    \item \textbf{Lymphatic disorders:} lymphoma (Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin) and chronic lymphocytic leukaemia
    \item \textbf{Plasma cell disorders:} myeloma and related conditions
    \item \textbf{Bleeding disorders:} haemophilia, von Willebrand disease, and platelet disorders
    \item \textbf{Clotting disorders:} deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, and thrombophilia
    \item \textbf{Bone marrow failure:} aplastic anaemia and bone marrow failure syndromes
    \item \textbf{Immune-related blood disorders:} autoimmune haemolytic anaemia, immune thrombocytopenia, and neutropenia
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Patients are usually referred to a haematologist for specific findings.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Anaemia: fatigue, breathlessness, pallor, and dizziness
    \item Abnormal blood counts: low or high red cells, white cells, or platelets
    \item Easy bruising or bleeding, or unexplained blood clots
    \item Swollen lymph nodes or an enlarged spleen
    \item Unexplained fever, weight loss, or night sweats
    \item Recurrent infections suggesting white cell problems
    \item Bone pain, which can occur in blood cancers
    \item Abnormal blood films or findings in bone marrow tests
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Blood test abnormalities have many possible causes.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Iron, B12, or folate deficiency as causes of anaemia
    \item Chronic disease and inflammation causing abnormal counts
    \item Medication effects on blood counts
    \item Infections causing lymph node enlargement, including glandular fever
    \item Liver, kidney, and thyroid disease affecting blood counts
    \item Autoimmune disease causing cytopenias
    \item Primary haematological malignancy, which haematologists diagnose and treat
    \item Reactive changes in blood counts from other illness
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a GP for persistent fatigue, unusual bruising or bleeding, unexplained fever or weight loss, or swollen lymph nodes. A GP can arrange initial blood tests and refer to a haematologist when needed.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden severe bleeding or bleeding that will not stop
    \item Sudden chest pain, breathlessness, or a painful swollen leg, which may indicate a blood clot
    \item High fever with rigors in a person with a blood condition or low white cell count
    \item Sudden severe headache or neurological symptoms in a person with a bleeding disorder
    \item Signs of severe anaemia, including chest pain, fainting, or severe breathlessness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Haematologists use a range of tests to diagnose blood disorders.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Full blood count and blood film:} the first-line tests, examining cell counts and appearances
    \item \textbf{Iron studies, B12, and folate:} for anaemia
    \item \textbf{Clotting tests and platelet function:} for bleeding disorders
    \item \textbf{Thrombophilia testing:} for unexplained clotting
    \item \textbf{Bone marrow aspiration and biopsy:} essential for diagnosing leukaemia, lymphoma, and bone marrow disorders
    \item \textbf{Immunophenotyping and cytogenetics:} molecular tests that classify blood cancers and guide treatment
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} CT, PET, and ultrasound for lymph nodes, spleen, and lymphoma
    \item \textbf{Lymph node biopsy:} for suspected lymphoma
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Haematologists provide comprehensive care for blood disorders.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Medical treatment:} iron and vitamin replacement, blood transfusions, and medicines for blood counts
    \item \textbf{Cancer treatment:} chemotherapy, targeted therapies, immunotherapy, and participation in clinical trials
    \item \textbf{Anticoagulation:} management of blood-thinning medicines for clotting disorders
    \item \textbf{Factor replacement:} clotting factor concentrates for haemophilia and related disorders
    \item \textbf{Immunosuppression:} for immune-mediated blood disorders
    \item \textbf{Stem cell transplantation:} for severe blood cancers and bone marrow failure
    \item \textbf{Supportive care:} managing infections, transfusions, and complications
    \item \textbf{Long-term follow-up:} monitoring of chronic blood conditions and surveillance after treatment
    \item \textbf{Multidisciplinary care:} working with oncologists, pathologists, radiologists, and nurses
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Complications of blood disorders, including anaemia-related organ damage and bleeding
    \item Complications of blood cancers and their treatment, including infections and transfusion dependence
    \item Side effects of chemotherapy and immunotherapy
    \item Complications of stem cell transplantation
    \item Thrombosis and bleeding with anticoagulant therapy
    \item Iron overload from repeated transfusions
    \item Psychological and practical burden of chronic blood disease
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The outlook for blood disorders has improved substantially. Many anaemias and clotting disorders are fully manageable with treatment, and survival for many blood cancers has improved greatly with modern therapies. Lymphomas and acute leukaemias in particular have much better outcomes than in the past, and some blood cancers are now curable. Chronic conditions such as myeloproliferative neoplasms and haemophilia are well controlled with ongoing specialist care. Haematological care is characterised by strong research and improving treatments, offering good prospects for most patients.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Eat a diet adequate in iron, B12, and folate to prevent common anaemias
    \item Attend recommended screening and see a GP for persistent symptoms
    \item Take prescribed medicines, including iron and anticoagulants, exactly as directed
    \item Attend all haematology appointments and blood tests
    \item Report unusual bleeding, bruising, fever, or new symptoms promptly
    \item Protect against infections with vaccination and good hygiene, especially during treatment
    \item Maintain a healthy lifestyle and seek support for the emotional impact of blood disease
    \item Carry medical information about your condition and medicines
    \item Know the warning signs that require urgent care
\end{itemize}
