\chapter{MDMA (ecstasy)}
 
\section*{Definition and Overview}
MDMA (3,4-methylenedioxymethamphetamine), commonly known as "ecstasy" or "molly," is a synthetic psychoactive drug that possesses both stimulant and mild hallucinogenic properties. It is chemically related to both amphetamines and mescaline. In the brain, MDMA acts as a potent monoamine releaser, primarily increasing the synaptic concentration of serotonin, and to a lesser extent, dopamine and norepinephrine. This leads to its characteristic psychological effects: profound feelings of empathy, social connectedness, euphoria, and heightened sensory perception. Globally and in Australia, MDMA is widely used as a recreational substance and is classified as a Schedule 9 controlled substance. However, in 2023, Australia's Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) reclassified MDMA under Schedule 8, allowing authorized psychiatrists to prescribe it under strict protocols for the treatment of post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD).
 
\section*{Epidemiology}
MDMA is the third most commonly used illicit stimulant in Australia, after cannabis and cocaine. According to national drug strategy surveys, lifetime prevalence of MDMA use among Australians aged 14 and over is approximately 10--12\%, with annual use around 2--3\%. The highest rates of use are observed in young adults aged 18 to 29 years, often in party, festival, or club environments. MDMA-related clinical emergencies (such as hyperthermia, hyponatreamia, and serotonin syndrome) are major concerns at mass gatherings, particularly when pills contain unknown doses or are adulterated with highly toxic synthetic stimulants.
 
\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
MDMA acts through the following main neurochemical mechanisms:
 
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Serotonin transporter (SERT) reversal:} MDMA binds to SERT and reverses its direction, pumping massive amounts of serotonin out of presynaptic vesicles into the synaptic cleft.
    \item \textbf{Dopamine and norepinephrine release:} it similarly increases dopamine and norepinephrine release by reversing DAT and NET transporters, causing stimulant and sympathomimetic effects.
    \item \textbf{Oxytocin release:} the surge in serotonin triggers the release of oxytocin, a hormone associated with social bonding, empathy, and trust.
    \item \textbf{ADH secretion and thermoregulation:} MDMA stimulates vasopressin (antidiuretic hormone, ADH) secretion, which impairs water excretion, and alters hypothalamic thermoregulation, making users vulnerable to water intoxication and hyperthermia.
\end{itemize}
 
\section*{Clinical Features}
The effects of MDMA typically begin 30 to 45 minutes after ingestion, peak at 1 to 2 hours, and last for 4 to 6 hours:
 
\begin{itemize}
    \item Desired effects: intense feelings of euphoria, emotional warmth, empathy, self-acceptance, and increased physical energy
    \item Physical sympathomimetic signs: tachycardia, hypertension, pupillary dilation (mydriasis), diaphoresis, and bruxism (compulsive jaw-clenching/teeth-grinding)
    \item Acute adverse effects: severe anxiety, panic, agitation, paranoia, and visual distortions
    \item Signs of severe toxicity: hyperthermia (body temperature \textgreater{}40\textdegree{}C), hyponatreamia (due to excessive water intake combined with ADH secretion), seizures, and serotonin syndrome
    \item The "comedown" (24--72 hours post-use): fatigue, depressed mood, irritability, anxiety, difficulty concentrating, and sleep disturbances ("suicide Tuesday," driven by temporary serotonin depletion)
\end{itemize}
 
\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Amphetamine or methamphetamine toxicity:} presents with similar hyperactivity and tachycardia, but lacks the profound empathogenic and social bonding features, and has a longer duration of action.
    \item \textbf{Serotonin syndrome from therapeutic drugs:} presenting with clonus, hyperreflexia, and hyperthermia, distinguished by a history of starting or increasing doses of SSRIs, SNRIs, or MAOIs.
    \item \textbf{Heat stroke (non-drug induced):} presents with hyperthermia and altered mental status in hot environments, but lacks the specific history of MDMA use and associated signs like bruxism or hyponatreamia.
    \item \textbf{Primary psychotic episode:} acute mania or schizophrenia presenting with agitation, differentiated by history and toxicology.
\end{itemize}
 
\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Immediate medical care is critical if someone shows signs of physical or psychological distress after MDMA use, especially in hot environments.
 
\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Extreme hyperthermia: hot, dry skin or profuse sweating, confusion, and rapid breathing
    \item Seizures, convulsions, or loss of consciousness
    \item Signs of hyponatreamia: severe headache, confusion, vomiting, and lethargy
    \item Chest pain, severe palpitations, or difficulty breathing
    \item Extreme agitation, panic, paranoia, or suicidal ideation
\end{itemize}
 
\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clinical evaluation:} assessing vital signs (heart rate, blood pressure, temperature) and cognitive status.
    \item \textbf{Serum electrolytes:} critical to measure sodium levels to check for severe hyponatreamia.
    \item \textbf{Urine toxicology screen:} rapid immunoassays detect amphetamine-class compounds; gas chromatography-mass spectrometry is used for definitive MDMA identification.
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} full blood count, renal function, creatine kinase (CK, to check for rhabdomyolysis), and liver function tests.
    \item \textbf{ECG:} to monitor for arrhythmias or signs of myocardial ischaemia.
\end{itemize}
 
\section*{Management}
Treatment of acute toxicity is supportive and focused on cooling and fluid management:
 
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hyperthermia management:} aggressive physical cooling (ice baths, cold intravenous fluids, evaporation techniques) is the highest priority.
    \item \textbf{Hyponatreamia management:} mild hyponatreamia is treated with fluid restriction; severe symptomatic hyponatreamia (sodium \textless{}120 mmol/L with seizures or coma) requires careful administration of hypertonic saline (3\% NaCl).
    \item \textbf{Benzodiazepines:} first-line (e.g., diazepam) to control agitation, seizures, muscle rigidity, and sympathomimetic vitals.
    \item \textbf{Bruxism prevention:} use of mouthguards and avoiding excessive jaw movement.
    \item \textbf{Psychological support:} reassurance in a low-stimulus environment, and long-term counseling for substance use.
\end{itemize}
 
\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe hyperthermia leading to rhabdomyolysis, disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), and acute kidney injury
    \item Cerebral oedema and brain herniation from severe hyponatreamia
    \item Acute liver failure (hepatotoxicity)
    \item Persistent depression, anxiety, and memory deficits due to serotonin system neurotoxicity
\end{itemize}
 
\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The prognosis for mild to moderate MDMA intoxication is excellent, with symptoms resolving as the drug is metabolized. For severe toxicity (extreme hyperthermia or hyponatreamia), the prognosis depends on the timeliness of medical intervention. Early, aggressive cooling and correct management of sodium levels prevent permanent organ damage and mortality. Relapse rates for MDMA abuse are high, but long-term outcomes are improved with psychotherapy and support.
 
\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Avoid illicit MDMA use, as the purity and dose of pills are highly unpredictable
    \item If using, avoid drinking excessive amounts of plain water, which worsens hyponatreamia; opt for sports drinks containing electrolytes, and limit intake to no more than 500 mL per hour if active
    \item Take regular breaks from dancing in hot, crowded venues to prevent heat exhaustion and hyperthermia
    \item Never mix MDMA with alcohol, stimulants, or prescription antidepressants (especially MAOIs or SSRIs, which dramatically increase the risk of serotonin syndrome)
    \item Keep a sober friend informed if you choose to use MDMA so they can monitor your safety and call for help if needed
\end{itemize}
