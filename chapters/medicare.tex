\chapter{Medicare}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Medicare is Australia's universal healthcare scheme, established in 1984 under the Hawke Government, designed to provide all Australian citizens, permanent residents, and eligible overseas visitors with access to free or highly subsidised medical, hospital, and pharmaceutical services. The core philosophy of Medicare is to ensure that equity of access to health services is based on clinical need rather than the ability to pay. It operates as a single-payer system funded by the federal government through general taxation and a dedicated Medicare levy. The system comprises three main pillars: subsidised medical services via the Medicare Benefits Schedule (MBS), subsidised essential prescription drugs via the Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme (PBS), and free treatment as a public patient in state-run public hospitals.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Medicare covers over 26 million individuals residing in Australia. In the 2022--2023 financial year, Medicare processed over 450 million services, demonstrating the vast scale and high utilisation of the scheme. The system is funded primarily by a 2.0\% Medicare levy on the taxable income of most taxpayers, with low-income earners exempt or paying a reduced rate. An additional Medicare Levy Surcharge (MLS) of 1.0\% to 1.5\% is applied to higher-income earners who do not hold appropriate private hospital cover, serving as an incentive to relieve pressure on the public system. Expenditure is highest among infants and older Australians (aged 65 and above), reflecting the demographic distribution of complex and chronic healthcare needs.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
While aetiology and pathophysiology relate to disease processes, the structural foundation and operational mechanisms of the Medicare system are built on specific legislation and funding structures.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Medicare Benefits Schedule (MBS):} A comprehensive listing of medical services, consultations, surgeries, and diagnostic procedures, each assigned a unique item number and a corresponding schedule fee set by the government.
    \item \textbf{Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme (PBS):} A national program that subsidises the cost of necessary medicines, capping out-of-pocket costs for general patients and concession cardholders.
    \item \textbf{Commonwealth-State Health Agreements:} Joint funding arrangements where the federal government provides capital to states and territories, which are constitutionally responsible for operating the public hospital systems.
    \item \textbf{Medicare Safety Nets:} Financial protection mechanisms that lower out-of-pocket costs for out-of-hospital services once a patient or family unit reaches a set financial threshold within a calendar year.
    \item \textbf{Reciprocal Health Care Agreements (RHCA):} Bilateral international agreements allowing visitors from countries like the UK, New Zealand, and parts of Europe to access essential medical care while in Australia.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
The scope of clinical services covered by Medicare ensures comprehensive care across the primary, secondary, and tertiary health sectors.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{GP and specialist consultations:} Partial or complete subsidy of fees for consultations with general practitioners, obstetricians, paediatricians, and other medical specialists.
    \item \textbf{Public hospital care:} Full coverage of all medical, nursing, surgical, and accommodation costs for patients admitted as public patients in any public hospital in Australia.
    \item \textbf{Diagnostic investigations:} Subsidised pathology services (blood, urine, and tissue analyses) and diagnostic imaging (X-rays, ultrasounds, CT scans, and specific MRI scans) ordered by a medical practitioner.
    \item \textbf{Allied health services:} Limited subsidised sessions (e.g. physiotherapy, podiatry, occupational therapy) when managed under a GP-coordinated Chronic Disease Management plan.
    \item \textbf{Mental health services:} Subsidised psychological therapy sessions under the Better Access to Psychiatrists, Psychologists and General Practitioners initiative.
    \item \textbf{Child Dental Benefits Schedule (CDBS):} Capped dental benefit funding providing basic dental services to eligible children aged 0 to 17 years.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
In the context of health service navigation, Medicare must be differentiated from other public schemes, private health insurance, and alternative medical pathways.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Medicare vs.\ Private Health Insurance (PHI):} Medicare covers public hospital services and out-of-hospital medical fees. PHI covers private hospital accommodation, theatre fees, choice of doctor, and "extras" like dental, optical, and physiotherapy that are generally excluded from Medicare.
    \item \textbf{Medicare vs.\ National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS):} Medicare funds acute, chronic, and rehabilitative medical care. The NDIS funds non-clinical supports, equipment, and home modifications to improve functional independence for people with permanent disabilities.
    \item \textbf{Medicare vs.\ Department of Veterans' Affairs (DVA) cards:} DVA health cards provide separate, comprehensive health coverage specifically for eligible military veterans and their dependants, often paying higher rebate rates than Medicare.
    \item \textbf{Medicare vs.\ Workers' Compensation / Third-Party Insurance:} Medical costs arising from workplace injuries or motor vehicle accidents are covered by compulsory state insurance schemes rather than Medicare.
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Australians and eligible residents should access Medicare-subsidised GP services for routine health concerns, chronic disease management, and preventative health checks.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Acute, life-threatening emergencies requiring immediate treatment in a public hospital emergency department (which is always free of charge under Medicare).
    \item Severe, crushing chest pain or suspected myocardial infarction.
    \item Acute severe shortness of breath or respiratory arrest.
    \item Sudden, unexplained weakness, facial droop, or difficulty speaking.
    \item Severe trauma, major burns, or loss of consciousness.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Accessing Medicare benefits requires formal enrollment, verification of eligibility, and tracking of health service usage.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Enrolment and Medicare card:} Verification of eligibility (Australian citizenship, permanent residency, or bridging visa) and issuance of a unique Medicare card and number.
    \item \textbf{MBS item tracking:} Coding and recording of medical services using specific MBS item numbers (e.g. Item 23 for a standard GP consultation) to calculate rebates.
    \item \textbf{Safety Net registration:} Automatic or manual tracking of out-of-pocket expenses via Services Australia to trigger the Medicare Safety Net and PBS Safety Net rebates once thresholds are breached.
    \item \textbf{My Health Record link:} Integration of Medicare billing history, immunization records, and PBS prescription logs into a secure national digital health record portal.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Navigating and managing care within the Medicare framework involves understanding billing options, referral pathways, and integrated chronic care structures.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Bulk-billing:} A billing arrangement where a medical practitioner accepts the Medicare benefit (85\% to 100\% of the schedule fee) as full payment for the service, resulting in no out-of-pocket expense for the patient.
    \item \textbf{Co-payments and gap fees:} Managing out-of-pocket costs when clinicians charge above the Medicare rebate. The patient pays the total fee and is subsequently refunded the rebate amount.
    \item \textbf{Chronic Disease Management (CDM) plans:} GPs write GP Management Plans (GPMP) and Team Care Arrangements (TCA) to coordinate multi-disciplinary care, providing rebates for up to five allied health sessions per year.
    \item \textbf{Mental Health Treatment Plans (MHTP):} GP-prepared plans that enable patients to access Medicare rebates for up to 10 individual psychological sessions per calendar year.
    \item \textbf{PBS safety net card:} Obtaining a safety net card once the threshold is met, making further prescription medicines either free (for concession cardholders) or significantly cheaper (for general patients) for the rest of the year.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
Despite its success, structural and economic challenges within the Medicare system can result in service delivery issues and financial strain for patients.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Increasing out-of-pocket costs:} Rising GP and specialist gap fees due to the freeze on Medicare rebate indexation, leading to financial barriers to healthcare access.
    \item \textbf{Elective surgery waiting lists:} Significant delays and waiting times for non-urgent elective surgeries in public hospitals, impacting quality of life.
    \item \textbf{Geographical health disparities:} A lack of bulk-billing clinics, specialists, and diagnostic services in rural, regional, and remote areas of Australia.
    \item \textbf{Exclusion of dental and optical care:} General exclusion of routine dental and adult optical services from Medicare, leading to untreated oral health issues and high private costs.
    \item \textbf{GP workforce shortage:} Decreased rates of medical graduates choosing general practice, leading to longer booking wait times and reduced clinic availability.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Medicare has successfully positioned Australia as a global leader in health outcomes, contributing to one of the highest life expectancies in the world (currently approximately 83.2 years) and low infant mortality rates. However, the system's long-term sustainability faces pressure from an ageing population, a rising prevalence of complex chronic diseases, and the high cost of emerging medical technologies. Continuous systemic reform, increased funding for primary care (such as the Strengthening Medicare reforms), and integration with digital health systems are critical to maintaining universal coverage.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
Patients can maximise the benefits of Medicare to maintain their health and reduce personal medical costs through proactive preventative steps.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Accessing free health assessments:} Booking Medicare-funded health assessments for specific groups, such as 45--49 year-olds at risk of chronic disease, or individuals aged 75 and over.
    \item \textbf{Participating in screening programs:} Utilizing federally funded cancer screening registers (BowelCancer, BreastScreen, Cervical Screening) linked to Medicare.
    \item \textbf{Linking family members:} Linking Medicare cards online through Services Australia to combine family out-of-pocket costs and reach the Safety Net thresholds faster.
    \item \textbf{Registering with MyMedicare:} Registering with a preferred general practice to access additional Medicare benefits, such as longer telehealth consultations and integrated care plans.
    \item \textbf{Utilising the PBS safety net:} Actively asking pharmacists to track and record all family prescriptions to ensure safety net limits are monitored.
\end{itemize}
