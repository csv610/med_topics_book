\chapter{Personal Hygiene}
\index{Personal Hygiene}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. Hygiene advice should be practical, respectful, and adapted to health, culture, age, disability, and available resources.}

\section*{What it includes}
Personal hygiene includes washing the body and hands, dental care, toilet hygiene, menstrual care, clean clothing, wound care, and safe handling of body fluids. Good hygiene reduces the spread of some infections and supports comfort and dignity, but illness is not proof of poor hygiene.

\section*{Practical habits}
Wash hands with soap and water after using the toilet, before preparing or eating food, after caring for someone who is ill, and after contact with body fluids. If soap and water are unavailable, an alcohol-based hand rub can be used when hands are not visibly dirty. Bathe or wash as needed, clean teeth with fluoride toothpaste, keep nails reasonably clean, and follow instructions for dressings or medical devices.

\section*{Skin and genital care}
Use gentle products and avoid douching, harsh scrubbing, or fragranced products that irritate skin. Keep skin folds dry, change absorbent products regularly, and wash hands before and after changing them. Do not share razors, toothbrushes, towels, or personal medical equipment.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek medical advice for persistent itching, rash, sores, pain, unusual discharge, fever, infected wounds, or symptoms that do not improve with simple care. Get urgent help for rapidly spreading redness, severe pain, facial swelling, breathing difficulty, or confusion.
