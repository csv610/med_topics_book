\chapter{Lactational amenorrhoea method (LAM)}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Lactational amenorrhoea method (LAM) is a temporary, natural method of family planning that relies on the physiological state of postpartum infertility induced by exclusive breastfeeding. The term "lactational" refers to breastfeeding, while "amenorrhoea" refers to the absence of menstruation. When practiced strictly according to specific clinical criteria, LAM is highly effective, offering over 98\% contraceptive efficacy during the first six months postpartum. This method serves as an accessible and cost-effective option, particularly in resource-limited settings, while simultaneously promoting optimal infant nutrition and maternal-infant bonding.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Globally, the use of LAM varies widely, depending on cultural breastfeeding practices, maternal education, and the integration of family planning into postpartum care programmes. In Australia, although breastfeeding initiation rates at hospital discharge are high (exceeding 90\%), exclusive breastfeeding declines to approximately 15\% to 39\% by six months, which limits the population-level utility and duration of LAM. The method is most commonly utilised in the first few months postpartum, with its effectiveness restricted to the first six months after childbirth, after which menstruation typically resumes or supplementary feeding is introduced.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The contraceptive effect of lactation is driven by neuroendocrine pathways triggered by infant suckling, which suppress the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Suckling stimulus:} Mechanical stimulation of the nipple by the infant activates sensory pathways that ascend to the maternal hypothalamus.
    \item \textbf{Gonadotrophin-releasing hormone (GnRH) suppression:} The neural signals inhibit the pulsatile release of GnRH from the hypothalamus.
    \item \textbf{Luteinising hormone (LH) suppression:} Reduced GnRH pulsatility prevents the normal surges of LH from the anterior pituitary gland, which are essential for follicular maturation and ovulation.
    \item \textbf{Prolactin elevation:} Suckling stimulates prolactin secretion, which supports milk production and acts synergistically to suppress ovarian follicular development.
    \item \textbf{Oestrogen deficiency:} Suppressed follicular development leads to low levels of circulating oestrogens, maintaining the endometrium in a quiescent state and preventing menstruation.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
The clinical presentation of a woman successfully using LAM is characterised by specific maternal physiological states and breastfeeding behaviours.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Persistent amenorrhoea:} The complete absence of menstrual bleeding or spotting after the postpartum bleeding (lochia) has ceased, usually defined as after the first 56 days postpartum.
    \item \textbf{Exclusive breastfeeding:} The infant receives only breast milk, with no other liquids, solids, or formula, or very minimal non-breast milk feeds that do not disrupt the feeding pattern.
    \item \textbf{Frequent feeding intervals:} Breastfeeding occurs at least every four hours during the day and at least every six hours during the night.
    \item \textbf{Infant age under six months:} The infant is less than six months old, as nutritional requirements beyond this age require the introduction of complementary foods, which reduces suckling frequency.
    \item \textbf{Lack of pacifier use:} The absence of pacifier or dummy use, which can reduce the infant's demand to suckle directly at the breast.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
When a breastfeeding woman experiences unexpected vaginal bleeding or prolonged amenorrhoea that does not align with LAM criteria, other physiological or pathological conditions must be considered.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Return of fertility:} Spontaneous resumption of normal ovulatory cycles despite ongoing breastfeeding, which can occur before the first menstrual bleed.
    \item \textbf{Postpartum haemorrhage:} Delayed bleeding caused by retained products of conception or subinvolution of the uterus, typically occurring within the first few weeks.
    \item \textbf{Pregnancy:} A new pregnancy occurring if ovulation occurs prior to the resumption of menses, particularly if the criteria for LAM are not fully met.
    \item \textbf{Endometrial hyperplasia or pathology:} Abnormal uterine bleeding arising from local endometrial changes, infections, or polyps.
    \item \textbf{Hypothyroidism or hyperprolactinaemia:} Underlying endocrine disorders that cause pathological amenorrhoea independent of lactation.
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Maternal and infant wellness require monitoring during the postpartum period, and transitions in contraceptive methods should be discussed with a general practitioner or lactation consultant.
\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden, severe postpartum vaginal haemorrhage (soaking more than one pad per hour).
    \item Signs of severe systemic infection or sepsis, including high fever, chills, and confusion.
    \item Severe, localised chest pain or sudden shortness of breath.
    \item Extreme lower abdominal pain accompanied by dizziness or fainting, which may indicate an ectopic pregnancy.
\end{itemize}
Other reasons to seek medical care include noticing any vaginal spotting or bleeding after the first 56 days postpartum, planning to introduce complementary foods, or if the infant reaches six months of age, which necessitates transitioning to another reliable method of contraception.

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
The evaluation of LAM candidacy and efficacy relies primarily on clinical assessment rather than laboratory or imaging tests, although diagnostic evaluations are indicated if pregnancy or other pathology is suspected.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clinical history:} A thorough assessment to confirm that all three LAM criteria are met: the infant is under six months old, the mother remains amenorrheic, and breastfeeding is exclusive or near-exclusive.
    \item \textbf{Urine pregnancy test:} Conducted immediately if the mother reports any vaginal bleeding, spotting, or symptoms suggestive of pregnancy to rule out a new gestation.
    \item \textbf{Thyroid function tests:} Performed if amenorrhoea persists after breastfeeding ceases or if abnormal bleeding patterns occur, to rule out thyroid dysfunction.
    \item \textbf{Pelvic ultrasound:} Utilised if abnormal uterine bleeding occurs, to evaluate for retained products of conception or endometrial pathology.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
The management of LAM involves strict adherence to breastfeeding protocols and timely preparation for transitioning to alternative, compatible contraceptive methods.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Exclusive breastfeeding education:} Instructing the mother to breastfeed on demand, ensuring no feeding intervals exceed four hours during the day and six hours at night, and avoiding infant formula or solid foods.
    \item \textbf{Avoidance of pacifiers and pumps:} Encouraging direct suckling rather than using breast pumps or pacifiers, as mechanical pumping may not suppress GnRH as effectively as direct infant suckling.
    \item \textbf{Contraceptive transition planning:} Selecting and preparing an alternative, breast-milk compatible contraceptive method (such as a progestogen-only pill, subdermal implant, hormonal intrauterine device, or barrier methods) before the six-month postpartum mark or when breastfeeding frequency decreases.
    \item \textbf{Lactation support:} Accessing support from maternal and child health nurses or international board-certified lactation consultants (IBCLCs) to manage feeding difficulties and maintain milk supply.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Unplanned pregnancy:} The primary complication, occurring if any of the three LAM criteria are violated or if ovulation occurs before menses resume.
    \item \textbf{Mastitis or breast engorgement:} Inflammatory breast conditions associated with frequent, intense breastfeeding schedules.
    \item \textbf{Maternal exhaustion:} Fatigue resulting from strict, round-the-clock feeding demands, particularly the requirement for night feeds.
    \item \textbf{Delayed transition to other contraception:} Increased risk of unintended pregnancy if alternative methods are not initiated immediately when LAM criteria are no longer met.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The prognosis for women using LAM is excellent when the method is followed correctly, with a typical-use failure rate of approximately 2\% and a perfect-use failure rate of under 1\% in the first six months. The amenorrhoea resolves spontaneously as breastfeeding frequency decreases or ceases, with a return of normal menstrual cycles and fertility. Long-term outcomes are positive, promoting prolonged breastfeeding which offers significant immunological benefits to the infant and reduces the mother's long-term risks of breast and ovarian cancers.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Keep a daily log or record of breastfeeding times to ensure intervals do not exceed the four-hour daytime and six-hour night-time limits.
    \item Avoid supplementing breast milk with water, juice, formula, or solid foods before the infant is six months of age.
    \item Prepare an alternative contraceptive method in advance, discussing options with a healthcare provider by the fourth or fifth postpartum month.
    \item Seek immediate lactation support if breastfeeding becomes painful, as issues like cracked nipples or poor latch can reduce feeding frequency.
    \item Maintain adequate maternal hydration and nutrition to support lactation and maternal wellbeing.
\end{itemize}
