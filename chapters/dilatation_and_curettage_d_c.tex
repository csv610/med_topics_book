\chapter{Dilatation and Curettage (D\&C)}
\index{Dilatation and Curettage (D\&C)}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Dilatation and curettage, commonly called a D\&C, is a surgical procedure in which the cervix is widened (dilatation) and the lining of the uterus is scraped away (curettage). It is performed for several reasons: to investigate abnormal bleeding by obtaining a sample of the uterine lining for testing, to remove tissue after a miscarriage, or to treat conditions such as retained products of conception. A D\&C is usually performed under general anaesthetic or sedation and takes around 10--15 minutes, and most women go home the same day. This chapter explains why a D\&C is done, what to expect, and the recovery.

\section*{Epidemiology}
A D\&C is a common gynaecological procedure. It is frequently performed after miscarriage to remove retained tissue, for abnormal uterine bleeding to obtain diagnostic samples, and occasionally to terminate an early pregnancy or remove polyps. The use of D\&C for diagnosis has declined with the wider availability of outpatient procedures such as hysteroscopy and endometrial biopsy, but it remains an important and effective procedure.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Diagnosis:} samples of the uterine lining are examined for abnormal cells, including in women with abnormal bleeding or post-menopausal bleeding
    \item \textbf{Miscarriage:} removal of retained products of conception after a miscarriage to prevent infection and bleeding
    \item \textbf{Treatment:} removal of polyps, and treatment of heavy bleeding in some cases
    \item \textbf{Procedure:} instruments are passed through the cervix to scrape or suction the uterine lining
    \item \textbf{Alternatives:} hysteroscopy, endometrial biopsy, and suction curettage are used in some situations
    \item \textbf{Why it is done:} a D\&C provides tissue for diagnosis and clears the uterus when needed
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A D\&C is usually a day procedure under general anaesthetic or sedation
    \item It takes about 10--15 minutes
    \item Cramping and light bleeding are expected afterwards for several days
    \item The tissue removed is sent to a laboratory for analysis when indicated
    \item Most women return to normal activities within a few days
    \item Sexual intercourse, tampons, and swimming are avoided for one to two weeks
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item D\&C versus hysteroscopy and endometrial biopsy for diagnosis
    \item D\&C versus medical management of miscarriage
    \item D\&C versus other treatments for abnormal bleeding
    \item Reasons for the procedure: diagnostic versus therapeutic
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor for abnormal or heavy bleeding, post-menopausal bleeding, or after a miscarriage, as these conditions may need a D\&C. After the procedure, seek medical care for heavy bleeding, fever, severe pain, or unusual discharge.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Very heavy bleeding after a D\&C, soaking pads rapidly
    \item High fever, severe abdominal pain, or foul-smelling discharge (possible infection)
    \item Fainting, dizziness, or collapse
    \item Chest pain or difficulty breathing after the anaesthetic
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pre-procedure assessment:} history, examination, pregnancy test, and ultrasound where relevant
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} full blood count and blood group where needed
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} ultrasound to assess the uterus and retained tissue
    \item \textbf{Histology:} laboratory analysis of the tissue removed
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} review of results and recovery
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Anaesthesia:} general anaesthetic or sedation
    \item \textbf{The procedure:} dilatation of the cervix and curettage or suction of the uterine lining
    \item \textbf{Pain relief:} pain medicines for after the procedure
    \item \textbf{Infection prevention:} antibiotics where there is a risk of infection
    \item \textbf{Post-procedure care:} rest, sanitary pads, and avoiding tampons and intercourse for one to two weeks
    \item \textbf{Results:} discussion of pathology results and any further treatment needed
    \item \textbf{Return to activities:} most women resume normal activities within a few days
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Bleeding, which is usually light but can occasionally be heavy
    \item Infection of the uterus
    \item Perforation of the uterus, which is rare
    \item Damage to the cervix, also rare
    \item Scarring (Asherman's syndrome) in rare cases after repeated procedures
    \item Complications of anaesthesia
    \item Emotional effects after miscarriage-related procedures
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
A D\&C is a safe and effective procedure with a quick recovery. Diagnostic D\&C provides accurate information about the uterine lining, and therapeutic D\&C resolves bleeding and prevents complications of retained tissue. Most women recover fully within a few days, and serious complications are rare. For women who have had a miscarriage, a D\&C resolves the physical process, and emotional support may be needed alongside physical recovery.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Attend pre-procedure appointments and follow fasting instructions
    \item Arrange for someone to drive you home after the anaesthetic
    \item Use sanitary pads rather than tampons for the first weeks
    \item Rest and take pain relief as advised
    \item Avoid intercourse, swimming, and heavy lifting as advised
    \item Report heavy bleeding, fever, or severe pain promptly
    \item Seek emotional support after a miscarriage if needed
\end{itemize}
