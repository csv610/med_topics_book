\chapter{Itchy Scalp}
\index{Itchy Scalp}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
An itchy scalp is a common and irritating problem caused by conditions such as dandruff and seborrhoeic dermatitis, dry skin, head lice, fungal infection, psoriasis, and allergic reactions to hair products. The itch ranges from mild flaking to severe, constant scratching that disturbs sleep and damages the scalp. Most causes are easily treated with medicated shampoos, improved hair and scalp care, and, where needed, medical treatment. Identifying the cause is important, because head lice need different treatment from dandruff, and some conditions, such as scalp psoriasis and fungal infection, benefit from specific therapies.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Dandruff is very common, affecting around half of adults at some time, and seborrhoeic dermatitis affects around 3 to 5 per cent of people, with peaks in infancy and between ages 30 and 60. Head lice are common in school-age children and spread easily through close head contact. Scalp psoriasis affects a significant minority of people with psoriasis. Itchy scalp is more common in men, in cold dry weather, with stress, and in people with oily skin. Most people self-treat with shampoos, and many improve without medical care.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The scalp has many sebaceous glands, and conditions that affect sebum and skin turnover cause itching and flaking. Dandruff and seborrhoeic dermatitis are linked to an overgrowth of a yeast called Malassezia, which feeds on sebum and produces irritants that increase skin cell turnover, causing flakes and inflammation. Dry scalp from cold weather, harsh shampoos, or over-washing reduces the skin barrier and itches. Head lice are tiny insects that bite the scalp and lay eggs, causing intense itching, especially behind the ears and at the nape of the neck. Fungal infection of the scalp, mainly in children, causes scaling and patchy hair loss. Scalp psoriasis is an immune-driven condition with thick silvery scales, and contact dermatitis follows allergy or irritation from hair products.

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Itching of the scalp, which may be mild or severe
    \item White or yellow flakes of skin in the hair and on the shoulders (dandruff)
    \item Oily, red, scaly patches, mainly on the scalp, eyebrows, and behind the ears in seborrhoeic dermatitis
    \item A crawling or tickling feeling with intense night-time itching, suggesting head lice
    \item Visible nits (eggs) attached to the hair shafts
    \item Patchy hair loss with scaling in fungal infection
    \item Thick silvery scales in psoriasis
    \item Redness, soreness, or weeping with allergy or infection
    \item Scratch marks and secondary infection from chronic scratching
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
The cause of scalp itching should be identified, as treatments differ.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dandruff and seborrhoeic dermatitis:} the most common causes
    \item \textbf{Dry scalp:} itching with fine, dry flakes and no redness
    \item \textbf{Head lice:} intense itching with nits and live lice
    \item \textbf{Scalp psoriasis:} thick scales and well-defined plaques
    \item \textbf{Fungal infection (tinea capitis):} scaling with hair loss, mainly in children
    \item \textbf{Contact dermatitis:} allergy or irritation from hair products
    \item \textbf{Other conditions:} eczema, folliculitis, and rarely skin cancers
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Medical assessment is appropriate for scalp itching that is severe, persistent, or associated with hair loss, soreness, weeping, or discharge. Children with suspected head lice or fungal infection should be assessed, and families should be checked for lice. A scalp rash that does not respond to medicated shampoos warrants review, as does any new, changing, or bleeding lump.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Rapidly spreading redness, pain, or swelling of the scalp
    \item Fever with a scalp infection
    \item A large, bleeding, or rapidly changing scalp lesion
    \item Signs of a severe allergic reaction, including swelling of the face or difficulty breathing
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History and examination:} inspection of the scalp, hair, and skin elsewhere
    \item \textbf{Lice detection:} wet combing and looking for nits and live lice
    \item \textbf{Skin scrapings:} microscopy and culture for fungal infection
    \item \textbf{Allergy testing:} patch testing for contact dermatitis in selected cases
    \item \textbf{Assessment of psoriasis and eczema:} review of skin elsewhere on the body
    \item \textbf{Biopsy:} rarely needed for persistent or unusual rashes
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} review of response to treatment
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Anti-dandruff shampoos:} containing zinc pyrithione, selenium, ketoconazole, or salicylic acid, used regularly
    \item \textbf{Seborrhoeic dermatitis care:} medicated shampoo with gentle scalp massage, and treatment of affected facial skin
    \item \textbf{Scalp moisturising:} for dry scalp, with gentle shampoos and conditioner
    \item \textbf{Head lice treatment:} medicated lotions or products and thorough wet combing, treating the whole household
    \item \textbf{Antifungal treatment:} oral or topical medicines for fungal infection
    \item \textbf{Psoriasis treatment:} coal tar, vitamin D, or steroid preparations, and specialist care
    \item \textbf{Treatment of allergy and infection:} stopping culprits and treating secondary infection
    \item \textbf{Avoiding scratching:} short nails, cool compresses, and stress management
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Scratch marks, sores, and secondary bacterial infection
    \item Damage to hair and, rarely, scarring hair loss
    \item Spread of head lice and fungal infection to others
    \item Chronic discomfort and sleep disturbance
    \item Psychological effects of visible dandruff or hair loss
    \item Over-treatment with strong products, causing further irritation
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Most causes of an itchy scalp respond well to treatment. Dandruff and seborrhoeic dermatitis are controlled with regular medicated shampoo use, and dry scalp settles with improved care. Head lice are cured with correct treatment and household co-treatment, and fungal infection responds to antifungal therapy. Scalp psoriasis is chronic but manageable with topical treatment and specialist support. Once the cause is identified, most people can keep their scalp comfortable with simple routines.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Wash the hair regularly with an appropriate shampoo
    \item Use an anti-dandruff shampoo regularly if you are prone to flaking
    \item Avoid harsh products, over-washing, and very hot water
    \item Do not share brushes, combs, hats, or pillows if lice are present
    \item Check children's hair regularly for lice
    \item Rinse thoroughly and avoid leaving products on the scalp
    \item Protect the scalp from the sun
    \item Manage stress, which flares dandruff, psoriasis, and eczema
    \item Seek medical advice for hair loss, soreness, or persistent symptoms
\end{itemize}
