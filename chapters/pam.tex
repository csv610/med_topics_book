\chapter{Primary Amebic Meningoencephalitis (PAM)}
\index{Primary Amebic Meningoencephalitis (PAM)}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This is an emergency medical topic. This chapter is for general education and does not replace urgent clinical care.}

\section*{What it is}
Primary amebic meningoencephalitis (PAM) is a rare, rapidly progressive infection of the brain caused by the free-living amoeba \textit{Naegleria fowleri}. It can occur when contaminated warm fresh water or soil enters the nose; swallowing the water does not cause PAM.

\section*{Symptoms}
Early symptoms may resemble meningitis, including severe headache, fever, nausea, vomiting, and stiff neck. Confusion, sensitivity to light, loss of balance, seizures, reduced consciousness, and rapidly worsening neurological problems can follow.

\section*{Diagnosis and treatment}
Because PAM is rare and resembles other forms of meningitis, clinicians need urgent specialist and laboratory support. Testing may include cerebrospinal-fluid examination and molecular or microscopic testing. Treatment uses a combination of medicines in hospital, but outcomes remain poor; early recognition is important.

\section*{Reducing risk}
Avoid forcing warm fresh water up the nose during swimming, diving, water sports, or ritual nasal rinsing. Use nose clips where appropriate and use distilled, sterile, or boiled-and-cooled water for nasal irrigation. These measures reduce risk but cannot eliminate it completely.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek emergency care immediately for severe headache, fever, stiff neck, vomiting, confusion, seizures, loss of balance, or rapidly worsening illness after possible fresh-water or nasal-water exposure. Do not wait for symptoms to become severe.
