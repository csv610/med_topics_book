\chapter{Fabry Disease}
\index{Fabry Disease}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Fabry disease is a rare, inherited condition in which the body cannot break down certain fats properly because of a missing or faulty enzyme. These fats build up in the cells of the blood vessels, kidneys, heart, and nervous system, causing pain, skin rashes, and, over time, damage to the kidneys, heart, and brain. Fabry disease is caused by a mutation on the X chromosome, so it affects males more severely, though many females also have symptoms. With enzyme replacement therapy and other treatments, the condition can be managed, and outcomes have improved greatly.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Fabry disease is rare, affecting approximately 1 in 40,000 to 60,000 males, with estimates varying by region. Because symptoms can be vague and are often misdiagnosed, the condition is under-recognised and diagnosis is often delayed by years. It affects all ethnic groups, and early diagnosis, including newborn screening in some places, allows treatment before major organ damage occurs.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Fabry disease is caused by a deficiency of the enzyme alpha-galactosidase A.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Genetic cause:} a mutation in the GLA gene on the X chromosome reduces or removes the enzyme
    \item \textbf{Inheritance:} X-linked; males with the mutation are more severely affected, while females are carriers who can have symptoms ranging from mild to severe
    \item \textbf{Accumulation:} fats called globotriaosylceramide build up in cells throughout the body
    \item \textbf{Effects:} the build-up damages small blood vessels, the kidneys, the heart, and nerves
    \item \textbf{Progression:} symptoms begin in childhood in males and progress to organ failure in adulthood
    \item \textbf{The enzyme:} replacement of the missing enzyme is the basis of specific treatment
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Symptoms often begin in childhood.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Burning pain in the hands and feet, often triggered by heat, exercise, or fever
    \item Small, dark red skin spots, mainly between the waist and knees
    \item Reduced sweating or absent sweating
    \item Cloudy or reduced vision from corneal changes
    \item Abdominal pain, diarrhoea, and nausea
    \item Hearing loss and ringing in the ears
    \item In later life: progressive kidney disease, heart problems, and strokes
    \item Fatigue
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
The symptoms of Fabry disease overlap with other conditions.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Growing pains and rheumatic conditions in children
    \item Neuropathy from diabetes and other causes
    \item Chronic fatigue and irritable bowel syndrome
    \item Kidney disease from other causes
    \item Heart failure and stroke from other causes
    \item Other lysosomal storage disorders
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Anyone with unexplained burning pain in the hands and feet, characteristic skin spots, reduced sweating, or a family history of kidney, heart, or stroke disease at a young age should be assessed by a GP and referred to a genetic service.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Signs of stroke, including sudden weakness, facial drooping, or difficulty speaking
    \item Chest pain or palpitations
    \item Sudden severe abdominal pain
    \item Severe swelling or reduced urine, which may indicate kidney failure
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Diagnosis is confirmed with enzyme and genetic testing.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Enzyme testing:} blood tests measure the level of alpha-galactosidase A; a low level confirms the diagnosis in males
    \item \textbf{Genetic testing:} identifies the GLA gene mutation and is essential for females and for family screening
    \item \textbf{Family screening:} testing of at-risk relatives
    \item \textbf{Organ assessment:} kidney function, heart tests, and hearing tests
    \item \textbf{Eye examination:} for the characteristic corneal changes
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management combines enzyme replacement with organ care.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Enzyme replacement therapy:} regular infusions of the missing enzyme, which slow the build-up and reduce symptoms
    \item \textbf{Chaperone therapy:} an oral medicine for people with certain mutations
    \item \textbf{Pain management:} medicines for the burning nerve pain
    \item \textbf{Kidney care:} medicines to protect the kidneys, and dialysis or transplant for kidney failure
    \item \textbf{Heart care:} monitoring and treatment of heart involvement
    \item \textbf{Stroke prevention:} medicines to reduce stroke risk
    \item \textbf{Family planning:} genetic counselling
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Progressive kidney disease and kidney failure
    \item Heart failure and heart rhythm problems
    \item Strokes, which can occur at a young age
    \item Chronic neuropathic pain
    \item Reduced quality of life from the many symptoms
    \item The psychological impact of a chronic genetic illness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Without treatment, Fabry disease shortens life, mainly through kidney, heart, and brain complications. With enzyme replacement therapy started early, the build-up is slowed, symptoms are reduced, and life expectancy is much improved. Outcomes depend on early diagnosis and comprehensive specialist care.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Seek genetic testing if there is a family history
    \item Start enzyme replacement therapy early if diagnosed
    \item Attend regular kidney, heart, and hearing reviews
    \item Avoid triggers of the burning pain, such as heat and exertion, where possible
    \item Manage blood pressure and other risk factors
    \item Connect with support organisations for information and community
    \item Attend genetic counselling for family planning
\end{itemize}
