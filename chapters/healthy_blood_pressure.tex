\chapter{Healthy Blood Pressure}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Blood pressure is the pressure of blood against the walls of the arteries as the heart pumps it around the body. It is measured as two numbers: systolic pressure (during the heartbeat) and diastolic pressure (between beats). A healthy blood pressure for most adults is below 120/80 mmHg. High blood pressure (hypertension) is defined as persistently 140/90 mmHg or higher, and it is a major risk factor for heart attack, stroke, kidney disease, and dementia. Blood pressure rises with age and is influenced by diet, weight, physical activity, alcohol, salt, and stress. Healthy blood pressure is maintained with lifestyle measures and, when needed, medicines, and regular measurement is essential because hypertension has no symptoms.

\section*{Epidemiology}
High blood pressure is one of the most common conditions in Australia, affecting around 1 in 3 adults, and its prevalence rises with age, affecting more than half of people over 65. Around a third of people with hypertension do not know they have it, because it causes no symptoms. High blood pressure is a leading contributor to heart disease, stroke, and kidney failure, and is responsible for more than 25,000 deaths in Australia each year. Lowering blood pressure by even a few points substantially reduces the risk of cardiovascular events.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Blood pressure is determined by the heart's output and the resistance of the arteries.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cardiac output:} the amount of blood the heart pumps each minute
    \item \textbf{Peripheral resistance:} the resistance of the small arteries, influenced by their diameter and tone
    \item \textbf{Blood volume:} salt and fluid retention increase blood volume and pressure
    \item \textbf{Hormonal control:} the kidneys and hormones such as renin-angiotensin and adrenaline regulate pressure
    \item \textbf{Arterial stiffness:} arteries stiffen with age, raising systolic pressure
    \item \textbf{Risk factors:} salt, obesity, inactivity, alcohol, smoking, stress, and genetics
    \item \textbf{Damage:} sustained high pressure damages the heart, brain, kidneys, eyes, and arteries
    \item \textbf{White-coat effect:} blood pressure can rise temporarily in the clinic
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
High blood pressure is usually symptomless, which is why it is called the silent killer.

\begin{itemize}
    \item No symptoms in the vast majority of people
    \item Headaches in some people with very high readings
    \item Symptoms of complications when they occur: chest pain, breathlessness, visual changes
    \item Findings on routine measurement during health checks
    \item Hypertension in pregnancy, which requires monitoring
    \item Hypertensive crisis: very high readings with headache, chest pain, or confusion, requiring urgent care
    \item Blood pressure readings that vary between visits and settings
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Blood pressure readings and its causes need careful interpretation.

\begin{itemize}
    \item White-coat hypertension: high readings in the clinic but normal at home
    \item Masked hypertension: normal readings in the clinic but high at home
    \item Secondary hypertension: caused by kidney disease, hormonal conditions, or medicines
    \item Pregnancy-related hypertension, including pre-eclampsia
    \item Isolated systolic hypertension, common in older people
    \item Normal variation with activity, stress, and caffeine
    \item Medication-induced rises in blood pressure
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
All adults should have blood pressure measured at least every two years, and more often with risk factors. See a doctor for a high reading to confirm it and plan management.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Very high blood pressure with severe headache, chest pain, breathlessness, or confusion
    \item Symptoms of stroke: facial drooping, arm weakness, or difficulty speaking
    \item Chest pain or symptoms of a heart attack
    \item Severe headache with visual disturbance in pregnancy
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Diagnosis requires repeated measurement and, when needed, home or ambulatory monitoring.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clinic measurement:} repeated readings with a properly calibrated device
    \item \textbf{Home monitoring:} readings at home over several days confirm the diagnosis
    \item \textbf{Ambulatory monitoring:} 24-hour monitoring provides the most accurate picture
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} kidney function, glucose, and cholesterol
    \item \textbf{Urine tests:} protein and kidney assessment
    \item \textbf{ECG:} checks the heart
    \item \textbf{Risk assessment:} absolute cardiovascular risk calculation
    \item \textbf{Secondary cause screening:} when hypertension is severe, early, or resistant
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management combines lifestyle measures and, when needed, medicines.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reduce salt:} limiting salt lowers blood pressure significantly
    \item \textbf{Eat well:} a diet rich in vegetables, fruit, whole grains, and low in saturated fat (such as the DASH-style diet)
    \item \textbf{Maintain a healthy weight:} weight loss lowers blood pressure
    \item \textbf{Be active:} regular physical activity lowers blood pressure
    \item \textbf{Limit alcohol:} reducing alcohol intake
    \item \textbf{Do not smoke:} smoking damages arteries
    \item \textbf{Manage stress:} relaxation and sleep
    \item \textbf{Medicines:} several classes of blood pressure medicines are highly effective, often used in combination
    \item \textbf{Monitoring:} regular blood pressure checks track control
    \item \textbf{Treatment of secondary causes:} when an underlying cause is found
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Heart attack, heart failure, and angina
    \item Stroke and transient ischaemic attack
    \item Kidney disease and kidney failure
    \item Damage to the eyes, including blindness
    \item Peripheral artery disease
    \item Aortic aneurysm and dissection
    \item Dementia, including vascular dementia
    \item Complications in pregnancy, including pre-eclampsia
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Treatment of high blood pressure is highly effective. Lowering blood pressure reduces the risk of stroke by around a third to a half and heart attacks by around a fifth, and it protects the kidneys and brain. Most people achieve target blood pressure with a combination of lifestyle measures and one or more medicines, which are safe and well tolerated. With early detection and good control, people with hypertension can expect a normal life expectancy and avoidance of major complications.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Have blood pressure measured regularly
    \item Know your numbers and your target
    \item Limit salt, eat a balanced diet, and maintain a healthy weight
    \item Exercise regularly
    \item Limit alcohol and do not smoke
    \item Manage stress and get enough sleep
    \item Take blood pressure medicines exactly as prescribed
    \item Monitor blood pressure at home if advised
    \item Attend follow-up appointments
    \item Seek urgent care for very high readings with symptoms
\end{itemize}
