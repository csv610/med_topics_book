\chapter{Muscle aches and pains}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Muscle aches and pains, clinically referred to as myalgia, represent a common symptom characterised by generalised or localised discomfort, tenderness, or stiffness within one or more muscle groups. This symptom is not a distinct disease entity but rather a physiological or pathological manifestation of an underlying condition. Muscle aches can range in intensity from mild, transient soreness to severe, chronic pain that significantly limits physical function and quality of life. The clinical presentation is broad, reflecting diverse pathophysiological processes including mechanical strain, systemic infections, inflammatory disorders, metabolic abnormalities, and drug-induced toxicities.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Myalgia is one of the most frequent reasons for seeking primary medical care globally. Population-based surveys estimate that musculoskeletal pain affects approximately 30\% of the global population, with a higher prevalence in females and older adults. Generalised muscle aches associated with seasonal viral infections, such as influenza and COVID-19, affect millions of individuals annually. Chronic widespread muscle pain syndromes, such as fibromyalgia, have an estimated global prevalence of 2--5\%. Occupational physical demands, sedentary lifestyles, and recreational athletic pursuits also contribute significantly to the high incidence of localized muscle aches across all age groups.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The pathways leading to muscle pain are multifactorial, involving inflammatory, ischaemic, metabolic, or neurogenic mechanisms.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Infectious agents:} Systemic viral infections (e.g., influenza, Epstein-Barr virus, COVID-19) release pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-6 (IL-6) and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-$\alpha$), which sensitise peripheral nociceptors.
    \item \textbf{Physical overuse and microtrauma:} Intense or unaccustomed physical activity, particularly eccentric contractions, leads to microscopic tearing of muscle fibres. This triggers an inflammatory repair response, causing delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS).
    \item \textbf{Inflammatory and autoimmune diseases:} Conditions like polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR), systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), and inflammatory myopathies (polymyositis and dermatomyositis) directly target muscle tissue or surrounding fascia.
    \item \textbf{Drug-induced myopathy:} HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors (statins) are well-known to cause myalgia or myopathy, ranging from mild discomfort to severe rhabdomyolysis, likely due to mitochondrial dysfunction or coenzyme Q10 depletion.
    \item \textbf{Metabolic and electrolyte disturbances:} Deficiencies in electrolytes (potassium, calcium, magnesium) or endocrine disorders (hypothyroidism) disrupt the action potentials and contractility of muscle cells.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
The presentation of muscle aches varies based on the underlying pathology, but typically features several characteristic signs.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Muscle tenderness and dull pain:} Generalised or localised soreness that is exacerbated by movement, physical exertion, or direct pressure.
    \item \textbf{Stiffness and restricted mobility:} A sensation of tightness, particularly after periods of rest or inactivity, which improves gradually with gentle movement.
    \item \textbf{Systemic symptoms:} Co-existing fever, chills, fatigue, and arthralgias, which point toward an infectious or systemic inflammatory cause.
    \item \textbf{Muscle weakness:} Subjective or objective loss of muscle strength, which may be due to pain-induced inhibition or direct muscle fibre damage (myopathy).
    \item \textbf{Sleep disturbance and cognitive fatigue:} Chronic muscle aches are frequently associated with non-restorative sleep, low mood, and difficulty concentrating.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Distinguishing between physiological muscle soreness and systemic inflammatory or toxic myopathies is vital for initiating correct therapy.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS):} Transient, localized muscle pain appearing 24--72 hours after unaccustomed exercise, resolving without intervention.
    \item \textbf{Polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR):} Characterised by severe pain and stiffness in the shoulder and pelvic girdles in patients over 50 years, accompanied by high ESR and CRP.
    \item \textbf{Statin-induced myopathy:} Muscle pain or weakness beginning after statin initiation, which may or may not be accompanied by elevated creatine kinase (CK).
    \item \textbf{Fibromyalgia:} A chronic, non-inflammatory syndrome characterized by widespread musculoskeletal pain, fatigue, sleep disturbance, and cognitive dysfunction.
    \item \textbf{Polymyositis:} An autoimmune myopathy presenting with subacute, progressive, symmetrical proximal muscle weakness, elevated CK, and characteristic histological findings on muscle biopsy.
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
While most muscle aches are benign and self-limiting, certain signs warrant urgent medical investigation.
\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden, severe muscle pain accompanied by dark-coloured (tea-coloured or cola-coloured) urine, indicating possible rhabdomyolysis.
    \item Muscle aches associated with difficulty breathing, swallowing, speaking, or moving the limbs.
    \item High fever, severe headache, confusion, and a stiff neck.
    \item Muscle pain accompanied by severe chest pressure, radiating pain, or shortness of breath.
\end{itemize}
Patients should consult their GP if muscle aches persist for more than two to three weeks without improvement, are accompanied by joint swelling or unexplained weight loss, or occur shortly after starting a new medication.

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Investigations aim to identify systemic causes, metabolic imbalances, or signs of muscle tissue breakdown.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Creatine kinase (CK):} The key marker for muscle injury; elevated levels indicate sarcolemmal damage, as seen in myositis, trauma, or drug toxicity.
    \item \textbf{Inflammatory markers (CRP and ESR):} Significantly elevated in systemic inflammatory conditions like PMR or systemic vasculitis.
    \item \textbf{Electrolytes and renal function:} Evaluates serum potassium, sodium, calcium, magnesium, and creatinine to detect imbalances or renal impairment.
    \item \textbf{Thyroid function tests (TSH and Free T4):} Indicated to rule out hypothyroidism as a metabolic cause of myalgia and fatigue.
    \item \textbf{Rheumatologic serology:} Tests for antinuclear antibodies (ANA) or extractable nuclear antigens (ENA) when connective tissue diseases are suspected.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management strategies combine pharmacological relief, lifestyle interventions, and treatment of the underlying cause.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Analgesics and anti-inflammatories:} Use of paracetamol or short-term oral non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) to manage acute discomfort.
    \item \textbf{Non-pharmacological physical therapies:} Application of heat packs for chronic muscle tension or ice packs for acute sports-related injuries.
    \item \textbf{Exercise and rehabilitation:} Gradual reintroduction of physical activity, stretching, or low-impact exercises (swimming, cycling) to maintain muscle length and tone.
    \item \textbf{Medication review:} Consideration of dose adjustments, switching, or temporarily withholding suspected agents (such as statins) under clinical supervision.
    \item \textbf{Nutritional and electrolyte supplementation:} Correction of vitamin D, magnesium, or potassium deficiencies when identified.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
Failure to diagnose or manage underlying causes of muscle aches can lead to significant morbidity.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Acute kidney injury:} Caused by severe rhabdomyolysis where myoglobin precipitates in the renal tubules, leading to acute tubular necrosis.
    \item \textbf{Deconditioning and atrophy:} Prolonged inactivity due to fear of pain leads to progressive muscle wasting, joint stiffness, and loss of physical conditioning.
    \item \textbf{Chronic pain syndrome:} Inadequate treatment of acute pain can lead to nervous system sensitisation, culminating in chronic generalised pain.
    \item \textbf{Psychosocial impact:} Persistent muscle pain can severely disrupt sleep, impair work capacity, and contribute to anxiety and depression.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The prognosis for most patients experiencing transient muscle aches is excellent, with symptoms resolving completely once the causative factor (such as a viral infection or strenuous exercise) is removed or self-limits. For patients with drug-induced myalgia, symptoms typically resolve within weeks of drug cessation. Chronic inflammatory diseases require long-term management, but with modern immunomodulatory treatments and rehabilitation, the majority of patients achieve significant symptom control and maintain good functional capacity.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
Practical self-care strategies can help prevent muscle aches and minimise their impact.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Gradual exercise training:} Pacing physical activities and avoiding sudden increases in duration or intensity to prevent microtrauma.
    \item \textbf{Adequate hydration:} Maintaining fluid intake during exercise and hot weather to prevent dehydration-induced cramping and muscle injury.
    \item \textbf{Ergonomic optimization:} Setting up work environments to support healthy posture and reduce repetitive strain on neck and back muscles.
    \item \textbf{Regular stretching and mobility:} Incorporating flexibility exercises and regular breaks during sedentary tasks to prevent muscle shortening and stiffness.
    \item \textbf{Healthy sleep and stress reduction:} Prioritising sleep hygiene and stress management to prevent tension-related muscle pain.
\end{itemize}
