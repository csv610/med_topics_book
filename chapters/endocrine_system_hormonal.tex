\chapter{Endocrine System and Hormones}
\index{Endocrine System and Hormones}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
The endocrine system is the collection of glands that produce and release hormones, the chemical messengers that regulate the body's activities. Unlike the nervous system, which acts quickly through nerve impulses, the endocrine system acts more slowly but its effects are long-lasting and far-reaching. Hormones control growth, metabolism, reproduction, mood, blood pressure, blood sugar, and much more. Understanding the endocrine system helps explain how conditions such as diabetes, thyroid disease, and growth problems develop, and how they are treated.

\section*{Epidemiology}
The endocrine system is involved in many of the most common diseases. Diabetes, a disorder of the pancreatic hormone insulin, affects more than one in twenty people. Thyroid disorders are common, especially in women and with age. Hormone-related conditions affect people across the lifespan, from congenital disorders in childhood to menopause and age-related hormonal changes in later life.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The endocrine system works through a series of glands and hormones.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hypothalamus:} the brain's control centre, linking the nervous and endocrine systems
    \item \textbf{Pituitary gland:} the "master gland," which controls the other glands by releasing stimulating hormones
    \item \textbf{Thyroid:} produces thyroxine, controlling metabolism, growth, and energy
    \item \textbf{Parathyroid glands:} regulate calcium
    \item \textbf{Adrenal glands:} produce cortisol, adrenaline, and aldosterone, managing stress, blood pressure, and salt balance
    \item \textbf{Pancreas:} produces insulin and glucagon, controlling blood sugar
    \item \textbf{Ovaries and testes:} produce sex hormones, controlling reproduction and secondary sexual characteristics
    \item \textbf{Feedback control:} glands are regulated by negative feedback, so hormone levels stay balanced
    \item \textbf{Hormone action:} hormones travel in the blood and bind to specific receptors on target cells
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Symptoms arise when the system is out of balance.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Fatigue, weight change, and changes in energy
    \item Changes in appetite, temperature tolerance, and sweating
    \item Changes in heart rate and blood pressure
    \item Changes in skin, hair, and nail health
    \item Mood changes and sleep disturbance
    \item Changes in growth and development
    \item Menstrual changes, fertility problems, and sexual function changes
    \item Increased thirst, urination, and hunger with diabetes
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Hormonal symptoms overlap with many other conditions.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Depression, anxiety, and chronic stress
    \item Nutritional problems and eating disorders
    \item Chronic illness and infection
    \item Medication side effects
    \item Normal changes of ageing and menopause
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Persistent unexplained fatigue, weight change, or other hormonal symptoms should be discussed with a GP, who can arrange appropriate testing.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Very high blood sugar with vomiting, confusion, or rapid breathing
    \item Severe weakness, collapse, or vomiting in someone with adrenal disease
    \item Palpitations with chest pain or fainting
    \item Sudden severe headache with visual problems
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
The endocrine system is assessed with blood tests and other investigations.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hormone blood tests:} thyroid, cortisol, glucose, sex hormones, and others
    \item \textbf{Glucose tolerance testing:} for diabetes
    \item \textbf{Urine tests:} for hormone products
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} ultrasound, CT, or MRI of the glands
    \item \textbf{Dynamic tests:} stimulation and suppression tests of gland function
    \item \textbf{Bone density testing:} for hormonal effects on bone
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management of endocrine problems depends on the specific condition.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hormone replacement:} for deficiency, such as thyroxine, insulin, or sex hormones
    \item \textbf{Hormone suppression:} for overactivity, with medicines, surgery, or radiotherapy
    \item \textbf{Blocking agents:} to reduce the effects of hormones
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle measures:} diet, exercise, and weight control
    \item \textbf{Monitoring:} regular checks of hormone levels and complications
    \item \textbf{Specialist care:} endocrinologists for complex conditions
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Complications of untreated hormone imbalance, including heart, bone, and metabolic disease
    \item Growth and development problems
    \item Infertility
    \item Endocrine emergencies
    \item Effects on quality of life and mental health
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
With appropriate treatment, most endocrine conditions are well controlled, and people live full lives. Modern hormone replacement and other treatments have transformed the outlook for diabetes, thyroid disease, and other conditions. The endocrine system's balance can usually be restored and maintained with good care.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Eat a balanced diet and exercise regularly to support hormonal health
    \item Manage stress, which affects the adrenal system
    \item Attend regular health checks
    \item Take hormone medicines as prescribed
    \item Report new symptoms promptly
    \item Learn about the glands and hormones relevant to your health
\end{itemize}
