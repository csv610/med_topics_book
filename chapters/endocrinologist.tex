\chapter{Endocrinologist}
\index{Endocrinologist}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
An endocrinologist is a specialist doctor who diagnoses and treats disorders of the endocrine system, the glands and hormones that regulate the body. Endocrinologists manage conditions such as diabetes, thyroid disease, adrenal disorders, osteoporosis, polycystic ovary syndrome, and problems with growth and puberty. They are also involved in the management of obesity and metabolic disease, and in hormone-related cancers. An endocrinologist works with GPs to investigate complex hormonal problems and to provide long-term specialist care.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Endocrinologists care for a large and growing number of people. Diabetes is the most common condition they manage, along with thyroid disease, which affects a significant proportion of the population. Referrals to endocrinologists have increased with the rising prevalence of diabetes, obesity, and metabolic disease, and with better recognition of conditions such as polycystic ovary syndrome and osteoporosis.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Endocrinologists apply the science of hormones to diagnosis and treatment.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Training:} endocrinologists complete medical school, physician training, and specialist training in endocrinology and metabolism
    \item \textbf{Assessment:} they take a detailed history, examine the relevant glands, and order and interpret hormone tests
    \item \textbf{Diagnosis:} many conditions require dynamic testing of how glands respond to stimulation or suppression
    \item \textbf{Treatment:} they prescribe hormone replacement or suppression, and coordinate with other specialists for surgery and radiotherapy
    \item \textbf{Long-term care:} they monitor chronic conditions and their complications over many years
    \item \textbf{Research:} many endocrinologists are involved in research that improves the understanding and treatment of hormonal disease
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
People are referred to an endocrinologist for many reasons.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Diabetes, particularly when control is difficult or complications develop
    \item Thyroid problems, including overactive and underactive thyroid, nodules, and goitre
    \item Adrenal gland disorders, including Cushing's syndrome and adrenal insufficiency
    \item Pituitary disorders, including hormone deficiencies and tumours
    \item Osteoporosis and calcium disorders
    \item Polycystic ovary syndrome and other hormonal causes of irregular periods or infertility
    \item Growth and puberty problems in children and adolescents
    \item Obesity and metabolic syndrome
    \item Menopause and testosterone deficiency
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Endocrinologists are one of several specialists involved in hormonal and metabolic care.
\begin{itemize}
    \item GPs manage many hormonal conditions, including straightforward diabetes and thyroid disease
    \item Diabetes educators, dietitians, and podiatrists are part of the diabetes care team
    \item Gynaecologists manage hormone-related reproductive conditions
    \item Paediatric endocrinologists specialise in children's hormonal problems
    \item Surgeons treat gland tumours, often alongside the endocrinologist
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
A GP referral is needed to see an endocrinologist. Referral is appropriate when diagnosis is unclear, when treatment is not working, when complications develop, or when specialist management is needed for complex or rare conditions.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Very high blood sugar with vomiting, confusion, or rapid breathing
    \item Severe weakness, collapse, or vomiting in a person with adrenal disease
    \item Palpitations with chest pain or fainting
    \item Sudden severe headache with visual changes
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
The endocrinologist's investigations are guided by the suspected condition.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hormone blood tests:} thyroid, cortisol, growth hormone, and sex hormones
    \item \textbf{Glucose testing:} fasting, oral glucose tolerance, and continuous glucose monitoring
    \item \textbf{Urine tests:} hormone breakdown products
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} ultrasound, CT, MRI, and nuclear medicine scans of the glands
    \item \textbf{Dynamic tests:} stimulation and suppression tests of gland function
    \item \textbf{Bone density scanning:} for osteoporosis
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
The endocrinologist provides diagnosis, treatment, and ongoing care.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Diabetes care:} medicines, insulin, glucose monitoring, and complication screening
    \item \textbf{Thyroid treatment:} thyroxine replacement or antithyroid treatment
    \item \textbf{Hormone replacement:} for adrenal, pituitary, and sex hormone deficiencies
    \item \textbf{Bone care:} osteoporosis medicines and monitoring
    \item \textbf{Reproductive health:} management of PCOS and other hormonal causes of fertility problems
    \item \textbf{Coordination of care:} working with surgeons, radiologists, and other specialists
    \item \textbf{Education:} helping people understand and self-manage their condition
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Uncontrolled diabetes and its complications without specialist care
    \item Missed diagnoses of complex hormonal conditions
    \item Complications of hormonal treatments
    \item Endocrine emergencies
    \item The burden of long-term chronic disease without coordinated care
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
With endocrinologist care, most hormonal disorders are well managed, and complications are reduced. Diabetes control, thyroid disease, and adrenal conditions all have excellent outcomes with specialist treatment. The endocrinologist's role in long-term monitoring prevents complications and improves quality of life.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item See your GP for regular checks of blood glucose and thyroid where indicated
    \item Ask for a referral to an endocrinologist when specialist care is needed
    \item Bring your medicines, glucose records, and questions to appointments
    \item Follow the treatment plan and attend reviews
    \item Take hormone medicines exactly as prescribed
    \item Report new symptoms between appointments
\end{itemize}
