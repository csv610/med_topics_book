\chapter{Male sexual problems}
 
\section*{Definition and Overview}
Male sexual problems, clinically referred to as male sexual dysfunction, encompass a wide range of difficulties that prevent a man or a couple from experiencing satisfaction from sexual activity. These issues are categorized into four primary areas: erectile dysfunction (ED, difficulty achieving or maintaining an erection), ejaculatory disorders (such as premature, delayed, absent, or retrograde ejaculation), desire disorders (loss of libido), and pain disorders (such as dyspareunia or painful ejaculation). Sexual function is a complex process involving physiological systems (cardiovascular, endocrine, neurological), psychological factors, and interpersonal dynamics. A malfunction in any of these components can lead to sexual problems, which frequently cause significant emotional distress, low self-esteem, and relationship conflict.
 
\section*{Epidemiology}
Male sexual problems are highly prevalent and rise steeply with age. Erectile dysfunction affects approximately 20--30\% of men over 40, with the prevalence exceeding 50--70\% in men aged 70 and older. Premature ejaculation is the most common sexual problem in younger men, affecting approximately 20--30\% of men regardless of age. Delayed or absent ejaculation is less common, affecting 1--4\% of men. Despite the high prevalence, many men do not seek medical advice due to social stigma, embarrassment, or the misconception that sexual dysfunction is an inevitable part of ageing.
 
\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The causes of male sexual problems are broadly classified into organic (physical), psychogenic, and drug-induced:
 
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cardiovascular disease:} atherosclerosis, hypertension, and peripheral vascular disease impair blood flow to the penis, which is the most common organic cause of ED.
    \item \textbf{Neurological disorders:} conditions such as diabetes (diabetic neuropathy), multiple sclerosis, stroke, or pelvic surgery (e.g., radical prostatectomy) damage the nerves controlling erection and ejaculation.
    \item \textbf{Endocrine disorders:} hypogonadism (low testosterone), hyperprolactinaemia, and thyroid disorders.
    \item \textbf{Medications:} antihypertensives (beta-blockers), antidepressants (SSRIs), anti-androgens, and sedatives.
    \item \textbf{Psychogenic factors:} performance anxiety, chronic stress, depression, low self-esteem, and relationship discord.
\end{itemize}
 
\section*{Clinical Features}
The clinical presentation depends on the specific type of dysfunction:
 
\begin{itemize}
    \item Erectile dysfunction: persistent inability to achieve or maintain an erection sufficient for satisfactory sexual performance, and loss of morning erections (suggesting organic cause)
    \item Premature ejaculation: ejaculation occurring within one minute of penetration, or before the individual wishes, accompanied by a lack of control
    \item Delayed or absent ejaculation: significant delay in or complete inability to achieve ejaculation despite adequate stimulation
    \item Retrograde ejaculation: semen flowing backward into the bladder during orgasm rather than out of the urethra, resulting in a "dry" orgasm and cloudy urine after intercourse
    \item Dyspareunia: pain in the penis, perineum, or scrotum during or after sexual activity, or painful ejaculation
\end{itemize}
 
\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Organic vs. Psychogenic dysfunction:} organic dysfunction is typically gradual, constant, and associated with physical comorbidities and absent morning erections; psychogenic dysfunction is often sudden, situational (present with one partner but not during masturbation), and morning erections are preserved.
    \item \textbf{Erectile dysfunction vs. low libido:} ED is a physical inability to obtain an erection despite sexual desire; low libido is a lack of interest in sex itself.
    \item \textbf{Peyronie's disease:} physical bending of the penis during erection causing pain or difficulty with penetration, distinguished by palpable fibrous plaques.
    \item \textbf{Urinary tract infection (UTI) or prostatitis:} causing painful ejaculation and dysuria, differentiated by urinalysis and culture.
\end{itemize}
 
\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Men should consult a general practitioner or a urologist if they experience persistent sexual difficulties that cause distress, affect their relationships, or are accompanied by other symptoms (such as painful ejaculation, blood in semen, or voiding difficulties).
 
\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Priapism: a painful erection lasting longer than 4 hours, which is a urological emergency requiring urgent decompression to prevent permanent tissue damage
    \item Sudden, severe chest pain, shortness of breath, or collapse during or after sexual activity
\end{itemize}
 
\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Detailed clinical and sexual history:} evaluating the onset, duration, severity, partner dynamics, and morning erections.
    \item \textbf{Physical examination:} focusing on secondary sexual characteristics, assessment of peripheral pulses, sensory testing of the perineum, and examination of the penis and testicles.
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} fasting glucose, HbA1c, lipid profile, and early morning total testosterone, prolactin, and TSH.
    \item \textbf{Urinalysis:} to screen for infection or retrograde ejaculation (presence of sperm in post-ejaculatory urine).
    \item \textbf{Specialised vascular tests:} penile duplex ultrasound (using intracavernosal injection of vasoactive agents) to evaluate penile blood flow in selected cases.
\end{itemize}
 
\section*{Management}
Treatment is tailored to the specific diagnosis and patient preferences:
 
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pharmacological for ED:} phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE5) inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil) are first-line. Intracavernosal injections (alprostadil) or urethral suppositories are options for those who fail or cannot take oral agents.
    \item \textbf{Pharmacological for Premature Ejaculation:} selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs, like dapoxetine) used on-demand or daily, or topical anaesthetic sprays/creams.
    \item \textbf{Hormone therapy:} testosterone replacement therapy (TRT) for hypogonadism.
    \item \textbf{Psychosexual therapy:} cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), couples therapy, and sensate focus exercises to reduce performance anxiety and improve communication.
    \item \textbf{Devices and surgery:} vacuum erection devices (VED) or surgical implantation of a penile prosthesis for severe refractory ED.
\end{itemize}
 
\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe relationship strain, sexual dissatisfaction, and marital breakdown
    \item Depression, anxiety, and profound loss of self-esteem and masculinity
    \item Unexplained male-factor infertility (especially in ejaculatory dysfunction or retrograde ejaculation)
    \item Permanent erectile tissue damage (fibrosis) in untreated priapism
\end{itemize}
 
\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The prognosis for male sexual problems is highly favorable, with modern treatments offering effective solutions for almost all cases of erectile dysfunction and premature ejaculation. Successful outcomes are enhanced when partners are involved in the treatment process and when psychosexual counselling is combined with medical therapy. Identifying and managing physical causes (like diabetes and cardiovascular disease) not only restores sexual function but also improves long-term systemic health.
 
\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Maintain a healthy cardiovascular system: eat a balanced diet, exercise regularly, and maintain a healthy weight
    \item Avoid smoking, limit alcohol intake, and avoid recreational drugs, all of which adversely affect vascular and neurological function
    \item Discuss sexual side effects of medications with your doctor early to explore alternative options
    \item Manage chronic diseases (such as diabetes, hypertension, and depression) diligently
    \item Practice open communication with your partner to reduce performance anxiety and build intimacy
\end{itemize}
