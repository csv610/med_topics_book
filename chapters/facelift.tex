\chapter{Facelift}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
A facelift, known medically as rhytidectomy, is a cosmetic surgical procedure that tightens the skin and deeper tissues of the face and neck to reduce the signs of ageing. It can improve sagging skin, jowls, and loose neck skin, producing a smoother, more youthful appearance. A facelift is a significant operation performed under general or local anaesthetic, with a recovery period of a few weeks. It is a personal decision, and it is important to understand the benefits, risks, and realistic outcomes before proceeding.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Facelifts are among the most commonly performed cosmetic surgical procedures in Australia, with thousands performed each year, mostly in people in their 40s to 70s. The procedure is chosen for cosmetic reasons, and its popularity reflects the desire of many people to address the visible signs of ageing. As with all cosmetic surgery, choosing a qualified, experienced surgeon is essential for good outcomes.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The face ages through changes in the skin, fat, and deeper tissues.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Skin:} the skin loses collagen and elasticity, becoming looser and more wrinkled
    \item \textbf{Fat:} facial fat shifts and is lost, contributing to hollowing and jowls
    \item \textbf{Deeper tissues:} the muscles and connective tissue of the face relax and sag
    \item \textbf{Effects:} sagging cheeks, jowls, and a loose neck develop
    \item \textbf{The facelift:} the surgeon lifts and tightens the deeper tissue layers, removes or repositions excess skin, and re-drapes the skin smoothly
    \item \textbf{Limits:} a facelift addresses sagging, but it does not remove fine lines, sun damage, or wrinkles around the eyes, which need other treatments
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
People consider a facelift for specific concerns.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sagging of the cheeks and mid-face
    \item Jowls along the jawline
    \item Loose, crepey skin and excess folds in the neck
    \item A tired or aged appearance
    \item The desire to look more rested and youthful
    \item Concerns about the fit between appearance and self-image
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Other procedures address related concerns.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Eyelid surgery for sagging eyelids
    \item Brow lift for a drooping forehead
    \item Fillers and fat transfer for volume loss
    \item Botox and skin treatments for lines and skin quality
    \item Non-surgical skin tightening, with modest results
    \item The right procedure depends on the specific concerns
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
People considering a facelift should have a consultation with a specialist plastic surgeon, who assesses suitability, discusses expectations, and explains the procedure and risks. Medical assessment is needed for any complications after surgery.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chest pain, breathlessness, or signs of a blood clot after surgery
    \item Sudden severe swelling, bleeding, or bruising
    \item Signs of infection, including fever and spreading redness
    \item Sudden vision changes or weakness of the face
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Before a facelift, the person is fully assessed.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Surgical consultation:} discussion of goals, the procedure, and realistic outcomes
    \item \textbf{Medical assessment:} general health, medicines, and anaesthetic review
    \item \textbf{Smoking status:} smoking greatly increases complications and is discouraged
    \item \textbf{Photographs:} taken for planning and comparison
    \item \textbf{Consent:} a full discussion of benefits, risks, and recovery
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
The procedure and recovery are structured.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Anaesthesia:} general or local anaesthetic with sedation
    \item \textbf{Incision:} hidden around the ear and hairline
    \item \textbf{The lift:} deeper tissues are lifted and tightened, and excess skin is removed
    \item \textbf{Closure:} the incisions are closed with fine stitches
    \item \textbf{Recovery:} swelling and bruising for a few weeks, with dressings and drains sometimes used
    \item \textbf{Return to activity:} most people return to work within two to three weeks
    \item \textbf{Final result:} the result matures over months
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Bleeding and bruising, including a haematoma requiring drainage
    \item Infection
    \item Scarring, which is usually well hidden but may be visible
    \item Nerve injury causing temporary or permanent numbness or weakness
    \item Skin loss or poor healing, more likely in smokers
    \item Unsatisfactory results, including asymmetry
    \item The risks of anaesthesia
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
A well-performed facelift produces a natural, long-lasting improvement, typically lasting about ten years. The results depend on the surgeon's skill, the quality of the skin, and general health. Complications are uncommon with an experienced surgeon, and most people are very satisfied with the outcome.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Choose a specialist plastic surgeon with experience in facelifts
    \item Stop smoking well before surgery
    \item Discuss expectations honestly at the consultation
    \item Follow the post-operative instructions on dressings, position, and activity
    \item Avoid sun and use sunscreen to protect the results
    \item Attend follow-up appointments
    \item Allow time for the swelling to settle before judging the result
\end{itemize}
