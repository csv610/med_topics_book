\chapter{Eye Strain}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Eye strain, also called asthenopia, is the tiredness, discomfort, and aching of the eyes that follows prolonged use, particularly close work such as reading, driving, or staring at screens. Digital eye strain, related to computers, phones, and tablets, has become increasingly common. Eye strain is uncomfortable but not harmful to the eyes, and it usually settles with rest and simple measures. It can also be a sign that glasses are needed or that the prescription is out of date.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Eye strain affects most people at some point, and digital eye strain is widespread, with a large proportion of adults reporting symptoms related to screen use. It is more common in people with uncorrected vision problems, poor lighting, and long periods of close work. Eye strain does not cause permanent eye damage, but it causes discomfort, headaches, and reduced productivity.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Eye strain results from overuse of the focusing and eye-tracking muscles.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Prolonged focusing:} close work keeps the focusing muscles contracted for long periods
    \item \textbf{Reduced blinking:} screen use reduces blinking, causing dry eyes and discomfort
    \item \textbf{Uncorrected vision:} an outdated or absent prescription makes the eyes work harder
    \item \textbf{Poor lighting:} glare, dim light, and screen reflections increase strain
    \item \textbf{Poor posture:} awkward positions and screen distances add to the strain
    \item \textbf{Accommodation:} the eye's ability to focus at different distances declines with age, increasing strain for near work
    \item \textbf{Effects:} the muscles fatigue, and the eye surface dries, producing discomfort and headache
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Symptoms develop during or after prolonged visual tasks.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Tired, sore, or burning eyes
    \item Eye discomfort and a heavy feeling
    \item Headaches, often around the forehead
    \item Blurred or double vision at times
    \item Dry eyes and increased watering
    \item Difficulty focusing after screen use
    \item Neck, shoulder, and back ache from poor posture
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Other conditions cause similar symptoms.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Uncorrected refractive error, requiring glasses
    \item Dry eye syndrome
    \item Migraine, which causes more severe headaches with other symptoms
    \item Glaucoma and other eye conditions, which can cause eye discomfort
    \item Eye strain is a diagnosis of exclusion after these are considered
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See an optometrist or doctor if eye strain is persistent, if symptoms occur without screen use, or if there is any change in vision. Regular eye examinations detect the need for glasses.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden loss of vision
    \item Sudden severe eye pain
    \item A red eye with severe pain and nausea
    \item Sudden floaters with flashes
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Assessment determines whether glasses are needed.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History:} screen use, work habits, and symptoms
    \item \textbf{Visual acuity and refraction:} checks the need for glasses
    \item \textbf{Eye examination:} to exclude other conditions
    \item \textbf{Assessment of ergonomics:} screen position, lighting, and distance
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management is simple and effective.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The 20-20-20 rule:} every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds
    \item \textbf{Blink regularly:} conscious blinking moistens the eyes
    \item \textbf{Correct the environment:} adjust lighting, reduce glare, and position the screen at arm's length
    \item \textbf{Use glasses:} update the prescription for computer or reading work
    \item \textbf{Take breaks:} regular rests from screens and close work
    \item \textbf{Lubricating drops:} for dry eyes associated with screen use
    \item \textbf{Good posture:} a comfortable, upright position
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Headaches and fatigue
    \item Reduced productivity at work
    \item Discomfort that leads to avoiding necessary tasks
    \item The underlying vision problem being missed if no eye examination is done
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Eye strain settles with rest and simple measures, and it does not damage the eyes. Most people find that a combination of breaks, better lighting, and the correct glasses eliminates the symptoms. Regular eye examinations ensure that any prescription changes are detected.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Follow the 20-20-20 rule
    \item Position the screen at arm's length and slightly below eye level
    \item Reduce glare with blinds and screen filters
    \item Blink often and use lubricating drops if needed
    \item Have regular eye examinations
    \item Rest the eyes during long tasks
    \item Adjust lighting to avoid dim rooms and bright screens together
\end{itemize}
