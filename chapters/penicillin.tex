\chapter{Penicillin}
\index{Penicillin}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. It does not replace advice from a prescriber or pharmacist.}

\section*{What it is}
Penicillins are a family of antibiotics used for some bacterial infections. Examples include phenoxymethylpenicillin, amoxicillin, flucloxacillin, and co-amoxiclav. The choice depends on the suspected infection, local resistance patterns, allergies, pregnancy or breastfeeding, kidney function, and other medicines.

\section*{How it is used}
Take a prescribed penicillin exactly as directed and complete the course unless a clinician tells you to stop. Do not share antibiotics or use leftover doses. Antibiotics do not treat viral illnesses such as most colds and influenza. Ask a clinician or pharmacist what to do if you vomit after a dose or miss one; do not double the next dose without advice.

\section*{Side effects and allergy}
Nausea, abdominal discomfort, and diarrhoea can occur and do not by themselves prove an allergy. Tell your healthcare professional what happened, when it started, and which medicine and dose were involved. A suspected allergy should be recorded accurately; a clinician may assess whether testing or a supervised challenge is appropriate. Do not deliberately re-take a penicillin to test yourself.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Get emergency help immediately for trouble breathing, wheezing, throat or tongue swelling, faintness, or a rapidly spreading itchy rash. Seek urgent medical advice for blistering or peeling skin, severe diarrhoea (especially with blood), yellowing of the skin or eyes, or symptoms that are rapidly worsening.

\section*{Interactions and precautions}
Before treatment, tell the prescriber about previous reactions to penicillins or related antibiotics, kidney or liver disease, pregnancy or breastfeeding, and all medicines and supplements. Some penicillins interact with other medicines, and doses may need adjustment. Never stop an essential antibiotic or change the dose without advice, except while seeking emergency help for a severe allergic reaction.
