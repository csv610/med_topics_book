\chapter{Kids and Mental Health}
\index{Kids and Mental Health}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Children's mental health is about how children think, feel, behave, and cope with life, and it is as important as physical health. Around one in seven children and adolescents experiences a mental health condition, including anxiety, depression, attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), and behavioural problems, and half of all mental health conditions begin before age 14. This chapter explains the signs of mental health problems in children, the common conditions, when to seek help, and how parents, schools, and communities support children's emotional wellbeing. Early recognition and treatment give children the best chance of thriving.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Mental health conditions affect around one in seven children and young people in many countries, and anxiety is the most common. Rates of mental health problems have risen over recent decades, particularly in adolescent girls, and the COVID-19 pandemic increased distress in young people. Boys are more often diagnosed with ADHD and behavioural problems, while girls more often experience anxiety and depression. Only a minority of children with mental health conditions receive professional help, and waiting times for services can be long. Suicide is a leading cause of death in young people, making prevention and early support critical.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Children's mental health is shaped by the interaction of genetics, brain development, temperament, and the environment. Risk factors include family mental illness, parental stress and conflict, abuse and neglect, bullying, school difficulties, social disadvantage, and major life events such as grief and moving. Protective factors include secure attachment, supportive parents, friendships, engagement at school, and community connection. The developing brain is highly responsive, and both risk and protective factors shape emotional regulation, learning, and behaviour. Mental health conditions in children are brain and mind conditions with biological and environmental roots, not a result of bad parenting, and they respond well to early, evidence-based help.

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Anxiety: excessive worry, avoidance, physical symptoms such as tummy aches, and difficulty separating from parents
    \item Low mood: sadness, irritability, loss of interest, and changes in sleep and appetite
    \item Behaviour problems: tantrums, defiance, and aggression
    \item ADHD: inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity that impair function
    \item Withdrawal from friends and activities
    \item Falling school performance and reluctance to attend school
    \item Physical symptoms without a clear medical cause
    \item Self-harm and thoughts of suicide in older children and teenagers
    \item Changes in eating and sleeping
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
The presentation of mental distress in children has many possible causes.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Anxiety disorders:} separation, social, and generalised anxiety
    \item \textbf{Depression:} persistent low mood and loss of interest
    \item \textbf{ADHD:} attention and hyperactivity difficulties
    \item \textbf{Autism:} differences in social communication and behaviour
    \item \textbf{Behavioural and conduct disorders:} persistent disruptive behaviour
    \item \textbf{Trauma and abuse:} responses to adverse experiences
    \item \textbf{Physical illness:} sleep, thyroid, and other conditions
    \item \textbf{Learning difficulties:} affecting school and behaviour
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Parents should seek help if a child's distress or behaviour is persistent, severe, or affecting school, friendships, or family life; if there are concerns about self-harm or suicide; or if there are major changes in mood, sleep, or eating. The GP is the first step, and schools, headspace, and child and adolescent mental health services provide further support. Trusted instincts matter: if you are worried about your child, help is available.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Immediate risk of suicide or self-harm
    \item A child who is severely distressed, agitated, or unsafe
    \item Threats of violence or harm to others
    \item Confusion, collapse, or an inability to care for themselves
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive assessment:} the child's development, behaviour, emotions, and family context
    \item \textbf{Interviews with parents and school:} reports from home and school
    \item \textbf{Screening questionnaires:} validated tools for anxiety, depression, and ADHD
    \item \textbf{Physical assessment:} excluding medical causes of symptoms
    \item \textbf{Risk assessment:} exploring self-harm and suicide thoughts
    \item \textbf{Referral:} to psychology, paediatrics, or child and adolescent mental health services
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Psychological therapies:} cognitive behavioural therapy and other evidence-based approaches
    \item \textbf{Parent support and training:} helping parents respond effectively
    \item \textbf{School involvement:} supports in the classroom and counselling at school
    \item \textbf{Family therapy:} improving communication and relationships
    \item \textbf{Medicines:} for some conditions, such as ADHD and depression, under specialist care
    \item \textbf{Addressing risk factors:} bullying, family stress, and trauma
    \item \textbf{Strengthening protective factors:} friendships, activities, and connection
    \item \textbf{Crisis support:} urgent care and safety planning when needed
    \item \textbf{Ongoing review:} monitoring progress and adjusting treatment
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Poor school performance and school refusal
    \item Difficulty forming friendships and social isolation
    \item Family conflict and stress
    \item Self-harm and suicide
    \item Substance use in adolescence
    \item Mental health problems persisting into adulthood
    \item Effects on physical health and development
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Children's mental health conditions respond well to early treatment. Most children with anxiety, depression, and ADHD improve substantially with psychological support, parenting help, and, where needed, medicines, and many recover fully. The developing brain is resilient, and strong family and school support improves outcomes. The most important step is recognising the problem early and seeking help, because early intervention prevents the difficulties from becoming entrenched.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Build warm, responsive relationships and spend regular time together
    \item Talk openly about feelings and listen without judgement
    \item Model healthy coping and self-care
    \item Support good sleep, activity, and nutrition
    \item Limit screens and social media, especially before bed
    \item Stay involved with school and teachers
    \item Encourage friendships, hobbies, and time outdoors
    \item Seek help early rather than waiting
    \item Look after your own mental health as a parent
    \item Know the crisis numbers and keep them accessible
\end{itemize}
