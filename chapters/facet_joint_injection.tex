\chapter{Facet Joint Injection}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
A facet joint injection is a procedure in which medicine is injected into the small joints at the back of the spine to diagnose and treat back or neck pain arising from these joints. The facet joints allow the spine to move, and they can become arthritic or irritated, causing pain that may spread to the buttocks, hips, or shoulders. The injection usually contains a local anaesthetic and a corticosteroid, and it is performed with imaging guidance. It is used both to confirm the source of the pain and to provide relief.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Back and neck pain are among the most common reasons for medical visits and a leading cause of disability. The facet joints are the source of pain in a significant minority of people with persistent spinal pain, particularly older people with arthritis. Facet joint injections are among the most commonly performed procedures in pain medicine, and they help both to diagnose and to treat this type of pain.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The facet joints can be a source of spinal pain.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Anatomy:} the facet joints sit at the back of the spine, one on each side at every level, guiding movement and bearing load
    \item \textbf{Arthritis:} wear and tear, or osteoarthritis, inflames the joints with age
    \item \textbf{Injury:} whiplash and other injuries can strain the joints
    \item \textbf{Pain referral:} pain from the facet joints is felt in the spine and spreads to nearby areas: the buttocks and hips from the lower back, and the shoulders and head from the neck
    \item \textbf{Mechanism:} inflammation of the joint lining stimulates pain nerves
    \item \textbf{The injection:} local anaesthetic blocks the pain, confirming the joint as the source, and corticosteroid reduces inflammation
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Facet joint pain has a characteristic pattern.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Pain in the lower back or neck, worse with bending backwards or twisting
    \item Pain that is eased by sitting and worsened by standing or arching
    \item Pain spreading to the buttocks, hips, or shoulders
    \item Morning stiffness and aching
    \item Tenderness over the spine
    \item Pain without nerve symptoms such as numbness or weakness, which point to other causes
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Other sources of spinal pain must be considered.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Disc problems, including herniated discs with nerve pain
    \item Sacroiliac joint pain
    \item Muscle strain and ligament pain
    \item Spinal stenosis
    \item Hip joint arthritis, which mimics lower back pain
    \item Serious causes: fracture, infection, and cancer, which require urgent assessment
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
People with persistent back or neck pain should be assessed by a GP, who can arrange imaging and referral to a pain specialist or musculoskeletal service.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness in the saddle area
    \item Sudden weakness or paralysis of the legs
    \item Severe back pain with fever
    \item After the injection: severe headache, new weakness, or fever
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Before an injection, the pain is investigated.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clinical assessment:} the pattern of pain and its movements
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} X-rays, CT, or MRI show joint arthritis and exclude other causes
    \item \textbf{Diagnostic injection:} a small amount of local anaesthetic alone is sometimes used to confirm the facet joints as the source
    \item \textbf{Pre-procedure checks:} medicines, allergies, and general health
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
The injection is part of a broader pain plan.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The procedure:} performed with X-ray guidance, with the person lying face down; takes about 20 to 30 minutes
    \item \textbf{The medicine:} local anaesthetic and corticosteroid
    \item \textbf{Immediate effect:} the anaesthetic provides quick relief that confirms the diagnosis
    \item \textbf{Longer effect:} the steroid reduces inflammation over days to weeks
    \item \textbf{Repeat injections:} a course of up to two or three if effective
    \item \textbf{Ablation:} for lasting relief, radiofrequency ablation of the nerves to the joints
    \item \textbf{Alongside care:} exercise, physiotherapy, and activity management
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Temporary increase in pain after the injection
    \item Flushing, raised blood sugar, and sleep disturbance from steroids
    \item Bleeding and infection, which are rare
    \item Nerve injury, which is very rare with imaging guidance
    \item Headache from dural puncture
    \item The side effects of repeated steroids
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Facet joint injections provide meaningful relief for many people with facet joint pain, particularly when combined with exercise and physiotherapy. The benefit can last from weeks to months, and radiofrequency ablation offers longer relief for those who respond. Most people improve, and the injection helps clarify the source of the pain so that care can be targeted.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Stay active and strengthen the core and back muscles
    \item Maintain a healthy weight
    \item Use good posture and lifting technique
    \item Follow the physiotherapy programme
    \item Report new weakness, numbness, or bladder problems urgently
    \item Discuss the benefits and risks of injections with your specialist
\end{itemize}
