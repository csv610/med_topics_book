\chapter{Human Metapneumovirus (HMPV)}
\index{Human Metapneumovirus (HMPV)}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Human metapneumovirus (HMPV) is a common respiratory virus that causes infections ranging from the common cold to severe bronchitis and pneumonia. It belongs to the same virus family as respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) and circulates mainly in winter and spring. HMPV affects people of all ages, but it is most serious in young children, older adults, and people with weakened immune systems or chronic lung and heart conditions. Most infections are mild and resolve on their own, and there is no specific antiviral treatment, with care focusing on symptom relief and support.

\section*{Epidemiology}
HMPV is one of the most common causes of respiratory infection worldwide; most children have been infected by age five, and reinfections occur throughout life. It causes a significant proportion of winter respiratory illnesses and hospitalisations in young children, similar in impact to RSV and influenza. In older adults and people with chronic disease, HMPV is an important cause of pneumonia and hospitalisation. Outbreaks occur in aged-care facilities and hospitals, and the virus is detected with increasing frequency as routine testing has expanded.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Virus:} human metapneumovirus, an RNA virus of the Pneumoviridae family
    \item \textbf{Transmission:} respiratory droplets and contact with contaminated surfaces
    \item \textbf{Seasonality:} circulates mainly in late winter and spring, with year-round low-level activity
    \item \textbf{Pathophysiology:} the virus infects the lining of the airways, causing inflammation, swelling, and increased mucus
    \item \textbf{Upper respiratory tract:} cold-like symptoms in most people
    \item \textbf{Lower respiratory tract:} bronchiolitis, bronchitis, and pneumonia in at-risk groups
    \item \textbf{Immunity:} infection gives partial, short-lived immunity, so reinfection is common
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Runny nose, cough, sore throat, and fever
    \item Headache, muscle aches, and fatigue
    \item Wheezing, breathlessness, and rapid breathing in more severe disease
    \item In infants and young children: bronchiolitis with cough and breathing difficulty
    \item In older adults: pneumonia, worsening of COPD and asthma
    \item Ear infections and croup can occur in children
    \item Symptoms typically last 5--7 days but may persist longer
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Other respiratory viruses: RSV, influenza, rhinovirus, adenovirus, and SARS-CoV-2
    \item Bacterial pneumonia and bronchitis
    \item Asthma, COPD exacerbations, and other chronic lung conditions
    \item Whooping cough in children
    \item Allergic rhinitis and sinusitis
    \item Testing distinguishes HMPV from these, though it often coexists with other viruses
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Most HMPV infections are managed at home with rest and fluids. See a doctor for breathing difficulty, persistent fever, dehydration, or worsening symptoms, and for infants, older adults, and people with chronic conditions who become unwell.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Difficulty breathing, rapid breathing, or blue lips
    \item Chest pain or severe breathlessness
    \item A baby who is floppy, lethargic, not feeding, or grunting
    \item High fever with confusion or drowsiness
    \item Severe dehydration
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clinical diagnosis:} most infections are diagnosed on symptoms alone
    \item \textbf{Rapid tests:} nose and throat swabs, including PCR panels that detect HMPV alongside other viruses
    \item \textbf{Chest X-ray:} for suspected pneumonia
    \item \textbf{Oxygen monitoring:} pulse oximetry in hospitalised patients
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} in severe or complicated cases
    \item \textbf{Public health reporting:} outbreaks in facilities are reported and managed
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Supportive care:} rest, fluids, and paracetamol for fever and discomfort
    \item \textbf{Symptom relief:} saline drops for congestion and honey for cough in older children
    \item \textbf{Hospital care:} oxygen, fluids, and breathing support for severe disease
    \item \textbf{No specific antiviral:} antibiotics do not help unless bacterial co-infection occurs
    \item \textbf{Management of chronic conditions:} adjust asthma and COPD treatment during infection
    \item \textbf{Infection control:} hand hygiene and staying home while unwell
    \item \textbf{Prevention of spread:} coughing into the elbow and cleaning surfaces
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Bronchiolitis and pneumonia, especially in infants and older adults
    \item Exacerbations of asthma and COPD
    \item Ear infections and sinusitis in children
    \item Respiratory failure requiring intensive care in high-risk patients
    \item Secondary bacterial infections
    \item Worsening of heart failure and other chronic conditions
    \item Outbreaks in aged care and hospitals with high-risk residents
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Most people recover fully from HMPV within a week to ten days without treatment. The outlook is excellent for healthy children and adults, while infants, older adults, and immunocompromised people are at higher risk of severe disease and hospitalisation. With supportive hospital care, even severe infections usually resolve. There is no vaccine yet, although several are in development, and prevention relies on hygiene and protecting high-risk groups.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Practise good hand hygiene, especially in winter
    \item Cover coughs and sneezes with the elbow
    \item Stay home when unwell to avoid spreading infection
    \item Clean frequently touched surfaces
    \item Protect high-risk family members from exposure where possible
    \item Maintain general health: immunisation against flu and COVID, good nutrition, and sleep
    \item Seek medical care early for infants and people with chronic conditions
    \item Follow public health advice during outbreaks
\end{itemize}
