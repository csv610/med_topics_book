\chapter{Metabolic Syndrome}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Metabolic syndrome is a cluster of interrelated metabolic abnormalities that collectively increase the risk of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, and all-cause mortality. It is not a single disease but a constellation of risk factors that commonly coexist: central (abdominal) obesity, elevated blood pressure, elevated fasting glucose, elevated triglycerides, and reduced HDL cholesterol. The underlying pathophysiology centres on insulin resistance and chronic low-grade inflammation driven by excess visceral adipose tissue. Diagnosis requires at least three of five components per harmonised criteria (IDF, NHLBI/AHA, WHI). Identifying metabolic syndrome enables early intervention to prevent progression to diabetes and cardiovascular events.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Metabolic syndrome affects approximately 30--35\% of Australian adults, with prevalence increasing with age (over 50\% in those >60 years). Indigenous Australians have a significantly higher prevalence and earlier onset. Global prevalence is rising in parallel with obesity and sedentary lifestyles. It is more common in men under 60 and women over 60. The syndrome confers a 2-fold increased risk of cardiovascular disease and a 5-fold increased risk of type 2 diabetes. Many people with metabolic syndrome are undiagnosed because components are often assessed in isolation rather than as a cluster.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Visceral adiposity:} excess visceral fat is metabolically active, releasing free fatty acids (FFAs), pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-$\alpha$, IL-6), and adipokines (leptin, resistin); reduced adiponectin
    \item \textbf{Insulin resistance:} FFAs impair insulin signalling in muscle and liver; compensatory hyperinsulinaemia; hepatic insulin resistance increases VLDL production and gluconeogenesis
    \item \textbf{Dyslipidaemia:} high triglycerides, low HDL, small dense LDL particles (atherogenic dyslipidaemia); driven by insulin resistance and increased hepatic VLDL secretion
    \item \textbf{Hypertension:} insulin resistance causes sympathetic overactivity, renal sodium retention, endothelial dysfunction, and vascular remodelling; hyperinsulinaemia stimulates vascular smooth muscle growth
    \item \textbf{Glucose dysregulation:} impaired fasting glucose (IFG) and impaired glucose tolerance (IGT) precede type 2 diabetes; beta-cell dysfunction eventually fails to compensate for insulin resistance
    \item \textbf{Inflammation and oxidative stress:} chronic low-grade inflammation (elevated CRP, fibrinogen); oxidative stress promotes endothelial dysfunction and atherosclerosis
    \item \textbf{Genetics and epigenetics:} polygenic susceptibility; thrifty genotype hypothesis; intrauterine growth restriction and fetal programming increase adult risk
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle drivers:} physical inactivity, energy-dense nutrient-poor diet, sleep disruption, chronic stress, smoking, alcohol excess
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Metabolic syndrome is typically asymptomatic until complications develop. Clinical clues include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Central obesity:} waist circumference $\ge$94 cm (men), $\ge$80 cm (women) for Europids; lower cut-offs for Asian populations ($\ge$90 cm men, $\ge$80 cm women)
    \item \textbf{Acanthosis nigricans:} velvety hyperpigmentation in axillae and neck, marker of insulin resistance
    \item \textbf{Skin tags:} multiple acrochordons, associated with hyperinsulinaemia
    \item \textbf{Signs of individual components:} elevated BP, xanthomas (severe dyslipidaemia), peripheral neuropathy (pre-diabetes)
    \item \textbf{Associated conditions:} NAFLD/MAFLD, obstructive sleep apnoea, PCOS, gout, osteoarthritis, depression
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Individual components in isolation:} hypertension, dyslipidaemia, IFG/IGT may exist without full metabolic syndrome
    \item \textbf{Secondary causes:} Cushing syndrome (central obesity, hypertension, glucose intolerance), hypothyroidism (dyslipidaemia, weight gain), acromegaly, medication effects (corticosteroids, antipsychotics, protease inhibitors)
    \item \textbf{Monogenic obesity syndromes:} rare (e.g., leptin receptor, MC4R mutations)
    \item \textbf{Lipodystrophy:} generalised or partial loss of adipose tissue with severe metabolic syndrome features
    \item \textbf{PCOS:} shares insulin resistance and central obesity; diagnose by Rotterdam criteria
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Screening is recommended for adults with BMI $\ge$25 kg/m$^2$ (or $\ge$23 for Asian populations), waist circumference above thresholds, family history of type 2 diabetes or premature CVD, or features of insulin resistance. A diagnosis of metabolic syndrome should trigger comprehensive cardiovascular risk assessment and lifestyle intervention.

\textbf{Urgent review for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Symptoms of cardiovascular disease (chest pain, dyspnoea, claudication)
    \item Symptoms of hyperglycaemia (polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss)
    \item Severe hypertension (SBP $\ge$180 or DBP $\ge$110)
    \item Acute pancreatitis (can be precipitated by severe hypertriglyceridaemia >10 mmol/L)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Diagnosis requires $\ge$3 of 5 criteria (harmonised IDF/NHLBI/AHA 2009):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Waist circumference:} population- and country-specific cut-points (Europid men $\ge$94 cm, women $\ge$80 cm)
    \item \textbf{Triglycerides:} $\ge$1.7 mmol/L (150 mg/dL) or on lipid-lowering therapy
    \item \textbf{HDL cholesterol:} $<$1.0 mmol/L (men), $<$1.3 mmol/L (women) or on therapy
    \item \textbf{Blood pressure:} SBP $\ge$130 or DBP $\ge$85 mmHg or on antihypertensive therapy
    \item \textbf{Fasting glucose:} $\ge$5.6 mmol/L (100 mg/dL) or on glucose-lowering therapy
\end{enumerate}
Investigations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Lipid profile:} fasting triglycerides, HDL, LDL, total cholesterol; consider non-HDL and apoB
    \item \textbf{Glucose metabolism:} fasting glucose, HbA1c, consider OGTT for IGT
    \item \textbf{Liver function:} ALT, GGT (surrogate for NAFLD/MAFLD)
    \item \textbf{Renal function:} eGFR, UACR (early diabetic nephropathy)
    \item \textbf{Inflammatory markers:} hs-CRP (optional, refines risk)
    \item \textbf{Cardiovascular risk assessment:} Australian absolute CVD risk calculator (age, sex, smoking, BP, cholesterol, diabetes, HDL)
    \item \textbf{Sleep study:} if symptoms of OSA
    \item \textbf{Hormonal:} testosterone (men with obesity), androgens/prolactin (women with PCOS features)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management is centred on intensive lifestyle intervention; pharmacotherapy targets individual components when lifestyle is insufficient.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle intervention (cornerstone):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Weight loss: 5--10\% body weight reduces all components; target 0.5--1 kg/week
        \item Diet: Mediterranean or DASH pattern; high fibre, whole grains, vegetables, legumes, nuts, olive oil; limit refined carbs, added sugars, saturated/trans fats, alcohol
        \item Physical activity: 150--300 min/week moderate aerobic + 2 sessions resistance training; reduce sedentary time
        \item Sleep: 7--9 hours/night; screen for OSA
        \item Stress management: mindfulness, CBT
        \item Smoking cessation: critical for CVD risk reduction
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pharmacotherapy by component:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Dysglycaemia:} metformin (first-line for prediabetes with BMI $\ge$25, age <60, or history of GDM); consider GLP-1 RAs (semaglutide, liraglutide) or SGLT2 inhibitors for weight loss + cardiorenal benefit in established diabetes
        \item \textbf{Hypertension:} ACEi/ARB first-line (renoprotective, improve insulin sensitivity); thiazide/thiazide-like or CCB as add-on; avoid beta-blockers as first-line if metabolic syndrome predominant (worsen insulin resistance)
        \item \textbf{Dyslipidaemia:} statin for primary/secondary CVD prevention per risk calculator; fenofibrate if triglycerides $\ge$5 mmol/L (pancreatitis prevention); omega-3 (icosapent ethyl) for high triglycerides on statin
        \item \textbf{Obesity:} GLP-1 RAs (semaglutide 2.4 mg, liraglutide 3 mg) or dual GIP/GLP-1 (tirzepatide) for BMI $\ge$30 or $\ge$27 with comorbidity; bariatric surgery for BMI $\ge$40 or $\ge$35 with comorbidity
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multidisciplinary care:} GP, dietitian, exercise physiologist, psychologist, endocrinologist, cardiologist as needed
    \item \textbf{Monitoring:} 3--6 monthly review of weight, waist, BP, lipids, HbA1c, LFTs, renal function; adjust therapy
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Type 2 diabetes:} 5-fold increased risk; progression from IFG/IGT over 5--10 years
    \item \textbf{Cardiovascular disease:} coronary artery disease, stroke, heart failure, peripheral arterial disease; 2-fold increased risk; accelerated atherosclerosis
    \item \textbf{NAFLD/MAFLD $\to$ NASH $\to$ cirrhosis:} hepatic manifestation of metabolic syndrome; leading cause of liver transplant
    \item \textbf{Chronic kidney disease:} hyperfiltration, albuminopathy, nephrosclerosis
    \item \textbf{Obstructive sleep apnoea:} bidirectional relationship with obesity and insulin resistance
    \item \textbf{PCOS and infertility:} hyperinsulinaemia drives ovarian androgen production
    \item \textbf{Cognitive decline and dementia:} vascular and metabolic contributions
    \item \textbf{Cancer:} increased risk of colorectal, breast (postmenopausal), endometrial, pancreatic, hepatocellular
    \item \textbf{Psychosocial:} depression, anxiety, weight stigma, reduced quality of life
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Metabolic syndrome is reversible with sustained lifestyle change. Weight loss of 5--10\% can normalise all components in many patients. The Diabetes Prevention Program showed 58\% reduction in diabetes incidence with intensive lifestyle intervention. Cardiovascular risk declines with each component corrected. Long-term maintenance is the challenge; relapse is common without ongoing support. Early identification in young adults offers the greatest window for prevention of irreversible end-organ damage.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Maintain healthy weight from young adulthood; prevent weight gain ($\le$0.5 kg/year)
    \item Follow Mediterranean/DASH dietary pattern lifelong
    \item Be physically active daily; minimise sitting time
    \item Limit alcohol to $\le$10 standard drinks/week, $\le$4 on any day
    \item Don't smoke; avoid second-hand smoke
    \item Prioritise sleep (7--9 hours); treat OSA
    \item Manage stress proactively
    \item Know your numbers: waist, BP, lipids, glucose; review annually from age 40 (or earlier if risk factors)
    \item Screen family members if metabolic syndrome diagnosed
    \item Engage with structured programs: Diabetes Prevention Program, Heart Foundation walking groups, Get Healthy Service
\end{itemize}
