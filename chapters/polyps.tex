\chapter{Polyps}
\index{Polyps}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. The meaning and significance of a polyp depend on where it is found and what the tissue shows under examination.}

\section*{What it is}
A polyp is a growth projecting from a mucous membrane, such as the lining of the bowel, nose, stomach, uterus, or bladder. Many are benign, but some can become cancerous or are associated with cancer risk. The term alone is not a diagnosis.

\section*{Symptoms}
Many polyps cause no symptoms and are found during screening or an examination. Depending on location, they may cause bleeding, altered bowel habits, abdominal discomfort, nasal blockage or loss of smell, abnormal vaginal bleeding, or urinary symptoms. These symptoms have many other causes.

\section*{Diagnosis and treatment}
Evaluation may involve endoscopy, imaging, examination, or biopsy. A polyp may be removed, sampled, monitored, or treated according to its size, location, appearance, pathology, symptoms, and family history. Follow-up intervals are individual; keep the plan provided by the clinician.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek urgent care for heavy bleeding, black stools, vomiting blood, severe abdominal pain, bowel obstruction symptoms, fainting, or rapidly worsening illness. Arrange review for persistent bleeding, unexplained weight loss, a change in bowel habits, recurrent nasal blockage, or abnormal vaginal bleeding.
