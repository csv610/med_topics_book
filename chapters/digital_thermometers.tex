\chapter{Digital Thermometers}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
A digital thermometer is an electronic device used to measure body temperature quickly and accurately. Digital thermometers have largely replaced mercury and glass thermometers and are available in several types: oral (under the tongue), ear (tympanic), forehead (temporal artery), underarm (axillary), and rectal. The best method depends on the age of the person and the situation, with rectal temperature the most accurate in young children. Measuring temperature correctly matters because fever guides decisions about medical care. This chapter explains the types of digital thermometers, how to use them, and how to interpret the results.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Fever is one of the most common reasons families measure temperature and seek medical advice, especially for children. Most households own a digital thermometer, and accurate measurement reduces unnecessary worry and missed illness. Ear and forehead thermometers are popular for convenience, while oral and rectal thermometers are the most accurate for clinical decisions. Incorrect technique is a common cause of misleading readings.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Body temperature:} normal temperature is around 36.5--37.5 degrees Celsius, varying with the site of measurement, time of day, and activity
    \item \textbf{Fever:} a temperature of 38 degrees or above usually indicates infection or inflammation
    \item \textbf{Oral thermometers:} measure temperature under the tongue; accurate in cooperative people over about five years
    \item \textbf{Ear thermometers:} measure infrared heat from the eardrum; fast but affected by earwax and ear infections
    \item \textbf{Forehead thermometers:} scan the temporal artery; convenient but less precise
    \item \textbf{Underarm thermometers:} convenient but read lower than core temperature
    \item \textbf{Rectal thermometers:} the most accurate method for babies and young children
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Digital thermometers display the temperature on a screen within seconds to a minute
    \item Oral measurement: place the tip under the tongue with the mouth closed for the required time
    \item Ear measurement: place the tip in the ear canal and press the button
    \item Forehead measurement: glide the scanner across the forehead
    \item Underarm measurement: place the tip in the armpit against the skin
    \item Rectal measurement: used in young children, with the thermometer lubricated and inserted gently
    \item Clean the thermometer between uses with soap and water or alcohol
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Measurement sites differ: rectal reads highest, then ear, oral, axillary, and forehead
    \item A normal reading at an inaccurate site can miss a significant fever
    \item Ear thermometers can be inaccurate with ear infections or wax
    \item Oral thermometers are inaccurate after hot or cold drinks
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Use a digital thermometer to assess fever and guide care. See a doctor for a baby under three months with any fever, for high or persistent fever, or for fever with other worrying symptoms.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A baby under three months with a temperature of 38 degrees or above
    \item Fever with a seizure, stiff neck, rash, or difficulty breathing
    \item Fever with confusion, drowsiness, or a very unwell child
    \item Any emergency signs of serious illness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Correct measurement:} using the right site for the person's age and situation
    \item \textbf{Interpretation:} understanding normal ranges for each measurement site
    \item \textbf{Repeated measurement:} confirming a high reading
    \item \textbf{Medical assessment:} the doctor considers the temperature alongside other symptoms
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Choose the right thermometer:} rectal for babies, oral or ear for older children and adults
    \item \textbf{Follow the instructions:} each device has specific use and cleaning instructions
    \item \textbf{Fever management:} paracetamol or ibuprofen for discomfort, fluids, and light clothing
    \item \textbf{Monitoring:} repeat measurements as advised
    \item \textbf{Seeking care:} follow the temperature thresholds that indicate medical review
    \item \textbf{Cleaning:} clean the thermometer between users to prevent infection spread
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Inaccurate readings from incorrect technique or site
    \item Missed serious illness when fever is underestimated
    \item Unnecessary anxiety from falsely high readings
    \item Mercury exposure from old glass thermometers, which should be replaced
    \item Rectal thermometer injury if used forcefully, which is rare
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Digital thermometers provide reliable temperature measurement when used correctly, supporting good decisions about fever care. Understanding which method to use and how to interpret readings helps families manage illness appropriately and recognise when medical care is needed. Replacing old mercury thermometers with digital ones is safer and just as effective.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Keep a digital thermometer at home and know how to use it
    \item Use the right site for the child's age
    \item Clean the thermometer after each use
    \item Store the thermometer with its cover and instructions
    \item Replace batteries as needed
    \item Dispose of mercury thermometers at a pharmacy or council facility
    \item Know the fever thresholds for seeking medical care
\end{itemize}
