\chapter{Edwards Syndrome}
\index{Edwards Syndrome}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Edwards syndrome, also known as trisomy 18, is a genetic condition caused by the presence of an extra copy of chromosome 18. The extra chromosome disrupts development, causing severe physical and intellectual disability, characteristic facial features, and malformations of the heart, brain, kidneys, and other organs. Most babies with Edwards syndrome do not survive beyond the first year of life, and a significant proportion do not survive birth. This chapter explains the condition, how it is diagnosed, and the care and support available for families.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Edwards syndrome occurs in approximately 1 in 5,000 live births, although it is much more common in early pregnancy, with most affected pregnancies ending in miscarriage. The risk increases with maternal age. It is the second most common autosomal trisomy after Down syndrome. Because of the severity of the condition, many parents choose testing during pregnancy, and decisions about the pregnancy are made with full counselling and support.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Edwards syndrome is caused by an error in cell division that leaves an extra copy of chromosome 18.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Full trisomy 18:} an extra copy of chromosome 18 in every cell, accounting for the great majority of cases
    \item \textbf{Mosaic trisomy:} the extra chromosome is present in only some cells, which can result in a milder picture
    \item \textbf{Partial trisomy:} only part of chromosome 18 is duplicated
    \item \textbf{Cause:} random error in egg or sperm formation; it is not inherited from the parents in the vast majority of cases
    \item \textbf{Maternal age:} risk increases with the mother's age
    \item \textbf{Effects:} the extra genetic material disrupts the development of many organs and systems, causing the characteristic features and severe intellectual disability
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
The features of Edwards syndrome are numerous and involve many body systems.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe growth restriction before birth and low birth weight
    \item Characteristic facial features, including a small head, small jaw, and low-set ears
    \item Clenched hands with overlapping fingers
    \item Rocker-bottom feet
    \item Congenital heart defects, present in most babies
    \item Kidney malformations
    \item Feeding difficulties and failure to thrive
    \item Severe intellectual disability
    \item Breathing difficulties and apnoea
    \item In babies who survive infancy: significant developmental delay and medical fragility
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Other chromosome and genetic conditions share some features.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Trisomy 13 (Patau syndrome), another severe trisomy
    \item Trisomy 21 (Down syndrome), which is generally milder
    \item Other chromosomal deletions and duplications
    \item Non-genetic causes of growth restriction and congenital malformations
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Families should have access to genetic counselling and specialist care from diagnosis. Pregnant women offered screening or diagnostic testing should discuss the results and their options with health professionals.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe breathing difficulty, apnoea, or blue colour in a baby
    \item Seizures or fits
    \item Signs of serious infection with fever
    \item Sudden deterioration in a baby's condition
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Diagnosis may be made before or after birth.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Prenatal screening:} combined first-trimester screening and non-invasive prenatal testing assess the risk of trisomy 18
    \item \textbf{Diagnostic testing:} chorionic villus sampling or amniocentesis confirm the diagnosis by analysing the baby's chromosomes
    \item \textbf{Prenatal ultrasound:} may show growth restriction, heart defects, and other features
    \item \textbf{Postnatal diagnosis:} blood tests for chromosome analysis confirm the diagnosis after birth
    \item \textbf{Further assessment:} echocardiography, ultrasound, and other tests map the malformations
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management is supportive and focuses on comfort and quality of life.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Prenatal counselling:} full information about the condition to help parents make decisions about the pregnancy and birth
    \item \textbf{Planning for birth:} discussion of resuscitation and the level of intervention the family wants
    \item \textbf{Palliative care:} comfort care for babies, with attention to feeding, breathing, and pain
    \item \textbf{Symptom management:} treatment of feeding difficulties, reflux, seizures, and infections
    \item \textbf{Family support:} counselling, palliative care teams, and connection with other families
    \item \textbf{Surgical decisions:} heart surgery and other interventions are considered carefully and individualised, given the overall prognosis
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Stillbirth and early infant death in the majority of cases
    \item Congenital heart failure
    \item Feeding problems and growth failure
    \item Recurrent infections
    \item Apnoea and respiratory failure
    \item Seizures
    \item Profound developmental delay in survivors
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The prognosis for Edwards syndrome is poor. Around half of babies born with the condition die within the first week, and only a small proportion survive to their first birthday. Babies with mosaic trisomy may have a longer survival with less severe features. Care focuses on comfort, dignity, and supporting the family, and many families find meaning and value in the time they have with their child, however short.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Discuss screening and testing options with a health professional in pregnancy
    \item Seek genetic counselling to understand the condition and the risk of recurrence
    \item Access palliative and bereavement support for the family
    \item Connect with support organisations for families affected by trisomy 18
    \item Take time to make decisions and ask questions without feeling rushed
    \item Look after your own emotional health as a parent or family member
\end{itemize}
