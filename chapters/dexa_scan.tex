\chapter{DEXA Scan}
\index{DEXA Scan}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
A DEXA scan, also called a DXA scan or bone densitometry, is a specialised type of X-ray that measures bone mineral density. The name stands for dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry, and the scan works by passing two X-ray beams of different energies through the body to calculate how much calcium and other minerals are packed into the bones. DEXA scans are the standard test for diagnosing osteoporosis and osteopenia, for assessing fracture risk, and for monitoring the response to treatment. They are quick, painless, and involve only a very small amount of radiation.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Osteoporosis is a major public health problem in many countries, affecting around one in four women over 65 and one in three men over 70, with many more having osteopenia (thinning bones below the osteoporosis threshold). One in two women and one in three men over 60 will have an osteoporotic fracture in their lifetime. DEXA scanning is recommended for people with risk factors such as previous fragility fracture, long-term steroid use, early menopause, or family history; public or private coverage depends on local eligibility rules.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Measurement:} the scan measures bone mineral density, usually at the hip and lumbar spine, which are the sites most likely to fracture
    \item \textbf{T-score:} the result is compared with the average for a healthy young adult; a T-score of -1.0 or above is normal, -1.0 to -2.5 indicates osteopenia, and -2.5 or below indicates osteoporosis
    \item \textbf{Z-score:} for younger people, the result is compared with people of the same age
    \item \textbf{Bone loss:} bones lose density with age, especially after menopause in women, when oestrogen falls
    \item \textbf{Mechanism:} the scan detects the mineral content per area of bone, which correlates with bone strength and fracture risk
    \item \textbf{Technology:} dual-energy beams allow bone to be distinguished from soft tissue for accurate measurement
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A DEXA scan itself causes no symptoms and takes about 10--20 minutes
    \item It is performed lying on a padded table, with a scanner arm passing over the body
    \item No special preparation is usually needed, though calcium tablets should be avoided for 24 hours before
    \item You should not have a DEXA scan if you are pregnant
    \item The results give a T-score used to diagnose osteoporosis or osteopenia
    \item Repeat scans may be done every 1--2 years to monitor treatment
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item DEXA scan versus other bone imaging: CT, MRI, and ultrasound are not standard for measuring bone density
    \item Peripheral DEXA (wrist, heel) versus central DEXA (hip and spine), with central scans being the reference standard
    \item Osteoporosis versus osteomalacia, a different condition of soft bones due to vitamin D deficiency
    \item Secondary causes of low bone density, such as steroid use, thyroid disease, or coeliac disease, which require separate assessment
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
A doctor will refer people for a DEXA scan when they have risk factors for osteoporosis or a fragility fracture. Discuss bone health with your GP if you are over 50, have had an early menopause, take steroids long-term, have had a fracture after a minor fall, or have a family history of osteoporosis.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A fracture from a minor fall, especially of the hip, wrist, or spine, which requires urgent medical care
    \item Sudden severe back pain, which may indicate a vertebral fracture
    \item Any injury that prevents weight-bearing
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{DEXA scan:} the definitive test, measuring bone density at the hip and spine
    \item \textbf{Fracture risk assessment:} tools such as FRAX combine the DEXA result with age, weight, and risk factors to estimate fracture risk
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} calcium, vitamin D, thyroid function, and kidney function to identify contributing factors
    \item \textbf{Medical history:} review of medications, menstrual history, fractures, and family history
    \item \textbf{Repeat scanning:} used to monitor bone density and the effect of treatment over time
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Normal results:} maintain bone health with calcium, vitamin D, exercise, and a healthy lifestyle
    \item \textbf{Osteopenia:} focus on prevention with lifestyle measures and review of risk factors
    \item \textbf{Osteoporosis:} treatment with medications such as bisphosphonates, denosumab, or other agents to reduce fracture risk
    \item \textbf{Calcium and vitamin D:} adequate intake is essential for bone health and for the effectiveness of treatment
    \item \textbf{Exercise:} weight-bearing and resistance exercise strengthen bone and reduce falls
    \item \textbf{Falls prevention:} home safety, balance training, and review of medicines that increase fall risk
    \item \textbf{Monitoring:} repeat DEXA scans to assess the response to treatment
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Fragility fractures of the hip, spine, and wrist if osteoporosis is untreated
    \item Chronic pain and disability after vertebral fractures
    \item Reduced mobility and independence
    \item Side effects of osteoporosis medications
    \item Anxiety about fracture risk
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
A DEXA scan provides the information needed to diagnose bone thinning early and start treatment before fractures occur. With appropriate treatment, bone density stabilises or improves, and fracture risk falls substantially. Hip fractures, which are associated with significant disability and mortality in older people, are largely preventable with timely diagnosis and treatment. The outlook for people diagnosed and treated early is very good.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Ensure adequate calcium through diet or supplements
    \item Maintain vitamin D levels through safe sun exposure or supplements
    \item Do regular weight-bearing and resistance exercise
    \item Do not smoke, and limit alcohol
    \item Ask your doctor whether a DEXA scan is appropriate for you
    \item Reduce fall risks at home, such as loose rugs and poor lighting
    \item Discuss osteoporosis treatment options with your doctor if diagnosed
\end{itemize}
