\chapter{Prader--Willi Syndrome}
\index{Prader--Willi Syndrome}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. Care should be coordinated by clinicians familiar with the syndrome and the person’s developmental needs.}

\section*{What it is}
Prader--Willi syndrome is a genetic condition caused by loss of activity of certain genes on chromosome 15. It commonly affects muscle tone, feeding, growth, learning, behaviour, hormones, and appetite. The pattern and severity vary between people.

\section*{Features}
Babies may have low muscle tone, weak feeding, and poor weight gain. Later, intense hunger and difficulty controlling food intake can develop, with risk of severe obesity if food access is not safely managed. Short stature, delayed puberty, sleep problems, learning disability, anxiety, repetitive behaviour, and skin picking may occur.

\section*{Assessment and treatment}
Diagnosis is confirmed with genetic testing. Support may include supervised nutrition and food security, physiotherapy, speech and feeding support, growth-hormone treatment for selected people, hormone replacement, sleep assessment, dental care, and educational or behavioural support. Medicines and surgery require careful review because responses and risks may differ.

\section*{Safety and daily living}
Food should be stored and offered according to an agreed plan, without shame or punitive language. Unexplained vomiting or abdominal pain is important because people may have reduced ability to vomit and may develop serious stomach problems. Families and care staff should share an emergency plan, monitor breathing and sleep, and support regular movement.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek urgent help for severe or persistent abdominal pain, repeated vomiting or retching, breathing difficulty, extreme sleepiness, sudden weakness, choking, or rapidly worsening behaviour or confusion. Arrange review for excessive daytime sleepiness, new swelling, changes in puberty, or significant mood or behaviour change.
