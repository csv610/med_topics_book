\chapter{Low Blood Pressure}
\index{Low Blood Pressure}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Low blood pressure (hypotension) means that the pressure of blood in the arteries is lower than usual, commonly taken as below about 90/60 mmHg. On its own, a low reading is not necessarily a problem -- many healthy young people, especially fit and athletic individuals, naturally have blood pressure in this range without any symptoms. It matters when low blood pressure causes symptoms such as dizziness or fainting, or when it drops so much that organs do not receive enough blood. One common form is postural (orthostatic) hypotension, in which blood pressure falls on standing up from sitting or lying. A sudden, severe drop in blood pressure that compromises organ perfusion is a medical emergency called shock.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Low blood pressure becomes more common with age, and postural hypotension affects a substantial proportion of older adults, particularly those taking blood pressure medicines or living with diabetes. Many people have naturally low blood pressure throughout their lives and never notice it. Shock from bleeding, infection (sepsis), heart problems, or severe dehydration can affect anyone and is a major cause of emergency admissions. Low blood pressure is often under-recognised because it is generally regarded as less important than high blood pressure, yet the falls and injuries it causes are a leading cause of harm in older people.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Blood pressure is maintained by the pumping action of the heart and the tone of the blood vessels, which are controlled by the nervous system and the kidneys. Low blood pressure arises when the heart cannot pump enough blood, when the blood vessels relax too much, or when the volume of fluid in the circulation is too low.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dehydration:} insufficient fluid from not drinking enough, vomiting, diarrhoea, or excessive sweating
    \item \textbf{Blood loss:} bleeding, including heavy periods or internal bleeding
    \item \textbf{Medicines:} blood pressure medicines, diuretics, and some antidepressants or prostate medicines
    \item \textbf{Prolonged bed rest:} reduces the body's ability to adjust to standing
    \item \textbf{Heart problems:} heart failure, slow or irregular heart rhythms, and heart attack reduce cardiac output
    \item \textbf{Endocrine problems:} underactive thyroid or Addison's disease (adrenal insufficiency)
    \item \textbf{Autonomic nerve damage:} from diabetes or Parkinson's disease interferes with blood pressure control
    \item \textbf{Pregnancy:} blood pressure often falls in the early stages of pregnancy
    \item \textbf{Severe causes:} sepsis, severe allergic reactions, and major trauma cause dangerous, sudden hypotension
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Dizziness or light-headedness, especially when standing up quickly
    \item Fainting or near-fainting (syncope), sometimes preceded by nausea or blurred vision
    \item Fatigue, weakness, and poor concentration
    \item Blurred or dimmed vision
    \item Feeling faint after meals (postprandial hypotension) or after prolonged standing
    \item Cold, clammy, pale skin with a rapid pulse -- features of shock when blood pressure drops suddenly
    \item In severe hypotension: confusion, drowsiness, reduced urine output, and collapse
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Symptoms of light-headedness and fainting can arise from many other conditions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Dehydration and anaemia from chronic blood loss
    \item Low blood sugar (hypoglycaemia), especially in people with diabetes
    \item Heart rhythm problems, heart attack, and other cardiac conditions
    \item Anxiety and panic attacks
    \item Inner ear (vestibular) problems causing dizziness
    \item Medications that cause dizziness as a side effect
    \item Other causes of collapse, such as seizures or stroke
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor if you have repeated dizziness or fainting, symptoms on standing or after meals, or low blood pressure that interferes with daily life. A medication review is important if you take blood pressure or other medicines. Seek urgent care for active bleeding, vomiting blood, or dark, tarry stool, or for fever accompanied by low blood pressure.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Fainting, collapse, or severe dizziness with a rapid or weak pulse
    \item Chest pain or difficulty breathing
    \item Confusion, drowsiness, or loss of consciousness
    \item Bleeding that is heavy or does not stop
    \item Signs of a severe allergic reaction, such as swelling of the face or throat
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Blood pressure measurement:} taken sitting and then again after standing to detect postural hypotension
    \item \textbf{Home monitoring:} blood pressure readings over several days at different times of day
    \item \textbf{History:} symptoms, medicines, fluid intake, and any recent illness or blood loss
    \item \textbf{Physical examination:} pulse, heart sounds, and signs of dehydration, bleeding, or anaemia
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} full blood count, kidney function, electrolytes, thyroid function, and blood sugar
    \item \textbf{ECG and heart monitoring:} to check the heart rate and rhythm
    \item \textbf{Tilt table test:} in selected cases, to reproduce fainting under controlled conditions
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Medicines review:} adjusting or stopping medicines that lower blood pressure, under medical advice
    \item \textbf{Fluids:} drinking more water, and increasing salt only if a doctor advises it
    \item \textbf{Rising slowly:} sitting on the edge of the bed for a moment before standing
    \item \textbf{Compression stockings:} thigh-high stockings can reduce pooling of blood in the legs
    \item \textbf{Meal changes:} smaller, more frequent meals if symptoms follow eating
    \item \textbf{Avoiding triggers:} avoiding alcohol, very hot showers, prolonged standing, and sudden position changes
    \item \textbf{Treating the cause:} treatment of heart, hormonal, or other underlying problems
    \item \textbf{Medication:} in persistent cases, medicines that raise blood pressure may be used under specialist advice
    \item \textbf{Shock:} treated urgently in hospital with intravenous fluids, blood products if needed, and support of the heart and organs
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Falls and fractures from fainting, a major cause of injury in older adults
    \item Head injuries from collapsing
    \item Reduced blood flow to organs, causing confusion, kidney injury, and, in shock, organ failure
    \item Impaired driving and work safety if symptoms occur without warning
    \item Anxiety about fainting and loss of independence
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
For most people, low blood pressure is benign or easily managed, and symptoms improve when fluids, medicines, and daily habits are adjusted. Postural hypotension often persists but is usually controlled well enough to prevent falls. The outlook for severe hypotension (shock) depends on its cause and on how quickly it is treated, but it is a life-threatening emergency. Underlying conditions such as heart disease or diabetes largely determine the long-term prognosis.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Drink enough fluid, especially in hot weather and during illness
    \item Stand up slowly from lying or sitting, and never rise suddenly
    \item Avoid prolonged standing and very hot baths or showers
    \item Limit alcohol, which can lower blood pressure
    \item Keep medicines under review and report dizziness or fainting to your doctor
    \item Use support stockings and take rests if symptoms persist
    \item If you feel faint, sit or lie down immediately and raise your legs
    \item Have heart and blood pressure checks as advised if you have diabetes or another long-term condition
\end{itemize}
