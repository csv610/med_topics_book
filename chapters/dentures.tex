\chapter{Dentures}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Dentures are removable replacements for missing teeth, made of acrylic, metal, or a combination, that restore the ability to chew and speak and support the appearance of the face. There are two main types: complete dentures, which replace all of the teeth in an arch, and partial dentures, which replace some missing teeth while clasping onto remaining teeth. Dentures can be fitted immediately after extractions or after the gums have healed. Modern dentures are comfortable and natural-looking when well made and properly cared for. This chapter explains the types of dentures, the fitting process, and how to care for them.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Dentures have historically been worn by a large proportion of older Australians, although the rate of complete tooth loss has fallen significantly with better preventive dental care. Partial dentures remain common among people who have lost some teeth, and many Australians wear full dentures. Around one in five adults over 65 has no natural teeth. Dentures are also used as a temporary solution while implants or bridges are planned. Access to affordable dentures is a factor in their use, particularly for older and concession patients.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tooth loss:} decay, gum disease, and trauma are the main causes of tooth loss leading to dentures
    \item \textbf{Bone changes:} after tooth loss, the jawbone shrinks over time, affecting how dentures fit
    \item \textbf{Complete dentures:} replace all teeth in an arch and are held in place by suction, muscle control, and adhesion
    \item \textbf{Partial dentures:} replace missing teeth and are retained by clasps on adjacent teeth
    \item \textbf{Immediate dentures:} fitted on the day of extraction, with relining needed as the gums heal and shrink
    \item \textbf{Implant-supported dentures:} fixed more securely by dental implants in the jawbone
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Difficulty chewing and eating certain foods before dentures
    \item Speech changes and gaps in the smile from missing teeth
    \item Face and lip changes as teeth and bone are lost
    \item With dentures: restored chewing, speech, and appearance
    \item Initial discomfort, sore spots, and excess saliva that settle with adjustment
    \item Signs of poor fit: looseness, rubbing, ulcers, and difficulty eating
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Complete versus partial dentures, depending on how many teeth remain
    \item Dentures versus dental implants versus bridges for missing teeth
    \item Immediate versus conventional dentures
    \item Implant-supported dentures for people who struggle with loose conventional dentures
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
People with missing teeth should discuss replacement options with a dentist. Anyone with a denture that is loose, uncomfortable, causes ulcers, or no longer fits should have it assessed, as continued use of a poor-fitting denture can damage the gums and bone. Regular dental reviews are recommended for denture wearers.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Difficulty breathing or swallowing, or choking on a denture
    \item Severe swelling, pain, or signs of infection in the mouth
    \item Injury to the mouth involving the denture
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dental examination:} assessment of remaining teeth, gums, and bone
    \item \textbf{X-rays:} check for remaining roots, bone health, and disease
    \item \textbf{Impressions and bite records:} used to construct the denture
    \item \textbf{Trial fittings:} the denture is tried in and adjusted before completion
    \item \textbf{Review after fitting:} checking comfort, fit, and the gums in the weeks after placement
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Construction:} impressions, jaw measurements, and trial fittings over several visits
    \item \textbf{Fitting:} the denture is inserted and adjusted
    \item \textbf{Adjustments:} sore spots are relieved over the first weeks as the mouth adapts
    \item \textbf{Relining:} dentures are relined as the gums and bone change, restoring fit
    \item \textbf{Replacement:} dentures are typically replaced every 5--8 years as they wear and the mouth changes
    \item \textbf{Implant-supported options:} for improved retention and comfort
    \item \textbf{Adhesives:} denture adhesives can help retention when properly used
    \item \textbf{Ongoing care:} regular reviews of the denture and the oral tissues
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sore spots and mouth ulcers from rubbing
    \item Denture stomatitis (thrush) under the denture from poor hygiene or fit
    \item Looseness and difficulty eating as bone shrinks
    \item Accelerated bone loss beneath dentures over many years
    \item Damage to remaining teeth from partial denture clasps
    \item Difficulty adapting, especially with complete lower dentures
    \item Costs of adjustment, relining, and replacement
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Most people adapt to dentures within a few weeks and regain good chewing and confidence. Complete upper dentures are usually well retained, while lower dentures are more difficult and may benefit from implants. With regular review, relining, and replacement when needed, dentures continue to function well for many years. Advances in materials and implant support have made dentures more comfortable and stable than ever.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Clean dentures daily with a soft brush and denture cleanser, and rinse after meals
    \item Remove dentures at night and store them in water or a denture solution
    \item Brush remaining natural teeth and gums to prevent decay and disease
    \item Attend regular dental check-ups for the denture and the mouth
    \item Have sore spots adjusted rather than leaving the denture out
    \item Handle dentures carefully to avoid breakage
    \item Keep dentures labelled or marked for identification in care settings
\end{itemize}
