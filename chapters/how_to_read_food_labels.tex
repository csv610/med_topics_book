\chapter{How to Read Food Labels}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Reading food labels is a key skill for making healthier food choices. In Australia, most packaged foods must display a nutrition information panel, an ingredients list, and, for many products, the Health Star Rating. The nutrition panel shows energy, protein, fat, saturated fat, carbohydrates, sugars, sodium, and other nutrients per serving and per 100 g. Understanding these components helps people compare products, control portion sizes, and manage conditions such as diabetes, high blood pressure, and heart disease. The ingredients list and claims also reveal what is really in the food.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Australians buy most of their packaged food without fully understanding labels, and diet-related conditions are common: two-thirds of adults are overweight or obese, and high intakes of sugar, salt, and saturated fat contribute to heart disease, diabetes, and cancer. The Health Star Rating system now appears on most packaged foods to simplify healthy choices. Nutrition knowledge, including label reading, is associated with healthier diets, and food labelling is a core public health strategy.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Nutrition information panel:} mandatory on most packaged foods, showing nutrients per serve and per 100 g
    \item \textbf{Per 100 g column:} the best way to compare products because serving sizes differ
    \item \textbf{Energy:} kilojoules (kJ) indicate the energy content
    \item \textbf{Fat and saturated fat:} saturated and trans fats raise blood cholesterol
    \item \textbf{Sugar:} includes added and natural sugars; high intakes contribute to weight gain
    \item \textbf{Sodium (salt):} high intakes raise blood pressure
    \item \textbf{Ingredients list:} listed in order of quantity, revealing what the product actually contains
    \item \textbf{Health Star Rating:} a 0.5--5 star summary of overall healthiness
    \item \textbf{Claims:} terms such as "low fat" and "no added sugar" have defined meanings but do not mean the product is healthy overall
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Choosing products with more stars and less saturated fat, sugar, and sodium
    \item Comparing per 100 g values rather than per-serve values
    \item Reading the ingredients list to identify added sugar, salt, and fats
    \item Understanding serving sizes and adjusting portions
    \item Using labels to manage diabetes, blood pressure, and cholesterol
    \item Recognising that "natural", "organic", and "premium" claims do not guarantee healthiness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Serving sizes vary between products and may be smaller than a realistic serve
    \item "No added sugar" does not mean low sugar, as fruit sugars remain
    \item "Low fat" products may be high in sugar
    \item Health Star Ratings consider some nutrients more than others
    \item Products without labels, such as fresh food, are generally the healthiest choices
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Food label skills help prevent and manage chronic disease; people with diabetes, kidney disease, or heart conditions can ask a dietitian for personalised guidance on reading labels and managing specific nutrients.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe allergic reactions to an ingredient (anaphylaxis); always check labels for allergens
    \item Difficulty breathing, swelling, or collapse after eating
    \item Any food allergy emergency; use an adrenaline autoinjector if available
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Check the per 100 g column:} compare saturated fat, sugar, and sodium across products
    \item \textbf{Check the stars:} aim for 3.5 stars or more
    \item \textbf{Read the ingredients:} the first ingredients make up most of the food
    \item \textbf{Check allergens:} allergen statements are mandatory
    \item \textbf{Understand the serving:} compare the per-serve values with what you actually eat
    \item \textbf{Look for additives:} recognise numbers and names of preservatives and colours if relevant
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Choose whole foods:} fresh fruit, vegetables, grains, and unprocessed protein need no labels
    \item \textbf{Compare products:} use per 100 g values to choose lower-salt, lower-sugar, and lower-saturated-fat options
    \item \textbf{Watch portion sizes:} match serves to the label and your needs
    \item \textbf{Limit discretionary foods:} chips, biscuits, and soft drinks are low in nutrients and high in energy
    \item \textbf{Use the stars:} the Health Star Rating simplifies comparison
    \item \textbf{Dietitian support:} personalised advice for medical conditions
    \item \textbf{Teach children:} label reading is a life skill
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Misreading labels leads to unintended high intakes of sugar, salt, and fat
    \item Misleading claims and marketing can encourage unhealthy purchases
    \item Inadvertent consumption of allergens causes severe reactions
    \item Poor food choices contribute to obesity, diabetes, and heart disease
    \item Confusion about serving sizes distorts intake
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
People who read food labels make consistently healthier choices, and label use is associated with better diet quality and lower risk of chronic disease. The Health Star Rating has made healthy choices easier for the general population. With basic label skills, most people can significantly improve the nutritional quality of their shopping without great effort. The benefits accumulate over a lifetime, supporting healthy weight, blood pressure, and heart health.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Use the per 100 g column to compare products
    \item Aim for 3.5 stars or more on packaged foods
    \item Choose products with short ingredients lists
    \item Check allergen labels carefully
    \item Limit foods high in saturated fat, sugar, and sodium
    \item Eat mostly whole, minimally processed foods
    \item Adjust portions to match your needs
    \item Ask a dietitian for help with medical conditions
\end{itemize}
