\chapter{Pharmacist}
\index{Pharmacist}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. The pharmacist’s exact services depend on local law, training, and the care setting.}

\section*{What a pharmacist does}
A pharmacist is a medicines professional who helps people use medicines safely and effectively. Pharmacists may check prescriptions, dispense and label medicines, explain how to use them, identify interactions or allergies, support adherence, advise about some minor illnesses, and contribute to vaccination, chronic-disease care, and medicines review where authorised.

\section*{What to tell the pharmacist}
Bring or describe all prescription medicines, over-the-counter products, vitamins, supplements, allergies, pregnancy or breastfeeding, kidney or liver disease, and what you are taking the medicine for. Tell them if you have missed doses, had a reaction, or cannot afford or swallow the medicine. Keep an up-to-date medicine list.

\section*{Medication safety}
Check the person’s name, medicine, strength, dose, route, timing, and duration. Ask what to do if a dose is missed, what side effects matter, and how to store or dispose of the medicine. Do not share prescription medicines or use someone else’s antibiotics. A pharmacist may need to refer you to a prescriber or emergency service rather than manage the problem in the pharmacy.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek emergency help for trouble breathing, swelling of the face or throat, collapse, severe chest pain, seizure, sudden confusion, or suspected overdose. Contact a pharmacist or clinician promptly for a serious or persistent adverse effect, a medication error, or symptoms that are worsening.
