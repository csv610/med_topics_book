\section*{Definition and Overview}
Vitamin B12 test is a pharmaceutical agent used to prevent, diagnose, treat, or manage a health condition. Medicines act through diverse mechanisms: replacing deficient substances, modifying physiological processes, destroying pathogens, or relieving symptoms. Safe and effective medication use requires understanding the indication, dose, route, adverse effects, interactions, and contraindications.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Approximately half of the adult population takes at least one prescription medicine, and polypharmacy (5 or more medicines) is common in older adults. Adverse drug reactions account for a significant proportion of hospital admissions, particularly in the elderly. Medication errors are among the most common preventable causes of patient harm in healthcare. Antimicrobial resistance, driven by overuse and misuse of antibiotics, is a growing global threat.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pharmacokinetics:} how the body handles the drug -- absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion
    \item \textbf{Pharmacodynamics:} how the drug affects the body -- receptor binding, enzyme inhibition, or channel modulation
    \item \textbf{Adverse drug reactions:} may be dose-related (Type A) or idiosyncratic/allergic (Type B)
    \item \textbf{Drug interactions:} pharmacokinetic (altering metabolism) or pharmacodynamic (additive or antagonistic effects)
    \item \textbf{Tolerance and dependence:} particularly with opioids, benzodiazepines, and other psychoactive agents
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Adverse effects vary by medicine but commonly include: nausea, headache, dizziness, drowsiness, or gastrointestinal disturbance
    \item Allergic reactions: rash, urticaria, angioedema, or anaphylaxis
    \item Symptoms of overdose: organ-specific toxicity (e.g.\ hepatotoxicity with paracetamol overdose)
    \item Withdrawal symptoms if certain medicines are stopped abruptly (e.g.\ opioids, benzodiazepines, corticosteroids)
    \item Therapeutic effect: improvement or resolution of the condition being treated
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
When a patient develops new symptoms while taking a medicine, consider:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Adverse drug reaction vs.\ progression of underlying disease
    \item Drug interaction vs.\ independent new condition
    \item Allergic reaction vs.\ non-allergic hypersensitivity
    \item Overdose (intentional or accidental) vs.\ therapeutic side effect
    \item Withdrawal vs.\ recurrence of the treated condition
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
\textbf{Call 000 for signs of a severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis): difficulty breathing, swelling of the face or throat, collapse, or widespread rash with hypotension.}

Otherwise seek urgent advice if:
\begin{itemize}
    \item You think too much medicine has been taken -- call the Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26 (Australia)
    \item You develop a rash, yellowing of the skin or eyes, or unusual bleeding
    \item You have severe or persistent side effects
    \item You are pregnant, breastfeeding, or planning pregnancy and are unsure whether a medicine is safe
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Medication review:} comprehensive review of all prescribed, over-the-counter, herbal, and recreational substances
    \item \textbf{Adherence assessment:} explore whether the medicine is being taken as prescribed
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} drug levels (e.g.\ lithium, phenytoin, warfarin INR), liver and renal function, full blood count
    \item \textbf{Therapeutic drug monitoring:} for medicines with a narrow therapeutic index
    \item \textbf{Allergy testing:} for suspected drug allergy
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Prescribing principles:} right drug, right dose, right route, right time, right patient
    \item \textbf{Take as prescribed:} do not stop or change a dose without medical advice
    \item \textbf{Read the Consumer Medicines Information (CMI)} for each medicine
    \item \textbf{Monitor:} for therapeutic effect and adverse reactions
    \item \textbf{Adjust:} dose for age, renal/hepatic function, and interactions
    \item \textbf{Deprescribing:} judicious withdrawal of medicines that are no longer needed or causing harm
    \item \textbf{Adverse reaction management:} dose reduction, switching to an alternative, or symptomatic treatment
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Adverse drug reactions -- ranging from mild to life-threatening
    \item Anaphylaxis -- a medical emergency
    \item Organ toxicity: hepatotoxicity, nephrotoxicity, cardiotoxicity, bone marrow suppression
    \item Drug interactions leading to treatment failure or toxicity
    \item Medication errors: wrong drug, wrong dose, wrong patient
    \item Antimicrobial resistance -- from inappropriate antibiotic use
    \item Dependence and withdrawal -- with opioids, benzodiazepines, and other agents
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Most medicines are safe and effective when used as prescribed. Adverse reactions are usually mild and reversible on stopping or adjusting the dose. Serious adverse reactions are uncommon but can be life-threatening. Medication-related harm is significantly reduced by reconciliation at care transitions, regular review, and patient education. Long-term outcomes depend on the underlying condition being treated and adherence to therapy.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Keep an up-to-date list of all medicines and bring it to every appointment
    \item Use one pharmacy where possible so interactions can be checked
    \item Never share prescription medicines
    \item Store medicines out of reach of children; dispose of expired medicines safely
    \item Avoid alcohol if the medicine warns against it
    \item Ask questions: know what each medicine is for and what to expect
    \item Report side effects to a doctor or pharmacist promptly
\end{itemize}
