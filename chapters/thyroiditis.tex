\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational reference; always seek professional advice for individual care.}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Thyroiditis means inflammation of the thyroid gland. Different types follow different timelines and immune triggers, but many share a recognisable hormone pattern: an early phase of thyrotoxicosis when stored hormone leaks into the blood, a later hypothyroid phase when stores are depleted and the damaged gland underproduces, and often a recovery phase. Major forms include Hashimoto (chronic lymphocytic) thyroiditis, subacute (De Quervain) thyroiditis, silent and postpartum painless thyroiditis, drug-induced thyroiditis, acute infectious thyroiditis (rare), and Riedel thyroiditis (rare fibrotic disease). Distinguishing thyroiditis from Graves disease matters because antithyroid drugs do not treat hormone leak and can cause unnecessary harm. This chapter explains types, symptoms, tests, and care with an local clinical emphasis.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Hashimoto thyroiditis is the commonest cause of primary hypothyroidism in iodine-sufficient countries and is far more frequent in women. Postpartum thyroiditis affects a significant minority of women in the year after birth, especially those with TPO antibodies. Subacute thyroiditis is less common, often follows a viral-type illness, and presents in middle adulthood with a painful gland. Checkpoint inhibitor cancer therapies have increased the incidence of immune-related thyroiditis in oncology clinics. Acute bacterial thyroiditis is rare because the gland is robustly encapsulated and iodine-rich. Seasonal clustering of subacute cases is described after viral outbreaks.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hashimoto thyroiditis:} chronic autoimmune destruction with TPO and thyroglobulin antibodies; gradual failure of hormone synthesis; goitre or atrophic gland
    \item \textbf{Subacute granulomatous thyroiditis:} likely post-viral; painful granulomatous inflammation; high ESR; low radioiodine uptake during thyrotoxic phase
    \item \textbf{Silent / postpartum thyroiditis:} autoimmune lymphocytic inflammation without severe pain; postpartum immune reconstitution unmasks disease
    \item \textbf{Drug-induced:} amiodarone type 2 destructive thyroiditis, lithium, interferons, tyrosine kinase and immune checkpoint inhibitors
    \item \textbf{Acute infectious:} bacterial seeding from fistula or immunosuppression; abscess risk
    \item \textbf{Riedel:} IgG4-related fibrotic process invading adjacent tissues
    \item \textbf{Hormone leak physiology:} destroyed follicles release T4/T3 independent of TSH stimulation---uptake scans are cold
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Painful tender goitre:} classic for subacute thyroiditis; pain may radiate to jaw or ear
    \item \textbf{Fever and malaise:} systemic inflammation in subacute disease
    \item \textbf{Painless thyrotoxic symptoms:} heat intolerance, tremor, tachycardia in silent or postpartum types
    \item \textbf{Later hypothyroid symptoms:} fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance as the course evolves
    \item \textbf{Hashimoto pattern:} often subtle chronic hypothyroidism; occasional transient hyper phase (hashitoxicosis)
    \item \textbf{Postpartum timing:} from a few months after delivery; may be mistaken for normal postnatal exhaustion or anxiety
    \item \textbf{Oncology patients:} sudden TFT shifts during or after immunotherapy
    \item \textbf{Hard fixed woody goitre:} raises Riedel or anaplastic cancer concerns---specialist urgency
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Graves disease versus painless thyroiditis:} TRAb, diffuse raised uptake in Graves versus low uptake in thyroiditis
    \item \textbf{Toxic nodule:} focal uptake, nodular exam
    \item \textbf{Pharyngitis and dental pain:} referred pain mimics; thyroid is tender itself in subacute disease
    \item \textbf{Haemorrhage into a thyroid cyst:} sudden pain and lump
    \item \textbf{Cellulitis and deep neck infection:} more superficial skin signs
    \item \textbf{Anaplastic carcinoma:} rapid hard mass in elderly---do not assume Riedel without expert assessment
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a GP for neck pain with swelling, unexplained thyrotoxic or hypothyroid symptoms, or postnatal mood and energy changes that feel extreme. Oncology patients should report palpitations or fatigue promptly during immunotherapy.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe neck swelling with breathing difficulty or inability to swallow saliva
    \item Chest pain, collapse, or irregular racing heartbeat with thyrotoxic features
    \item Signs of sepsis with a very tender thyroid and high fever
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{TSH, free T4, free T3:} document phase (thyrotoxic, hypo, recovery)
    \item \textbf{ESR or CRP:} often markedly raised in subacute thyroiditis
    \item \textbf{TPO antibodies:} support autoimmune and postpartum risk
    \item \textbf{TRAb:} helps exclude Graves when the picture is painless thyrotoxicosis
    \item \textbf{Radioiodine or technetium uptake:} low in destructive thyroiditis, high in Graves
    \item \textbf{Ultrasound:} heterogeneous inflamed gland; rules out abscess or dominant suspicious nodule
    \item \textbf{FNA:} if infection or cancer must be excluded
    \item \textbf{Serial TFTs:} every few weeks during evolving thyroiditis until stable
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Subacute pain control:} NSAIDs first; prednisolone for severe pain with planned taper
    \item \textbf{Beta-blockers:} ease tachycardic thyrotoxic symptoms during leak phase
    \item \textbf{No carbimazole for pure leak thyrotoxicosis:} it does not block already-formed hormone release
    \item \textbf{Levothyroxine:} for symptomatic hypothyroid phase or permanent Hashimoto failure
    \item \textbf{Postpartum care:} monitor; many recover, some remain hypothyroid; safe breastfeeding planning with medicines
    \item \textbf{Drug-related disease:} co-manage with cardiology or oncology; amiodarone decisions are specialist
    \item \textbf{Acute infection:} antibiotics and drainage if abscess forms
    \item \textbf{Riedel:} tamoxifen, steroids, or surgery in specialist centres
    \item \textbf{Education:} explain the triphasic course so patients are not surprised by shifting symptoms
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Permanent hypothyroidism:} especially Hashimoto and some postpartum or drug cases
    \item \textbf{Recurrent subacute episodes:} occasional
    \item \textbf{Steroid side effects:} when prolonged courses are needed
    \item \textbf{Misdiagnosis as Graves:} inappropriate antithyroid therapy or radioiodine
    \item \textbf{Abscess and systemic infection:} rare acute bacterial disease
    \item \textbf{Compression from fibrosis:} Riedel
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Subacute thyroiditis usually resolves over months; most recover normal function, though a minority stay hypothyroid. Silent and postpartum thyroiditis often remit, with a meaningful rate of later permanent hypothyroidism---follow-up testing matters. Hashimoto typically requires long-term replacement once overt hypothyroidism is established. Drug-induced courses vary with the agent. Overall, with correct diagnosis and phased treatment, outcomes are very good.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Know your antibody status if you have type 1 diabetes or other autoimmunity---ask about postpartum testing plans when pregnant
    \item Do not ignore extreme postnatal anxiety or weight change; request TFTs
    \item Take anti-inflammatory medicines with food and as directed; report stomach pain or black stools
    \item Attend repeat blood tests through all phases rather than stopping when you feel better
    \item Carry a medicine list if you take amiodarone, lithium, or immunotherapy
    \item Seek review before starting over-the-counter iodine supplements during recovery
\end{itemize}
