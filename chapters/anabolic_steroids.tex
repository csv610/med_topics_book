\chapter{Anabolic Steroids}
\index{Anabolic Steroids}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Anabolic steroids, also called anabolic-androgenic steroids, are synthetic versions of the male sex hormone testosterone. They promote the growth of muscle and bone (anabolic effects) while producing masculinising (androgenic) effects. In medicine they have a limited number of legitimate uses, such as treating delayed puberty, hypogonadism, and severe weight loss in chronic illness.

Far more often they are used without prescription to build muscle, improve athletic performance, or change appearance, and this non-medical use is associated with substantial physical and psychological harm. This chapter focuses on the medical aspects of anabolic steroids: their legitimate uses, how they work, the features of misuse, and the long-term consequences of unsupervised use.

\section*{Epidemiology}
The non-medical use of anabolic steroids is difficult to measure accurately, but surveys suggest that a small but notable proportion of people, predominantly men, have used them at some time. Use is concentrated among bodybuilders, weightlifters, and participants in strength sports, and among people with strong concerns about body image, a pattern sometimes called muscle dysmorphia.

Most users begin in their late teens or twenties. Use appears more common in certain occupational groups, such as security, fitness, and armed services, and it has been rising among adolescent boys and young men in many countries. People who use one performance-enhancing substance often use several, and many also use other recreational drugs, which complicates their care.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Taken in supraphysiological doses, anabolic steroids bind to intracellular receptors and increase protein synthesis, producing growth of muscle and bone. They are usually taken in cycles of several weeks, sometimes with several different steroids at once (stacking) and with doses gradually reduced towards the end (tapering). The effects and harms depend on the drug, dose, route, and individual susceptibility:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Suppression of natural testosterone:} the body stops producing its own testosterone, and this suppression may persist for months after use stops
    \item \textbf{Conversion to oestrogen:} some steroids are converted (aromatised) to oestrogen, causing breast growth in men
    \item \textbf{Androgenic effects:} deepening of the voice, hair growth, and acne, particularly in women
    \item \textbf{Organ effects:} the liver, heart, kidneys, skin, and blood-clotting system are all affected by high doses
    \item \textbf{Routes of use:} oral tablets, and intramuscular injections that are often given with shared or unsterile equipment
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Users may present with the intended effects or with complications of use:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Intended effects:} increased muscle mass and strength, often with reduced body fat
    \item \textbf{Skin:} acne, especially on the back and shoulders, and oily skin
    \item \textbf{Hormonal effects in men:} breast enlargement (gynaecomastia), shrinkage of the testicles, reduced libido, and erectile dysfunction
    \item \textbf{Hormonal effects in women:} deepened voice, growth of body and facial hair, clitoral enlargement, and menstrual disturbance, some of which may be permanent
    \item \textbf{Hair:} scalp hair loss in people who are genetically predisposed
    \item \textbf{Mood and behaviour:} irritability, aggression, grandiosity during use, and low mood, fatigue, or anxiety during and after cycles
    \item \textbf{Injection-site problems:} pain, bruising, lumps, abscesses, and infection
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Symptoms related to steroid use can resemble other conditions, and the diagnosis may be missed if use is not disclosed:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Testosterone deficiency (hypogonadism) of other causes in men who have recently stopped using
    \item Primary depression, anxiety, or other mental health conditions
    \item Liver disease of other causes, producing jaundice or fatigue
    \item Heart failure or hypertension unrelated to steroids
    \item Gynaecomastia from obesity, other medicines, or other endocrine causes
    \item Acne or skin infection as a separate problem
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek medical advice if:
\begin{itemize}
    \item You are using anabolic steroids and want support to stop safely
    \item You have used them for some time and notice low mood, erectile problems, or extreme fatigue after stopping
    \item You develop pain, redness, or swelling at an injection site
    \item You notice yellowing of the eyes or skin, dark urine, or unusual bleeding
    \item You develop chest pain, palpitations, or shortness of breath
    \item Women experience missed periods, deepening of the voice, or unwanted hair growth
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe chest pain, difficulty breathing, or collapse
    \item Sudden severe headache, weakness, or visual disturbance (possible stroke)
    \item Swelling of the leg with pain, suggestive of a blood clot
    \item Severe agitation, aggression, or self-harm with acute confusion
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
A non-judgemental approach encourages disclosure and accurate assessment.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History:} open questioning about which substances, doses, cycles, and routes are used, and for how long
    \item \textbf{Examination:} blood pressure, heart, breasts, testicles, skin, and injection sites
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} liver function, kidney function, lipid profile, and blood sugar
    \item \textbf{Hormone tests:} testosterone, oestrogen, and pituitary hormones to assess the body's own production
    \item \textbf{Heart tests:} electrocardiography, and echocardiography if heart structure or function is affected
    \item \textbf{Infection screening:} hepatitis B and C, HIV, and other blood-borne infections when equipment has been shared
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Supervised withdrawal:} tapering of doses with medical and psychological support, since stopping abruptly can cause a difficult withdrawal syndrome with low mood, fatigue, and reduced libido
    \item \textbf{Hormone restoration:} monitoring of testosterone after cessation, and treatment of persistent hypogonadism where appropriate
    \item \textbf{Treatment of complications:} medicines and specialist referral for acne, gynaecomastia, hypertension, dyslipidaemia, and liver abnormalities
    \item \textbf{Psychological support:} counselling for body-image concerns, mood problems, and steroid dependence
    \item \textbf{Harm reduction:} advice about injection hygiene and avoiding shared equipment for people who choose to continue using
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} repeat blood tests until hormone levels and organ function have normalised
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Cardiovascular disease: hypertension, enlargement of the heart, abnormal rhythms, and an increased risk of heart attack and stroke
    \item Liver damage, including cholestasis, tumours, and, rarely, liver failure
    \item Kidney damage and fluid retention
    \item Raised cholesterol and increased blood clotting, raising the risk of blood clots
    \item Acne, scarring, and severe skin infections
    \item Gynaecomastia and infertility that may persist for months or years
    \item Masculinising changes in women that may be permanent
    \item Reduced bone density in men after long-term use
    \item Transmission of hepatitis B and C, HIV, and other infections through shared needles
    \item Psychological problems, including aggression, depression, and dependence on steroids
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Many of the physical effects of anabolic steroids reverse when use stops, particularly with medical support. Acne, raised blood pressure, and abnormal liver tests usually settle within months. Breast growth in men may require surgery if it does not resolve. Natural testosterone production usually recovers, but it can take months or years, and some men are left with lasting testicular shrinkage or infertility. Some changes, including the masculinising effects in women and established heart or liver damage, may not fully reverse. People who stop using steroids with medical and psychological support generally have a good long-term outlook, so early help-seeking should be encouraged.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Be aware that non-medical use of anabolic steroids carries real and sometimes permanent health risks
    \item Never share needles, syringes, or vials; use new, sterile equipment for every injection
    \item Do not buy steroids from unregulated sources, because the contents are often unknown or contaminated
    \item Seek help early from a doctor, counsellor, or dedicated support service if you want to stop
    \item Do not stop abruptly without advice, as withdrawal can be difficult
    \item Focus on healthy training, nutrition, and recovery rather than rapid muscle gain
    \item Discuss body-image concerns or eating problems with a doctor before considering steroids
    \item Attend all follow-up blood tests and check-ups after stopping
\end{itemize}
