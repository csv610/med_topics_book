\chapter{Black Piedra}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Black piedra is a fungal infection of the hair shaft caused by the fungus \textit{Piedraia hortae}. It produces small, hard, black or dark-brown nodules attached to the hair, most often on the scalp, and occasionally on the beard, moustache, and body hair.

The condition is not dangerous and does not spread to the skin or other parts of the body, but it can make hair look and feel gritty, brittle, and untidy. Black piedra is most common in humid tropical and subtropical regions and is readily treated once it is correctly identified.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Black piedra occurs mainly in tropical and subtropical areas of the Americas, Africa, South-East Asia, and the Pacific, where heat and humidity favour the growth of the fungus. It is uncommon in cooler, drier climates.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Who is affected:} the infection affects both sexes and all ages, though it is more often reported in young adults
    \item \textbf{Risk factors:} poor hair hygiene, long hair, and infrequent washing or oiling of the hair are thought to increase the risk
    \item \textbf{Transmission:} not highly contagious, and most commonly acquired through shared hair care items
    \item \textbf{Importance:} it is not associated with serious illness and is primarily a cosmetic problem
\end{itemize}

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The infection is caused by a specific fungus that grows on the hair.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Causative organism:} \textit{Piedraia hortae}, a black yeast-like fungus, grows along the surface of the hair shaft
    \item \textbf{Mode of spread:} acquired through contact with spores on hairbrushes, combs, hats, or shared hair care items, and possibly through direct head contact
    \item \textbf{Environmental conditions:} warm, humid weather allows the fungus to thrive
    \item \textbf{Mechanism:} the fungus forms dense, dark colonies that cement onto the hair and produce hard nodules; it does not invade the hair root or the scalp skin
    \item \textbf{Hair damage:} the fungus can weaken the hair shaft, making it brittle and prone to breaking at the sites of the nodules
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Small, hard, black or dark-brown gritty nodules attached to the hair shafts
    \item Nodules feel like grains of sand along the hair and are difficult to dislodge
    \item Nodules are most often found on the scalp but can occur on the beard, moustache, and pubic hair
    \item Hair that is brittle and breaks off easily at the sites of the nodules
    \item A rough or dirty appearance to the hair despite regular washing
    \item No itching, scaling, or rash on the scalp skin, and no hair loss from the roots
    \item Usually no symptoms other than the cosmetic change and brittleness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{White piedra:} a different fungal infection producing soft, pale, whitish, or grey nodules on the hair
    \item \textbf{Head lice (pediculosis):} cause itching, with visible lice and eggs rather than hard black nodules
    \item \textbf{Nits:} empty egg cases attached to the hair, which are small and pale
    \item \textbf{Hair casts or dandruff-like debris:} material that can be brushed off easily
    \item \textbf{Seborrhoeic dermatitis and other scaling conditions:} which affect the scalp skin rather than the hair shafts
    \item \textbf{Trichomycosis:} a bacterial infection that forms yellow, red, or black sheaths around the hairs
    \item \textbf{Artifactual debris:} such as hairspray residue or foreign matter in the hair
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor if you notice persistent black, gritty nodules on the hair that do not wash or brush out, or if you are unsure whether the problem is head lice, a fungal infection, or something else. Medical assessment is helpful to confirm the diagnosis, because black piedra is easily confused with other hair and scalp conditions.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A rash, swelling, or oozing on the scalp that could indicate a bacterial infection
    \item Fever occurring together with a scalp or hair problem
    \item Widespread hair loss or bald patches appearing suddenly
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clinical examination:} inspection of the hair and scalp for hard, dark nodules firmly attached to the hair shafts
    \item \textbf{Microscopy:} a pulled hair examined under a microscope shows the characteristic hard, black nodular structures on the shaft
    \item \textbf{Microscopy with potassium hydroxide:} the hair is treated to make fungal structures easier to see
    \item \textbf{Fungal culture:} rarely needed, but \textit{Piedraia hortae} can be grown from infected hair in the laboratory
    \item \textbf{Dermatoscopy:} a hand-held magnifier helps distinguish the nodules from lice eggs and debris
    \item \textbf{Wood's lamp:} generally not helpful, because black piedra does not fluoresce
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Shaving or cutting the affected hair:} the simplest and most effective treatment, because the fungus lives only on the hair shaft
    \item \textbf{Topical antifungal treatment:} medicated shampoos or creams containing antifungal agents may help in milder cases
    \item \textbf{Oral antifungal medicine:} may be prescribed by a doctor for persistent or extensive infection
    \item \textbf{Washing and brushing:} regular shampooing to remove spores and prevent spread
    \item \textbf{Treatment of contacts:} household members can be checked and treated if affected
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} a recheck after a few weeks confirms that the nodules have gone and the hair is regrowing normally
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Cosmetic embarrassment and anxiety about the appearance of the hair
    \item Hair shaft brittleness and breakage at the sites of the nodules
    \item Recurrence if the source of infection, such as a shared comb or brush, is not removed
    \item Misdiagnosis as head lice, leading to unnecessary and ineffective treatment
    \item Permanent hair damage in rare cases if the infection persists untreated
    \item No spread to the skin, blood, or other organs, since black piedra remains confined to the hair
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Black piedra is a benign, cosmetic condition and does not affect general health. With treatment, particularly cutting the affected hair, the infection clears and the hair regrows normally within weeks to months.

Recurrence is possible if hygiene is poor or if contaminated hair care items continue to be used. Once the fungus is removed and preventive measures are followed, the outlook is excellent, and there is no lasting damage in the great majority of cases.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Wash and dry the hair regularly, especially in humid climates
    \item Do not share combs, brushes, hats, hair ties, or towels with other people
    \item Keep hair care items clean, and replace them after treatment
    \item Cut or shave hair that is heavily affected, and dispose of the cut hair carefully
    \item Check family members and close contacts for nodules, and treat them if affected
    \item In tropical regions, keep hair shorter and avoid long periods of damp, unwashed hair
    \item See a doctor for a firm diagnosis rather than treating presumed head lice
\end{itemize}
