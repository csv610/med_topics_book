\chapter{Shocks (electric)}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational draft; seek professional advice for individual care.}

\section*{Definition and scope}
Electric shock is an injury caused by current passing through the body. Severity depends on voltage, current, duration, pathway, skin resistance, and associated burns or trauma.

\section*{Immediate safety}
Do not touch a person who is still in contact with the source. Isolate the power safely, call emergency services, and begin CPR/AED use if the scene is safe and the person is unresponsive or not breathing normally.

\section*{Assessment}
Urgent assessment should consider loss of consciousness, chest pain, palpitations, breathing difficulty, neurological symptoms, burns, falls, and pregnancy. Children and high-voltage exposures require a low threshold for hospital review.

\section*{Treatment and follow-up}
Care may include resuscitation, cardiac monitoring, burn and wound care, treatment of trauma, and observation for delayed complications. Do not apply ice or break blisters. Arrange review for persistent pain, weakness, numbness, burns, or new cardiac symptoms.

\section*{Prevention}
Use residual-current protection, intact equipment, dry hands, appropriate insulation, and qualified electricians. Never work on live circuits.

\section*{Expanded clinical context}
This chapter addresses shocks electric, a topic that should be interpreted in the context of age, baseline health, medicines, comorbidities, goals, and access to care. It supports discussion with a qualified clinician and does not establish a diagnosis.

\section*{Assessment and decision points}
Review onset, duration, triggers, relevant exposures, physical findings, associated symptoms, functional impact, and immediate safety. Record the main concern, time course, risk factors, red flags, and the person’s questions. Use targeted investigations when their result is likely to change management.

\section*{Management and follow-up}
Care may include education, practical support, treatment of contributing conditions, medication or a procedure when indicated, and a shared follow-up plan. Explain expected improvement, possible adverse effects, and when reassessment is needed. Avoid starting, stopping, or sharing prescription medicines without professional advice.

\section*{Safety-netting}
Seek urgent help for severe or rapidly worsening symptoms, collapse, breathing difficulty, severe pain, confusion, dehydration, uncontrolled bleeding, or any immediate danger.

\section*{Practical prevention}
Use plain-language information, supportive routines, risk reduction, and professional review when symptoms persist, recur, or impair daily life. Keep written instructions and confirm how to access help outside routine appointments.

\section*{Limitations}
This educational expansion should be checked against current local guidance and authoritative topic-specific sources.
\section*{Communication and support}
After an electrical injury, document the event, arrange appropriate follow-up, and make sure the person knows which symptoms require urgent reassessment.
