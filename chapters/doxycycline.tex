\chapter{Doxycycline}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Doxycycline is an antibiotic from the tetracycline family used to treat a wide range of bacterial infections, including respiratory tract infections, acne, rosacea, skin and soft tissue infections, urinary tract infections, and sexually transmitted infections such as chlamydia. It also has anti-inflammatory effects, which makes it useful for conditions such as acne and rosacea, and it is used to prevent malaria in travellers. Doxycycline is taken by mouth, usually once or twice daily, and is generally well tolerated, though it has some important precautions, including sun sensitivity and effects on teeth in young children. This chapter explains how doxycycline works, its uses, and its side effects.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Doxycycline is one of the most widely prescribed antibiotics in Australia and worldwide. It is a common treatment for acne, which affects most adolescents, and for chlamydia, one of the most common sexually transmitted infections. It is also used for respiratory infections, tick-borne diseases, and malaria prophylaxis. Concern about antibiotic resistance has led to more careful use of doxycycline, and it is used only for infections where it is effective.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mechanism:} doxycycline stops bacteria from producing the proteins they need to grow and multiply
    \item \textbf{Broad spectrum:} it is effective against many types of bacteria, including chlamydia, rickettsia, and mycoplasma
    \item \textbf{Anti-inflammatory action:} it reduces inflammation, which helps in acne and rosacea
    \item \textbf{Malaria prevention:} it prevents the malaria parasite from developing
    \item \textbf{Absorption:} taken by mouth and absorbed well, with food reducing stomach upset
    \item \textbf{Duration:} treatment courses range from days for acute infections to months for acne
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Taken with a full glass of water, and while sitting or standing
    \item Avoid lying down for at least 30 minutes after taking it
    \item Common side effects: nausea, stomach upset, and diarrhoea
    \item Sensitivity to sunlight, causing easy sunburn
    \item Should not be taken with dairy products, antacids, or iron within two hours
    \item Effects on developing teeth and bones in children under eight and in pregnancy
    \item Yeast infections can occur, especially in women
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Doxycycline versus other antibiotics, chosen according to the infection
    \item Doxycycline versus other acne treatments
    \item Oral versus topical antibiotics for skin conditions
    \item Doxycycline versus other malaria prophylaxis options
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Doxycycline is a prescription medicine. See a doctor if the infection does not improve, if side effects are severe, or before taking it with other medicines. Seek urgent care for signs of a serious reaction.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe allergic reaction with swelling, rash, or difficulty breathing
    \item Severe diarrhoea, which may indicate a serious bowel infection
    \item Severe headache with blurred vision, which may indicate raised pressure in the head
    \item Difficulty swallowing or chest pain after taking the tablet
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Medical assessment:} the infection and its severity guide treatment
    \item \textbf{Microbiology:} swabs or cultures identify the organism where needed
    \item \textbf{Review of medicines:} checking for interactions
    \item \textbf{Pregnancy check:} doxycycline is avoided in pregnancy and breastfeeding
    \item \textbf{Monitoring:} review of response and side effects during longer courses
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dosing:} taken once or twice daily as prescribed, with water
    \item \textbf{Food:} may be taken with food if it causes stomach upset, but not with dairy products
    \item \textbf{Sun protection:} sunscreen and protective clothing, as doxycycline increases sun sensitivity
    \item \textbf{Complete the course:} finish the full course as directed
    \item \textbf{For acne:} long courses with regular review
    \item \textbf{For malaria:} started before travel and continued for four weeks after leaving
    \item \textbf{Managing side effects:} reporting and managing side effects with the doctor
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sunburn and photosensitivity
    \item Nausea, vomiting, and diarrhoea
    \item Yeast infections
    \item Tooth discolouration and enamel damage in children under eight
    \item Effects on the unborn baby in pregnancy
    \item Antibiotic resistance with unnecessary or incomplete use
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Doxycycline is highly effective for the infections it is prescribed for, with most people improving within days. Long-term use for acne produces good results over months. Side effects are usually mild and manageable, and serious reactions are rare. Taking it correctly, with water and sun precautions, gives the best outcomes.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Take doxycycline exactly as prescribed
    \item Take it with a full glass of water and remain upright
    \item Avoid dairy, antacids, and iron within two hours
    \item Use sunscreen and protect skin from the sun
    \item Complete the full course
    \item Report severe side effects promptly
    \item Store it away from heat and light
\end{itemize}
