\chapter{Gynecomastia}
\index{Gynecomastia}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Gynecomastia is the enlargement of breast gland tissue in males, caused by a hormonal imbalance in which the effect of oestrogen relative to testosterone is increased. It is one of the most common reasons men seek medical advice about their chest. It can occur at three peaks of life: in newborns (due to maternal hormones), during puberty, and in older age as testosterone levels fall. Gynecomastia usually appears as a tender, firm disc beneath the nipple, often on both sides, and is benign. Most cases resolve without treatment, but persistent, painful, or distressing cases can be treated with medicines or surgery.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Gynecomastia is common: studies suggest around one-third to two-thirds of men have some degree of breast tissue enlargement at some point. Pubertal gynecomastia affects up to 60--70\% of boys, usually resolving within two years. In adult men, prevalence increases with age, affecting up to 65\% of older men. Most cases are physiological (no underlying disease), but medicines, alcohol and drug use, hormonal disorders, and systemic disease account for a substantial minority. Only a small proportion of men seek treatment.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Gynecomastia occurs when the balance of hormones acting on breast tissue shifts toward oestrogen.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hormonal balance:} oestrogen stimulates, and testosterone inhibits, male breast tissue growth; an imbalance causes growth
    \item \textbf{Physiological causes:} newborn (maternal oestrogen), puberty (transient imbalance), and ageing (relative fall in testosterone)
    \item \textbf{Medicines:} anti-androgens, some blood pressure and antipsychotic medicines, digoxin, androgens and anabolic steroids, and others
    \item \textbf{Substances:} alcohol, marijuana, and other recreational drugs
    \item \textbf{Disease:} liver cirrhosis, kidney failure, hyperthyroidism, malnutrition, and rare tumours
    \item \textbf{Genetic:} Klinefelter syndrome and other conditions
    \item \textbf{Idiopathic:} no cause identified in around a quarter of cases
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Gynecomastia presents with characteristic findings.

\begin{itemize}
    \item A firm, rubbery, tender disc of tissue beneath the nipple
    \item Enlargement of one or both breasts
    \item Tenderness, which is common during active growth
    \item Gradual onset, usually over months
    \item No nipple discharge in simple gynecomastia
    \item Features of an underlying cause when present, including reduced libido or fatigue
    \item Normal testicles and genitalia in most cases
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
The main differentials are other causes of chest enlargement and breast lumps.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Pseudogynecomastia: chest enlargement due to fat rather than glandular tissue
    \item Male breast cancer: rare, but characterised by a hard, painless, fixed lump, often one-sided, sometimes with skin changes or nipple discharge
    \item Lipoma and other benign chest lumps
    \item Breast infection or cysts
    \item Testicular tumours causing hormonal gynecomastia
    \item Drug-related and substance-related causes
    \item Liver, kidney, or thyroid disease
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Men should see a doctor for any new breast lump, especially if it is hard, one-sided, growing, or associated with skin changes or nipple discharge. Persistent, painful, or distressing gynecomastia also warrants review to identify any treatable cause.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item This condition is not an emergency; however, seek urgent care for a rapidly growing chest mass, chest pain, or difficulty breathing
    \item Signs of infection, including severe pain, redness, and fever
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Assessment focuses on confirming the diagnosis and identifying any cause.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History:} age, onset, medicines, alcohol and drug use, and related symptoms
    \item \textbf{Examination:} confirming glandular tissue, and examining the testicles and chest
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} testosterone, oestrogen, thyroid, liver, and kidney function as indicated
    \item \textbf{Ultrasound or mammography:} when cancer is suspected or the diagnosis is unclear
    \item \textbf{Testicular ultrasound:} if a testicular cause is suspected
    \item \textbf{Observation:} in typical physiological cases, monitoring is all that is needed
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Most gynecomastia needs no treatment beyond reassurance.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reassurance and observation:} pubertal gynecomastia resolves in most boys within two years
    \item \textbf{Stopping the cause:} discontinuing culprit medicines or substances under medical advice
    \item \textbf{Treating underlying disease:} managing liver, kidney, thyroid, or hormonal conditions
    \item \textbf{Weight management:} reduces chest fat and improves appearance
    \item \textbf{Medicines:} tamoxifen or other hormone-modulating agents for painful or persistent cases
    \item \textbf{Surgery:} liposuction and glandular excision for persistent or distressing cases
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} monitoring for change
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Psychological distress and effects on body image and confidence
    \item Persistent breast tissue beyond puberty
    \item Pain and tenderness
    \item Complications of surgery when performed
    \item Missed diagnosis of male breast cancer if lumps are dismissed
    \item Complications of underlying disease if not detected
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The outlook for gynecomastia is very good. Pubertal gynecomastia resolves spontaneously in most boys. Gynecomastia from medicines or substances improves when the cause is removed. Idiopathic adult gynecomastia often persists but causes no health problems. Where treatment is desired, medicines and surgery are effective, with high satisfaction. Gynecomastia itself does not increase the risk of breast cancer, and most men are reassured by the knowledge that the condition is benign and common.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Avoid anabolic steroids and illicit substances
    \item Limit alcohol
    \item Maintain a healthy weight
    \item Check any new breast lump with a doctor
    \item Review medicines that can cause gynecomastia
    \item Reassure adolescent boys that the condition is normal and usually temporary
    \item Seek psychological support if body image is affected
    \item Attend follow-up if monitoring is recommended
\end{itemize}
