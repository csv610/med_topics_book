\chapter{Parkinson's Disease}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Parkinson's disease (PD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterised by the loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta and the presence of Lewy bodies (intracytoplasmic aggregates of $\alpha$-synuclein). It is the second most common neurodegenerative disease after Alzheimer's disease. The cardinal motor features are bradykinesia, resting tremor, rigidity, and postural instability (TRAP). Non-motor symptoms (cognitive, psychiatric, autonomic, sensory, sleep) often precede motor onset and dominate late-stage disease. PD is a clinical diagnosis; there is no definitive biomarker, though dopamine transporter imaging (DaTscan) supports diagnostic uncertainty.

\section*{Epidemiology}
In Australia, ~100,000 people live with PD (prevalence ~400/100,000); incidence ~20/100,000/year. Age is the strongest risk factor: prevalence 1\% at 60, 3\% at 80. Men are affected 1.5x more than women. Average age at diagnosis is ~65; young-onset PD (<50) accounts for 5--10\%. Indigenous Australians have lower recorded prevalence but likely underdiagnosis. Global prevalence is rising faster than any other neurological disorder due to ageing populations. Direct and indirect costs exceed \$10 billion annually in Australia.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{$\alpha$-Synuclein pathology:} misfolded $\alpha$-synuclein aggregates into Lewy bodies and Lewy neurites; Braak staging proposes caudo-rostral spread (olfactory bulb/dorsal motor nucleus of vagus $\to$ locus coeruleus $\to$ substantia nigra $\to$ limbic $\to$ neocortex)
    \item \textbf{Dopaminergic neuron loss:} 50--70\% loss in substantia nigra pars compacta at motor onset; putsamen most affected; leads to striatal dopamine deficiency and disrupted basal ganglia circuitry (direct/indirect pathways)
    \item \textbf{Genetics:} ~15\% have family history; monogenic forms rare but informative:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Autosomal dominant: \textit{SNCA} (PARK1/4, $\alpha$-synuclein multiplication/mutation), \textit{LRRK2} (PARK8, G2019S common in Ashkenazi/North African), \textit{VPS35}
        \item Autosomal recessive: \textit{PRKN} (PARK2, parkin, most common early-onset), \textit{PINK1} (PARK6), \textit{DJ-1} (PARK7)
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Risk factors:} age, male sex, pesticide/herbicide exposure (paraquat, rotenone), head trauma, dairy consumption; protective: smoking, caffeine, urate, physical activity, NSAIDs
    \item \textbf{Mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, neuroinflammation, lysosomal/GBA impairment:} convergent pathways
    \item \textbf{GBA mutations:} glucocerebrosidase mutations (Gaucher carriers) are the strongest common genetic risk factor (5--10\% PD); earlier onset, more cognitive decline
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Motor (cardinal):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Bradykinesia: slowness, decrement in amplitude/speed of repetitive movements, micrographia, hypomimia, hypophonia
        \item Resting tremor: 4--6 Hz, pill-rolling, distal > proximal, worsens with stress, improves with action, disappears in sleep
        \item Rigidity: lead-pipe or cogwheel (with tremor); neck and limbs
        \item Postural instability: late feature (Hoehn \& Yahr stage 3+); retropulsion, festination, freezing of gait
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Other motor:} stooped posture, reduced arm swing, shuffling gait, dystonia (foot, off-period), dysphagia, dysarthria, sialorrhoea, seborrhoea
    \item \textbf{Non-motor (often more disabling):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Neuropsychiatric: depression (40--50\%), anxiety, apathy, psychosis (visual hallucinations, delusions), impulse control disorders (dopamine agonist-related), dementia (80\% at 20 years)
        \item Autonomic: constipation (early), orthostatic hypotension, urinary urgency/frequency, sexual dysfunction, thermoregulatory impairment
        \item Sleep: REM sleep behaviour disorder (RBD, prodromal), excessive daytime sleepiness, insomnia, restless legs
        \item Sensory: hyposmia (prodromal, >90\%), pain, paresthesiae
        \item Cognitive: executive dysfunction, visuospatial impairment, attention fluctuation
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Prodromal phase (years before motor):} RBD, hyposmia, constipation, depression, subtle motor signs
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Atypical parkinsonism (Parkinson-plus):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Multiple system atrophy (MSA): cerebellar (MSA-C) or autonomic (MSA-P) predominant; poor levodopa response; ``hot cross bun'' sign (pons)
        \item Progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP): vertical gaze palsy, early falls (backward), axial rigidity, pseudobulbar affect; midbrain atrophy (``hummingbird'' sign)
        \item Corticobasal degeneration (CBD): asymmetric cortical signs (apraxia, alien limb, cortical sensory loss), myoclonus; asymmetric atrophy
        \item Dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB): dementia preceding or within 1 year of motor; fluctuating cognition, visual hallucinations, RBD
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Secondary parkinsonism:} drug-induced (antipsychotics, antiemetics -- metoclopramide, prochlorperazine; calcium channel blockers -- cinnarizine), vascular (lower body parkinsonism, white matter disease), normal pressure hydrocephalus (gait, urinary, dementia), toxins (MPTP, manganese, carbon monoxide)
    \item \textbf{Essential tremor:} action tremor, no bradykinesia/rigidity, family history, alcohol responsive
    \item \textbf{Functional (psychogenic) parkinsonism:} inconsistency, distractibility, abrupt onset, incongruent with organic patterns
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Refer to neurologist (ideally movement disorder specialist) for suspected PD. Early diagnosis enables timely treatment, clinical trial access, and multidisciplinary care.

\textbf{Urgent review for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden worsening (``off'' periods, medication changes, intercurrent illness)
    \item Falls with injury
    \item Psychosis (hallucinations, delusions) -- safety risk
    \item Dysphagia with aspiration risk
    \item Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (rigidity, hyperthermia, autonomic instability after dopamine agonist withdrawal or antipsychotic)
    \item Suicidal ideation (depression common)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clinical diagnosis:} UK Brain Bank / MDS clinical criteria: bradykinesia + (resting tremor OR rigidity); supportive criteria (clear beneficial response to levodopa, levodopa-induced dyskinesias, rest tremor, olfactory loss); exclusion criteria (atypical features, drug exposure, other causes)
    \item \textbf{DaTscan (SPECT with Ioflupane $^{123}$I):} reduced striatal dopamine transporter binding; supports nigrostriatal degeneration; does not differentiate PD from atypical parkinsonism; use for diagnostic uncertainty
    \item \textbf{MRI brain:} usually normal in early PD; excludes vascular, NPH, structural; midbrain/pons atrophy suggests PSP/MSA; volumetric analysis emerging
    \item \textbf{Bloods:} exclude Wilson disease (ceruloplasmin, copper) if young-onset; B12, folate, thyroid, copper, HIV, syphilis if atypical
    \item \textbf{Genetic testing:} consider if age <50, strong family history, Jewish ancestry (LRRK2 G2019S), or atypical features; panel testing (PRKN, PINK1, DJ-1, SNCA, LRRK2, GBA, VPS35)
    \item \textbf{Cardiac MIBG scintigraphy:} reduced cardiac sympathetic innervation (PD vs MSA); not routine
    \item \textbf{Polysomnography:} confirm RBD (prodromal marker)
    \item \textbf{Cognitive screening:} MoCA (better than MMSE for PD executive dysfunction); regular monitoring
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{General principles:} individualise; start low, go slow; treat most bothersome symptoms; address non-motor; multidisciplinary team (neurologist, PD nurse, physiotherapist, occupational therapist, speech pathologist, neuropsychologist, psychiatrist, social worker)
    \item \textbf{Motor -- dopaminergic therapy:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Levodopa/carbidopa or levodopa/benserazide: gold standard, most effective; start when function impaired; IR and CR formulations; dose fractionation for wearing off
        \item Dopamine agonists (pramipexole, ropinirole, rotigotine patch): monotherapy early (<65--70) to delay levodopa; side effects: impulse control disorders (ICDs), oedema, somnolence, hallucinations
        \item MAO-B inhibitors (rasagiline, safinamide): mild benefit, delay levodopa; safinamide add-on for wearing off
        \item COMT inhibitors (entacapone, opicapone): extend levodopa half-life for wearing off
        \item Amantadine: for dyskinesia (NMDA antagonism); extended-release for off + dyskinesia
        \item Apomorphine SC: rescue for severe off; pump for continuous infusion
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Advanced therapies (motor fluctuations, dyskinesia, refractory tremor):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Deep brain stimulation (DBS): STN or GPi; reversible, adjustable; best for levodopa-responsive fluctuations/dyskinesia/tremor; not for dementia, psychosis, severe balance/gait
        \item Levodopa-carbidopa intestinal gel (LCIG): continuous duodenal infusion; for severe fluctuations not DBS candidates
        \item Focused ultrasound (FUS): thalamotomy for tremor-dominant; unilateral
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Non-motor management:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Depression/anxiety: SSRI/SNRI; avoid paroxetine (anticholinergic); CBT
        \item Psychosis: reduce/stop anticholinergics, amantadine, dopamine agonists; quetiapine/clozapine (low dose); pimavanserin (5-HT2A inverse agonist, approved for PD psychosis)
        \item Orthostatic hypotension: fluid/salt, compression stockings, midodrine, fludrocortisone, droxidopa
        \item Constipation: fibre, fluids, exercise, laxatives (osmotic, stimulant); prokinetics (prucalopride)
        \item RBD: melatonin, clonazepam
        \item Dementia: rivastigmine (only approved cholinesterase inhibitor for PDD); donepezil, galantamine off-label; memantine
        \item ICDs: reduce/stop dopamine agonist; CBT; consider DBS
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rehabilitation:} LSVT BIG (amplitude training), LSVT LOUD (voice), gait/balance training, falls prevention, exercise (aerobic + resistance 150 min/week)
    \item \textbf{Palliative care:} early integration; advance care planning; symptom control; carer support
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Motor complications:} wearing off (end-of-dose), peak dose dyskinesia, on-off fluctuations, freezing of gait, falls
    \item \textbf{Non-motor:} cognitive decline/dementia, psychosis, depression/anxiety, autonomic dysfunction, sleep disorders, pain
    \item \textbf{Medication-related:} ICDs (15--20\% on agonists), hallucinations, oedema, nausea, orthostatic hypotension, dopamine dysregulation syndrome (addictive medication use)
    \item \textbf{Advanced therapy complications:} DBS (infection, hardware failure, speech worsening, cognitive decline, mood changes); LCIG (PEG tube complications, abdominal pain)
    \item \textbf{Aspiration pneumonia:} leading cause of death
    \item \textbf{Fractures:} from falls + osteoporosis
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
PD is progressive but highly variable. Median time to disability (Hoehn \& Yahr 3) ~7--10 years; to dementia ~15--20 years; life expectancy modestly reduced (1--5 years). Young-onset progresses slower but more dyskinesia. Levodopa response remains the best prognostic indicator. Non-motor symptoms increasingly determine late-stage QoL. DBS and advanced therapies extend functional independence. Research focus: disease modification (anti-$\alpha$-synuclein, GBA-targeted, LRRK2 inhibitors, neurotrophic factors, cell therapy).

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Exercise:} the only intervention with evidence for slowing progression; aerobic + resistance + balance + skill-based (dance, boxing, Tai Chi); start early, continue lifelong
    \item \textbf{Medication adherence:} take on time, every time; use pill timers; don't stop abruptly
    \item \textbf{Diet:} Mediterranean; protein redistribution (most protein at evening meal) if levodopa absorption affected; hydration, fibre for constipation
    \item \textbf{Sleep hygiene:} regular schedule, treat RBD, limit daytime naps
    \item \textbf{Falls prevention:} home safety, lighting, grab rails, footwear, vision checks, medication review
    \item \textbf{Cognitive engagement:} social activity, learning, puzzles, music
    \item \textbf{Mental health:} treat depression/anxiety early; mindfulness, CBT
    \item \textbf{Multidisciplinary care:} regular reviews with PD nurse, allied health
    \item \textbf{Advance care planning:} discuss preferences early while capacity intact
    \item \textbf{Support:} Parkinson's Australia, state organisations (Fight Parkinson's, Parkinson's NSW, etc.), Young Onset Parkinson's Exchange (YOPEX), clinical trials (Parkinson's Australia Clinical Trial Portal)
\end{itemize}
