\chapter{Physical Therapy}
\index{Physical Therapy}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. A physical therapist should tailor assessment and exercise to the person’s condition, goals, and safety needs.}

\section*{What it is}
Physical therapy is rehabilitation that helps improve movement, strength, balance, pain, function, and participation after injury, illness, surgery, or changes related to disability or ageing. It can be provided in hospital, clinic, community, work, school, or home settings.

\section*{Assessment}
The therapist may discuss symptoms, function, work and activities, previous treatment, medicines, and goals. They may assess movement, strength, balance, sensation, breathing, walking, or the way a task is performed. They should explain the plan and obtain consent.

\section*{Treatment}
Treatment may include education, graded exercises, hands-on techniques, gait or balance training, breathing exercises, advice about pacing, assistive devices, and changes to the home or workplace. Progress is reviewed and the plan adjusted. Exercises should be challenging but should not cause severe or persistent worsening.

\section*{Taking part safely}
Follow the prescribed frequency and technique, ask when instructions are unclear, and report new pain, swelling, dizziness, numbness, or weakness. Do not copy an exercise programme from another person without checking that it is appropriate.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Stop treatment and seek urgent help for chest pain, severe breathlessness, fainting, sudden weakness, new loss of bladder or bowel control, or rapidly worsening neurological symptoms. Contact the therapist or clinician for persistent severe pain or a significant decline in function.
