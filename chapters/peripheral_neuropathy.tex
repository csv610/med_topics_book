\chapter{Peripheral neuropathy}
\index{Peripheral neuropathy}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; seek professional advice for individual care.}

\section*{What it is}
Peripheral neuropathy is damage or dysfunction of nerves outside the brain and spinal cord. It often affects the feet and legs and may also affect the hands and arms. Causes include diabetes, alcohol, medicines, vitamin deficiency, kidney or thyroid disease, autoimmune illness, inherited disorders, and nerve compression.

\section*{Symptoms}
Symptoms may include burning pain, tingling, numbness, increased sensitivity, weakness, cramps, poor balance, or difficulty sensing temperature and injury. Some people develop changes in sweating, digestion, blood pressure, or sexual function when autonomic nerves are affected.

\section*{Assessment}
Assessment includes the pattern and progression of symptoms, medicines, alcohol and nutrition, family history, diabetes and other illnesses, neurological examination, and foot or skin examination. Blood tests may look for diabetes, vitamin B12 deficiency, thyroid disease, kidney disease, or other causes. Nerve-conduction tests or imaging are used selectively.

\section*{Management and prevention}
Treat the cause where possible and use an individualized plan for pain, sleep, mood, mobility, and falls prevention. People with diabetes should follow their glucose and foot-care plan. Check feet regularly, use well-fitting footwear, avoid heat injuries, and seek advice about wounds or loss of sensation. Medicines for nerve pain require clinician or pharmacist guidance.

\section*{When to seek urgent care}
Seek urgent help for rapidly progressive weakness, new loss of bladder or bowel control, severe back pain with numbness, difficulty breathing or swallowing, a spreading wound, a cold or discoloured foot, or sudden neurological change.
