\chapter{Obstructive Sleep Apnoea}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA) is a common sleep-related breathing disorder characterised by recurrent episodes of complete (apnoea) or partial (hypopnoea) upper airway obstruction during sleep, despite ongoing respiratory effort. These events cause intermittent hypoxia, hypercapnia, sleep fragmentation, and sympathetic activation. Severity is quantified by the apnoea-hypopnoea index (AHI): mild 5--15, moderate 15--30, severe >30 events/hour. OSA is strongly associated with obesity, but occurs in non-obese individuals due to craniofacial anatomy, upper airway neuromuscular control, and other factors. It is a major risk factor for cardiovascular disease, metabolic dysfunction, and neurocognitive impairment.

\section*{Epidemiology}
OSA affects approximately 20--30\% of Australian men and 10--15\% of women (moderate-severe AHI $\ge$15), with prevalence rising with age and obesity. Up to 80\% of moderate-severe OSA remains undiagnosed. Indigenous Australians have higher prevalence and severity. Risk factors: male sex, age >40, obesity (BMI $\ge$30: ~60\% have OSA), craniofacial abnormalities, family history, menopause, endocrine disorders (hypothyroidism, acromegaly). OSA contributes significantly to cardiovascular morbidity, motor vehicle accidents, workplace injuries, and healthcare costs (estimated >\$20 billion annually in Australia).

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Anatomical factors:} narrow/compliant upper airway; retrognathia, macroglossia, tonsillar hypertrophy, elongated soft palate, lateral pharyngeal wall collapse; fat deposition in parapharyngeal spaces with obesity
    \item \textbf{Neuromuscular factors:} reduced upper airway dilator muscle activity during sleep (genioglossus); impaired chemoresponsiveness; unstable ventilatory control (high loop gain)
    \item \textbf{Arousal threshold:} low threshold $\to$ frequent arousals preventing stable breathing; high threshold $\to$ prolonged events with severe desaturation
    \item \textbf{Consequences of events:} intermittent hypoxia $\to$ oxidative stress, systemic inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, sympathetic activation; sleep fragmentation $\to$ daytime sleepiness, cognitive impairment; intrathoracic pressure swings $\to$ atrial stretch, LV afterload increase
    \item \textbf{Phenotypes:} anatomical vs non-anatomical; guides therapy (anatomical responds to CPAP/surgery; non-anatomical may benefit from oxygen, acetazolamide, hypoglossal nerve stimulation)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Nocturnal:} loud snoring (often witnessed apnoeas), choking/gasping arousals, nocturia, insomnia, restless sleep, night sweats
    \item \textbf{Daytime:} excessive sleepiness (Epworth Sleepiness Scale $\ge$11), unrefreshing sleep, morning headache, poor concentration, memory impairment, mood changes, reduced libido
    \item \textbf{Associated conditions:} hypertension (resistant), atrial fibrillation, heart failure, stroke, type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, NAFLD, gout, depression, erectile dysfunction, motor vehicle accident risk (2--7x)
    \item \textbf{Physical:} obesity, large neck circumference (>43 cm men, >41 cm women), crowded oropharynx (Mallampati III/IV), retrognathia, nasal obstruction
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Central sleep apnoea:} no respiratory effort; Cheyne-Stokes (heart failure, stroke); opioid-induced; high altitude; CPAP-emergent
    \item \textbf{Primary snoring:} snoring without apnoeas/hypopnoeas (AHI <5); no daytime symptoms
    \item \textbf{Upper airway resistance syndrome:} subtle flow limitation, arousals, AHI <5; symptomatic
    \item \textbf{Obesity hypoventilation syndrome (OHS):} BMI $\ge$30, awake hypercapnia (PaCO2 >45 mmHg); often coexists with severe OSA
    \item \textbf{Narcolepsy:} excessive sleepiness with cataplexy, sleep paralysis, hallucinations; normal AHI
    \item \textbf{Chronic insufficient sleep / shift work / circadian disorders}
    \item \textbf{Medical causes of fatigue:} hypothyroidism, anaemia, depression, chronic fatigue syndrome, medication effects
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Screen high-risk patients: BMI $\ge$30, hypertension (especially resistant), atrial fibrillation, heart failure, type 2 diabetes, stroke/TIA, witnessed apnoeas, excessive sleepiness. Referral for sleep study if clinical suspicion.

\textbf{Urgent review for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sleepiness causing motor vehicle accidents or near-misses
    \item Suspected OHS (daytime hypercapnia, morning headache, peripheral oedema)
    \item Acute cardiovascular event in untreated severe OSA
    \item Perioperative risk: screen before major surgery (STOP-BANG)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Screening tools:} STOP-BANG (Snoring, Tired, Observed apnoea, Pressure/BP, BMI, Age, Neck, Gender), ESS, OSA50, Berlin Questionnaire
    \item \textbf{Home sleep apnoea test (HSAT / Level 2):} airflow, effort, oximetry, HR; validated for moderate-severe OSA in high pre-test probability; no EEG (underestimates AHI if sleep time overestimated)
    \item \textbf{In-lab polysomnography (PSG / Level 1):} gold standard; EEG, EOG, EMG, ECG, airflow, effort, oximetry, body position, video; required for complex cases, comorbidities, equivocal HSAT, central apnoea suspicion, hypoventilation
    \item \textbf{Key metrics:} AHI, ODI (oxygen desaturation index), mean/minimum SpO2, arousal index, sleep architecture, supine vs non-supine AHI
    \item \textbf{Additional:} blood pressure (24-h ABPM), echocardiogram (RV function, pulmonary hypertension), HbA1c, lipids, thyroid, CBC
    \item \textbf{Drug-induced sleep endoscopy (DISE):} for surgical planning; evaluates collapse pattern (VELPAL: Velum, Epiglottis, Lateral walls, Tongue base, Anterior-Posterior)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle (all patients):} weight loss (10\% $\to$ ~30\% AHI reduction); positional therapy (avoid supine); avoid alcohol/sedatives before bed; smoking cessation; regular exercise; treat nasal obstruction
    \item \textbf{CPAP (Continuous Positive Airway Pressure):} first-line for moderate-severe OSA (AHI $\ge$15) or symptomatic mild OSA; fixed or auto-titrating (APAP); adherence >4 h/night on 70\% nights for benefit; heated humidification, mask fitting, troubleshooting essential
    \item \textbf{Mandibular advancement splint (MAS):} first-line for mild-moderate OSA; alternative for CPAP intolerance; custom-fitted by dentist; titratable; side effects: dental changes, TMJ discomfort, salivation
    \item \textbf{Surgical:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Nasal surgery (septoplasty, turbinate reduction) -- improves CPAP tolerance, rarely cures OSA alone
        \item Palate surgery (UPPP, expansion sphincter pharyngoplasty, lateral pharyngoplasty) -- for palatal collapse
        \item Tongue base surgery (coblation, robotic, genioglossus advancement)
        \item Hypoglossal nerve stimulation (Inspire) -- for moderate-severe OSA, BMI <32-35, CPAP intolerant, favourable DISE pattern
        \item Maxillomandibular advancement (MMA) -- definitive for severe OSA with retrognathia; high success (>90\%)
        \item Bariatric surgery -- for BMI $\ge$35 with comorbidity; significant OSA improvement
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Medical/adjunctive:} no approved pharmacotherapy; acetazolamide (central apnoea, some OSA); oxygen (hypoxemia without CPAP); positional devices; myofunctional therapy (oropharyngeal exercises)
    \item \textbf{OHS:} CPAP or bilevel PAP (BPAP) to correct hypercapnia; weight loss; bariatric surgery
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} CPAP data download at 1, 3, 6, 12 months then annually; assess adherence, efficacy, mask fit, symptoms; repeat sleep study if weight change >10\%, persistent symptoms, or clinical change
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cardiovascular:} hypertension, atrial fibrillation, HFpEF/HFrEF, coronary artery disease, stroke, pulmonary hypertension, sudden cardiac death
    \item \textbf{Metabolic:} insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, dyslipidaemia, NAFLD/MAFLD
    \item \textbf{Neurocognitive:} impaired attention, memory, executive function; dementia risk; depression, anxiety
    \item \textbf{Safety:} motor vehicle accidents (2--7x risk), workplace injuries
    \item \textbf{Perioperative:} difficult airway, post-op respiratory depression, cardiac events
    \item \textbf{Quality of life:} reduced productivity, relationship strain, healthcare costs
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
CPAP adherence $\ge$4 h/night normalises AHI, reduces blood pressure (2--4 mmHg), improves sleepiness, quality of life, and cognitive function; reduces cardiovascular events in observational data (RCTs limited by crossover). Weight loss of 10--15\% can induce remission in many. MAS effective in mild-moderate. Surgical cure rates vary by procedure and selection. Long-term management requires ongoing adherence support, weight maintenance, and periodic reassessment.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Maintain healthy weight from young adulthood
    \item Limit alcohol, especially within 3 h of bedtime
    \item Avoid sedatives unless necessary; discuss alternatives with doctor
    \item Sleep on side (positional therapy devices if needed)
    \item Treat nasal congestion/allergies
    \item Regular exercise (improves upper airway tone, reduces fluid shift)
    \item If prescribed CPAP: use every night, all night; clean equipment; replace masks/filters per schedule; troubleshoot leaks early
    \item Report persistent sleepiness despite CPAP (residual sleepiness $\to$ evaluate for other causes)
    \item Inform surgeons/anaesthetists of OSA before any procedure
    \item Screen family members if OSA diagnosed (genetic component)
    \item Support: Sleep Health Foundation, Australasian Sleep Association, patient support groups
\end{itemize}
