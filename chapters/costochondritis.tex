\chapter{Costochondritis}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Costochondritis is inflammation of the cartilage that joins the ribs to the breastbone (sternum). It causes chest pain that is often sharp, localised, and worse with movement, deep breathing, or pressing on the area. It is a common, generally harmless condition that can mimic more serious causes of chest pain, particularly heart disease, which is why it deserves medical assessment. Costochondritis usually settles on its own over weeks to months, and treatment focuses on pain relief and reassurance. A related condition, Tietze syndrome, is a rarer variant with visible swelling of the same joints.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Costochondritis is one of the most common causes of chest pain in adults and adolescents seen in general practice and emergency departments. It affects men and women of all ages, with peaks in adolescence and in people over 40. In people presenting to emergency departments with chest pain, musculoskeletal causes such as costochondritis account for a substantial minority of cases. It can follow chest infections, heavy coughing, strenuous exercise, heavy lifting, or injury, but in many cases no clear trigger is identified.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The pain arises from inflammation or irritation of the costochondral joints, where the upper ribs meet the sternum.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Overuse and strain:} heavy lifting, vigorous exercise, or repeated upper-body movement
    \item \textbf{Coughing:} prolonged or forceful coughing, especially after respiratory infection
    \item \textbf{Injury:} a direct blow to the chest or a fall
    \item \textbf{Infection:} viral respiratory infections, and rarely bacterial infection of the joint
    \item \textbf{Arthritis:} osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, or ankylosing spondylitis can affect these joints
    \item \textbf{Idiopathic:} in many cases, no cause is found
    \item \textbf{Fibromyalgia:} diffuse musculoskeletal pain may include the chest wall
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
The hallmark is reproducible chest pain at specific points.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Sharp or aching pain on one side of the chest, near the breastbone
    \item Pain reproduced by pressing on the affected rib joints
    \item Pain worse with deep breathing, coughing, sneezing, or twisting
    \item Pain worse with certain movements or positions
    \item Tenderness over the cartilage, without visible swelling in typical costochondritis
    \item No shortness of breath, sweating, or pain radiating to the arm or jaw
    \item Symptoms that come and go over weeks or months
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Chest pain has serious causes that must be excluded first, especially in adults with risk factors.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Heart attack and unstable angina, particularly in older adults or those with cardiac risk factors
    \item Pulmonary embolism, pneumonia, pleurisy, and pneumothorax
    \item Aortic dissection, a medical emergency
    \item Gastro-oesophageal reflux and oesophagitis
    \item Pericarditis or myocarditis
    \item Anxiety and panic attacks
    \item Rib fracture, muscle strain, or other chest wall injury
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Any new chest pain should be assessed by a doctor, because it is not possible to tell costochondritis from more serious conditions by symptoms alone. An ECG and examination can help. Seek urgent care if the pain is severe, crushing, or associated with breathlessness, sweating, or radiation to the arm or jaw.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chest pain at rest, or pain lasting more than a few minutes
    \item Pain spreading to the arm, jaw, neck, or back
    \item Shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, or dizziness with chest pain
    \item Sudden severe chest pain or difficulty breathing
    \item Collapse or loss of consciousness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Diagnosis is clinical, once serious causes have been excluded.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History and examination:} the pain is reproduced by pressing on the costochondral joints, and the heart and lungs are normal on examination
    \item \textbf{ECG:} to exclude cardiac causes
    \item \textbf{Chest X-ray:} to exclude lung disease, fracture, or other abnormalities
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} troponin and inflammatory markers if cardiac or inflammatory disease is suspected
    \item \textbf{Further tests:} CT, ultrasound, or MRI in atypical or persistent cases
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Treatment is conservative and aims to relieve pain while the inflammation settles.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reassurance:} most cases settle with time and do not indicate heart disease
    \item \textbf{Pain relief:} paracetamol, ibuprofen, or other anti-inflammatory medicines
    \item \textbf{Heat or ice packs:} applied to the tender area
    \item \textbf{Activity modification:} avoid the movements, lifting, or exercise that aggravate the pain
    \item \textbf{Posture:} attention to posture, especially after prolonged sitting or desk work
    \item \textbf{Physiotherapy:} stretching and gentle exercise for persistent cases
    \item \textbf{Local steroid injection:} for severe or prolonged pain not responding to other measures
    \item \textbf{Treating the cause:} management of any cough, infection, or arthritis
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chronic or recurrent chest wall pain lasting many months
    \item Reduced activity and deconditioning from avoidance of movement
    \item Anxiety about the cause of the pain, especially before the diagnosis is made
    \item Rarely, infection of the joint (septic arthritis), needing antibiotics and drainage
    \item Over-investigation when the diagnosis is not recognised
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Most people with costochondritis improve within a few weeks, although the pain can linger for several months and can recur. Symptoms usually respond to simple pain relief and activity modification. The condition does not damage the heart or lungs, and once serious causes are excluded, the main treatment is time and reassurance. If pain persists beyond a few months or is severe, review is worthwhile to reconsider the diagnosis and treatment options.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Seek medical assessment for any new chest pain before assuming it is muscular
    \item Avoid heavy lifting and sudden strenuous activity until the pain settles
    \item Use good posture, especially at desks and screens
    \item Take simple pain relief regularly for a short period as advised
    \item Apply heat or ice to the tender area
    \item Return to activity gradually, with stretching as comfort allows
    \item Review with a doctor if pain is severe, persistent, or worsening
\end{itemize}
