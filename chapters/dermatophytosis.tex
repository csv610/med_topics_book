\chapter{Dermatophytosis}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Dermatophytosis is a fungal infection of the skin, hair, and nails caused by a group of fungi called dermatophytes. These infections are known by names that reflect their location: tinea corporis (ringworm of the body), tinea pedis (athlete's foot), tinea cruris (jock itch), tinea capitis (scalp), and tinea unguium (nail). Despite the name, ringworm is not caused by a worm. Dermatophyte infections are common, contagious, and easily spread by direct contact or via contaminated surfaces such as floors and towels. Most respond well to antifungal treatment.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Dermatophyte infections are among the most common skin conditions worldwide, affecting people of all ages. Athlete's foot is extremely common, particularly in adolescents and adults, and is more common in people who use communal showers or wear occlusive footwear. Tinea capitis mainly affects children. Tinea corporis and tinea cruris occur in both sexes and all ages. Nail infections become more common with age and in people with diabetes or poor circulation. In Australia, warm, humid conditions and recreational activities increase exposure.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dermatophytes:} fungi that live on keratin, the protein in skin, hair, and nails; common genera are Trichophyton, Microsporum, and Epidermophyton
    \item \textbf{Transmission:} direct contact with infected people or animals, and indirect contact via towels, floors, shoes, and gym surfaces
    \item \textbf{Risk factors:} warm, moist skin; occlusive footwear; communal bathing; sweating; and impaired immunity
    \item \textbf{Infection mechanism:} the fungus invades the superficial keratin layer, causing inflammation and the characteristic ring-shaped lesions
    \item \textbf{Nail infection:} fungi invade the nail plate, causing thickening and discolouration
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tinea corporis:} ring-shaped, scaly patches with a raised red border and clearer centre, often itchy
    \item \textbf{Tinea pedis:} itchy, scaling, and cracked skin between the toes and on the soles; may blister
    \item \textbf{Tinea cruris:} itchy red rash in the groin, sparing the scrotum, with a well-defined edge
    \item \textbf{Tinea capitis:} scaly patches on the scalp with broken hairs, sometimes with swelling and hair loss
    \item \textbf{Tinea unguium:} thickened, discoloured, and crumbly nails
    \item Itching ranges from mild to intense
    \item Lesions can spread or become multiple
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Eczema and dermatitis, which can mimic ring-shaped rashes
    \item Psoriasis, particularly involving the nails and scalp
    \item Pityriasis rosea and other scaling rashes
    \item Seborrhoeic dermatitis, which can affect the scalp and body
    \item Contact dermatitis and irritant reactions
    \item Other skin infections, including bacterial infections and scabies
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor for persistent, spreading, or multiple skin lesions, for scalp involvement, or for nail infections, which need prescription treatment. People with diabetes, weakened immunity, or symptoms that do not improve with over-the-counter antifungal creams should also seek review. Nail and scalp infections do not respond to simple creams and need specific treatment.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Spreading redness, pain, fever, or oozing, which may indicate secondary bacterial infection
    \item A scalp infection with significant swelling, pain, or fever
    \item Any severe or rapidly worsening skin infection
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clinical examination:} the appearance and location of the lesions often suggest the diagnosis
    \item \textbf{Skin scrapings:} examined under a microscope after treatment with potassium hydroxide
    \item \textbf{Fungal culture:} identifies the specific fungus and confirms the diagnosis
    \item \textbf{Wood's lamp:} some scalp and body fungi fluoresce under ultraviolet light
    \item \textbf{Nail clippings:} for microscopy and culture in nail infections
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} occasionally used to check for underlying conditions in resistant infection
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Topical antifungals:} creams, lotions, and sprays containing terbinafine, clotrimazole, or miconazole for skin infections
    \item \textbf{Duration:} treatment continues for 1--4 weeks and for one week after the rash clears
    \item \textbf{Oral antifungals:} terbinafine, griseofulvin, or itraconazole for scalp, nail, or widespread infections
    \item \textbf{Nail treatment:} long courses of oral antifungal medication, often with topical lacquers
    \item \textbf{Adjunct measures:} keeping the skin dry, wearing loose clothing, and treating contaminated footwear and surfaces
    \item \textbf{Family treatment:} checking and treating household contacts and pets, which can carry the fungus
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} review to confirm the infection has cleared and not recurred
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Secondary bacterial infection of broken skin
    \item Chronic or recurrent infection, especially of the feet and nails
    \item Spread to other body sites or other people
    \item Permanent nail damage or scalp scarring in severe cases
    \item Cellulitis of the lower leg in people with athlete's foot and poor circulation
    \item Side effects of oral antifungal medication, which are uncommon
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Skin dermatophyte infections respond well to antifungal treatment and usually clear completely within weeks. Tinea capitis and nail infections take longer, with nails needing several months of treatment, and recurrence is possible. Good hygiene and prevention reduce recurrence. With correct treatment, the vast majority of dermatophyte infections resolve fully without long-term effects.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Keep skin clean and dry, especially between the toes and in skin folds
    \item Wear shower shoes in communal showers and pools
    \item Change socks daily and alternate shoes to let them dry
    \item Do not share towels, clothing, or grooming items
    \item Wear loose, breathable clothing
    \item Treat infected pets and household members
    \item Complete the full course of antifungal treatment
    \item Keep nails short and avoid sharing nail clippers
\end{itemize}
