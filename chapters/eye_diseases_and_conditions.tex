\chapter{Eye Diseases and Conditions}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Eye diseases and conditions are the many disorders that affect the eye and vision. They range from common conditions such as refractive error, dry eye, and conjunctivitis to serious diseases including cataract, glaucoma, age-related macular degeneration, and diabetic retinopathy. Some eye conditions cause gradual, painless vision loss, which makes regular eye examinations essential, while others cause sudden symptoms requiring urgent care. This chapter provides an overview of the main types of eye disease, their effects, and the importance of early detection.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Eye disease is common and increases with age. Cataract is the leading cause of treatable vision loss worldwide, glaucoma and macular degeneration are leading causes of irreversible vision loss in older people, and diabetic retinopathy affects many people with diabetes. Refractive error affects a large proportion of the population. A significant amount of vision loss is avoidable with early detection and treatment.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Eye disease arises from many mechanisms.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Refractive error:} the shape of the eye prevents light focusing on the retina, causing blur
    \item \textbf{Cataract:} clouding of the lens with age
    \item \textbf{Glaucoma:} damage to the optic nerve, often from raised pressure in the eye
    \item \textbf{Macular degeneration:} damage to the central retina with age
    \item \textbf{Diabetic retinopathy:} damage to the retinal blood vessels from diabetes
    \item \textbf{Infections and inflammation:} conjunctivitis, keratitis, and uveitis
    \item \textbf{Dry eye:} problems with the tear film
    \item \textbf{Retinal detachment:} separation of the retina from the back of the eye, an emergency
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Symptoms vary widely.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Blurred or reduced vision
    \item Gradual, painless vision loss in cataract and macular degeneration
    \item Eye pain, redness, and discharge in infections
    \item Halos around lights and loss of peripheral vision in glaucoma
    \item Floaters, flashes, and a curtain over the vision in retinal detachment
    \item Itching and watering in allergy and dry eye
    \item A sudden loss of vision, which is an emergency
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Eye symptoms overlap across conditions.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Redness and discharge: infection, allergy, or dry eye
    \item Vision loss: cataract, glaucoma, diabetes, or stroke
    \item Floaters: benign ageing changes or retinal detachment
    \item Eye pain: infection, glaucoma, or referred pain
    \item Assessment by an eye professional distinguishes the causes
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Regular eye examinations detect disease early, and any new or sudden symptom requires assessment. Children, people with diabetes, and older adults need regular checks.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden loss of vision
    \item Sudden severe eye pain
    \item A red eye with severe pain and nausea
    \item Sudden floaters with flashes, which may indicate retinal detachment
    \item A chemical injury or penetrating injury to the eye
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Eye disease is diagnosed with examination and tests.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Visual acuity and refraction:} measuring vision and prescription
    \item \textbf{Slit-lamp examination:} the front of the eye
    \item \textbf{Pressure measurement:} for glaucoma
    \item \textbf{Dilated eye examination:} the retina and optic nerve
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} OCT and retinal photography
    \item \textbf{Visual field testing:} for glaucoma
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management depends on the condition.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Refractive error:} glasses and contact lenses
    \item \textbf{Cataract:} surgery, which is highly effective
    \item \textbf{Glaucoma:} eye drops, laser, and surgery to protect the optic nerve
    \item \textbf{Macular degeneration:} injections, laser, and lifestyle measures
    \item \textbf{Diabetic retinopathy:} blood sugar control, laser, and injections
    \item \textbf{Infections:} antibiotics and antivirals
    \item \textbf{Retinal detachment:} urgent surgery
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Irreversible vision loss in glaucoma, macular degeneration, and diabetic retinopathy
    \item Blindness from untreated infection or detachment
    \item Falls and injury from poor vision
    \item Reduced independence and quality of life
    \item The emotional impact of vision loss
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Many eye diseases are treatable, and vision loss is often preventable with early detection. Cataract surgery restores vision in the vast majority of cases, and modern treatments slow or halt glaucoma, macular degeneration, and diabetic retinopathy. Regular examinations and prompt attention to symptoms give the best outcomes.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Have regular eye examinations at every age
    \item Manage diabetes, blood pressure, and weight
    \item Do not smoke
    \item Protect eyes from sun and injury
    \item Eat a diet rich in fruit and vegetables
    \item Report any vision change promptly
    \item Use low-vision support if vision loss occurs
\end{itemize}
