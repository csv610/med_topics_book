\chapter{Malnutrition}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Malnutrition is a complex, multifactorial clinical state resulting from a deficiency, excess, or imbalance of energy, protein, and other nutrients. While the term encompasses both undernutrition (wasting, stunting, underweight, and micronutrient deficiencies) and overnutrition (obesity and diet-related non-communicable diseases), clinical discussions of malnutrition in medical settings primarily focus on undernutrition. In this context, malnutrition is characterised by a lack of nutrient intake or absorption that leads to altered body composition (decreased fat-free mass and body cell mass), resulting in diminished physical and mental function and impaired clinical outcomes from disease.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Malnutrition is a global health concern, affecting millions of people across all age groups and socioeconomic backgrounds. In developing countries, undernutrition is a major driver of paediatric mortality, contributing to nearly 45\% of deaths in children under five. In developed nations like Australia, undernutrition is highly prevalent in specific clinical and social cohorts. Approximately 20\% to 40\% of patients admitted to acute care hospitals are malnourished or at high risk of malnutrition. Furthermore, it affects up to 50\% of residents in aged care facilities. It is also highly prevalent among older individuals living alone, those with chronic illnesses, and individuals in remote or socioeconomically disadvantaged communities.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The aetiology of malnutrition can be classified into disease-related (with or without inflammation) and social or environmental factors, all of which disrupt nutritional homeostasis:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Inadequate nutritional intake:} Insufficient food consumption due to poor appetite, dental problems, dysphagia, or severe cognitive impairment (such as advanced dementia).
    \item \textbf{Malabsorption and digestive disorders:} Gastrointestinal pathologies including coeliac disease, Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, pancreatic insufficiency, or short bowel syndrome that impair nutrient absorption.
    \item \textbf{Disease-associated hypermetabolism:} Chronic inflammatory conditions, advanced cancer (causing cachexia), severe infections (such as tuberculosis or HIV), major burns, or trauma that increase basal energy expenditure and nitrogen loss.
    \item \textbf{Psychosocial and economic factors:} Poverty, food insecurity, social isolation, severe depression, or substance abuse that limit access to and preparation of nutritious foods.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
The clinical presentation of malnutrition varies based on severity, duration, and underlying causes, but typical features include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Unintentional weight loss:} Rapid, unexplained weight loss, commonly defined as losing more than 5\% of body weight in 3 months or more than 10\% in 6 months.
    \item \textbf{Muscle wasting and fat loss:} Prominent temporal wasting, hollow cheeks, visible clavicles, and marked reduction in subcutaneous fat stores (e.g., in the triceps and thoracic regions).
    \item \textbf{Asthenia and muscle weakness:} Severe fatigue, generalised weakness, and reduced handgrip strength, leading to decreased mobility and functional capacity.
    \item \textbf{Impaired wound healing:} Delayed healing of surgical incisions or minor cuts, and a high susceptibility to developing pressure injuries.
    \item \textbf{Immunological compromise:} Recurrent or prolonged infections, particularly respiratory and urinary tract infections, due to impaired cellular immunity.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
When evaluating a patient with signs of malnutrition, other underlying medical conditions causing weight loss and wasting must be ruled out:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Endocrine disorders:} Uncontrolled diabetes mellitus, hyperthyroidism (thyrotoxicosis), or Addison's disease, which cause weight loss despite normal or increased dietary intake.
    \item \textbf{Occult malignancies:} Gastrointestinal, haematological, or solid tumours that induce a pro-inflammatory state and metabolic changes leading to cancer cachexia.
    \item \textbf{Primary eating disorders:} Conditions such as anorexia nervosa or bulimia nervosa, where severe undernutrition is driven by psychiatric pathology.
    \item \textbf{Major depressive disorder:} Severe depression that presents with profound anorexia, loss of interest in eating, and subsequent rapid weight loss.
    \item \textbf{Chronic infectious diseases:} Conditions such as active tuberculosis or HIV/AIDS that present with chronic wasting syndrome.
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Individuals, family members, or caregivers should seek medical advice if they notice persistent loss of appetite, unintentional weight loss, or progressive weakness.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Severe dehydration and shock:} Confusion, extreme lethargy, rapid heart rate, low blood pressure, and cold, clammy skin.
    \item \textbf{Signs of refeeding syndrome:} Severe muscle weakness, shortness of breath, confusion, or irregular heartbeats (arrhythmias) occurring shortly after reintroducing food.
    \item \textbf{Sepsis:} High fever or hypothermia, rapid breathing, tachycardia, and acute confusion in a severely malnourished patient.
    \item \textbf{Altered consciousness:} Loss of consciousness, stupor, or extreme drowsiness where the patient cannot be easily awakened.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
A definitive diagnosis is based on validated screening tools, anthropometrics, and laboratory tests to identify nutrient deficiencies and underlying causes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Nutritional screening tools:} Routine clinical assessment using validated tools such as the Malnutrition Universal Screening Tool (MUST) or the Subjective Global Assessment (SGA).
    \item \textbf{Anthropometric measurements:} Serial tracking of body weight, height, body mass index (BMI), mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC), and skinfold thickness.
    \item \textbf{Haematology and biochemistry:} Full blood count to assess for microcytic or macrocytic anaemia, renal function, liver function, and serum electrolytes.
    \item \textbf{Micronutrient assays:} Checking blood levels of iron, vitamin B12, folate, vitamin D, zinc, selenium, and copper.
    \item \textbf{Inflammatory biomarkers:} C-reactive protein (CRP) and erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) to determine if malnutrition is driven by an underlying inflammatory process.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management is multi-disciplinary, involving dietitians, doctors, nurses, and speech pathologists to safely restore nutritional status and treat underlying diseases:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dietary modifications:} Developing high-protein, high-energy meal plans with small, frequent portions, and modifying food textures for patients with swallowing difficulties.
    \item \textbf{Oral nutritional supplements (ONS):} Prescribing nutrient-dense, clinically formulated liquid supplements to be consumed between meals.
    \item \textbf{Enteral nutrition:} Initiating tube feeding via a nasogastric (NG) tube or percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy (PEG) tube when oral intake is unsafe or insufficient.
    \item \textbf{Parenteral nutrition:} Administering intravenous nutrition via a central or peripheral venous catheter when the gastrointestinal tract is non-functional or inaccessible.
    \item \textbf{Electrolyte and vitamin replacement:} Slowly correcting electrolyte deficits and administering vitamins (especially thiamine and B-complex vitamins) before reintroducing nutrition to prevent refeeding syndrome.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
Untreated malnutrition has severe consequences across multiple organ systems:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sarcopenia and functional decline:} Loss of muscle mass and strength, leading to a high risk of falls, hip fractures, and loss of physical independence.
    \item \textbf{Refeeding syndrome:} A life-threatening condition characterised by severe fluid and electrolyte shifts (particularly hypophosphataemia, hypokalaemia, and hypomagnesaemia) that occurs when nutrition is reintroduced too rapidly.
    \item \textbf{Impaired respiratory function:} Atrophy of diaphragmatic and intercostal muscles, leading to reduced vital capacity and an increased risk of respiratory failure.
    \item \textbf{Decubitus ulcers:} Development of severe pressure injuries due to thin skin, reduced subcutaneous cushioning, and poor tissue repair.
    \item \textbf{Neurological disorders:} Cognitive impairment, confusion, and peripheral neuropathy due to deficiencies in thiamine (B1), B12, and other essential vitamins.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The prognosis of malnutrition depends largely on its severity, its underlying cause, and the promptness of intervention. When identified early and managed with appropriate nutritional support, patients demonstrate significant improvements in muscle strength, wound healing, immune function, and quality of life. In hospital settings, nutritional intervention reduces complications, shortens length of stay, and lowers readmission rates. However, in patients with advanced, terminal illnesses (such as end-stage malignancy), malnutrition (cachexia) is often refractory to standard nutritional therapies and is associated with poor outcomes.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
Preventative strategies focus on early identification, dietary fortification, and maintaining functional independence:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Regular monitoring and screening:} Routine nutritional assessments during GP visits, hospital admissions, and entry into residential care.
    \item \textbf{Food fortification:} Enhancing the energy and protein density of regular foods by adding milk powder, cheese, cream, butter, or eggs.
    \item \textbf{Social and physical support:} Promoting communal dining to encourage eating, and providing assistive cutlery or feeding assistance for those with physical or cognitive barriers.
    \item \textbf{Adequate hydration:} Ensuring consistent fluid intake, as dehydration can suppress appetite and worsen fatigue.
\end{itemize}
