\chapter{Placenta praevia}
\index{Placenta praevia}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; seek urgent maternity advice for bleeding during pregnancy.}

\section*{What it is}
Placenta praevia occurs when the placenta lies low in the uterus and partly or completely covers the cervix, the opening through which the baby is born. A low placenta found earlier in pregnancy may move away from the cervix as the uterus grows; follow-up imaging determines the position later in pregnancy.

\section*{Symptoms and assessment}
The typical symptom is painless, bright-red vaginal bleeding in the second half of pregnancy, although bleeding can be heavy and pain does not exclude another serious placental problem. Ultrasound, including a transvaginal scan when needed, locates the placenta safely. Tell the maternity team about every episode of bleeding, pain, contractions, fluid loss, or reduced fetal movement.

\section*{Management}
Care depends on bleeding, gestational age, placental position, fetal wellbeing, and other risks. It may include observation, hospital care, blood tests, medicines to support fetal maturity when indicated, and planning the timing and mode of birth. Do not insert anything into the vagina or have sex if the maternity team has advised pelvic precautions.

\section*{When to seek emergency care}
Contact the maternity unit or local emergency service immediately for any vaginal bleeding in later pregnancy, especially heavy bleeding, dizziness, fainting, abdominal pain, contractions, reduced fetal movement, or feeling unwell. Do not drive yourself if bleeding is heavy or you feel faint.
