\chapter{Myocarditis}
\index{Myocarditis}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; seek professional advice for individual care.}

\section*{What it is}
Myocarditis is inflammation of the heart muscle. It can follow an infection or result from an immune reaction, medicine, toxin, or another condition. Inflammation can reduce the heart’s pumping ability or disturb its electrical rhythm.

\section*{Symptoms}
Symptoms may include chest pain, shortness of breath, tiredness, reduced exercise tolerance, a fast or irregular heartbeat, light-headedness, fainting, or flu-like symptoms. Some people have few symptoms, so a recent illness does not by itself confirm or exclude myocarditis.

\section*{Diagnosis}
Assessment may include an examination, electrocardiogram, blood tests including cardiac injury markers, chest imaging, echocardiography, and cardiac MRI. Further tests, including coronary assessment or biopsy, are used selectively. Other urgent causes of chest pain and breathlessness must also be considered.

\section*{Treatment and recovery}
Treatment depends on severity and cause. It may include rest, medicines for heart failure or rhythm problems, treatment of an identified cause, and hospital or intensive-care support in severe cases. Avoid strenuous exercise and competitive sport until a cardiology team confirms that it is safe to return. Follow-up is important because heart function and rhythm can change after the acute illness.

\section*{When to seek urgent care}
Seek emergency help for unexplained chest pain, severe or worsening breathlessness, fainting, sustained palpitations, confusion, collapse, or rapidly worsening swelling or fatigue.
