\chapter{Arthroscopy of the shoulder}

\section*{Overview}
Arthroscopy of the shoulder is a surgical procedure used to diagnose or treat a medical condition. Surgery may be recommended when other treatments have not worked, when a structural problem needs to be corrected, or to remove diseased tissue. Surgical procedures are performed in hospitals by qualified surgeons and anaesthetists and may be carried out as day surgery or with a hospital stay, depending on the type and complexity of the operation.

\section*{Symptoms}
After surgery it is common to experience:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Pain, swelling, or bruising around the wound
    \item Tiredness and reduced energy for several days
    \item A mild fever in the first 24--48 hours
    \item Reduced appetite and changes in bowel function
    \item Some clear or blood-tinged fluid from the wound
\end{itemize}

\section*{Causes}
Surgery is recommended for reasons such as:
\begin{itemize}
    \item To remove a diseased or damaged structure
    \item To repair or reconstruct a structure after injury or disease
    \item To relieve symptoms such as pain or obstruction
    \item To diagnose a condition through biopsy or direct inspection
    \item To implant or replace a device, such as a joint or a stent
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis}
Before surgery the surgical team usually:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Takes a full medical history and examines you
    \item Orders blood tests, heart tracings (ECG), and other tests as needed
    \item Arranges imaging such as X-ray, ultrasound, CT, or MRI
    \item Reviews current medicines and allergies
    \item Discusses the benefits, risks, and alternatives, and seeks consent
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Anaesthesia: local, regional (such as spinal or epidural), or general, depending on the procedure
    \item The operation itself, performed using open or keyhole (minimally invasive) techniques
    \item Pain control with medicines such as paracetamol, anti-inflammatories, or stronger pain relievers
    \item Wound care with stitches, clips, or dressings as appropriate
    \item Rehabilitation, including guided movement, exercises, and physiotherapy
    \item Follow-up review to check healing and remove stitches or dressings
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prevention}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Stop smoking well before the operation to reduce anaesthetic and wound complications
    \item Maintain a healthy weight and manage chronic conditions such as diabetes
    \item Follow pre-operative instructions about fasting and medicines
    \item Arrange help at home for the recovery period
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
After surgery contact the surgical team or seek urgent care if any of the following occur:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe or worsening pain not helped by prescribed medicines
    \item Heavy bleeding from the wound
    \item Spreading redness, warmth, swelling, or pus (signs of infection)
    \item Fever above 38\textdegree{}C that does not settle
    \item Chest pain, shortness of breath, or calf pain and swelling (possible clot)
    \item Inability to pass urine or open the bowels
    \item Any concern that the wound is not healing
\end{itemize}
