\chapter{Prednisolone}
\index{Prednisolone}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; take only as prescribed.}

\section*{What it is}
Prednisolone is a corticosteroid medicine that reduces inflammation and suppresses immune activity. It is used for many conditions, and the dose and duration vary widely. Benefits must be balanced against infection, metabolic, bone, eye, mood, and adrenal risks.

\section*{Safe use}
Take it exactly as prescribed, usually with food if advised. Do not share it, double a missed dose, or change the dose without advice. After more than a short course or with a high dose, stopping suddenly can cause withdrawal or adrenal insufficiency; follow the tapering plan. Carry medicine information and ask what to do during serious illness, surgery, or vomiting.

\section*{Possible effects}
Short-term effects may include indigestion, sleep disturbance, increased appetite, fluid retention, mood change, and higher blood glucose. Longer or higher-dose treatment can increase infection risk, weaken bones, raise blood pressure, affect the eyes, slow growth in children, and suppress adrenal function. The prescribing team may monitor these risks.

\section*{When to seek urgent care}
Seek urgent help for severe infection symptoms, confusion, fainting, severe vomiting, black stools or vomiting blood, severe breathlessness, vision changes, marked weakness, or thoughts of self-harm. Contact the prescriber promptly for fever, persistent sore throat, unusual bruising, or significant mood changes.
