\chapter{Drug and Alcohol Rehabilitation}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Drug and alcohol rehabilitation, often called rehab, is a structured treatment program that helps people stop or reduce harmful substance use and rebuild their lives. Rehab programs address the physical, psychological, and social aspects of addiction and may include medical withdrawal support (detox), counselling, group therapy, education, and relapse-prevention skills. Programs range from outpatient counselling to residential rehabilitation, where people live at a facility for weeks or months. Rehabilitation is effective: with the right program and support, many people achieve lasting recovery. This chapter explains the types of rehab, what to expect, and how to access help.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Alcohol and drug problems affect a substantial proportion of Australians: around one in five adults drinks at risky levels, and hundreds of thousands use illicit drugs. Many people who need treatment do not access it, due to stigma, cost, waiting lists, and lack of services, especially in rural areas. Residential rehabilitation services have long waiting lists in Australia. Rates of recovery are improved when treatment is matched to the person's needs and continued with aftercare.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Addiction:} a chronic brain disorder in which substance use becomes compulsive despite harm
    \item \textbf{Physical dependence:} the body adapts to the substance, causing withdrawal when use stops
    \item \textbf{Withdrawal (detox):} medically managed withdrawal reduces symptoms and risks
    \item \textbf{Psychological factors:} counselling addresses the thoughts, feelings, and habits that drive use
    \item \textbf{Social factors:} housing, relationships, employment, and support networks are part of recovery
    \item \textbf{Relapse prevention:} skills and plans to maintain recovery after treatment
    \item \textbf{Aftercare:} ongoing support reduces the risk of relapse
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Rehab programs vary in intensity, duration, and approach
    \item Outpatient programs: regular counselling and groups while living at home
    \item Residential programs: live-in treatment over weeks to months
    \item Day programs: structured treatment during the day with nights at home
    \item Detoxification: medical supervision of withdrawal
    \item Therapy: individual and group counselling, cognitive-behavioural therapy, and family support
    \item Aftercare: follow-up, relapse prevention, and peer support
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Detoxification alone versus full rehabilitation, which includes therapy and aftercare
    \item Outpatient versus residential rehabilitation, matched to severity and circumstances
    \item Different therapy approaches, which suit different people
    \item Medically assisted treatment, such as methadone or buprenorphine for opioids, versus abstinence-based programs
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Anyone with a drug or alcohol problem should seek help early, as addiction is a treatable medical condition. A GP can assess, provide treatment, and refer to specialist services. People stopping alcohol, benzodiazepines, or heavy opioid use should seek medically supervised withdrawal.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Overdose or severe intoxication, with unconsciousness or breathing difficulty
    \item Severe withdrawal with seizures, confusion, or hallucinations
    \item Thoughts of self-harm or suicide
    \item Any crisis in which the person is at risk
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Support lines:} Alcohol and Drug Information Service (1800 250 015) and Lifeline (13 11 14).

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Assessment:} the substance, pattern of use, and its effects
    \item \textbf{Medical assessment:} physical and mental health
    \item \textbf{Assessment of dependence:} withdrawal symptoms, tolerance, and loss of control
    \item \textbf{Matching treatment:} the appropriate program and intensity for the person
    \item \textbf{Planning:} goals, detox needs, and aftercare arrangements
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Detoxification:} medically supervised withdrawal with symptom relief
    \item \textbf{Counselling:} individual therapy, including cognitive-behavioural therapy and motivational interviewing
    \item \textbf{Group programs:} relapse prevention, coping skills, and peer support
    \item \textbf{Family involvement:} family therapy and support for loved ones
    \item \textbf{Medication:} medicines to reduce cravings or block effects, such as for alcohol and opioid dependence
    \item \textbf{Residential rehabilitation:} structured live-in programs for more intensive treatment
    \item \textbf{Aftercare:} ongoing support, including 12-step groups and community services
    \item \textbf{Practical support:} housing, employment, and legal assistance
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Relapse, which is common and part of the recovery process
    \item Withdrawal complications without medical supervision
    \item Overdose during or after treatment when tolerance has fallen
    \item Underlying mental health problems
    \item Family breakdown, job loss, and financial problems
    \item Stigma and difficulty accessing services
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Rehabilitation works: many people who complete treatment achieve lasting recovery, and relapse is a normal part of recovery rather than a failure. Outcomes improve with treatment matched to the person, completed programs, and strong aftercare. Detoxification alone has high relapse rates, so ongoing treatment matters. With good support, most people can rebuild their health, relationships, and lives.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Seek help early rather than waiting for a crisis
    \item Talk to a GP about treatment options and referrals
    \item Ask about residential programs and waiting lists early
    \item Involve family and friends in the recovery process
    \item Attend aftercare and support groups after the program
    \item Plan for triggers and have a relapse-prevention plan
    \item Address underlying mental health and practical problems
    \item Be patient: recovery is a process, not an event
\end{itemize}
