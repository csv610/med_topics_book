\chapter{Encephalitis}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Encephalitis is inflammation of the brain, most often caused by a viral infection, although bacteria, fungi, and autoimmune conditions can also cause it. The inflammation disrupts brain function, causing fever, headache, confusion, seizures, and changes in behaviour or consciousness. Encephalitis is a serious and potentially life-threatening condition that requires urgent medical care, but many people make a good recovery with prompt treatment. This chapter explains the causes, symptoms, diagnosis, and treatment of encephalitis.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Encephalitis is rare but serious, affecting a small number of people per 100,000 each year. It can affect people of any age, with some viruses, such as herpes simplex, causing cases across all ages, while others, such as the mosquito-borne viruses found in Australia, including Murray Valley encephalitis and Japanese encephalitis, occur in specific regions and seasons. Vaccines and mosquito control have reduced the burden of some forms.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Encephalitis arises when an infectious agent or the immune system causes inflammation within the brain.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Herpes simplex virus:} the most common cause of sporadic viral encephalitis in Australia, and the most important to treat early
    \item \textbf{Other viruses:} varicella-zoster, enteroviruses, and Epstein-Barr virus
    \item \textbf{Mosquito-borne viruses:} Japanese encephalitis, Murray Valley encephalitis, and Kunjin virus, which occur in Australia
    \item \textbf{Autoimmune encephalitis:} the immune system attacks the brain, sometimes after an infection or cancer, including anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis
    \item \textbf{Mechanism:} infection or inflammation causes swelling and damage to brain tissue, disrupting function
    \item \textbf{Effects:} inflammation raises pressure inside the skull and damages nerve cells
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Symptoms develop over days and progress.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Fever and severe headache
    \item Confusion, disorientation, and altered behaviour
    \item Seizures or fits
    \item Drowsiness, progressing to reduced consciousness and coma
    \item Sensitivity to light and neck stiffness
    \item Weakness, paralysis, or difficulty speaking
    \item Personality changes and hallucinations
    \item In children: irritability, poor feeding, and vomiting
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Other conditions cause similar symptoms and must be distinguished.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Meningitis, infection of the membranes around the brain, which often occurs with encephalitis
    \item Brain abscess and other focal infections
    \item Stroke and brain tumours
    \item Severe migraine with neurological symptoms
    \item Sepsis and other systemic infections causing confusion
    \item Metabolic and toxic encephalopathies
    \item Psychiatric conditions, which can mimic early encephalitis
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Encephalitis is a medical emergency. Anyone with fever and confusion, seizures, severe headache, or altered behaviour should be taken to an emergency department immediately.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Confusion, drowsiness, or reduced consciousness
    \item Seizures or fits
    \item Severe headache with neck stiffness and fever
    \item Weakness, paralysis, or difficulty speaking
    \item Sudden behavioural change or hallucinations
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Diagnosis is urgent and uses several tests.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Lumbar puncture:} analysis of the cerebrospinal fluid, the key test, which shows inflammation and identifies the cause
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} full blood count, inflammatory markers, and specific virus tests
    \item \textbf{MRI of the brain:} shows areas of inflammation and rules out other causes
    \item \textbf{EEG:} records brain activity and can support the diagnosis
    \item \textbf{Throat and nasal swabs:} for virus identification
    \item \textbf{Treatment on suspicion:} antiviral treatment for herpes encephalitis is started immediately, without waiting for confirmation
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Treatment is urgent and supportive.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Antiviral medicines:} aciclovir for herpes simplex encephalitis, started as early as possible
    \item \textbf{Antibiotics:} for bacterial causes, while results are pending
    \item \textbf{Immunotherapy:} corticosteroids, plasma exchange, or immune therapies for autoimmune encephalitis
    \item \textbf{Supportive care:} intensive care for seizures, raised pressure, and breathing support
    \item \textbf{Seizure treatment:} anti-epileptic medicines
    \item \textbf{Rehabilitation:} physiotherapy, speech therapy, and cognitive rehabilitation after the acute illness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Permanent brain damage, including memory loss and cognitive impairment
    \item Epilepsy after recovery
    \item Weakness, paralysis, and movement disorders
    \item Speech and language problems
    \item Behavioural and personality changes
    \item In severe cases, coma and death
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The outlook depends on the cause, how quickly treatment starts, and the person's age and health. With early antiviral treatment, most people with herpes encephalitis survive, although a significant proportion are left with some lasting effects. Autoimmune encephalitis responds well to immunotherapy in many cases. Full recovery takes time, and rehabilitation is important.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Keep immunisations up to date, including Japanese encephalitis vaccine for travel to affected regions
    \item Prevent mosquito bites in affected areas with repellent, long clothing, and nets
    \item Seek urgent care for fever with confusion, seizures, or severe headache
    \item Follow the rehabilitation programme after recovery
    \item Attend follow-up for seizures or other lasting problems
\end{itemize}
