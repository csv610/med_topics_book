\chapter{Getting the Most Out of Your Pharmacist}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Community pharmacists are highly trained health professionals who are the most accessible members of the health care team. In Australia, pharmacists must complete a university degree and internship, and many hold additional qualifications in areas such as diabetes, mental health, and immunisation. Pharmacists dispense medicines prescribed by doctors, but they do much more: they provide advice on over-the-counter medicines and minor ailments, check for interactions and safety problems, administer vaccinations, help manage chronic conditions, and guide people to the right health services. Getting the most out of your pharmacist means using their expertise at every stage of your health care, from prevention and self-care to the safe use of prescription medicines.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Community pharmacies are the most frequently visited health services in Australia, with most people visiting a pharmacy several times a year. Around 90\% of Australians live within 2.5 km of a community pharmacy. Pharmacists now deliver a growing range of services beyond dispensing, including vaccinations (with many pharmacists trained to administer influenza, COVID-19, and travel vaccines), medication reviews, and health screening such as blood pressure and blood glucose checks. Medicines problems are a major source of harm in health care, and pharmacists are central to preventing medication errors and adverse reactions through their checks and advice.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
This chapter concerns using a health service effectively rather than a disease process. The ways pharmacists help include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Medicine safety:} checking prescriptions for interactions, incorrect doses, duplication, and allergies
    \item \textbf{Medicine information:} explaining how to take medicines, when to take them, and what side effects to expect
    \item \textbf{Minor ailment care:} recommending over-the-counter treatments for common problems such as colds, pain, skin conditions, and allergies
    \item \textbf{Screening and referral:} identifying when a symptom needs a doctor and guiding people to appropriate care
    \item \textbf{Chronic disease support:} monitoring blood pressure, blood glucose, asthma, and cholesterol, and supporting adherence
    \item \textbf{Prevention:} vaccinations, smoking cessation support, and healthy lifestyle advice
    \item \textbf{Medicine reviews:} structured reviews of all medicines, especially for older people taking many medicines
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Situations where seeing your pharmacist can genuinely help include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item A new prescription you do not fully understand
    \item Questions about how or when to take a medicine
    \item Mild symptoms such as coughs, colds, hay fever, mild pain, or minor wounds
    \item Choosing between over-the-counter products
    \item Concerns that a medicine is causing side effects
    \item Forgetting doses or difficulty managing many medicines
    \item Needing a repeat prescription when you cannot get a GP appointment
    \item Wanting a vaccination, a blood pressure check, or advice on quitting smoking
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
A pharmacist's role includes deciding when symptoms need a doctor rather than self-care:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Chest pain, breathlessness, or fainting, which need urgent medical care, not pharmacy advice
    \item Severe, persistent, or worsening symptoms that require medical assessment
    \item Symptoms in young babies, older people, or pregnant women that may need a doctor's review
    \item Blood in stools or urine, unexplained weight loss, or other red-flag symptoms
    \item Symptoms that fail to improve with self-care within a few days
    \item Any condition already under medical care where the pharmacist's advice should complement, not replace, the doctor's plan
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Pharmacists can help with many problems, but some situations require a doctor. Seek medical care for persistent or severe symptoms, symptoms with alarm features, or conditions not responding to appropriate self-care. For emergencies, do not go to a pharmacy.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chest pain, severe breathlessness, sudden weakness, or collapse
    \item Severe bleeding, major trauma, or suspected stroke
    \item Difficulty breathing, especially in a person with anaphylaxis
    \item Any sudden, severe symptom that worries you
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Pharmacists do not diagnose in the way doctors do, but they assess and advise within their scope:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Assessment:} taking a brief history of the symptom, including how long it has been present and any red flags
    \item \textbf{Medicine review:} checking all current medicines for interactions with a proposed treatment
    \item \textbf{Health checks:} blood pressure, blood glucose, cholesterol, and BMI measurements in many pharmacies
    \item \textbf{Medication management reviews:} comprehensive reviews arranged through GPs for people taking many medicines, funded by the government for eligible people
    \item \textbf{Referral:} recommending a GP appointment, nurse, or emergency care when needed
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
To get the most from your pharmacist:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Use one pharmacy:} having all your medicines dispensed at one pharmacy builds a complete record and improves safety checks
    \item \textbf{Ask questions:} ask about what the medicine is for, how to take it, what to avoid, and possible side effects
    \item \textbf{Bring a list:} carry an up-to-date list of all medicines, including over-the-counter and complementary products
    \item \textbf{Request a review:} ask for a medication review if you take many medicines or feel confused about them
    \item \textbf{Use their services:} take advantage of vaccinations, health checks, and disease management services
    \item \textbf{Ask about cheaper options:} pharmacists can advise about generic medicines and PBS concessions
    \item \textbf{Be honest:} tell the pharmacist about other medicines, allergies, pregnancy, and health conditions
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
Common problems when people do not use pharmacy services well include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Medicine interactions when over-the-counter products are bought without checking
    \item Duplicate medicines when more than one product contains the same active ingredient
    \item Missed or incorrect doses from not understanding instructions
    \item Side effects that are reported late because people do not know what to expect
    \item Delayed treatment of serious symptoms that a pharmacist would have referred
    \item Unnecessary spending on products that are not needed or are less effective
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
People who engage with their pharmacist have better medication safety and adherence, and many minor ailments are resolved without a GP visit, reducing pressure on the health system. Pharmacy-delivered services, from immunisation to chronic disease support, are associated with improved health outcomes. Building a relationship with your pharmacist, like with your GP, pays dividends across a lifetime of health care.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Keep a current written list of all medicines and allergies and share it with every health professional
    \item Use the same pharmacy for all prescriptions
    \item Ask your pharmacist about vaccinations and preventive health checks
    \item Store medicines correctly, away from heat and moisture, and check expiry dates
    \item Dispose of unwanted medicines at a pharmacy's safe disposal bin
    \item Never share prescription medicines with others
    \item Ask before buying anything that could interact with your existing medicines
    \item If symptoms do not improve, see your GP rather than buying more products
\end{itemize}
