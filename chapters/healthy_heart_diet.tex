\chapter{Healthy Heart Diet}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
A healthy heart diet is an eating pattern that reduces the risk of heart disease and stroke by supporting healthy blood pressure, cholesterol, weight, and blood glucose. It is based on plenty of vegetables and fruit, whole grains, legumes, nuts, fish, and lean protein, with limited saturated fat, added sugar, salt, and processed foods. This pattern is sometimes described as a Mediterranean-style or DASH-style diet, and it is endorsed by the Heart Foundation and major cardiology organisations. A healthy heart diet is not a short-term diet but a sustainable way of eating that benefits the heart at any age.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Heart disease is the leading cause of death in Australia, and diet is one of its most important modifiable risk factors. High blood pressure, high cholesterol, obesity, and type 2 diabetes are all strongly influenced by diet. Around half of Australian adults have high blood pressure or elevated cholesterol, and poor diet contributes to these. Studies show that a heart-healthy diet can reduce the risk of heart attacks and strokes by 20--30\% or more, and improvements in diet at any age produce benefits.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
A healthy heart diet reduces cardiovascular risk through several mechanisms.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Lowering blood pressure:} reducing salt and eating potassium-rich foods lowers blood pressure
    \item \textbf{Lowering cholesterol:} replacing saturated and trans fats with unsaturated fats improves cholesterol levels
    \item \textbf{Weight management:} a diet high in fibre and lower in energy supports a healthy weight
    \item \textbf{Blood glucose control:} whole grains and fibre slow glucose absorption
    \item \textbf{Reducing inflammation:} plant foods, fish, and nuts reduce inflammation
    \item \textbf{Protecting blood vessels:} antioxidants and plant compounds protect the artery lining
    \item \textbf{Saturated fat:} found in fatty meat, butter, and processed foods, raises LDL ("bad") cholesterol
    \item \textbf{Salt:} raises blood pressure
    \item \textbf{Added sugar:} contributes to weight gain and diabetes risk
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
A heart-healthy eating pattern includes:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Plenty of vegetables and fruit, at least five serves of vegetables and two of fruit daily
    \item Whole grains instead of refined grains
    \item Fish, especially oily fish, two or more times a week
    \item Legumes, nuts, and seeds
    \item Lean meat and poultry in moderation
    \item Low-fat dairy or alternatives
    \item Unsaturated oils, such as olive oil
    \item Very limited processed foods, takeaways, and sugary drinks
    \item Low salt intake
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Dietary advice is individualised to health conditions.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Diabetes, which requires attention to carbohydrate and glucose control
    \item Kidney disease, which affects protein, potassium, and phosphate
    \item High blood pressure, which emphasises salt reduction
    \item High cholesterol, which emphasises fat quality
    \item Heart failure, which may require fluid and salt limits
    \item Food allergies and intolerances
    \item Cultural and personal preferences
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor for heart disease risk assessment, including blood pressure and cholesterol, and ask about a heart-healthy diet as part of your plan. An accredited practising dietitian can provide individualised advice.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chest pain, pressure, or tightness
    \item Breathlessness, especially with dizziness or collapse
    \item Symptoms of stroke, including facial drooping, arm weakness, or difficulty speaking
    \item Palpitations with fainting
    \item Any medical emergency related to the heart
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Dietary advice is guided by cardiovascular risk assessment.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Blood pressure measurement}
    \item \textbf{Cholesterol testing:} LDL, HDL, and triglycerides
    \item \textbf{Blood glucose and HbA1c:} diabetes risk assessment
    \item \textbf{Weight and waist measurement}
    \item \textbf{Absolute cardiovascular risk:} calculated from age, blood pressure, cholesterol, and other factors
    \item \textbf{Dietary review:} identifying areas for improvement
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} repeat measurements track progress
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
A heart-healthy diet is built with practical, sustainable changes.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Increase vegetables and fruit:} fill half the plate at each meal
    \item \textbf{Choose whole grains:} wholegrain bread, rice, pasta, and oats
    \item \textbf{Eat fish:} aim for two serves weekly, including oily fish such as salmon and sardines
    \item \textbf{Include legumes and nuts:} beans, lentils, chickpeas, and unsalted nuts
    \item \textbf{Limit saturated fat:} reduce fatty meats, butter, cream, and processed foods
    \item \textbf{Cut salt:} aim for under 5 g of salt daily; cook without salt and limit processed foods
    \item \textbf{Reduce added sugar:} limit sugary drinks, lollies, and desserts
    \item \textbf{Drink water:} as the main drink, and limit alcohol
    \item \textbf{Cook heart-healthy:} grill, bake, and steam rather than fry
    \item \textbf{Maintain a healthy weight:} portion control supports weight management
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Progress of heart disease with an unhealthy diet
    \item Weight gain and obesity
    \item Poor control of blood pressure, cholesterol, and glucose
    \item Nutrient deficiencies from overly restrictive diets
    \item Confusion from conflicting diet advice
    \item Higher costs if expensive "superfoods" are sought
    \item Difficulty sustaining changes without support
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Adopting a heart-healthy diet reduces the risk of heart attack, stroke, and death from cardiovascular disease, and the benefits appear within months. Blood pressure and cholesterol fall with dietary change, and combined with other healthy habits, a heart-healthy diet can prevent a large proportion of heart disease. Improvements at any age, even after a heart event, substantially reduce the risk of further events. A healthy heart diet is one of the most powerful tools for a long, healthy life.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Eat plenty of vegetables, fruit, and whole grains
    \item Include fish, legumes, and nuts in your diet
    \item Limit saturated fat, salt, and added sugar
    \item Drink water and limit alcohol
    \item Cook at home and limit takeaway
    \item Read food labels for fat, salt, and sugar
    \item Maintain a healthy weight and be active
    \item Do not smoke
    \item Have blood pressure and cholesterol checked regularly
    \item Seek dietitian advice for individualised support
\end{itemize}
