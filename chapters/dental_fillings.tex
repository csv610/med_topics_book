\chapter{Dental Fillings}
\index{Dental Fillings}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
A dental filling is a restoration used to repair a tooth damaged by decay, fracture, or wear. The dentist removes the damaged part of the tooth, cleans the cavity, and fills it with a material that restores the tooth's shape and function. Fillings stop decay from spreading, relieve pain, and prevent further damage and tooth loss. Modern filling materials include tooth-coloured composite resin, glass ionomer, and metal amalgam. This chapter explains when fillings are needed, the materials used, and how to care for a filled tooth.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Dental fillings are among the most common procedures in dentistry. Tooth decay affects the majority of people at some point in life; around one in three children has decay by school age, and most adults have had at least one filling. Fillings are increasingly placed in childhood and early adulthood, and re-treatment may be needed when fillings wear or fail over decades. Tooth-coloured composite fillings have largely replaced metal amalgam for visible teeth, although amalgam remains used in some situations.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tooth decay:} plaque bacteria ferment sugars, producing acid that dissolves enamel and dentine, creating a cavity
    \item \textbf{Fractures:} chipped or cracked teeth from trauma, grinding, or biting hard objects may need filling
    \item \textbf{Wear:} abraded or eroded surfaces can be restored
    \item \textbf{Progression:} untreated decay spreads deeper into the tooth, reaching the pulp and causing pain and infection
    \item \textbf{Filling function:} the filling seals the cavity, restores shape, and prevents further damage
    \item \textbf{Failure over time:} fillings can wear, chip, or develop decay at the margin, requiring replacement
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Toothache, pain on eating sweet, hot, or cold food, and visible holes or dark spots indicate possible decay
    \item Early decay is often painless and detected only on examination or X-ray
    \item After filling: the tooth may be briefly sensitive, which usually settles
    \item A well-placed filling restores comfortable chewing
    \item Signs a filling may need attention: pain, roughness, food trapping, or a cracked or lost filling
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Tooth decay requiring filling versus early enamel lesions that can be managed with fluoride
    \item Filling versus crown or onlay for larger areas of damage
    \item Toothache from decay versus pulpitis, gum disease, or sinus pain
    \item Filling versus fissure sealant for prevention of decay in healthy teeth
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a dentist for toothache, visible holes, sensitivity, or any sign of decay. Regular check-ups allow early decay to be filled with smaller, simpler restorations. Prompt treatment of a lost or broken filling prevents further damage.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe toothache with facial swelling, fever, or difficulty breathing or swallowing
    \item A tooth broken in a serious injury
    \item Uncontrolled bleeding from the mouth
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dental examination:} inspection of the teeth with a probe and mirror
    \item \textbf{X-rays:} detect decay between teeth and beneath old fillings
    \item \textbf{Assessment of depth:} whether decay involves the pulp, which determines if a filling or root canal treatment is needed
    \item \textbf{Material selection:} choice of filling material based on the tooth, the size of the cavity, and aesthetic needs
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Removal of decay:} the dentist removes the decayed tissue and shapes the cavity
    \item \textbf{Placement:} the filling material is placed, shaped, and polished
    \item \textbf{Composite resin:} tooth-coloured, bonded to the tooth, and ideal for visible areas
    \item \textbf{Amalgam:} durable silver-coloured metal, used for back teeth in some situations
    \item \textbf{Glass ionomer:} releases fluoride and is used in certain situations, including children's teeth
    \item \textbf{Desensitisation:} sensitive teeth after filling usually settle within days to weeks
    \item \textbf{Replacement:} worn or failed fillings are replaced at check-ups
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sensitivity, which usually resolves
    \item Further decay at the margin of the filling
    \item Chipping, cracking, or loss of the filling
    \item Pain from pulp involvement, requiring root canal treatment
    \item Allergic reactions to materials in rare cases
    \item Recurrent decay under an old filling
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
With good oral hygiene and regular dental care, fillings typically last 5--15 years depending on the material, the size of the restoration, and oral habits. Composite fillings, in particular, have excellent longevity when well placed and cared for. Fillings stop the progression of decay, restore function, and preserve natural teeth, and most people remain comfortable and fully functional with their fillings for many years.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste and floss daily to prevent new decay
    \item Limit sugary and acidic foods and drinks
    \item Attend regular check-ups so fillings and teeth are monitored
    \item Avoid biting very hard objects that can crack teeth and fillings
    \item Report pain, sensitivity, or a lost filling promptly
    \item Consider fluoride treatments and fissure sealants to prevent decay
\end{itemize}
