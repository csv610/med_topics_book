\chapter{Endometrial Cancer}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Endometrial cancer is cancer that begins in the endometrium, the lining of the uterus. It is the most common gynaecological cancer in Australia and, when detected early, it has an excellent outlook. The most common symptom is abnormal bleeding, particularly bleeding after the menopause, which leads to early diagnosis in most women. Treatment usually involves surgery to remove the uterus, and often further treatment depending on the stage and type. This chapter explains the risk factors, symptoms, diagnosis, and treatment of endometrial cancer.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Endometrial cancer is the most common cancer of the female reproductive organs, with thousands of new cases diagnosed in Australia each year. It occurs mainly after the menopause, with most cases in women over 60. The incidence has risen with increasing rates of obesity, a major risk factor. Because most women present with bleeding and are diagnosed early, survival rates are high compared with other gynaecological cancers.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Endometrial cancer is strongly linked to the effects of oestrogen on the uterine lining.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Oestrogen exposure:} unopposed oestrogen, without the balancing effect of progesterone, causes the endometrium to grow, and prolonged stimulation can lead to cancer
    \item \textbf{Obesity:} fat tissue produces oestrogen, increasing risk in post-menopausal women
    \item \textbf{Hormone therapy:} oestrogen-only hormone replacement therapy increases risk, while combined therapy does not
    \item \textbf{Other risk factors:} early first period, late menopause, no pregnancies, diabetes, and tamoxifen use
    \item \textbf{Genetics:} Lynch syndrome and a family history of bowel or uterine cancer increase risk
    \item \textbf{Pathways:} most cancers develop from hyperplasia and are slow-growing, while a smaller proportion are aggressive
    \item \textbf{Protective factors:} pregnancy, the oral contraceptive pill, and physical activity reduce risk
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
The symptoms of endometrial cancer are usually noticeable early.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Bleeding after the menopause, the classic and most common symptom
    \item Heavy, prolonged, or irregular periods in women before the menopause
    \item Bleeding between periods
    \item A watery, blood-stained discharge
    \item Pelvic pain and pressure in advanced disease
    \item Pain during intercourse
    \item Weight loss and fatigue in advanced disease
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Abnormal bleeding has many causes that must be investigated.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Endometrial hyperplasia and polyps
    \item Fibroids and adenomyosis
    \item Cervical cancer and cervical polyps
    \item Vaginal and vulval causes of bleeding
    \item Hormonal imbalance and menopause-related changes
    \item Bleeding disorders and medication effects
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Any bleeding after the menopause must be investigated. Women with heavy or irregular periods should also seek assessment if the bleeding is new, persistent, or heavy.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Very heavy vaginal bleeding with faintness or dizziness
    \item Severe pelvic or abdominal pain
    \item Fever with pelvic pain
    \item Bleeding in pregnancy with pain
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Diagnosis is made by sampling the endometrium.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Endometrial biopsy:} a clinic procedure to sample the lining, the first-line test
    \item \textbf{Transvaginal ultrasound:} measures the thickness of the endometrium
    \item \textbf{Hysteroscopy:} a camera inspection of the uterine cavity with targeted biopsy
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} MRI and CT to determine the stage and plan surgery
    \item \textbf{Genetic testing:} for Lynch syndrome in selected women
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Treatment depends on the stage, type, and the woman's health.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Surgery:} hysterectomy with removal of the fallopian tubes and ovaries is the main treatment for early disease
    \item \textbf{Lymph node assessment:} removal or sampling of lymph nodes to check for spread
    \item \textbf{Radiotherapy:} for higher-risk disease or when surgery is not possible
    \item \textbf{Hormone therapy:} progesterone treatment for advanced or recurrent disease, and for women who wish to preserve fertility in very early cancer
    \item \textbf{Chemotherapy:} for aggressive types and advanced disease
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} regular reviews to detect recurrence
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Spread of cancer to the lymph nodes and other organs
    \item Recurrence after treatment
    \item Complications of surgery, including bleeding, infection, and effects of removing the ovaries
    \item Effects of radiotherapy and chemotherapy
    \item The emotional impact of a cancer diagnosis
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The outlook for endometrial cancer is very good because most cases are diagnosed early with the symptom of bleeding. The five-year survival for early-stage disease is high, and even advanced disease is treatable. Outcomes depend on the stage, the tumour type, and treatment, and most women are cured with surgery alone.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Report any post-menopausal bleeding immediately
    \item Maintain a healthy weight through diet and activity
    \item Exercise regularly
    \item Discuss the risks and benefits of hormone therapy with a doctor
    \item Consider genetic testing if you have a strong family history
    \item Attend follow-up after treatment
    \item Seek support for the emotional effects of diagnosis and treatment
\end{itemize}
