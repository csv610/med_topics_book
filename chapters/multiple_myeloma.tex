\chapter{Multiple Myeloma}
\index{Multiple Myeloma}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Multiple myeloma is a malignant neoplasm of plasma cells characterised by clonal proliferation of plasma cells in the bone marrow, production of a monoclonal immunoglobulin (M-protein), and associated end-organ damage (CRAB criteria: hyperCalcaemia, Renal impairment, Anaemia, Bone lesions). It is the second most common haematological malignancy after non-Hodgkin lymphoma. The disease arises from a post-germinal-centre B-cell that has undergone malignant transformation. Precursor states include monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (MGUS) and smouldering multiple myeloma (SMM). Modern therapy with proteasome inhibitors, immunomodulatory drugs, monoclonal antibodies, and cellular therapies has transformed myeloma into a chronic, manageable disease for many patients.

\section*{Epidemiology}
In many countries, approximately 2,500 new cases are diagnosed annually. Incidence increases sharply with age (median diagnosis ~70 years); rare under 40. Men are slightly more affected than women (1.3:1). Indigenous people have higher incidence and poorer outcomes. The age-standardised incidence is rising, partly due to improved detection and an ageing population. Five-year survival has improved from <30\% in the 1990s to >60\% currently, with median survival now 8--10 years and a subset achieving long-term disease control. MGUS prevalence is ~3\% in adults >50 years, ~5\% >70 years; progression risk ~1\% per year.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Genetic subgroups:} hyperdiploid (trisomies of odd-numbered chromosomes, ~50\%, better prognosis) vs non-hyperdiploid with primary IGH translocations: t(11;14) (CCND1, ~20\%), t(4;14) (MMSET/FGFR3, ~15\%, high-risk), t(14;16) (MAF, ~5\%, high-risk), t(14;20) (MAFB, ~2\%)
    \item \textbf{Secondary events:} MYC rearrangements, RAS mutations (KRAS, NRAS), TP53 deletions/mutations (del17p, high-risk), 1q gain/amplification (high-risk), del13q
    \item \textbf{Bone marrow microenvironment:} plasma cells interact with stromal cells, osteoclasts, osteoblasts, immune cells; RANKL/OPG imbalance drives osteolytic lesions; VEGF, DKK1 suppress osteoblastogenesis
    \item \textbf{M-protein:} intact immunoglobulin (IgG ~55\%, IgA ~25\%) or light-chain only (~20\%); non-secretory myeloma (~3\%)
    \item \textbf{Renal injury:} light-chain cast nephropathy (myeloma kidney), light-chain deposition disease, amyloidosis, hypercalcaemia, hyperviscosity
    \item \textbf{Immune dysfunction:} hypogammaglobulinaemia, T-cell exhaustion, impaired NK function; predisposes to infection
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CRAB features (end-organ damage):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{C}alcaemia: corrected Ca $\ge$2.75 mmol/L or $>$0.25 mmol/L above ULN
        \item \textbf{R}enal: creatinine $\ge$177 $\mu$mol/L (2 mg/dL) or eGFR $<$40 mL/min
        \item \textbf{A}naemia: Hb $<$100 g/L or $>$20 g/L below LLN
        \item \textbf{B}one: lytic lesions on imaging ($\ge$5 mm), osteoporosis with vertebral compression fractures
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Other presentations:} bone pain (back, ribs), fatigue (anaemia), recurrent infections, renal failure, hypercalcaemia symptoms (confusion, polyuria, constipation), peripheral neuropathy, spinal cord compression (emergency), hyperviscosity syndrome (IgM/IgA)
    \item \textbf{Asymptomatic:} MGUS (M-protein $<$30 g/L, plasma cells $<$10\%, no CRAB), SMM (M-protein $\ge$30 g/L or plasma cells 10--60\%, no CRAB)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{MGUS:} M-protein $<$30 g/L, plasma cells $<$10\%, no CRAB; monitor
    \item \textbf{SMM:} intermediate; higher risk of progression; risk stratification (IMWG, Mayo, Spanish models); consider early intervention in high-risk (clinical trials)
    \item \textbf{AL amyloidosis:} light-chain deposition in organs; may coexist with myeloma; heart, kidney, nerve, liver, GI involvement
    \item \textbf{Light chain deposition disease (LCDD):} non-fibrillar light-chain deposits, predominantly kidney
    \item \textbf{POEMS syndrome:} Polyneuropathy, Organomegaly, Endocrinopathy, M-protein, Skin changes; $\lambda$ light chain; Castleman disease overlap; VEGF elevated
    \item \textbf{Plasma cell leukaemia:} circulating plasma cells $>$20\% or $>$2 $\times$ 10$^9$/L; aggressive
    \item \textbf{Solitary plasmacytoma:} single bone or extramedullary lesion; no systemic disease; radiotherapy curative in many
    \item \textbf{Waldenstr\"om macroglobulinaemia:} IgM paraprotein, lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma; MYD88 L265P mutation
    \item \textbf{Secondary causes of M-protein:} chronic infections, autoimmune, other lymphoproliferative disorders
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Refer to haematologist for any M-protein detected, unexplained anaemia, renal impairment, hypercalcaemia, or lytic bone lesions. Urgent assessment for spinal cord compression, hyperviscosity, or acute renal failure.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Spinal cord compression (back pain, leg weakness, sensory level, urinary retention)
    \item Hyperviscosity syndrome (visual changes, confusion, bleeding, stroke-like symptoms)
    \item Severe hypercalcaemia (confusion, dehydration, cardiac arrhythmia)
    \item Sepsis in immunocompromised patient
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Serum/urine electrophoresis and immunofixation:} identifies and quantifies M-protein; heavy chain and light chain type
    \item \textbf{Serum free light chains (FLC):} $\kappa$ and $\lambda$ concentrations and ratio; sensitive for light-chain only and non-secretory disease; involved/uninvolved ratio $\ge$100 is a myeloma-defining event
    \item \textbf{Bone marrow biopsy:} plasma cell percentage, morphology, flow cytometry (CD138+, CD38+, CD19-, CD56+/-), FISH for cytogenetics (t(4;14), t(14;16), t(14;20), del17p, 1q gain, MYC)
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} whole-body low-dose CT (preferred over skeletal survey); MRI spine/pelvis for equivocal lesions or cord compression; PET-CT for SMM risk stratification or oligo-progressive disease
    \item \textbf{Bloods:} FBC, creatinine, eGFR, calcium, albumin, LDH, $\beta$2-microglobulin (prognostic), quantitative immunoglobulins, ESR/CRP
    \item \textbf{Staging:} Revised International Staging System (R-ISS): ISS stage (based on $\beta$2M, albumin) + high-risk cytogenetics (del17p, t(4;14), t(14;16)) + high LDH
    \item \textbf{Renal biopsy:} if cast nephropathy uncertain; light-chain immunofluorescence
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{General:} bisphosphonates (zoledronic acid) or denosumab for bone disease; anticoagulation (aspirin or LMWH) with IMiD-based regimens; infection prophylaxis (aciclovir, PJP prophylaxis with TMP-SMX if high-dose steroids); IVIG for recurrent infections with hypogammaglobulinaemia; vaccination (pneumococcal, influenza, COVID, shingles -- avoid live)
    \item \textbf{Newly diagnosed -- transplant-eligible (typically age $<$70--75, fit):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Induction: triplet preferred -- bortezomib + lenalidomide + dexamethasone (VRd) or daratumumab + VRd (D-VRd, per PERSEUS/CASSIOPEIA); 4--6 cycles
        \item Stem cell collection: mobilisation with G-CSF +/- plerixafor
        \item High-dose melphalan (200 mg/m$^2$) + autologous stem cell transplant (ASCT); consider tandem transplant in trials
        \item Maintenance: lenalidomide until progression (improves PFS and OS); consider bortezomib addition for high-risk
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Newly diagnosed -- transplant-ineligible:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Daratumumab + lenalidomide + dexamethasone (DRd, per MAIA) or bortezomib + lenalidomide + dexamethasone (VRd-lite) or daratumumab + bortezomib + melphalan + prednisone (D-VMP, per ALCYONE)
        \item Continuous therapy until progression
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Relapsed/refractory:} sequential triplet regimens; choices based on prior exposure, refractoriness, cytogenetics, frailty:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Anti-CD38 mAbs: daratumumab, isatuximab (combine with PIs, IMiDs, pomalidomide)
        \item Proteasome inhibitors (PIs): bortezomib, carfilzomib, ixazomib
        \item Immunomodulatory drugs (IMiDs): lenalidomide, pomalidomide
        \item XPO1 inhibitor: selinexor + dexamethasone
        \item BCMA-targeted: teclistamab, talquetamab, elranatamab (bispecifics); ide-cel, cilta-cel (CAR-T) -- later lines
        \item Belantamab mafodotin (ADC) -- withdrawn in US, available elsewhere
        \item Clinical trials strongly encouraged
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Renal impairment:} bortezomib-based (no dose adjustment); daratumumab; plasmapheresis for cast nephropathy (controversial); early haematology-nephrology collaboration
    \item \textbf{High-risk cytogenetics (del17p, t(4;14), t(14;16), 1q gain):} quadruplet induction (D-VRd), early transplant, proteasome inhibitor maintenance, clinical trials
    \item \textbf{Supportive care:} analgesia (avoid NSAIDs), physiotherapy, nutrition, psychosocial, advance care planning
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Renal failure:} cast nephropathy, hypercalcaemia, amyloidosis, light-chain deposition disease, drug toxicity
    \item \textbf{Bone disease:} pathological fractures, spinal cord compression, chronic pain; osteonecrosis of jaw (bisphosphonates/denosumab)
    \item \textbf{Infection:} encapsulated organisms (pneumococcus, Haemophilus), viral (VZV, COVID), fungal; leading cause of death
    \item \textbf{Peripheral neuropathy:} bortezomib (dose-reduce/subcutaneous), thalidomide, disease-related (amyloid)
    \item \textbf{Second primary malignancies:} therapy-related MDS/AML (melphalan/lenalidomide), solid tumours
    \item \textbf{Cardiac:} carfilzomib (hypertension, heart failure), amyloidosis infiltration
    \item \textbf{Thromboembolism:} IMiD-based regimens (prophylaxis mandatory)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Prognosis is heterogeneous. R-ISS Stage I: median OS >10 years; Stage III high-risk: median OS 3--5 years. Depth of response (MRD negativity by NGS/flow at $10^{-5}$--$10^{-6}$) is the strongest predictor of long-term PFS and OS. Sustained MRD-negative status off therapy is the operational definition of ``functional cure'' for a subset. Novel agents (bispecifics, CAR-T, trispecifics, menin inhibitors, GPRC5D-targeted) are extending options for heavily pre-treated patients. Quality of life is maintainable with chronic therapy for many.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item No proven prevention for myeloma; MGUS/SMM monitoring enables early intervention
    \item Adherence to maintenance and supportive medications
    \item Infection vigilance: prompt antibiotics for fever; stay current on vaccinations (avoid live)
    \item Bone health: calcium, vitamin D, weight-bearing exercise, dental review before bisphosphonates/denosumab
    \item Renal protection: hydration, avoid NSAIDs/contrast, monitor creatinine
    \item Neuropathy monitoring: report symptoms early for dose modification
    \item Thromboprophylaxis compliance with IMiDs
    \item Psychosocial support: Myeloma some countries, Leukaemia Foundation, peer support groups
    \item Advance care planning discussions early in disease trajectory
\end{itemize}
