\chapter{Ear Thermometers}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Ear thermometers measure body temperature by sensing the heat radiated from the eardrum and surrounding tissues using an infrared sensor. Because the eardrum shares its blood supply with the temperature-regulating centre of the brain, ear temperature is considered a good reflection of core body temperature. Ear thermometers are quick and convenient, which makes them popular in households and clinics, particularly for children. This chapter explains how ear thermometers work, how to use them accurately, and their advantages and limitations compared with other methods.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Fever is one of the most common reasons parents seek medical care for children, and accurate temperature measurement guides treatment decisions. Ear thermometers are widely used because they give a reading in seconds and do not disturb a sleeping child. Their accuracy depends on correct technique and on the child's ear canal size and condition, which means results can vary, particularly in young infants.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Body temperature is regulated by the hypothalamus in the brain, and it varies normally through the day, with activity, and with illness.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Core temperature:} the eardrum's temperature closely tracks core temperature because of its shared blood supply with the hypothalamus
    \item \textbf{Infrared measurement:} the thermometer detects infrared radiation emitted by the eardrum and converts it to a temperature reading
    \item \textbf{Fever:} the hypothalamus raises the set point in response to infection, so the eardrum temperature rises correspondingly
    \item \textbf{Limitations:} earwax, an ear infection, a curved ear canal, or incorrect placement can all affect the reading
    \item \textbf{Age factors:} the small and curved ear canals of babies make accurate placement more difficult
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Using an ear thermometer correctly is a skill.
\begin{itemize}
    \item A fast reading, usually within one to two seconds
    \item Minimal disturbance, making it suitable for sleeping children
    \item Readings can be affected by earwax, ear infection, and improper positioning
    \item Results are less reliable in infants under about six months
    \item A normal ear temperature is approximately 36 to 37.5 degrees Celsius
    \item Ear temperatures tend to run slightly higher than armpit temperatures
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Different thermometers suit different situations.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Digital underarm (axillary) thermometers are the recommended first choice for young children in many guidelines
    \item Rectal thermometers give the most accurate core temperature and are used in infancy
    \item Oral thermometers suit older children and adults
    \item Temporal artery (forehead) thermometers are convenient but less accurate
    \item Mercury thermometers are no longer recommended because of the risk of breakage and poisoning
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Fever itself is a symptom, not a disease, and most fevers in children are caused by minor viral infections. Medical advice should be sought for a fever in an infant under three months, a fever lasting more than a few days, a very high fever, or a fever accompanied by worrying symptoms such as a rash, difficulty breathing, seizures, or lethargy.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A fever with a stiff neck, severe headache, or a rash that does not fade when pressed
    \item A fever with difficulty breathing or bluish lips
    \item A seizure or fit
    \item Unresponsiveness or extreme lethargy
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Using an ear thermometer properly requires attention to technique.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{New probe cover:} use a clean cover for each measurement
    \item \textbf{Correct positioning:} gently pull the ear back and insert the probe snugly into the ear canal, aiming at the eardrum
    \item \textbf{Consistent technique:} measure the same ear each time, and repeat for confirmation
    \item \textbf{Calibration:} check the device periodically and follow the manufacturer's instructions
    \item \textbf{Context:} interpret the reading with the child's overall condition, not in isolation
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Ear thermometers are a tool, and their value lies in how the result is used.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Treat the child, not the number:} comfort, fluids, and rest matter more than the exact reading
    \item \textbf{Fever medicines:} paracetamol or ibuprofen can be used for a child who is uncomfortable, rather than purely because of the number
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} re-measure if the child deteriorates or the fever persists
    \item \textbf{Alternative methods:} use an underarm thermometer for young infants where ear readings may be unreliable
    \item \textbf{Medical review:} seek care for fevers in young infants or with warning signs
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Inaccurate readings leading to undertreated or overtreated fever
    \item False reassurance from a low reading taken with poor technique
    \item Unnecessary panic from a high reading that does not reflect true temperature
    \item The risk of ear canal injury if the thermometer is inserted forcefully
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Ear thermometers are reliable when used correctly in suitable children and adults, giving a quick and useful estimate of core temperature. Used alongside the person's overall condition, they support safe decisions about care. Understanding the limitations of any single method, and confirming important readings, produces the best outcomes.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Read and follow the manufacturer's instructions for your thermometer
    \item Use a new probe cover for each reading
    \item Position the thermometer correctly in the ear
    \item Use an underarm thermometer for babies under six months
    \item Check a high reading with a repeat measurement
    \item Treat the person's comfort and symptoms, not just the number
    \item Keep medicines out of reach and follow dosing guidelines
\end{itemize}
