\section*{Nine Possible Warning Signs}
The following changes may indicate that someone needs support, especially when persistent or affecting daily life:

\begin{enumerate}
    \\item Persistent sadness, irritability, or hopelessness
    \\item Loss of interest or pleasure
    \\item Major changes in sleep
    \\item Major changes in appetite or energy
    \\item Difficulty concentrating or functioning
    \\item Excessive worry, panic, or fear
    \\item Withdrawal from usual relationships or activities
    \\item Unusual thoughts, perceptions, or behaviour
    \\item Thoughts of self-harm or suicide
\end{enumerate}

\section*{What to Do}
A warning sign is not a diagnosis. Talk with a GP or mental-health professional when changes persist, cause distress, or impair functioning. Ask directly about safety when self-harm or suicide is a concern.

\section*{Emergency Help}
Call 000 or attend an emergency department for immediate danger, serious self-harm, severe confusion, or inability to stay safe. In Australia, Lifeline is available on 13 11 14.
