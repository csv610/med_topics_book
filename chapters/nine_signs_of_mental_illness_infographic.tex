\section*{Nine Possible Warning Signs}
The following changes may indicate that someone needs support, especially when persistent or affecting daily life:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Persistent sadness, irritability, or hopelessness
    \item Loss of interest or pleasure
    \item Major changes in sleep
    \item Major changes in appetite or energy
    \item Difficulty concentrating or functioning
    \item Excessive worry, panic, or fear
    \item Withdrawal from usual relationships or activities
    \item Unusual thoughts, perceptions, or behaviour
    \item Thoughts of self-harm or suicide
\end{enumerate}

\section*{What to Do}
A warning sign is not a diagnosis. Talk with a GP or mental-health professional when changes persist, cause distress, or impair functioning. Ask directly about safety when self-harm or suicide is a concern.

\section*{Assessment}
A clinician asks about the person's symptoms, duration, severity, sleep, medicines, alcohol or drug use, physical health, functioning, and safety. They may speak with the person alone and, with consent, with someone who knows them. Physical examination or tests may be useful when a medical condition or medicine effect could be contributing. There is no single test that diagnoses every mental-health condition.

\section*{Management and Support}
Support depends on the person's needs and may include listening without judgement, practical help, psychological therapy, treatment of a physical condition, medicines prescribed by a qualified clinician, and follow-up. A person should not be labelled from this list alone; persistent or impairing changes deserve a professional assessment.

\section*{Emergency Help}
Contact your local emergency services or attend an emergency department for immediate danger, serious self-harm, severe confusion, or inability to stay safe. In many countries, a local crisis service is available; use the service appropriate to your jurisdiction.
\section*{Safety-netting}
Seek urgent help for immediate danger, suicidal intent, psychosis, severe confusion, inability to meet basic needs, or rapidly worsening symptoms.
