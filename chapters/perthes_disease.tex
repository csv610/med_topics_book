\chapter{Perthes' Disease}
\index{Perthes' Disease}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. A child with a persistent limp or hip, thigh, or knee pain should be assessed.}

\section*{What it is}
Perthes' disease is a childhood hip condition in which the blood supply to the femoral head is reduced. The bone softens and remodels over time. The process can last several years, and the final shape of the hip influences later movement and arthritis risk.

\section*{Symptoms}
A child may limp, tire with activity, or have pain in the hip, groin, thigh, or knee. Hip movement, especially turning the leg inward or moving it out to the side, may be limited. Symptoms can come and go, and knee pain may obscure the hip problem.

\section*{Diagnosis and follow-up}
Assessment includes examination of the hips and gait and usually X-rays. Early X-rays can be normal, so repeat assessment or MRI may be considered if symptoms persist. Orthopaedic follow-up and imaging monitor the stage, shape, and movement of the hip.

\section*{Treatment}
Treatment depends on age, stage, hip movement, and severity. It may include activity modification, pain relief, physiotherapy, crutches, casts or braces, and sometimes surgery to keep the femoral head well positioned in the socket. Do not impose restrictions or exercises without the treating team’s advice.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Arrange prompt assessment for a new or persistent limp, hip or knee pain, or reduced movement. Seek urgent care if the child cannot bear weight, has fever with a hot or swollen joint, severe sudden pain, or appears seriously unwell.
