\chapter{Balanitis and Balanoposthitis}
\index{Balanitis and Balanoposthitis}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Balanitis is inflammation of the glans (the rounded head) of the penis. When the foreskin is also inflamed, the condition is called balanoposthitis. Both are common and occur almost exclusively in uncircumcised boys and men, because the warm, moist area under the foreskin is a place where irritation and infection can easily develop.

Most cases are caused by a yeast (fungal) infection such as candida, by irritation from soaps and chemicals, or by skin conditions. Balanitis is usually not serious and settles with good hygiene and simple treatment, but it can be a sign of diabetes or, rarely, of a sexually transmitted infection. Recurrent or persistent inflammation can cause tightening of the foreskin and, in a small number of cases, lead to more serious problems, so it is worth assessing.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Balanitis is common, affecting roughly 1 in 30 boys and men over a lifetime. It is most frequent in uncircumcised males, in boys before puberty, and in men with diabetes, especially when blood glucose is poorly controlled. Poor hygiene, over-vigorous washing, and the use of irritating soaps and bubble baths are common contributing factors. Balanitis caused by sexually transmitted infections is less common and mainly affects sexually active adults.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The glans and foreskin become inflamed because something disrupts the normal skin and its protective environment. Causes include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Fungal infection:} candida (thrush) is the most common cause, encouraged by moisture, warmth, and high blood sugar
    \item \textbf{Irritant contact dermatitis:} soaps, bubble baths, shower gels, fragrances, laundry products, or spermicides
    \item \textbf{Bacterial infection:} skin bacteria such as streptococci or staphylococci can infect an already irritated area
    \item \textbf{Sexually transmitted infections:} herpes, syphilis, and others, often with ulcers or other features
    \item \textbf{Skin conditions:} psoriasis, seborrhoeic dermatitis, lichen planus, and lichen sclerosus
    \item \textbf{Trauma or friction:} from vigorous sex, masturbation, or tight clothing
    \item \textbf{Poor hygiene:} trapped smegma, or conversely, washing too aggressively
    \item \textbf{A tight foreskin (phimosis):} makes cleaning difficult and traps irritants
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Redness, swelling, and soreness of the glans and foreskin
    \item Itching, burning, or a stinging sensation, especially on urination
    \item A discharge under the foreskin -- often thick, white, and cheesy with candida
    \item An unpleasant smell
    \item A tight or painful foreskin that is hard to retract
    \item Small spots, ulcers, or a rash on the glans
    \item Pain during sex or masturbation
    \item In severe cases, difficulty passing urine
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Candida (thrush) balanitis versus irritant dermatitis versus bacterial infection
    \item Sexually transmitted infections (herpes, syphilis, gonorrhoea), particularly with ulcers or a history of risk
    \item Skin conditions: psoriasis, seborrhoeic dermatitis, lichen planus
    \item Lichen sclerosus: a chronic condition causing pale, scarred patches, more common in boys
    \item Premalignant or malignant conditions of the glans (rare), which should be considered if a lesion does not heal
    \item Balanitis in an uncircumcised man that is recurrent should raise the possibility of diabetes
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor if the problem does not settle with gentle washing within a few days, if there is a discharge, pain, or sores, or if it keeps coming back.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Inability to pass urine
    \item A retracted foreskin that cannot be pulled back over the glans (paraphimosis -- an emergency)
    \item Sudden severe swelling, with fever and chills
    \item A tight, swollen foreskin with an urgent need to urinate
\end{itemize}

See a GP for:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Persistent or recurrent balanitis
    \item Discharge, bleeding, or sores on the glans
    \item Pain or difficulty with urination
    \item Symptoms of undiagnosed diabetes (excessive thirst, frequent urination, weight loss)
    \item Any non-healing or growing lesion on the penis
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History and examination:} symptoms, hygiene habits, products used, sexual history, and diabetes symptoms; inspection of the glans and foreskin
    \item \textbf{Swabs:} a swab of any discharge for microscopy and culture to identify candida or bacteria
    \item \textbf{Testing for STIs:} if there is a sexual risk or suspicious sores
    \item \textbf{Blood glucose:} a urine or blood glucose test to look for diabetes, especially with recurrent infection
    \item \textbf{Biopsy:} rarely needed, but considered for a persistent, non-healing, or suspicious lesion
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hygiene:} wash the glans gently with warm water and dry well; do not scrub or use harsh soaps
    \item \textbf{Antifungal treatment:} a topical antifungal cream for candida, applied as directed
    \item \textbf{Anti-inflammatory treatment:} a mild topical corticosteroid for dermatitis or eczema-like causes, under medical advice
    \item \textbf{Antibiotics:} for confirmed bacterial infection
    \item \textbf{Treating underlying conditions:} good blood glucose control in diabetes, treatment of skin conditions, and treatment of STIs if found
    \item \textbf{Circumcision:} may be recommended for recurrent balanoposthitis, phimosis, or lichen sclerosus
    \item \textbf{Emergency care:} paraphimosis needs urgent reduction by a doctor
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Recurrent episodes, which are common when the underlying cause is not addressed
    \item Phimosis: tightening of the foreskin that becomes hard to retract
    \item Paraphimosis: the retracted foreskin becomes stuck and can cut off the blood supply to the glans -- an emergency
    \item Scarring and narrowing of the urinary opening (meatal stenosis)
    \item Balanitis xerotica obliterans (lichen sclerosus), which can cause long-term scarring and is linked with a small risk of penile cancer
    \item Discomfort and embarrassment affecting sexual relationships and quality of life
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Most episodes of balanitis and balanoposthitis settle quickly with good hygiene and the right treatment, usually within a week. The condition often recurs if the cause is ongoing -- for example, poorly controlled diabetes, continued use of irritants, or a tight foreskin. In children, the problem usually settles with hygiene measures and many outgrow it. When a specific cause such as candida or dermatitis is treated, the outlook is excellent. Persistent or recurrent balanitis should be reviewed, since treating the underlying cause both resolves the inflammation and reduces the risk of complications.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Wash the glans and foreskin daily with warm water and dry thoroughly
    \item Pull the foreskin back gently to clean, but do not force it
    \item Avoid perfumed soaps, bubble baths, and shower gels on the area
    \item Wear loose, cotton underwear and avoid tight clothing that traps moisture
    \item Control blood glucose if you have diabetes
    \item Use condoms and practise safe sex
    \item If the foreskin is tight or cleaning is difficult, see a doctor rather than struggling with hygiene alone
    \item Seek prompt review for any lesion that does not heal
\end{itemize}
