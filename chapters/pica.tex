\chapter{Pica}
\index{Pica}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; seek professional advice for individual care.}

\section*{What it is}
Pica is repeated eating of substances that are not food and do not provide normal nutrition, such as soil, clay, paper, chalk, soap, hair, or ice. It can occur in children and adults and may be associated with iron or zinc deficiency, pregnancy, developmental conditions, or mental-health and social factors.

\section*{Risks and symptoms}
Risks depend on the substance and may include poisoning, infection, dental damage, constipation, bowel blockage, choking, or nutritional deficiency. Lead, contaminated soil, chemicals, and sharp objects can be especially dangerous. Symptoms may include abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, bloating, constipation, tiredness, or anaemia.

\section*{Assessment and treatment}
Assessment should be non-judgemental and include the substance, amount, duration, access, nutritional intake, pregnancy, development, mental health, and safeguarding. Blood tests may check iron, zinc, anaemia, or lead; other tests depend on the substance and symptoms. Treatment addresses deficiencies or poisoning, removes hazards, and may include behavioural, developmental, nutritional, or mental-health support.

\section*{When to seek urgent care}
Seek emergency help after swallowing a chemical, lead-containing material, medication, battery, magnet, sharp object, or a large amount of material, or for choking, severe abdominal pain, repeated vomiting, a swollen abdomen, confusion, seizure, or collapse.
