\chapter{Pancreatitis}
\index{Pancreatitis}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; seek professional advice for individual care.}

\section*{What it is}
Pancreatitis is inflammation of the pancreas. Acute pancreatitis starts suddenly and may settle with treatment; chronic pancreatitis persists or recurs and can cause lasting damage. Common causes include gallstones and alcohol, but medicines, high blood triglycerides or calcium, genetic conditions, injury, and other illnesses can also contribute.

\section*{Symptoms}
Acute pancreatitis commonly causes severe, persistent upper-abdominal pain that may spread to the back, nausea, vomiting, fever, a fast pulse, or abdominal tenderness. Chronic pancreatitis may cause recurrent pain, weight loss, diarrhoea, or difficulty absorbing nutrients. These symptoms have other possible causes and need assessment.

\section*{Diagnosis}
Clinicians use the history and examination, pancreatic blood tests, and sometimes ultrasound, CT, or MRI. Imaging may help find gallstones or complications and is not required in every case. Assessment also looks for effects on hydration, breathing, kidney function, blood sugar, and other organs.

\section*{Treatment}
Acute pancreatitis generally needs urgent hospital assessment. Treatment may include intravenous fluids, pain and nausea control, early nutrition when safe, treatment of gallstones or another cause, and monitoring for complications. Severe disease may need intensive care; antibiotics are used when an infection is present or specifically indicated, not routinely for all cases.

\section*{When to seek urgent care}
Seek emergency help for sudden severe abdominal pain that does not settle, pain spreading to the back, repeated vomiting, fever with worsening illness, fainting, a fast heartbeat, breathing difficulty, confusion, or a swollen abdomen.

\section*{Prevention and follow-up}
Follow advice about alcohol, smoking, medicines, diet, gallstones, triglycerides, and diabetes. Recurrent or chronic pancreatitis may require digestive-enzyme treatment, nutrition support, and specialist follow-up.
