\chapter{Dental Check-Up}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
A dental check-up is a routine visit to a dentist to assess the health of the teeth, gums, and mouth, and to detect problems early before they cause pain or damage. A typical check-up involves a clinical examination, X-rays when needed, professional cleaning, and advice on oral hygiene and diet. Regular check-ups are the cornerstone of preventive dental care, allowing early treatment of decay and gum disease and reducing the need for complex and costly procedures later. This chapter explains what happens at a check-up, how often one is needed, and how to prepare.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Regular dental attendance is associated with better oral health, yet a substantial minority of Australians do not visit a dentist regularly. Around half of Australians visit the dentist at least once a year, while others attend only when they have pain. People who attend regularly are more likely to have decay and gum disease detected early, need fewer extractions and complex treatments, and report better oral health. Children from families with regular dental visits also have better oral health outcomes.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Early detection:} most dental problems begin painlessly; X-rays and probing reveal decay and gum disease before symptoms develop
    \item \textbf{Decay progression:} cavities enlarge slowly, and small lesions can be treated with simple fillings or remineralisation
    \item \textbf{Gum disease:} gingivitis is reversible with cleaning and better hygiene, but untreated it progresses to periodontitis with bone loss
    \item \textbf{Prophylaxis:} professional removal of plaque and calculus (tartar) that cannot be removed by brushing alone
    \item \textbf{Risk assessment:} the check-up identifies individual risk factors, such as diet, dry mouth, or medical conditions, allowing targeted prevention
    \item \textbf{Screening:} oral cancer and other soft tissue problems can be detected at check-ups
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The examination covers the teeth, gums, tongue, cheeks, and other oral tissues
    \item The dentist checks for decay, broken or worn teeth, gum inflammation, and signs of oral disease
    \item X-rays may be taken to examine areas between teeth and beneath the gums
    \item Professional cleaning removes plaque and stain and polishes the teeth
    \item The dentist discusses findings and recommends treatment or preventive measures
    \item A check-up also reviews existing fillings, crowns, and appliances
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Check-up versus problem-focused visit: a check-up is routine, while a problem visit addresses a specific symptom
    \item Early decay versus staining, which may not require treatment
    \item Gingivitis versus periodontitis, which require different treatment
    \item Worn teeth from grinding versus decay
    \item Benign oral lesions versus potentially serious conditions, which may require biopsy or referral
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Most people need a dental check-up every 6--12 months, although the recommended interval depends on individual risk. People at higher risk, including smokers, people with diabetes, those with gum disease, and pregnant women, may need more frequent visits. People with no natural teeth should still have regular oral examinations, especially if they wear dentures.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe dental pain with facial swelling, fever, or difficulty breathing or swallowing
    \item Uncontrolled bleeding from the mouth after dental treatment
    \item Any serious mouth injury
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Medical and dental history:} review of health conditions, medicines, habits, and previous dental treatment
    \item \textbf{Clinical examination:} inspection and probing of teeth and gums
    \item \textbf{X-rays:} bitewing X-rays for decay between teeth and panoramic X-rays for overall assessment
    \item \textbf{Periodontal examination:} measurement of gum pockets and bleeding
    \item \textbf{Oral cancer screening:} examination of the soft tissues, lips, and neck
    \item \textbf{Referral:} to specialists when complex problems are found
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Professional cleaning:} removal of plaque and calculus, and polishing
    \item \textbf{Fluoride treatment:} applications to strengthen enamel in people at high risk of decay
    \item \textbf{Fissure sealants:} protective coatings for the chewing surfaces of children's back teeth
    \item \textbf{Preventive advice:} personalised instruction on brushing, flossing, diet, and products such as electric toothbrushes
    \item \textbf{Treatment planning:} scheduling of fillings, scaling, or other treatment identified at the check-up
    \item \textbf{Recall scheduling:} an agreed interval for the next check-up based on risk
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle advice:} smoking cessation and dietary guidance where relevant
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Missed early decay or gum disease if check-ups are skipped
    \item Progression to pain, infection, abscesses, and tooth loss
    \item More complex and expensive treatment when problems are advanced
    \item Undetected oral cancer in people who do not attend
    \item Dental anxiety preventing attendance
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Regular dental check-ups are one of the most effective preventive health measures available. People who attend regularly have lower rates of decay, gum disease, and tooth loss, and treatment costs are lower over time. The check-up provides early detection, professional cleaning, and tailored prevention that keep the mouth healthy for life. The long-term outcome of preventive dental care is excellent.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Book a check-up every 6--12 months, or as recommended by your dentist
    \item Take children for their first check-up when their first teeth appear
    \item Between visits, brush twice daily, floss daily, and limit sugary foods
    \item Prepare for the visit: note any concerns, medicines, and changes in health
    \item Ask questions about your oral health and recommended care
    \item Do not wait for pain before seeing a dentist
\end{itemize}
