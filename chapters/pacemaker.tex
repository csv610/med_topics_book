\chapter{Pacemaker}
\index{Pacemaker}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; seek professional advice for individual care.}

\section*{What it is}
A pacemaker is an implanted device that monitors the heart rhythm and sends electrical impulses when the heartbeat is too slow or conduction is abnormal. It may have one or more leads, or be a leadless device. It does not treat every cause of palpitations or replace other heart treatment.

\section*{Why it may be needed}
A cardiology team may recommend a pacemaker for symptomatic slow heart rate, heart block, or selected rhythm and heart-failure conditions. Assessment includes symptoms, an electrocardiogram, rhythm monitoring, other medical causes, and discussion of alternatives and expected benefit.

\section*{Implantation and follow-up}
Implantation is usually performed under local anaesthetic with monitoring, although the approach depends on the device and person. Follow wound-care and activity instructions. Regular device checks assess the battery, leads, settings, and rhythm; carry the device identification information and tell clinicians before procedures such as MRI.

\section*{Possible complications}
Complications can include bleeding, infection, lead movement, collapsed lung, blood-vessel problems, or device malfunction. Battery replacement is eventually needed. Follow the device team’s advice about activity and electrical or magnetic equipment rather than assuming all devices are unsafe.

\section*{When to seek urgent care}
Seek emergency help for collapse, severe breathlessness, chest pain, sustained palpitations, new confusion, or fainting. Contact the device team promptly for wound redness or drainage, fever, persistent hiccups, swelling, recurrent dizziness, or a new change in symptoms.
