\chapter{Dermatologist}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
A dermatologist is a specialist doctor who diagnoses and treats conditions of the skin, hair, and nails. Dermatology covers a huge range of conditions, from common problems such as acne, eczema, and psoriasis to skin cancers, including melanoma, as well as hair loss and nail disorders. Dermatologists complete medical school and specialist training, and they perform procedures including skin cancer surgery, biopsies, and some cosmetic treatments. This chapter explains what dermatologists do, what conditions they treat, and how to see one.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Skin conditions are among the most common reasons people visit doctors, affecting people of all ages. Skin cancer is particularly significant in Australia, which has one of the highest rates of melanoma and non-melanoma skin cancer in the world, so dermatologists play a central role in skin cancer diagnosis and treatment. Acne affects most adolescents, eczema and psoriasis affect millions, and skin conditions account for a substantial proportion of GP consultations. Dermatologists are relatively few compared with the demand for their services, so waiting lists for specialist care can be long.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Training:} dermatologists complete medical school, hospital training, and a specialist fellowship in dermatology
    \item \textbf{Breadth of conditions:} they manage inflammatory skin disease, skin cancer, infections, hair and nail disorders, and pigmentary conditions
    \item \textbf{Procedures:} dermatologists perform skin checks, biopsies, excision of skin cancers, cryotherapy, and surgical reconstruction
    \item \textbf{Specialist skills:} dermatopathology (microscopic diagnosis), phototherapy, and systemic treatments for severe disease
    \item \textbf{Referral pathway:} in Australia, most patients see a dermatologist through a GP referral, which is needed for Medicare rebates
    \item \textbf{Subspecialties:} dermatologists may focus on skin cancer, paediatric dermatology, or cosmetic dermatology
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A dermatologist provides expert diagnosis when skin problems are severe, persistent, atypical, or not responding to treatment
    \item They assess suspicious moles and skin lesions for cancer
    \item They manage chronic conditions such as psoriasis, eczema, and hidradenitis suppurativa
    \item They perform surgical removal of skin cancers and biopsy of rashes
    \item They treat hair loss, nail disorders, and hyperhidrosis
    \item Consultation includes history, full skin examination, and a management plan
    \item Follow-up may include regular skin checks for high-risk patients
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item GP versus dermatologist care: many skin conditions are managed by GPs, with dermatologists for complex cases
    \item Dermatologist versus other specialists for skin symptoms with other features
    \item Different diagnoses for similar rashes, which is why specialist assessment matters
    \item Cosmetic procedures: dermatologists and other practitioners both offer them, with different qualifications
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a GP first for most skin concerns. A referral to a dermatologist is appropriate for persistent or severe skin disease, suspicious lesions, skin cancer risk, or conditions not responding to treatment. People with many moles, a personal or family history of melanoma, or a history of sun exposure should have regular skin checks, and a dermatologist may be recommended.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A mole or lesion that is bleeding, changing rapidly, or causing symptoms with fever
    \item Sudden widespread rash with fever, blisters, or feeling very unwell
    \item Difficulty breathing or facial swelling suggesting a severe allergic reaction
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Consultation:} detailed history of the skin problem and its treatment
    \item \textbf{Skin examination:} whole-body examination, often with dermoscopy (magnified examination of lesions)
    \item \textbf{Biopsy:} removal of a small sample for laboratory diagnosis
    \item \textbf{Excision:} surgical removal of skin cancers with appropriate margins
    \item \textbf{Photography:} mole mapping for monitoring high-risk patients
    \item \textbf{Patch testing:} for allergic contact dermatitis
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} for conditions with systemic involvement
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Medical treatment:} topical, oral, and injected medications for inflammatory and infectious skin disease
    \item \textbf{Skin cancer surgery:} excision with margin control, including Mohs micrographic surgery for some cancers
    \item \textbf{Phototherapy:} controlled light treatment for psoriasis and eczema
    \item \textbf{Biologics and systemic therapies:} advanced treatments for severe psoriasis, eczema, and other conditions
    \item \textbf{Procedures:} cryotherapy, laser treatment, and other techniques
    \item \textbf{Preventive care:} skin checks, sun-safety advice, and surveillance for high-risk patients
    \item \textbf{Cosmetic dermatology:} treatments for cosmetic concerns, which are generally not covered by Medicare
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Delays in diagnosis of skin cancer when lesions are not assessed promptly
    \item Progression of chronic skin disease without specialist care
    \item Complications of treatments, including surgical complications and side effects of systemic therapy
    \item Scarring and cosmetic concerns after procedures
    \item Cost and waiting times for specialist care
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Most skin conditions are effectively diagnosed and treated by dermatologists, with excellent outcomes for the majority of patients. Skin cancer outcomes depend on early detection, and regular skin checks by a dermatologist dramatically improve the chance of detecting melanoma and other cancers early, when they are highly curable. Chronic conditions such as psoriasis and eczema can be well controlled with modern treatments. Overall, specialist dermatology care offers high-quality, effective management for the full range of skin disease.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Protect skin from the sun with sunscreen, clothing, hats, and shade
    \item Check your skin regularly and see a doctor for new or changing lesions
    \item Attend recommended skin checks if you have risk factors
    \item See a GP first for most skin problems
    \item Follow treatment plans and attend specialist follow-up
    \item Manage chronic conditions with daily care as advised
    \item Ask your doctor about whether a dermatology referral is warranted
\end{itemize}
