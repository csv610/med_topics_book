\chapter{Pituitary gland}
\index{Pituitary gland}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; seek specialist advice for individual care.}

\section*{What it does}
The pituitary gland is a small gland at the base of the brain. It releases hormones that influence the thyroid, adrenal glands, reproductive organs, growth, breast milk production, and the body’s water balance.

\section*{Possible problems}
Pituitary disorders may cause too much or too little hormone. Symptoms depend on the hormone involved and may include changes in growth, periods, fertility, sexual function, weight, energy, thirst, urination, blood pressure, or body temperature. A larger tumour may cause headaches or visual-field changes by pressing on nearby structures.

\section*{Assessment}
Assessment may include a detailed history, examination, hormone blood tests, visual-field testing, and MRI. Hormone results must be interpreted in context, and medicines, pregnancy, stress, and other illnesses can affect them. An endocrinology team may coordinate care with ophthalmology or neurosurgery.

\section*{Treatment}
Treatment depends on the disorder and may include hormone replacement, medicines to reduce hormone production or shrink a tumour, surgery, radiation, or monitoring. Do not start, stop, or change steroid or other hormone treatment without specialist advice because some deficiencies can be life-threatening.

\section*{When to seek urgent care}
Seek emergency help for a sudden severe headache, sudden loss or change of vision, confusion, collapse, severe vomiting, extreme weakness, or rapidly worsening illness. People prescribed emergency steroid treatment should follow their individual sick-day and emergency plan.
