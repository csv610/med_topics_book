\chapter{Porphyria}
\index{Porphyria}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. Suspected acute porphyria requires prompt specialist or emergency assessment.}

\section*{What it is}
Porphyrias are uncommon disorders in which the body cannot make haem normally. Different types mainly cause acute attacks affecting the nervous system and abdomen, skin sensitivity to light, or both. Many are inherited, but symptoms and inheritance vary by type.

\section*{Symptoms}
An acute attack may cause severe abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, constipation, dark urine, fast heart rate, high blood pressure, anxiety, confusion, weakness, seizures, or breathing muscle weakness. Cutaneous porphyria may cause fragile skin, blisters, pain, or burning after light exposure. Symptoms overlap with many other conditions.

\section*{Diagnosis}
Testing depends on the presentation and type. During a suspected acute attack, urine porphobilinogen and related tests are collected promptly, often with protection from light. Blood, urine, stool, and genetic tests may be used for other types. A specialist should interpret results; a family member’s diagnosis does not prove that symptoms have the same cause.

\section*{Treatment and prevention}
Acute attacks may require hospital treatment, pain and nausea control, correction of fluid or electrolyte problems, and specific haem treatment. Severe weakness, seizures, confusion, or breathing problems need urgent care. Avoid fasting, crash diets, alcohol, smoking, and known personal triggers. Check every new prescription, over-the-counter medicine, and supplement with a clinician or a trusted porphyria medicines database.

\section*{Long-term care}
Specialist follow-up may monitor kidney, liver, blood pressure, mental health, and recurrent attacks. A written emergency information card or medical alert can help clinicians choose safe medicines. Genetic counselling may help relatives understand testing and inheritance.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek emergency help for severe unexplained abdominal pain with vomiting, confusion, seizures, new weakness, dark urine with acute symptoms, inability to breathe normally, or rapidly worsening illness.
