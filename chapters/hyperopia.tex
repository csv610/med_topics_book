\chapter{Hyperopia}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Hyperopia, commonly called long-sightedness, is a common vision condition in which nearby objects appear blurry while distant objects are seen clearly. It occurs when the eyeball is too short or the cornea is too flat, so light focuses behind the retina instead of on it. Many people, especially children, have mild hyperopia that is corrected naturally by the eye's focusing muscle and causes no symptoms. When hyperopia is significant, it causes blurred near vision, eyestrain, and headaches, and it is corrected with glasses, contact lenses, or surgery.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Hyperopia is common, affecting around 5--10\% of Australian children and up to a third of adults to some degree. Many people have mild hyperopia that needs no correction, and the eye's ability to compensate decreases with age, which is why some people first notice symptoms in their forties. Significant hyperopia in childhood can cause squinting (strabismus) and lazy eye (amblyopia) if uncorrected, which is why vision screening in children matters.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Eye anatomy:} the eyeball is shorter than average, or the cornea is too flat
    \item \textbf{Refraction:} light entering the eye focuses behind the retina, so near objects are blurred
    \item \textbf{Accommodation:} the eye's lens changes shape to focus; young eyes can compensate for mild hyperopia
    \item \textbf{Ageing:} with age, the lens stiffens and compensation is lost
    \item \textbf{Genetics:} hyperopia runs in families
    \item \textbf{Pathophysiology:} the image is formed behind the retina, and the eye works harder to focus, causing strain
    \item \textbf{High hyperopia:} causes constant blur and can impair vision development in children
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Blurred vision for close work such as reading and screens
    \item Eyestrain, eye discomfort, and fatigue after near work
    \item Headaches, especially at the end of the day
    \item Squinting to see clearly
    \item Children may rub their eyes, avoid close work, or have poor attention
    \item In children, a turning eye (squint) or lazy eye
    \item Many people with mild hyperopia have no symptoms
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Myopia (short-sightedness), which blurs distance vision
    \item Presbyopia, the age-related loss of near focusing that affects everyone over 40
    \item Astigmatism, which blurs vision at all distances
    \item Other causes of headaches and eyestrain
    \item Eye examinations distinguish these conditions
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See an optometrist or ophthalmologist for blurred vision, eyestrain, or headaches, and for any concern about a child's vision. Children should have vision screening at routine checks.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden loss of vision
    \item Eye pain, redness, or injury
    \item Double vision or flashing lights
    \item Sudden headache with vision changes
    \item Any emergency eye condition
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Eye examination:} visual acuity testing at distance and near
    \item \textbf{Refraction:} determines the lens power needed
    \item \textbf{Retinoscopy:} objective measurement, especially in children
    \item \textbf{Children:} assessment of eye alignment and vision development
    \item \textbf{Regular reviews:} prescriptions change with age
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Glasses:} the standard treatment, worn for near work or all the time
    \item \textbf{Contact lenses:} for those who prefer them
    \item \textbf{Children:} glasses may be needed to prevent squint and lazy eye
    \item \textbf{Refractive surgery:} laser procedures (LASIK, PRK) can correct hyperopia in suitable adults
    \item \textbf{Patching:} for amblyopia in children with a lazy eye
    \item \textbf{No treatment needed:} for mild asymptomatic hyperopia
    \item \textbf{Regular eye checks:} to update prescriptions
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Eyestrain, headaches, and fatigue with uncorrected significant hyperopia
    \item Squint and amblyopia in uncorrected children
    \item Reduced performance at school or work
    \item Falls and accidents from uncorrected vision
    \item Complications of surgery, which are uncommon
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Hyperopia is easily corrected with glasses or contact lenses, and the outlook is excellent. Children treated early avoid squint and lazy eye and develop normal vision. Laser surgery provides permanent correction for suitable adults. Prescriptions may change over time, but regular eye checks keep vision clear at all ages.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Have regular eye examinations, including children's vision screening
    \item Wear glasses or contact lenses as prescribed
    \item Take breaks from close work to reduce eyestrain
    \item Use good lighting for reading and screens
    \item Attend follow-up eye checks
    \item Seek early review of any vision changes
    \item Protect eyes from injury with safety glasses where appropriate
    \item Discuss surgical options with an eye specialist if interested
\end{itemize}
