\chapter{Ectopic Pregnancy}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
An ectopic pregnancy occurs when a fertilised egg implants and grows outside the uterus, most commonly in a fallopian tube. The pregnancy cannot survive in this location, and if it grows, it can rupture the tube, causing life-threatening internal bleeding. Ectopic pregnancy is a medical emergency, and early diagnosis is essential. Treatment involves medication to end the pregnancy or surgery to remove it, and with prompt care most women recover fully and can conceive again in the future.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Ectopic pregnancy affects approximately 1 to 2 per cent of all pregnancies and is the leading cause of maternal death in the first trimester. It occurs most often in women aged 25 to 34, and the rate has risen in recent decades, partly because of improved diagnosis and partly because of risk factors such as assisted reproduction. A history of a previous ectopic pregnancy, tubal surgery, or pelvic infection increases the risk significantly.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
An ectopic pregnancy usually results from damage or dysfunction in the fallopian tube that slows or stops the passage of the fertilised egg to the uterus.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Previous ectopic pregnancy:} the strongest risk factor
    \item \textbf{Pelvic infection:} chlamydia and gonorrhoea can damage the tubes, and pelvic inflammatory disease increases risk
    \item \textbf{Tubal surgery:} including sterilisation and previous tubal repair
    \item \textbf{Assisted reproduction:} in vitro fertilisation and other fertility treatments
    \item \textbf{Contraceptive devices:} the IUD reduces the risk of pregnancy overall, but if pregnancy occurs with an IUD in place it is more likely to be ectopic
    \item \textbf{Smoking and age:} both increase risk
    \item \textbf{Mechanism:} the embryo implants in the tubal wall, where it grows; as it enlarges, it can rupture the tube, causing bleeding into the abdomen
    \item \textbf{Other sites:} rare ectopic sites include the cervix, ovary, and abdominal cavity
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Symptoms usually appear between six and ten weeks of pregnancy.
\begin{itemize}
    \item A missed period and a positive pregnancy test
    \item Vaginal bleeding, which may be light and mistaken for a period
    \item Abdominal or pelvic pain, often on one side, which may be sharp or cramping
    \item Shoulder tip pain, caused by blood irritating the diaphragm
    \item Dizziness, faintness, or collapse from internal bleeding
    \item Pain on opening the bowels or urinating
    \item Some women have no symptoms at all until rupture
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
The symptoms of ectopic pregnancy overlap with other early pregnancy problems.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Miscarriage and threatened miscarriage
    \item Normal early pregnancy with pain or spotting
    \item Ovarian cyst, including torsion or rupture
    \item Pelvic inflammatory disease
    \item Appendicitis
    \item Urinary tract infection or kidney stones
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Any pregnant woman with abdominal pain or bleeding in early pregnancy should be assessed urgently by a GP, emergency department, or early pregnancy service.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden severe abdominal or pelvic pain
    \item Shoulder tip pain with abdominal pain and a positive pregnancy test
    \item Collapse, fainting, or severe dizziness
    \item Heavy bleeding with faintness
    \item Severe pain on one side of the abdomen
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Ectopic pregnancy is diagnosed with a combination of tests.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pregnancy test:} confirms the pregnancy
    \item \textbf{Transvaginal ultrasound:} visualises the uterus and tubes to find the pregnancy and check for free fluid, which may indicate bleeding
    \item \textbf{Serial hCG blood tests:} beta-hCG levels that rise abnormally slowly suggest a non-viable or ectopic pregnancy
    \item \textbf{Examination:} assessment of tenderness and signs of internal bleeding
    \item \textbf{Laparoscopy:} used both to diagnose and treat in some cases
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Treatment depends on the size, location, and whether the tube has ruptured.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Expectant management:} a small ectopic pregnancy that is resolving on its own may be monitored without treatment
    \item \textbf{Medical treatment:} methotrexate injection stops the pregnancy from growing, allowing the body to absorb it; suitable for small, unruptured ectopic pregnancies
    \item \textbf{Surgery:} laparoscopic removal of the ectopic pregnancy, either preserving the tube (salpingotomy) or removing it (salpingectomy)
    \item \textbf{Emergency surgery:} for ruptured ectopic pregnancy with internal bleeding, to stop the bleeding and remove the pregnancy
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} monitoring of hCG levels until they return to zero, and counselling about future fertility
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Rupture of the fallopian tube with life-threatening internal bleeding
    \item Shock and, rarely, death if untreated
    \item Loss of a fallopian tube, which can affect future fertility
    \item Recurrence, with a higher risk of another ectopic pregnancy
    \item Psychological effects, including grief and anxiety
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
With prompt diagnosis and treatment, the outlook is very good. The maternal mortality of ectopic pregnancy is low in Australia because of early diagnosis and excellent emergency care. Future fertility depends on the health of the remaining tubes and the cause of the ectopic pregnancy, but many women conceive successfully afterwards, including with assisted reproduction when needed.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Practise safe sex and get tested for chlamydia and gonorrhoea to protect the tubes
    \item Seek early pregnancy care and mention any symptoms of pain or bleeding promptly
    \item If you have had an ectopic pregnancy, discuss early ultrasound in future pregnancies
    \item Attend follow-up appointments until hCG levels return to normal
    \item Give yourself time to recover physically and emotionally
    \item Seek support or counselling if you experience grief or anxiety
\end{itemize}
