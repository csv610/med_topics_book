\chapter{Female Health}
\index{Female Health}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Female health encompasses the full range of physical, mental, and social health issues affecting women and people with female reproductive systems across their lives. It spans reproductive health, including menstruation, contraception, pregnancy, and menopause; breast and gynaecological health; and the ways in which heart disease, osteoporosis, autoimmune disease, and mental health conditions affect women differently from men. Female health is more than the absence of disease: it includes health promotion, screening, and the recognition that social factors shape women's health outcomes. This chapter provides an overview of the key health issues affecting women, the screening programs available, and how to maintain good health through the life stages.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Women live longer than men on average in many countries, but spend more of their later years living with illness. Heart disease, stroke, and dementia are leading causes of death in women, while breast cancer is the most commonly diagnosed cancer, affecting 1 in 7 women. One in three women experiences intimate partner violence or sexual violence in her lifetime, and women have higher rates of anxiety, depression, and autoimmune diseases than men. Around 12\% of women experience infertility, and menstrual conditions such as endometriosis affect up to 1 in 9. These patterns change across the lifespan, and preventive care, particularly screening, has dramatically reduced deaths from cervical and breast cancer.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The health issues women face arise from biological, hormonal, and social factors.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reproductive hormones:} oestrogen and progesterone drive the menstrual cycle, pregnancy, and menopause, and influence bone density, blood vessels, and the brain
    \item \textbf{Menstrual health:} the cycle involves complex signalling between the brain, pituitary, and ovaries, and can be disrupted by hormonal, structural, and inflammatory conditions
    \item \textbf{Pregnancy and childbirth:} the physiological demands of pregnancy, and complications such as pre-eclampsia and gestational diabetes, have long-term health implications
    \item \textbf{Menopause:} falling oestrogen at menopause affects bone density, cardiovascular risk, vaginal health, and symptoms such as hot flushes
    \item \textbf{Hormone-sensitive disease:} breast and gynaecological cancers respond to hormonal and genetic factors, including BRCA mutations
    \item \textbf{Immune differences:} women have stronger immune responses, which protect against some infections but drive higher rates of autoimmune disease
    \item \textbf{Social determinants:} caregiving roles, gender-based violence, and inequities in work and income shape women's health and access to care
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Health concerns change across the life stages, and the "features" vary accordingly.
\begin{itemize}
    \item In adolescence: menstruation, period pain, irregular cycles, and pelvic and sexual health education
    \item In reproductive years: contraception, fertility, pregnancy care, menstrual disorders, endometriosis, and polycystic ovary syndrome
    \item In midlife: perimenopause and menopausal symptoms, including hot flushes, sleep disturbance, and mood changes
    \item Breast health: lumps, pain, and discharge, with breast screening from age 40--50
    \item Gynaecological symptoms: abnormal bleeding, pelvic pain, discharge, and prolapse
    \item Urinary health: incontinence and urinary tract infections, which are common and treatable
    \item Mental health: anxiety, depression, and postnatal mood disorders
    \item Long-term health: heart disease, osteoporosis, and dementia risk in later life
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Because symptoms such as pelvic pain and abnormal bleeding have many causes, careful assessment is needed.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Menstrual disorders: fibroids, polyps, endometriosis, adenomyosis, and polycystic ovary syndrome
    \item Abnormal bleeding: hormonal imbalance, pregnancy-related conditions, infection, and, importantly, gynaecological cancer in older women
    \item Pelvic pain: gynaecological, urinary, bowel, and musculoskeletal causes
    \item Breast lumps: benign conditions, cysts, and cancer
    \item Fatigue and mood changes: hormone-related, mental health, thyroid disease, and anaemia
    \item Urinary symptoms: infection, overactive bladder, and prolapse
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Women should see a doctor for new or unusual bleeding, persistent pelvic pain, breast changes, symptoms that affect quality of life, or concerns about mental health. Regular preventive visits are important at every age.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Very heavy vaginal bleeding soaking pads within an hour, especially with faintness
    \item Severe abdominal or pelvic pain
    \item Bleeding in pregnancy with pain or faintness, which may indicate ectopic pregnancy or miscarriage
    \item Chest pain, sudden breathlessness, or signs of stroke
    \item Signs of sepsis after childbirth or gynaecological procedures: fever with severe pain, confusion, or collapse
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History and examination:} menstrual, obstetric, gynaecological, sexual, and family history; blood pressure and body mass index; breast and pelvic examination as indicated
    \item \textbf{Cervical screening:} HPV-based cervical screening every 5 years from age 25 to 74
    \item \textbf{Breast screening:} mammograms every 2 years for women aged 50--74 through the local breast-screening programme, with surveillance for higher-risk women from 40
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} full blood count, iron studies, thyroid function, and hormone profiles as indicated
    \item \textbf{Pelvic ultrasound:} for bleeding, pain, or suspected structural conditions
    \item \textbf{Imaging and biopsy:} mammography, ultrasound, and biopsy for breast concerns; endometrial sampling for abnormal bleeding in older women
    \item \textbf{Bone density testing:} for women at risk of osteoporosis around menopause
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Menstrual and gynaecological conditions:} hormonal treatments, NSAIDs, intrauterine devices, and surgery where needed, with endometriosis managed by a multidisciplinary approach
    \item \textbf{Contraception and fertility:} a range of contraceptive methods, and investigation and treatment of infertility where desired
    \item \textbf{Menopause:} lifestyle measures, hormone replacement therapy (HRT) after individualised discussion of risks and benefits, and vaginal oestrogen for genitourinary symptoms
    \item \textbf{Cancer treatment:} surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and hormone therapy delivered by specialist teams
    \item \textbf{Cardiovascular risk:} blood pressure, cholesterol, and diabetes management, which are under-treated in women compared with men
    \item \textbf{Bone health:} calcium, vitamin D, weight-bearing exercise, and treatment of osteoporosis
    \item \textbf{Pelvic floor health:} physiotherapy for incontinence and prolapse, and continence services
    \item \textbf{Mental health care:} psychological therapies, medication, and perinatal mental health services
    \item \textbf{Support for violence:} screening and referral pathways for women experiencing abuse
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Untreated gynaecological cancer, which is why bleeding and screening are taken seriously
    \item Osteoporosis and fractures after menopause
    \item Heart disease and stroke, the leading causes of death in women
    \item Chronic pain and reduced fertility from conditions such as endometriosis
    \item Postnatal depression and other mental health disorders
    \item Complications of pregnancy and the postpartum period, including haemorrhage and sepsis
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The health of women has improved markedly over recent decades. Cervical cancer deaths have fallen by more than half since screening began, and breast cancer survival continues to improve with early detection and better treatment. Menopause symptoms can be effectively managed, osteoporosis prevented, and cardiovascular risk reduced with timely care. The gaps that remain, including under-recognition of heart disease in women and the delays women with endometriosis face in diagnosis, are increasingly the focus of health policy. For most conditions, early detection and consistent preventive care produce excellent outcomes.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Attend cervical screening every 5 years from age 25 and breast screening from age 50
    \item Know your body and report persistent changes in periods, bleeding, pain, or breast tissue
    \item Exercise regularly, including weight-bearing activity for bone health
    \item Eat a balanced diet with adequate calcium and vitamin D, and limit alcohol
    \item Do not smoke, and keep alcohol within guidelines
    \item Manage blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood sugar with regular check-ups
    \item Have a heart health check from age 45 and a bone density assessment when indicated
    \item Seek help early for mental health, relationship, or safety concerns
    \item Talk to a doctor about contraception, fertility, and menopause planning
\end{itemize}
