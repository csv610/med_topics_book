\chapter{Ecstasy}
\index{Ecstasy}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Ecstasy, also known as MDMA, is a synthetic illegal drug that acts as both a stimulant and a hallucinogen. It is taken mainly by young people at parties, dance events, and music festivals, usually as tablets or capsules. Ecstasy produces feelings of euphoria, energy, emotional warmth, and increased sensory awareness, but it carries serious risks, including overheating, dehydration, seizures, and, rarely, death. Because tablets vary widely in strength and may contain other drugs, every dose is unpredictable. This chapter explains how ecstasy works, the risks involved, and what to do in an emergency.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Ecstasy is one of the more commonly used illegal drugs among young people, with use concentrated in people in their late teens and twenties. Use at music festivals and nightclubs is common, and the drug is frequently combined with alcohol and other substances. While most people who try ecstasy do so occasionally and do not become dependent, the drug accounts for a significant share of drug-related emergency presentations and some deaths each year, often from overheating or from tablets of unexpected strength.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
MDMA acts on the brain's chemical messengers to produce its effects.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mechanism:} MDMA triggers the release of serotonin, dopamine, and noradrenaline, producing euphoria, energy, and emotional openness
    \item \textbf{Effects:} increased heart rate, blood pressure, and body temperature; dilated pupils; reduced appetite and thirst
    \item \textbf{Risks:} the stimulation of physical activity and hot environments can cause dangerous overheating and dehydration
    \item \textbf{Adulteration:} tablets often contain other drugs, including amphetamines, ketamine, or synthetic cathinones, making effects unpredictable
    \item \textbf{After-effects:} serotonin depletion causes low mood, fatigue, and poor concentration in the days after use
    \item \textbf{Tolerance and harms:} repeated use loses its effect, and heavy use is linked to memory problems and mental health difficulties
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
The effects of ecstasy appear within 20 to 60 minutes and last several hours.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Euphoria, increased energy, and feelings of closeness and empathy
    \item Increased heart rate, blood pressure, and body temperature
    \item Sweating, jaw clenching, and teeth grinding
    \item Dilated pupils and sensitivity to light
    \item Nausea and reduced appetite
    \item Anxiety, panic, and confusion in some people
    \item Overheating and dehydration, which can progress to collapse, seizures, or kidney damage
    \item In the following days: low mood, fatigue, and irritability
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
The effects and harms of ecstasy can resemble other conditions.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Other stimulant drugs, including amphetamines and cocaine
    \item Heat-related illness and heatstroke in hot environments
    \item Panic attacks and acute anxiety
    \item Serotonin syndrome from medicines or drug combinations
    \item Meningitis or other infections causing fever and confusion
    \item Psychosis and other mental health emergencies
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Anyone who becomes unwell after taking ecstasy should be assessed by medical staff at the venue or a doctor. People experiencing anxiety, palpitations, or overheating should stop dancing, cool down, and drink water.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Collapse, unconsciousness, or seizures
    \item Very high body temperature, confusion, or agitation
    \item Chest pain, palpitations, or severe breathlessness
    \item Vomiting repeatedly or signs of severe dehydration
    \item Behaviour that is violent, paranoid, or out of control
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Treatment in the emergency setting is guided by the clinical picture.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History:} what was taken, how much, when, and with what else
    \item \textbf{Examination:} temperature, heart rate, blood pressure, hydration, and mental state
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} electrolytes, kidney function, and muscle enzymes if overheating or dehydration is suspected
    \item \textbf{ECG:} to assess the heart if there are palpitations or chest pain
    \item \textbf{Monitoring:} observation for complications such as overheating or seizures
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management focuses on safety, cooling, and supportive care.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cooling and hydration:} moving to a cool area, removing excess clothing, and drinking water
    \item \textbf{Observation and reassurance:} most people recover with rest, fluids, and time
    \item \textbf{Treatment of complications:} intravenous fluids, cooling measures, and medicines for seizures or agitation
    \item \textbf{Aftercare:} support for low mood in the days after use
    \item \textbf{Harm reduction:} honest education about risks, pill testing at festivals, and avoiding mixing drugs
    \item \textbf{Support services:} drug and alcohol counselling for people concerned about their use
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Dangerous overheating and dehydration, which can cause organ failure and death
    \item Seizures and fits
    \item Heart rhythm problems and raised blood pressure
    \item Hyponatraemia, or dangerously low salt levels, from drinking too much water without salt
    \item Serotonin syndrome
    \item Low mood, anxiety, and memory problems after use
    \item Risk-taking behaviour leading to injury
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Most people who take ecstasy do not experience serious harm, and the effects wear off within hours. However, the drug can be fatal, usually through overheating, dehydration, or an unexpectedly strong or adulterated tablet, and every dose carries risk. Serious complications are treatable when emergency care is sought early. Most people who use ecstasy do not develop dependence, but those who do can be helped by drug treatment services.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The safest choice is not to use ecstasy at all
    \item If you do use it: pace yourself, take water but not excessive amounts, and take breaks from dancing
    \item Never mix ecstasy with alcohol, other drugs, or medicines
    \item Go with friends and look out for each other
    \item Seek medical help immediately if anyone becomes unwell
    \item Check in on your mood and sleep in the days after use
    \item Talk to a GP or drug service if use is becoming frequent or concerning
\end{itemize}
