\chapter{Posterior Vaginal Wall Prolapse}
\index{Posterior Vaginal Wall Prolapse}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. Pelvic-floor symptoms are common and deserve confidential assessment.}

\section*{What it is}
A posterior vaginal wall prolapse, often called a rectocele, occurs when the tissue between the rectum and the back wall of the vagina weakens and the rectum bulges towards the vagina. Other types of pelvic organ prolapse can occur at the same time.

\section*{Symptoms}
Some people have no symptoms. Possible symptoms include a feeling of vaginal pressure or a bulge, difficulty emptying the bowel, needing to press near the vagina or perineum to pass stool, constipation, discomfort during sex, or low pelvic or back discomfort. Symptoms may vary and are not specific to prolapse.

\section*{Assessment and treatment}
Assessment usually includes questions about bladder and bowel function and a pelvic examination, with consent and privacy. Treatment depends on symptoms and may include managing constipation, pelvic-floor muscle training with a trained physiotherapist, reducing heavy straining, a vaginal pessary, topical treatment when appropriate, or surgery for selected troublesome cases. A prolapse does not always worsen, and observation is reasonable when symptoms are mild.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Arrange review for a new bulge, persistent difficulty passing stool, bleeding, pain, or urinary or bowel changes. Seek urgent care for severe pelvic pain, inability to pass urine or stool with swelling, heavy bleeding, fever, or a prolapse that becomes very painful or cannot be gently returned as previously instructed.
