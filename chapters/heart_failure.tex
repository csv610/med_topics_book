\chapter{Heart Failure}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Heart failure is a chronic condition in which the heart muscle is unable to pump enough blood to meet the body's needs, or is only able to do so at abnormally high filling pressures. It is not a single disease but a clinical syndrome caused by many different heart conditions, most commonly coronary artery disease, high blood pressure, and valvular disease. Heart failure causes breathlessness, fatigue, and fluid retention, and tends to progress over time. Although it cannot usually be cured, modern treatment substantially improves symptoms, quality of life, and survival.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Heart failure affects approximately 580,000 Australians, and the number is rising with the ageing population and improved survival from heart attacks. It is primarily a condition of older adults, affecting about one in ten people over 75. Around 55,000 Australians are hospitalised with heart failure each year, and it is one of the most common reasons for hospital admission in people over 65. Despite advances, heart failure still carries a significant burden: around half of people with advanced heart failure die within five years of diagnosis, though outcomes are better with modern therapy.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Coronary artery disease:} heart attacks damage the heart muscle, leaving scarred, weakened regions
    \item \textbf{Hypertension:} long-standing high blood pressure forces the heart to work harder and stiffens the muscle
    \item \textbf{Valvular heart disease:} faulty valves overload the heart chambers
    \item \textbf{Cardiomyopathies:} diseases of the heart muscle itself, including inherited, viral, and alcohol-related forms
    \item \textbf{Arrhythmias:} persistent fast rhythms such as atrial fibrillation weaken the heart over time
    \item \textbf{Pathophysiology:} reduced pumping (systolic failure) or impaired relaxation (diastolic failure with preserved ejection fraction) raises pressures behind the heart, causing lung congestion and fluid retention, while reduced output causes fatigue and poor organ perfusion
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Breathlessness on exertion, which progresses to breathlessness at rest
    \item Orthopnoea: difficulty breathing when lying flat, relieved by propping up
    \item Paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea: waking at night gasping for breath
    \item Swelling of the ankles, legs, and abdomen from fluid retention
    \item Rapid weight gain from fluid accumulation
    \item Fatigue, weakness, and reduced exercise tolerance
    \item Cough, especially at night, and wheeze from fluid in the lungs
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chronic lung disease, such as COPD, can mimic breathlessness and fatigue
    \item Obesity and physical deconditioning cause breathlessness on exertion
    \item Anaemia and thyroid disease can produce fatigue and breathlessness
    \item Kidney disease and venous insufficiency cause leg swelling
    \item Pneumonia and pulmonary embolism present with acute breathlessness
    \item Diagnosis requires objective assessment of cardiac function, not symptoms alone
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor promptly for new or worsening breathlessness, swelling, fatigue, or reduced exercise tolerance, and follow a heart failure action plan for planned medication adjustments when symptoms worsen.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe breathlessness at rest, inability to lie flat, or gasping for air
    \item Chest pain or pressure
    \item Fainting, collapse, or palpitations with dizziness
    \item Rapid, large weight gain with swelling and breathlessness
    \item Confusion or reduced consciousness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Echocardiogram:} the key test, measuring the heart's pumping strength (ejection fraction), chamber sizes, and valve function
    \item \textbf{B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) blood test:} elevated levels support the diagnosis and help exclude heart failure in breathless patients
    \item \textbf{Electrocardiogram:} detects rhythm problems, prior heart attack, and strain patterns
    \item \textbf{Chest X-ray:} shows fluid in the lungs and an enlarged heart
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} kidney function, electrolytes, thyroid, iron studies, and full blood count
    \item \textbf{Exercise testing and cardiac MRI:} in selected cases to refine diagnosis and guide treatment
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Diuretics:} remove excess fluid, rapidly relieving breathlessness and swelling
    \item \textbf{ACE inhibitors or ARBs and beta-blockers:} reduce the heart's workload and improve survival
    \item \textbf{Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists and SGLT2 inhibitors:} additional therapies with proven survival benefit
    \item \textbf{Device therapy:} implantable defibrillators and cardiac resynchronisation therapy for selected patients
    \item \textbf{Surgery:} valve repair or replacement, and coronary revascularisation where appropriate
    \item \textbf{Fluid and salt restriction:} reducing salt intake and monitoring daily weight
    \item \textbf{Exercise and rehabilitation:} supervised cardiac rehabilitation improves symptoms and outcomes
    \item \textbf{Disease management programs:} structured monitoring and education reduce hospitalisations
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Acute decompensation requiring emergency hospital admission
    \item Arrhythmias, including sudden cardiac death
    \item Kidney dysfunction and worsening renal failure
    \item Liver congestion and dysfunction
    \item Anaemia, which worsens symptoms
    \item Malnutrition and muscle wasting in advanced disease
    \item Depression, reduced quality of life, and premature death
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Heart failure is a serious condition, but outcomes have improved substantially with modern therapy. Many people live for years with good quality of life when treatment is optimised and followed consistently. Prognosis depends on the cause, the pumping strength of the heart, the response to treatment, and adherence to medication and lifestyle measures. With comprehensive care, hospitalisations and deaths are significantly reduced. Advanced heart failure is managed with specialist care, and options include mechanical support and transplantation for selected patients.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Control blood pressure, cholesterol, and diabetes to prevent damage to the heart
    \item Quit smoking, limit alcohol, and maintain a heart-healthy diet
    \item Stay active within comfortable limits and take part in cardiac rehabilitation
    \item Take medicines exactly as prescribed and never stop suddenly
    \item Weigh yourself daily and report a gain of 2 kg or more over a few days
    \item Limit salt, avoid excessive fluids if advised, and manage stress
    \item Have an influenza and pneumococcal vaccination and a written action plan
\end{itemize}
