\chapter{Mouth and teeth}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
The mouth (oral cavity) and teeth constitute the initial segment of the gastrointestinal tract and play a critical role in mastication, digestion, speech, and facial structure. The oral cavity is bounded by the lips, cheeks, hard and soft palates, and the throat, and it contains key structures such as the tongue, salivary glands, and dentition. Humans possess two successive sets of teeth: primary (deciduous) teeth numbering 20, and permanent teeth numbering up to 32 (including the third molars or wisdom teeth). Maintaining the anatomical and physiological integrity of the mouth and teeth is essential not only for oral function but also for preventing systemic inflammatory conditions, as oral health is closely linked to cardiovascular health, diabetes management, and pregnancy outcomes.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Oral diseases represent some of the most common chronic conditions globally. According to the World Health Organization, dental caries (tooth decay) affects nearly 3.5 billion people worldwide, with untreated decay of permanent teeth being the single most prevalent health condition. In Australia, oral health surveys indicate that approximately 30\% of adults have untreated tooth decay, and more than 20\% suffer from moderate-to-severe periodontitis (gum disease). Periodontitis prevalence increases significantly with age, affecting over 50\% of adults over the age of 65. Indigenous Australians and individuals residing in rural or low socioeconomic areas experience disproportionately higher rates of dental caries, tooth loss, and limited access to dental care.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Oral diseases arise from the complex interaction of microbial biofilms, dietary habits, host immune responses, and structural anatomy.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Microbial biofilm:} A structured community of microorganisms (dental plaque), including Streptococcus mutans and Porphyromonas gingivalis, that adheres to the tooth surface and gingival tissues.
    \item \textbf{Acid demineralisation:} Fermentation of dietary sugars (sucrose, glucose) by cariogenic bacteria produces organic acids, lowering oral pH and causing demineralisation of the tooth enamel.
    \item \textbf{Chronic gingival inflammation:} Plaque accumulation at the gingival margin triggers an inflammatory host response, leading to gingivitis (reversible) and, if unchecked, periodontitis (destruction of the periodontal ligament and alveolar bone).
    \item \textbf{Salivary gland hypofunction:} Reduced saliva flow (xerostomia) due to medications, ageing, or autoimmune disease (such as Sjögren's syndrome) deprives the mouth of vital buffering capacity, minerals, and antimicrobial proteins.
    \item \textbf{Traumatic and mechanical wear:} Enamel loss due to attrition (bruxism or teeth grinding), abrasion (over-aggressive brushing), or erosion (chemical dissolution by gastric acid or acidic beverages).
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Clinical features of mouth and teeth disorders range from localised pain to visible structural and mucosal changes.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dental pain and sensitivity:} Sharp pain triggered by hot, cold, sweet, or acidic stimuli (dentine hypersensitivity) or constant throbbing pain (acute pulpitis).
    \item \textbf{Gingival bleeding and recession:} Erythematous, oedematous gums that bleed easily upon brushing or flossing, accompanied by recession exposing the tooth roots.
    \item \textbf{Halitosis:} Persistent malodour arising from volatile sulphur compounds produced by anaerobic bacteria on the posterior tongue or in periodontal pockets.
    \item \textbf{Tooth mobility and loss:} Loosening of teeth due to loss of supporting alveolar bone structure in advanced periodontal disease.
    \item \textbf{Mucosal lesions and xerostomia:} Oral dryness, difficulty swallowing, or mucosal changes such as leukoplakia (white patches), erythroplakia (red patches), or aphthous ulcers.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Oral symptoms must be evaluated to distinguish between primary dental pathologies, mucosal diseases, and referred systemic pain.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dental pulpitis vs.\ Maxillary sinusitis:} Maxillary sinus inflammation can present as referred pain to the upper molar teeth, but is distinguished by sinus congestion and pain on percussion of multiple teeth.
    \item \textbf{Trigeminal neuralgia:} Severe, lancinating, neuropathic pain along the trigeminal nerve branches that can mimic acute toothache but lacks clinical dental pathology.
    \item \textbf{Temporomandibular joint dysfunction:} Causes jaw pain, clicking, and restricted opening, which can be mistaken for dental pain.
    \item \textbf{Oral candidiasis:} Fungal infection (thrush) presenting as wipeable white plaques, distinguishing it from non-wipeable leukoplakia.
    \item \textbf{Atypical odontalgia:} Chronic, continuous burning pain in teeth or extraction sites without apparent clinical or radiographic cause.
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Patients should seek regular dental check-ups every 6 to 12 months. Prompt dental assessment is required for toothache, bleeding gums, tooth mobility, or any non-healing oral ulcer lasting longer than two weeks.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Rapidly spreading facial swelling, difficulty swallowing, or compromised airway (suggestive of Ludwig's angina or severe dental space infection).
    \item Avulsion (knocked-out) of a permanent tooth due to trauma, requiring immediate emergency dental replantation (ideally within 30--60 minutes).
    \item Uncontrolled haemorrhage from the mouth or jaw following dental extraction or trauma.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Comprehensive oral assessment combines clinical examination with specialised diagnostic imaging.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Visual and tactile examination:} Using a dental mirror and periodontal probe to inspect tooth surfaces for decay, assess gum pocket depths, and check for mucosal lesions.
    \item \textbf{Dental radiographs:} Bitewing, periapical, or orthopantomogram (OPG) X-rays to detect interproximal caries, evaluate bone levels, and identify apical pathology (such as abscesses).
    \item \textbf{Pulp vitality testing:} Applying thermal (cold/hot) or electrical stimuli to assess the sensory response and health of the dental pulp.
    \item \textbf{Oral biopsy:} Indicated for chronic, suspicious mucosal lesions (leukoplakia, non-healing ulcers) to screen for oral squamous cell carcinoma.
    \item \textbf{Sialometry:} Measurement of salivary flow rate in patients presenting with severe dry mouth.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management of mouth and teeth disorders focuses on restorative, preventative, and surgical interventions.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Restorative dentistry:} Removal of dental caries and placement of restorations (fillings) using composite resin, glass ionomer, or dental amalgam, and root canal therapy for irreversible pulpitis.
    \item \textbf{Periodontal therapy:} Professional scaling and root planing (cleaning) to remove calculus (tartar) and subgingival plaque, coupled with antimicrobial mouthwashes.
    \item \textbf{Surgical procedures:} Extraction of non-restorable teeth or impacted third molars, and surgical placement of dental implants or prosthodontics (bridges, dentures).
    \item \textbf{Fluoride therapy:} Application of topical fluoride varnishes or high-fluoride toothpastes to promote enamel remineralisation and arrest early caries.
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle and dietary counselling:} Restricting intake of free sugars, managing dry mouth with salivary substitutes, and wearing custom occlusal splints for bruxism.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
Untreated oral diseases can lead to severe localised infections, tooth loss, and systemic health complications.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dental abscess and cellulitis:} Spread of infection from the tooth pulp into surrounding fascial spaces (such as Ludwig's angina), which can be life-threatening.
    \item \textbf{Advanced tooth loss:} Impaired masticatory function leading to nutritional deficiencies, speech difficulties, and facial collapse.
    \item \textbf{Systemic inflammation:} Strong associations between chronic periodontitis and increased risk of cardiovascular disease, poorly controlled diabetes, and adverse pregnancy outcomes (such as pre-term birth).
    \item \textbf{Aspiration pneumonia:} Aspiration of pathogenic oral bacteria in elderly or debilitated patients, leading to severe lung infections.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The prognosis for oral health is excellent when preventive practices are maintained and dental interventions are sought early. Superficial dental caries and gingivitis are fully reversible or manageable. Advanced periodontitis requires lifelong maintenance therapy but can be stabilised to prevent further tooth loss. Innovations in restorative materials, implants, and digital dentistry have significantly improved the aesthetic and functional outcomes for patients requiring tooth replacement. Adherence to oral hygiene practices and regular professional care is the single most important factor determining long-term prognosis.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
Effective self-care is the cornerstone of maintaining long-term oral and dental health.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Brush twice daily:} Use a soft-bristled toothbrush and fluoride toothpaste to brush for at least two minutes, ensuring all tooth surfaces are cleaned.
    \item \textbf{Floss daily:} Clean between the teeth daily using dental floss or interdental brushes to remove plaque from areas a toothbrush cannot reach.
    \item \textbf{Limit sugar intake:} Reduce the frequency and amount of sugary and acidic foods and drinks, particularly between meals, to prevent acid attacks.
    \item \textbf{Drink fluoridated water:} Drink tap water where fluoridated, as fluoride strengthens enamel and helps prevent tooth decay.
    \item \textbf{Avoid tobacco products:} Quit smoking and avoid chewing tobacco, which are major risk factors for periodontal disease and oral cancer.
\end{itemize}
