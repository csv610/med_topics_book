\chapter{Overactive bladder}
\index{Overactive bladder}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; seek professional advice for individual care.}

\section*{What it is}
Overactive bladder describes urinary urgency, usually with frequent urination and waking at night, with or without urgency leakage. It may occur with or without urge incontinence. Infection, medicines, constipation, diabetes, neurological disease, prostate or pelvic-floor problems, and excess bladder irritants can cause similar symptoms.

\section*{Symptoms and assessment}
Record urgency, frequency, night-time urination, leakage, pain, blood in urine, fluid intake, medicines, bowel symptoms, and how symptoms affect life. Assessment may include examination, urine testing, bladder diary, and selected tests for residual urine or other causes. Blood in urine or painful urination should not simply be labelled overactive bladder.

\section*{Management}
Treatment may include bladder training, pelvic-floor therapy, adjusting bladder irritants and fluid timing without causing dehydration, treating constipation or infection, and reviewing medicines. If symptoms continue, a clinician may discuss medicines, nerve stimulation, injections, or other procedures. Treatment should reflect pregnancy, age, mobility, prostate or pelvic conditions, and cognitive or neurological health.

\section*{When to seek urgent care}
Seek urgent help for inability to pass urine, blood clots or heavy blood in urine, fever with flank pain, severe abdominal pain, confusion, new leg weakness, or rapidly worsening illness. Arrange review for new, persistent, or bothersome urinary symptoms.
