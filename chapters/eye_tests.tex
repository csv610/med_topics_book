\chapter{Eye Tests}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Eye tests, also called eye examinations, are a series of checks that assess vision and eye health. They measure how clearly the person can see, determine the need for glasses, and examine the eyes for disease such as glaucoma, cataract, and diabetic retinopathy. Eye tests are performed by optometrists and ophthalmologists and are recommended for everyone, with more frequent tests for children, older adults, and people with diabetes or a family history of eye disease. Many eye conditions have no early symptoms, which is why regular tests are essential.

\section*{Epidemiology}
A large proportion of Australians need glasses or contact lenses, and eye disease becomes increasingly common with age. Glaucoma, cataract, and macular degeneration are often silent in the early stages, and diabetic retinopathy can progress without symptoms. Regular eye tests detect these conditions at treatable stages, reducing avoidable vision loss.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Eye tests assess the many functions of the eye.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Visual acuity:} how clearly the eye sees at a distance
    \item \textbf{Refraction:} how the eye focuses light, determining the glasses prescription
    \item \textbf{Eye coordination:} how the two eyes work together
    \item \textbf{Field of vision:} the full area the eye can see
    \item \textbf{Eye pressure:} measured to screen for glaucoma
    \item \textbf{The retina and optic nerve:} examined to detect disease
    \item \textbf{Colour vision:} tested in some circumstances
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Eye tests are recommended at different ages.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Children: tests at school age to detect vision problems that affect learning
    \item Adults: a test every two years, or more often with age or risk factors
    \item People with diabetes: annual tests for diabetic retinopathy
    \item Older adults: more frequent tests for glaucoma, cataract, and macular degeneration
    \item People with symptoms: tests whenever vision changes or symptoms develop
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
A comprehensive eye test covers more than just the prescription.
\begin{itemize}
    \item The eye chart measures acuity, but it does not detect eye disease
    \item Examination of the retina, pressure, and fields is needed for disease detection
    \item A test result is interpreted alongside symptoms and history
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Regular eye tests are part of preventive health care. Urgent assessment is needed for sudden vision loss, severe eye pain, a red painful eye, or sudden floaters with flashes.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden loss of vision
    \item Sudden severe eye pain
    \item A red eye with severe pain and nausea
    \item Sudden floaters with flashes or a shadow in the vision
    \item A chemical injury or penetrating eye injury
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
A comprehensive eye test includes several components.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History:} symptoms, health conditions, medicines, and family history
    \item \textbf{Visual acuity:} reading letters on a chart
    \item \textbf{Refraction:} determining the glasses prescription
    \item \textbf{Slit-lamp examination:} the front of the eye
    \item \textbf{Tonometry:} eye pressure measurement
    \item \textbf{Dilated examination:} the retina and optic nerve
    \item \textbf{Additional tests:} visual fields, OCT, and retinal photography as needed
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
The results guide what happens next.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Prescription:} glasses or contact lenses where needed
    \item \textbf{Normal results:} reassurance and a plan for the next test
    \item \textbf{Eye disease:} referral to an ophthalmologist or treatment
    \item \textbf{Diabetes:} coordination with diabetes care
    \item \textbf{Monitoring:} scheduled follow-up for people at risk
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Missed eye disease when tests are not done
    \item Vision loss from glaucoma, diabetes, or other silent conditions
    \item Falls and reduced independence from poor vision
    \item The impact of an outdated prescription on work and daily life
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Regular eye tests prevent vision loss by detecting disease early. Most eye conditions are treatable when found in time, and the prescription is easily corrected with glasses. Eye tests are quick, painless, and covered by most health funds and Medicare in appropriate circumstances.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Book regular eye tests at every age
    \item Have annual eye tests if you have diabetes
    \item Test more often if you have a family history of eye disease
    \item Report any vision change between tests
    \item Protect the eyes from sun and injury
    \item Bring your glasses and a list of medicines to the test
    \item Follow the recommendations for follow-up
\end{itemize}
