\chapter{Groin Pain or Swelling}
\index{Groin Pain or Swelling}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Groin pain or swelling is a common symptom with many possible causes. The groin is the area where the lower abdomen meets the thigh, and it contains important structures including muscles, tendons, lymph nodes, blood vessels, and the inguinal canal, through which the spermatic cord passes in men and the round ligament in women. Causes range from muscle strain, hernias, and swollen lymph nodes to testicular, hip, kidney, and urinary conditions, and in some cases serious emergencies such as a strangulated hernia or testicular torsion. Because the causes vary so widely, groin pain or swelling should be assessed by a doctor, particularly if it is sudden, severe, or accompanied by other symptoms.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Groin pain and swelling are extremely common reasons for visiting a GP. Muscle and tendon strains are among the most frequent causes, particularly in people who play sports involving running, kicking, and changes of direction. Inguinal hernias affect around 25\% of men at some stage and are the most common cause of a groin lump in men. Swollen lymph nodes in the groin commonly follow leg or foot infections. Testicular torsion, one of the most serious causes of acute groin pain in young men, affects about 1 in 4,000 males under 25 and requires emergency treatment.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The many causes of groin pain and swelling reflect the structures in the region.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Muscle and tendon injury:} strains of the adductor (groin) muscles and hip flexors from sport or sudden movement
    \item \textbf{Hernia:} abdominal contents bulge through a weakness in the abdominal wall into the groin (inguinal or femoral hernia)
    \item \textbf{Lymph nodes:} enlarged nodes from infections of the leg, foot, or genitals, or from malignancy
    \item \textbf{Testicular problems:} torsion (twisting of the testicle and its blood supply), epididymitis, and tumours
    \item \textbf{Hip problems:} osteoarthritis, labral tears, and fractures referring pain to the groin
    \item \textbf{Kidney and urinary:} kidney stones and urinary tract infections causing referred pain
    \item \textbf{Other:} gynaecological conditions in women, including ovarian cysts and ectopic pregnancy
    \item \textbf{Emergencies:} strangulated or incarcerated hernia, testicular torsion, and ectopic pregnancy require urgent care
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
The features of groin pain or swelling depend on the cause.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Aching or sharp pain in the groin, which may be worse with activity, coughing, or lifting
    \item A lump or bulge in the groin that may appear on standing and disappear when lying down (hernia)
    \item Swollen, tender lymph nodes, often with a leg or foot infection
    \item Testicular pain, swelling, or a heavy feeling (testicular conditions)
    \item Pain with hip movement or weight-bearing (hip conditions)
    \item Radiating pain to the back or inner thigh (kidney or hip causes)
    \item Fever, redness, or a warm area suggesting infection
    \item Nausea, vomiting, or an inability to pass gas or stool with a tender lump (strangulated hernia)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Groin pain has a broad differential, and identifying the cause is essential.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Inguinal and femoral hernias
    \item Adductor and hip flexor strains
    \item Hip osteoarthritis, labral tears, and femoral neck stress fractures
    \item Testicular torsion, epididymitis, orchitis, and testicular cancer
    \item Swollen inguinal lymph nodes from infection or malignancy
    \item Kidney stones, urinary tract infection, and pyelonephritis
    \item In women: ovarian cysts, pelvic inflammatory disease, and ectopic pregnancy
    \item Osteitis pubis and sports hernias
    \item Referred pain from the lumbar spine
    \item Deep vein thrombosis and other vascular conditions
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor for groin pain or swelling that is persistent, worsening, or associated with a lump, fever, or other symptoms. Seek urgent care for sudden severe pain, a tender lump that cannot be pushed back, or testicular pain in a young male.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden, severe groin or testicular pain, which may indicate testicular torsion (a surgical emergency)
    \item A tender, firm groin lump with vomiting, abdominal pain, or the inability to pass gas or stool, which may indicate a strangulated hernia
    \item Sudden severe pelvic or abdominal pain in a woman who may be pregnant, which may indicate an ectopic pregnancy
    \item Fever with spreading redness, or signs of sepsis
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Diagnosis is guided by history and examination, with tests as needed.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clinical history:} onset, nature of the pain, aggravating factors, and associated symptoms
    \item \textbf{Examination:} inspection and palpation of the groin, checking for lumps, tenderness, and hernias, with the person standing and lying down
    \item \textbf{Urine tests:} for infection or blood
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} inflammatory markers, and tumour markers or other tests where relevant
    \item \textbf{Ultrasound:} the key imaging test, showing hernias, testicular problems, lymph nodes, and hip effusions
    \item \textbf{CT or MRI:} for deeper structures, hip problems, or suspected complications
    \item \textbf{Scrotal Doppler ultrasound:} urgent in suspected testicular torsion
    \item \textbf{Referral:} to surgeons, urologists, gynaecologists, or orthopaedic specialists as indicated
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Treatment depends entirely on the underlying cause.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Muscle strain:} rest, ice, graduated return to activity, and physiotherapy
    \item \textbf{Hernia:} elective repair (herniotomy) is usually recommended, especially if symptomatic; a strangulated hernia requires emergency surgery
    \item \textbf{Lymph node infection:} treatment of the underlying infection
    \item \textbf{Testicular torsion:} emergency surgery to untwist and fix the testicle
    \item \textbf{Epididymitis:} antibiotics and pain relief
    \item \textbf{Hip conditions:} physiotherapy, pain management, and surgery where appropriate
    \item \textbf{Kidney stones:} pain relief and specialist management
    \item \textbf{Gynaecological conditions:} treatment appropriate to the cause
    \item \textbf{Testicular cancer:} urgent specialist referral and treatment
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Strangulation of a hernia, which is life-threatening if untreated
    \item Loss of a testicle if torsion is not treated within hours
    \item Ruptured ectopic pregnancy with life-threatening bleeding
    \item Chronic groin pain and reduced sporting performance from untreated strains
    \item Delayed diagnosis of testicular or other cancer
    \item Progression of infections to abscess or sepsis
    \item Complications of surgery where needed
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The outlook depends on the cause. Muscle strains resolve over days to weeks with appropriate rehabilitation. Hernias are repaired safely and effectively. Testicular torsion and ectopic pregnancy have excellent outcomes when treated within hours, and poor outcomes when delayed. Most causes of groin pain are benign and treatable, and the key to good outcomes is an accurate diagnosis, which is why persistent or severe symptoms should always be assessed by a doctor.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Warm up and stretch before sport, and build groin muscle strength gradually
    \item Use proper technique in sports involving kicking and changes of direction
    \item See a doctor for any new lump in the groin, especially one that changes size
    \item Check your testicles regularly for lumps, and seek prompt review of any change
    \item Seek urgent care for sudden severe groin or testicular pain
    \item Treat leg and foot infections promptly to prevent lymph node problems
    \item Maintain a healthy weight, which reduces strain on the groin and hernia risk
    \item Avoid heavy lifting with poor technique
    \item Follow physiotherapy and rehabilitation advice after a groin strain
\end{itemize}
