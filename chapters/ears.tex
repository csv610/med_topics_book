\chapter{Ears}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
The ears are the organs of hearing and balance. Each ear has three parts: the outer ear, which collects sound; the middle ear, which transmits sound vibrations through the eardrum and tiny bones; and the inner ear, which converts vibrations into nerve signals for hearing and contains the balance organs. Understanding how the ears work explains why they are vulnerable to infection, noise damage, and ageing, and why protecting them matters. This chapter provides an overview of ear structure and function, common problems, and the basics of ear health.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Ear and hearing problems are among the most common health concerns across the lifespan. Middle ear infections are common in early childhood, noise-related hearing damage affects many people exposed to loud sound at work or leisure, and age-related hearing loss affects a large proportion of older adults. Hearing loss is a leading contributor to years lived with disability worldwide, and balance disorders become more common with age.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The ear works through a series of carefully coordinated steps.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Outer ear:} the pinna and ear canal collect and funnel sound waves to the eardrum; the canal also produces protective wax
    \item \textbf{Middle ear:} sound waves vibrate the eardrum, and three tiny bones, the ossicles, transmit and amplify the vibration to the inner ear; the eustachian tube equalises pressure with the throat
    \item \textbf{Inner ear:} the cochlea converts vibrations into electrical signals sent to the brain along the auditory nerve; the semicircular canals and vestibule sense head position and movement for balance
    \item \textbf{Conductive hearing loss:} problems in the outer or middle ear prevent sound reaching the inner ear, as with wax, infection, or otosclerosis
    \item \textbf{Sensorineural hearing loss:} damage to the cochlea, auditory nerve, or brain pathways, as with ageing, noise, and certain medicines
    \item \textbf{Balance:} the inner ear balance organs send constant information to the brain, and disruption causes vertigo and dizziness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Symptoms of ear problems reflect the functions of hearing and balance.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Hearing loss, which may be gradual, sudden, one-sided, or both-sided
    \item Ear pain, discharge, itching, or fullness
    \item Tinnitus, or ringing, buzzing, or hissing in the ears
    \item Dizziness, vertigo, or a feeling of imbalance
    \item In children: speech delay, inattention, pulling at the ears, and recurrent infections
    \item Muffled hearing after exposure to loud noise
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Ear symptoms can be confused with other conditions.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Head and neck pain from dental, jaw, throat, or neck problems
    \item Neurological causes of dizziness, including migraine and vestibular conditions
    \item Systemic conditions affecting hearing, including diabetes and autoimmune disease
    \item Medications that affect the ears, including some antibiotics and diuretics
    \item Psychological factors such as anxiety amplifying tinnitus
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor for new or worsening hearing loss, ear pain, discharge, persistent tinnitus, or dizziness that affects daily life. Hearing should be checked regularly with age and after significant noise exposure.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden hearing loss, especially in one ear
    \item Sudden severe vertigo with vomiting or inability to walk
    \item Severe ear pain with swelling behind the ear or facial weakness
    \item Fluid or blood from the ear after a head injury
    \item Signs of serious infection with fever and confusion
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Assessment of ear problems combines examination and specialised tests.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Otoscopy:} examination of the ear canal and eardrum
    \item \textbf{Hearing tests:} pure-tone audiometry and speech testing measure the type and degree of hearing loss
    \item \textbf{Tympanometry:} assesses middle ear function
    \item \textbf{Balance testing:} vestibular assessment for dizziness
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} CT or MRI for suspected structural problems
    \item \textbf{Referral:} to an ear, nose, and throat specialist or audiologist when needed
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management depends on the problem identified.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Infections:} appropriate treatment, which is often watchful waiting with pain relief
    \item \textbf{Hearing loss:} hearing aids, assistive listening devices, cochlear implants, and communication strategies
    \item \textbf{Tinnitus:} sound therapy, hearing aids, and psychological support
    \item \textbf{Vertigo:} vestibular rehabilitation and medicines for acute attacks
    \item \textbf{Wax:} professional removal when impacted
    \item \textbf{Surgery:} for conditions such as otosclerosis, cholesteatoma, and chronic infection
    \item \textbf{Prevention:} noise protection and hearing surveillance
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Permanent hearing loss from untreated infections, noise, or disease
    \item Speech and language delay in children
    \item Falls and injury from balance disorders
    \item Social isolation, depression, and reduced quality of life from hearing loss
    \item Chronic infection and cholesteatoma
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The prognosis for ear problems is good when they are recognised and managed early. Many infections and wax problems resolve completely, and hearing aids effectively manage most hearing loss. Newborn hearing screening and early intervention for children protect speech development. Protecting the ears from noise and seeking prompt care give the best long-term outcomes.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Protect ears from loud noise with earplugs or ear muffs
    \item Keep personal listening devices at a safe volume
    \item Dry ears after swimming and bathing
    \item Never insert cotton buds or other objects into the ears
    \item Have hearing checked with age and after noise exposure
    \item Seek early treatment for ear infections
    \item Keep children's immunisations up to date
\end{itemize}
