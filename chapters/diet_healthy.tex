\chapter{Healthy Diet}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
A healthy diet provides the body with the right balance of foods and nutrients to maintain health, energy, and wellbeing, and to reduce the risk of chronic disease. The Australian Dietary Guidelines recommend eating a wide variety of nutritious foods from the five food groups: vegetables and legumes, fruit, grain foods, lean meats and alternatives, and dairy or dairy alternatives, while limiting foods high in saturated fat, added salt, and added sugar. A healthy diet is about overall patterns over time rather than single foods, and it works alongside physical activity, sleep, and healthy weight. This chapter explains what a healthy diet looks like and how to put it into practice.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Poor diet is one of the leading contributors to ill health in Australia, linked to heart disease, type 2 diabetes, some cancers, and obesity, which affects around two-thirds of Australian adults. Most Australians do not eat the recommended amounts of vegetables and fruit, and many consume too much sugar, salt, and processed food. Improving diet at a population level is a major public health priority, and healthy eating is also associated with better mental health and quality of life.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Five food groups:} vegetables and legumes, fruit, grain foods, lean meats and alternatives, and dairy or alternatives
    \item \textbf{Vegetables and fruit:} rich in vitamins, minerals, fibre, and antioxidants, and linked to lower disease risk
    \item \textbf{Grain foods:} choose wholegrain and high-fibre varieties for energy and gut health
    \item \textbf{Protein foods:} lean meats, fish, eggs, legumes, nuts, and tofu support muscles and repair
    \item \textbf{Dairy:} provides calcium and protein; reduced-fat varieties are recommended for most adults
    \item \textbf{Limit:} foods high in saturated fat, added sugar, and salt, including fast food, soft drinks, cakes, and biscuits
    \item \textbf{Hydration:} water is the best drink; limit sugary drinks and alcohol
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A healthy diet is balanced across food groups and appropriate to age, sex, and activity
    \item Serving sizes differ between groups and life stages
    \item Foods high in fat, sugar, and salt should be eaten only sometimes and in small amounts
    \item A healthy diet supports energy, concentration, and stable mood
    \item It contributes to a healthy weight, healthy heart, and strong bones
    \item Dietary needs change through life, including in pregnancy, childhood, and older age
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Healthy eating versus fad diets and restrictive plans, which are not evidence-based
    \item Dietary guidance for general health versus medical nutrition therapy for conditions such as diabetes or kidney disease
    \item Food allergies and intolerances, which require specific avoidance rather than general restriction
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Most people can improve their diet with general guidance, but people with medical conditions, special dietary needs, or concerns about nutrition should seek advice from a doctor or accredited practising dietitian.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe allergic reactions to food, with difficulty breathing or swelling
    \item Choking on food
    \item Any emergency related to food or drink
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dietary assessment:} review of eating patterns against the Australian Dietary Guidelines
    \item \textbf{Health assessment:} weight, blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood glucose where relevant
    \item \textbf{Nutritional tests:} blood tests for iron, vitamin D, or other nutrients when deficiencies are suspected
    \item \textbf{Dietitian referral:} for individualised advice and medical nutrition therapy
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Eat a variety of foods:} include vegetables and fruit at every meal
    \item \textbf{Choose wholegrains:} wholegrain breads, rice, and pasta for fibre
    \item \textbf{Include protein:} lean meats, fish, eggs, legumes, and nuts
    \item \textbf{Include dairy or alternatives:} for calcium, choosing reduced-fat where appropriate
    \item \textbf{Limit discretionary foods:} foods high in fat, sugar, and salt
    \item \textbf{Drink water:} and limit sugary drinks and alcohol
    \item \textbf{Plan meals:} cooking at home helps control ingredients
    \item \textbf{Read labels:} check saturated fat, sugar, and sodium content
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Overweight and obesity from excess energy intake
    \item Heart disease, stroke, and type 2 diabetes
    \item Some cancers, including bowel cancer
    \item Nutrient deficiencies when the diet is limited or unbalanced
    \item Poor bone health and dental disease
    \item Reduced energy, concentration, and mental health
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
A healthy diet is one of the most powerful ways to protect health, reduce disease risk, and improve quality of life. Improvements in diet can lower blood pressure and cholesterol, improve blood glucose, support weight management, and reduce the risk of chronic disease at any age. Healthy eating is achievable for most people with practical planning, and small sustainable changes accumulate into significant long-term benefits.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Eat at least five serves of vegetables and two serves of fruit each day
    \item Fill half the plate with vegetables at main meals
    \item Choose wholegrain options and limit refined grains
    \item Limit fast food, sugary drinks, and packaged snacks
    \item Cook more meals at home and plan ahead
    \item Eat mindfully and watch portion sizes
    \item Drink water as the main drink
    \item Seek professional advice for individualised guidance
\end{itemize}
