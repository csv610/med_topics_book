\chapter{Parvovirus B19}
\index{Parvovirus B19}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; seek professional advice for individual care.}

\section*{What it is}
Parvovirus B19 is a human virus spread mainly through respiratory secretions and, less commonly, blood or during pregnancy from a pregnant person to the fetus. In healthy children it often causes fifth disease, a mild rash illness. Adults may develop joint pain, and some people have no symptoms.

\section*{Who needs extra care}
People with a chronic blood disorder that causes haemolysis, a weakened immune system, or pregnancy may need medical advice after exposure or symptoms. Infection can rarely cause severe anaemia or fetal complications. A rash often appears after the period when a person is most contagious, so exposure history still matters.

\section*{Diagnosis and treatment}
Diagnosis is often clinical. Antibody or viral-DNA testing may be used when pregnancy, immune suppression, chronic anaemia, or an atypical illness makes the result important. Most healthy people need rest, fluids, and symptom relief. Severe anaemia or persistent infection requires specialist treatment.

\section*{When to seek medical care}
Contact a clinician promptly after exposure or new rash or joint pain during pregnancy, or if the person has a chronic blood disorder or weakened immune system. Seek urgent care for marked pallor, severe tiredness, breathlessness, fainting, chest pain, or rapidly worsening illness.

\section*{Prevention}
There is no vaccine or specific preventive medicine. Hand hygiene, respiratory etiquette, cleaner air, and staying home while acutely unwell can reduce spread. Follow local public-health and clinical advice for high-risk settings.
