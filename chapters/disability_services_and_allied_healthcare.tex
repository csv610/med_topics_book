\chapter{Disability Services and Allied Healthcare}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Disability services are supports provided to people with disability to help them live independently, participate in the community, and achieve their goals. Allied healthcare is the term for health professions other than doctors and nurses, including physiotherapists, occupational therapists, speech pathologists, psychologists, and social workers, who play a central role in supporting people with disability. In Australia, the National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) funds many disability supports, while other services are provided through the health system. This chapter explains the types of disability services, the allied health professions involved, and how to access support.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Around one in six Australians has some form of disability, and the NDIS supports over 500,000 participants. People with disability have higher rates of health problems and face barriers to health care, and allied health services are essential for rehabilitation, daily living support, and communication. Demand for disability services has grown with the NDIS, and allied health professionals work across disability, rehabilitation, aged care, and community settings. Many people with disability need coordinated care across multiple services.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Types of disability:} physical, intellectual, sensory, psychosocial, and developmental disabilities
    \item \textbf{Physiotherapy:} improves movement, strength, and function
    \item \textbf{Occupational therapy:} helps people perform daily activities and adapt their environment
    \item \textbf{Speech pathology:} supports communication and swallowing
    \item \textbf{Psychology:} supports mental health and behaviour
    \item \textbf{Social work:} supports families, care coordination, and advocacy
    \item \textbf{NDIS:} the national scheme funding supports for eligible people with disability
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Disability services range from daily living support to therapy and assistive technology
    \item Allied health assessments identify needs and goals
    \item Therapies are delivered one-on-one, in groups, or in the community
    \item Services are often coordinated through a care team
    \item The NDIS provides funding for reasonable and necessary supports
    \item Access can be through the NDIS, Medicare, public health services, or private providers
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item NDIS-funded supports versus health system services, which are funded differently
    \item Therapy services versus daily living support and personal care
    \item The roles of different allied health professions, which complement each other
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
People with disability and their families should seek support early from their GP, allied health professionals, and disability services. A GP can coordinate care, refer to allied health, and support NDIS access. See a doctor for new or worsening health symptoms alongside disability care.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Medical emergencies, including difficulty breathing, chest pain, or severe injury
    \item Any sudden deterioration in health or function
    \item Situations where a person with disability is at risk of harm
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Assessment of needs:} allied health assessments of function, communication, and support needs
    \item \textbf{Medical assessment:} diagnosis and management of health conditions
    \item \textbf{NDIS eligibility:} assessment against access criteria
    \item \textbf{Planning:} development of goals and support plans
    \item \textbf{Review:} regular review of services and outcomes
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Allied health therapy:} physiotherapy, occupational therapy, speech pathology, and psychology
    \item \textbf{Daily living support:} personal care, community access, and supported accommodation
    \item \textbf{Assistive technology:} equipment, aids, and technology to support independence
    \item \textbf{Communication support:} speech therapy, alternative and augmentative communication, and interpreting
    \item \textbf{Behaviour support:} positive behaviour support plans
    \item \textbf{Case coordination:} a coordinated approach across services
    \item \textbf{NDIS planning:} development and management of the participant's plan
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Difficulty accessing services and long waiting lists
    \item Fragmented care across multiple providers
    \item Financial stress from out-of-pocket costs
    \item Unmet health needs in people with disability
    \item Carer stress and burnout
    \item Social isolation and reduced community participation
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
With good support, most people with disability achieve improved independence, function, and quality of life. Allied health services are effective at building skills, and the NDIS has expanded access to support for many Australians. Early intervention and coordinated care produce the best outcomes. While navigating services can be complex, most families find effective support with the right information and advocacy.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Seek early assessment and referral when support is needed
    \item Ask your GP about care plans and allied health referrals
    \item Learn about the NDIS and how to access it
    \item Build a coordinated care team with clear communication
    \item Advocate for the needs of the person with disability
    \item Look after your own health as a carer
    \item Connect with disability advocacy and support organisations
\end{itemize}
