\chapter{Mastectomy --- breast cancer treatment or prevention}
\index{Mastectomy --- breast cancer treatment or prevention}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Mastectomy is a surgical procedure involving the partial or complete removal of one or both breasts, primarily performed as a therapeutic intervention for diagnosed breast cancer or as a prophylactic (risk-reducing) measure in individuals at high genetic risk of developing the disease. Depending on the extent of tissue removal and preservation of overlying skin or the nipple-areola complex, mastectomies are classified into simple (total), modified radical, radical, skin-sparing, and nipple-sparing types. For treatment, mastectomy aims to achieve local oncological clearance. For prevention, bilateral prophylactic mastectomy significantly reduces breast cancer incidence in high-risk groups, such as carriers of BRCA1 or BRCA2 genetic mutations.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Breast cancer is the most common cancer diagnosed in women globally and in many countries. In many countries, approximately 20,000 women and 150 men are diagnosed with breast cancer each year. Among these, a significant proportion undergo mastectomy, either as their primary surgical treatment or following disease recurrence. The rate of contralateral prophylactic mastectomy (removal of the healthy breast in addition to the cancerous breast) and bilateral prophylactic mastectomy in unaffected high-risk women has risen dramatically over the last two decades. This trend is driven by increased access to genetic testing (e.g. for BRCA mutations) and advancements in immediate breast reconstruction techniques.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Mastectomy is indicated for treating established malignant disease or preventing it in individuals with severe predisposing factors:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multicentric or large breast tumours:} Breast cancers that are present in multiple quadrants of the breast or are too large relative to breast size to permit breast-conserving surgery (lumpectomy).
    \item \textbf{Prior chest wall radiation:} Patients who have previously received radiation therapy (e.g. for Hodgkin lymphoma) and cannot safely undergo the additional radiation required after lumpectomy.
    \item \textbf{Inflammatory breast cancer:} An aggressive form of breast cancer presenting with diffuse skin involvement, requiring systemic chemotherapy followed by mastectomy.
    \item \textbf{Patient preference:} Opting for mastectomy to avoid postoperative radiation therapy or to reduce anxiety regarding breast cancer recurrence.
    \item \textbf{High-risk genetic mutations:} Inherited mutations in BRCA1, BRCA2, TP53, or PTEN genes that carry up to an 80\% lifetime risk of developing breast cancer.
    \item \textbf{Strong family history:} A significant family history of pre-menopausal breast or ovarian cancer without an identified genetic mutation.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Patients undergoing therapeutic mastectomy present with signs of breast cancer, while prophylactic patients present with high genetic risk:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Painless breast lump:} The most common initial presentation of breast cancer, typically firm, irregular, and fixed to surrounding tissue.
    \item \textbf{Skin changes:} Dimpling (peau d'orange), puckering, redness, or warmth of the breast skin.
    \item \textbf{Nipple retraction or discharge:} Newly onset inversion of the nipple or spontaneous bloody or clear nipple discharge.
    \item \textbf{Axillary lymphadenopathy:} Palpable, firm lymph nodes in the axilla (armpit).
    \item \textbf{Asymmetrical breast changes:} Rapid changes in breast shape or size.
    \item \textbf{Asymptomatic screening detection:} Abnormal microcalcifications or masses identified on routine mammography in patients without physical symptoms.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Before proceeding to mastectomy, benign breast conditions must be ruled out:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Fibroadenoma:} A benign, mobile, rubbery breast lump common in younger women.
    \item \textbf{Fibrocystic breast changes:} Benign, fluid-filled cysts and fibrous tissue changes that fluctuate with the menstrual cycle.
    \item \textbf{Mastitis or breast abscess:} A painful bacterial infection of the breast tissue, presenting with redness and fever.
    \item \textbf{Fat necrosis:} A benign lump that develops after local breast trauma, resembling breast cancer on imaging.
    \item \textbf{Ductal ectasia:} A benign widening of the milk ducts causing nipple discharge and local tenderness.
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Individuals who notice any new breast lump, skin changes, or nipple abnormalities should seek medical care promptly.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Signs of systemic sepsis:} High fever, rapid heart rate, confusion, or severe chills postoperatively.
    \item \textbf{Severe postoperative haemorrhage:} Active, heavy bleeding or rapid expansion of a haematoma under the chest wall dressings.
    \item \textbf{Severe shortness of breath:} Sudden chest pain or dyspnoea, which may indicate a pulmonary embolism.
\end{itemize}
Other reasons to contact the surgical team include a postoperative temperature over 38 degrees Celsius, excessive clear yellow or bloody fluid drainage from surgical drains, or spreading redness and swelling around the surgical site.

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
A comprehensive workup is essential for planning a mastectomy and staging the disease:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Triple assessment:} The gold standard diagnostic pathway consisting of clinical examination, breast imaging (mammography and/or ultrasound), and tissue biopsy.
    \item \textbf{Core needle biopsy:} Performed under ultrasound guidance to obtain tissue for histological analysis, hormone receptor status (ER, PR), and HER2 status.
    \item \textbf{Breast MRI:} Used to evaluate tumor size, assess for multicentric disease, and screen the contralateral breast, particularly in high-risk genetic carriers.
    \item \textbf{Sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB):} Intra-operative mapping and removal of the first lymph nodes draining the breast to assess for metastatic spread.
    \item \textbf{Genetic testing and counselling:} Performed to identify BRCA1, BRCA2, or other high-risk mutations in patients with a strong family history.
    \item \textbf{Staging scans:} CT scans of the chest and abdomen, and a bone scan, indicated for advanced or clinically aggressive tumours to rule out distant metastases.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Mastectomy is part of a multidisciplinary treatment plan involving surgeons, oncologists, and reconstructive specialists:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Surgical procedure:} Performed under general anaesthesia. The surgeon removes the breast tissue, including the fascia of the pectoralis major muscle. In modified radical mastectomy, axillary lymph node dissection is also performed. Immediate or delayed breast reconstruction may be performed using implants or autologous tissue flaps (e.g. DIEP flap).
    \item \textbf{Adjuvant therapies:} Depending on pathology, patients may require chemotherapy, endocrine (hormone) therapy (e.g. tamoxifen, aromatase inhibitors), immunotherapy, or post-mastectomy radiation therapy.
    \item \textbf{Postoperative supportive care:} Placement of temporary closed-suction drains to prevent fluid accumulation (seroma), pain management (regional nerve blocks and oral analgesics), and early shoulder rehabilitation exercises.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
Potential complications associated with mastectomy include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Seroma formation:} Accumulation of fluid in the surgical cavity after drain removal, often requiring simple needle aspiration.
    \item \textbf{Haematoma:} Collection of blood under the skin flaps that may require surgical evacuation.
    \item \textbf{Surgical site infection:} Cellulitis or abscess of the chest wall or reconstructed breast, requiring antibiotics.
    \item \textbf{Flap or nipple necrosis:} Insufficient blood supply to the skin flaps or retained nipple-areola complex, which can lead to tissue death and wound healing delays.
    \item \textbf{Lymphedema:} Swelling of the arm on the side of surgery, secondary to axillary lymph node removal or radiation.
    \item \textbf{Chronic pain and phantom breast syndrome:} Persistent numbness, hypersensitivity, or neuropathic pain in the chest wall and axilla.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The prognosis for breast cancer patients undergoing mastectomy is highly dependent on the stage and molecular subtype of the cancer. Early-stage breast cancer (Stage I and II) has a 5-year relative survival rate exceeding 90\% to 95\% in many countries. For prevention, bilateral prophylactic mastectomy reduces the risk of developing breast cancer by approximately 90\% to 95\% in BRCA mutation carriers. Most patients adjust well to the physical and psychological changes over time, particularly when supported by comprehensive reconstructive options, breast care nurses, and psychological counselling.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
While the need for mastectomy cannot always be prevented, self-care is critical for a safe recovery:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Manage surgical drains:} Measure and record drain output daily, and keep the insertion sites clean to prevent infection.
    \item \textbf{Perform shoulder rehabilitation:} Follow the prescribed progressive stretching and range-of-motion exercises to prevent shoulder stiffness ("frozen shoulder").
    \item \textbf{Prevent lymphedema:} Avoid blood draws, blood pressure measurements, or injections in the arm on the side of axillary surgery, and wear a compression sleeve if travelling by air.
    \item \textbf{Wear supportive garments:} Use a soft, front-closing surgical bra or binder to support the chest wall and reconstruction.
    \item \textbf{Attend follow-up oncology appointments:} Participate in regular surveillance, including clinical breast examinations and mammograms of any remaining breast tissue.
\end{itemize}
