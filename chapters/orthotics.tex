\chapter{Orthotics}
\index{Orthotics}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. An orthotist or rehabilitation team should assess fit and use of an orthosis.}

\section*{What it is}
An orthosis is an externally applied device that supports, positions, protects, or assists a body part. Examples include ankle-foot orthoses, knee braces, spinal braces, wrist splints, shoe inserts, and devices for pressure relief or contracture management.

\section*{Assessment and fitting}
An orthotist considers the person’s diagnosis, movement, strength, sensation, skin, goals, footwear, work, and environment. The device should be fitted and adjusted for comfort and function. A gradual wearing schedule may be advised, with regular review as the body or condition changes.

\section*{Using an orthosis safely}
Follow instructions about wearing time, footwear, cleaning, and checks. Inspect the skin after removing the device, especially over bony areas. Do not alter padding or straps without advice. A device that is painful, loose, damaged, or no longer appropriate may cause injury and needs review.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Remove the device if advised and seek prompt help for persistent pain, numbness, tingling, swelling, blisters, open skin, colour change, or loss of movement. Seek urgent care for severe pain, a cold or pale limb, sudden weakness, breathing difficulty, or rapidly worsening symptoms.
