\chapter{HCG Test}
\index{HCG Test}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
The HCG test detects human chorionic gonadotropin (HCG), a hormone produced by the placenta in pregnancy. It is the basis of all pregnancy tests, whether performed at home on urine or in a laboratory on blood. The test is used to confirm pregnancy, to monitor early pregnancy, and to help diagnose ectopic pregnancy, miscarriage, and some rare pregnancy-related tumours. Urine tests give a simple positive or negative result, while blood tests measure the exact level. The test is highly accurate when used correctly, but the timing of the test matters, because HCG only becomes detectable a week or more after conception.

\section*{Epidemiology}
HCG testing is one of the most widely used medical tests. Home pregnancy tests are used by millions of women, and laboratory HCG tests are among the most common pathology tests in many countries. Around 1 in 20 pregnancy tests are negative on first use despite pregnancy being present, usually because they were taken too early. Ectopic pregnancy, which affects around 1 in 80--100 pregnancies, relies on serial HCG testing with ultrasound for diagnosis. HCG is also used to monitor gestational trophoblastic disease and, rarely, as a tumour marker.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The HCG test is based on the biology of the pregnancy hormone.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Production:} HCG is secreted by the trophoblast cells of the early placenta from implantation onwards
    \item \textbf{Appearance:} HCG appears in the blood about 8--11 days after conception and in urine one to two days later
    \item \textbf{Rise:} levels double roughly every 48--72 hours in early normal pregnancy, peaking at 8--10 weeks
    \item \textbf{Urine tests:} detect HCG with antibodies; most tests are positive from the first day of a missed period
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} measure either the presence or the exact quantity of HCG
    \item \textbf{Sensitivity:} modern tests detect very low levels, but timing and urine concentration affect results
    \item \textbf{False results:} false positives are rare but can occur with some medical conditions, medicines, or testing errors
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
The HCG test is used in specific situations.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Confirming pregnancy
    \item Investigating missed or irregular periods
    \item Investigating bleeding or pain in early pregnancy
    \item Monitoring early pregnancy when there is a risk of complications
    \item Diagnosing and monitoring ectopic pregnancy
    \item Confirming complete miscarriage
    \item Screening in early pregnancy for chromosomal conditions
    \item Monitoring after treatment for molar pregnancy
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
A positive or negative HCG result must be interpreted in context.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Normal intrauterine pregnancy
    \item Ectopic pregnancy
    \item Failing or miscarried pregnancy
    \item Molar pregnancy
    \item Multiple pregnancy
    \item Test too early, giving a false negative
    \item Rare HCG-producing tumours
    \item Medicines containing HCG, which are used in some fertility treatments
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
A positive home pregnancy test should be confirmed and followed up with a doctor for antenatal care. Seek medical advice promptly for bleeding, pain, or any concern in early pregnancy.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe abdominal or pelvic pain with a positive pregnancy test
    \item Heavy vaginal bleeding with faintness, dizziness, or collapse
    \item Shoulder-tip pain with vaginal bleeding
    \item Signs of shock or severe illness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
The test is performed on urine or blood, and results guide further tests.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Urine test:} home or clinic testing, giving a positive or negative result
    \item \textbf{Quantitative blood test:} measures the exact HCG level
    \item \textbf{Serial testing:} repeat measurements 48--72 hours apart show the trend
    \item \textbf{Ultrasound:} confirms the location and viability of the pregnancy
    \item \textbf{Interpretation:} results are interpreted with symptoms, dates, and imaging
    \item \textbf{Repeat testing:} often needed to confirm the outcome
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management follows from the test result and the clinical situation.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Positive test:} confirm and begin antenatal care
    \item \textbf{Negative test with symptoms:} repeat the test or arrange a blood test
    \item \textbf{Abnormal trends:} serial testing and ultrasound to diagnose ectopic or failing pregnancy
    \item \textbf{Ectopic pregnancy:} medical or surgical treatment
    \item \textbf{Miscarriage:} supportive care and monitoring
    \item \textbf{Molar pregnancy:} evacuation and monitoring
    \item \textbf{After treatment:} HCG is followed until it is undetectable
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item False negatives from testing too early
    \item False positives from rare causes
    \item Delayed diagnosis of ectopic pregnancy if a negative result is trusted despite symptoms
    \item Anxiety and confusion from borderline or unexpected results
    \item Complications of the underlying condition being investigated
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The HCG test is highly reliable when used correctly. It confirms pregnancy early, and in combination with ultrasound it allows the safe management of early pregnancy complications, including ectopic pregnancy, which is now rarely fatal because of early diagnosis. Serial HCG monitoring ensures complete resolution after treatment of ectopic or molar pregnancy. For most women, a positive HCG test is the first step on the path to a healthy pregnancy.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Follow the test instructions, including the timing, for accurate home results
    \item Test after a missed period for the most reliable result
    \item Use first-morning urine, which is most concentrated
    \item Confirm a positive result with a doctor
    \item Attend early antenatal care
    \item Seek prompt care for bleeding or pain in pregnancy
    \item Attend serial HCG and ultrasound tests as recommended
    \item Seek support after a miscarriage or ectopic pregnancy
\end{itemize}
