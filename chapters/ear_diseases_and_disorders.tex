\chapter{Ear Diseases and Disorders}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Ear diseases and disorders are conditions that affect any part of the ear: the outer ear, the middle ear, or the inner ear. They range from common and minor problems such as earwax build-up and swimmer's ear to serious conditions such as middle ear infection, hearing loss, tinnitus, and vertigo. The ear has two vital functions: hearing and balance, and disorders of the ear can therefore cause hearing loss, dizziness, ringing in the ears, and pain. This chapter provides an overview of the main types of ear disease, their causes, and when to seek help.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Ear conditions are among the most common reasons for GP visits and are a major cause of disability worldwide. Middle ear infections are extremely common in children, with most children experiencing at least one before school age. Hearing loss affects a large and growing proportion of older adults, and tinnitus is experienced by a significant minority of the population. Vertigo and balance disorders become more common with age and are a leading cause of falls in older people.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The ear is divided into three parts, each with its own disorders.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Outer ear:} conditions include earwax impaction, swimmer's ear (otitis externa), and foreign bodies; the skin of the ear canal becomes infected or irritated
    \item \textbf{Middle ear:} conditions include acute otitis media, glue ear, and cholesteatoma; the middle ear connects to the throat via the eustachian tube, which can become blocked
    \item \textbf{Inner ear:} conditions include sensorineural hearing loss, Meniere's disease, and vestibular disorders; the inner ear contains the cochlea for hearing and the semicircular canals for balance
    \item \textbf{Common mechanisms:} infection, blockage, trauma, noise damage, age-related degeneration, and genetic conditions
    \item \textbf{Eustachian tube dysfunction:} a central problem in middle ear disease, affecting pressure equalisation and drainage
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Symptoms depend on the part of the ear affected.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Ear pain, which may be sharp, throbbing, or dull
    \item Hearing loss, which may be sudden or gradual, and one-sided or both-sided
    \item Discharge from the ear, which may be watery, bloody, or purulent
    \item Itching or fullness in the ear
    \item Ringing, buzzing, or hissing sounds in the ear (tinnitus)
    \item Dizziness, vertigo, or imbalance
    \item In children: pulling at the ear, irritability, fever, and poor sleep
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Pain or symptoms attributed to the ear may have other causes.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Dental problems, including tooth abscess and impacted wisdom teeth
    \item Temporomandibular joint disorders
    \item Throat infections, including tonsillitis and pharyngitis
    \item Sinusitis
    \item Referred pain from the neck or cervical spine
    \item Neurological conditions causing dizziness
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor for ear pain, discharge, hearing loss, tinnitus, or dizziness that is new, persistent, or worrying. Sudden hearing loss and severe vertigo require urgent assessment.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden hearing loss, especially in one ear
    \item Severe vertigo with vomiting, weakness, or inability to walk
    \item Sudden severe ear pain with swelling behind the ear or facial weakness
    \item Head injury with bleeding or fluid from the ear
    \item Signs of a serious infection with fever and confusion
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Diagnosis usually begins with examination and simple tests.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Otoscopy:} examination of the ear canal and eardrum
    \item \textbf{Hearing tests:} audiometry measures the type and degree of hearing loss
    \item \textbf{Tympanometry:} assesses middle ear function and pressure
    \item \textbf{Vestibular tests:} balance assessments for dizziness
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} CT or MRI scans for suspected cholesteatoma, tumours, or complications
    \item \textbf{Referral:} to an ear, nose, and throat specialist for complex or persistent problems
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management depends on the specific disorder.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Infections:} antibiotics for bacterial infections, with pain relief; many infections resolve on their own
    \item \textbf{Earwax:} removal by a clinician when it causes blockage
    \item \textbf{Glue ear:} monitoring, and sometimes grommet insertion or adenoidectomy in persistent cases
    \item \textbf{Hearing loss:} hearing aids, cochlear implants, and communication support
    \item \textbf{Tinnitus:} sound therapy, hearing aids, and psychological support
    \item \textbf{Vertigo:} vestibular rehabilitation, medicines for acute attacks, and treatment of the underlying cause
    \item \textbf{Surgery:} for cholesteatoma, chronic infections, and some structural problems
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Permanent hearing loss
    \item Chronic middle ear infection and cholesteatoma, which can damage the ear structures
    \item Spread of infection, including mastoiditis and, rarely, intracranial infection
    \item Falls and injury from imbalance
    \item Social isolation and reduced quality of life from hearing loss and tinnitus
    \item Speech and language delay in children with persistent hearing problems
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The outlook varies widely. Many ear conditions, including most infections and earwax problems, resolve fully with treatment. Hearing loss is usually permanent but is well managed with hearing aids and support. Chronic and inner ear conditions such as cholesteatoma and Meniere's disease require specialist care but can be managed effectively. Early treatment of childhood ear infections protects hearing and development.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Protect ears from loud noise with earplugs or ear muffs
    \item Dry ears carefully after swimming and bathing
    \item Never insert objects into the ear, including cotton buds
    \item Keep the volume down on personal listening devices
    \item Have hearing checked if you notice difficulty hearing, especially with age
    \item See a doctor promptly for ear pain, discharge, or hearing changes
    \item Keep children's immunisations up to date to prevent infections
\end{itemize}
