\chapter{Infection Prevention and Control}
\index{Infection Prevention and Control}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Infection prevention and control (IPC) is the set of practices that reduce the spread of infections in homes, communities, and healthcare settings. It includes hand hygiene, respiratory etiquette, cleaning and disinfection, vaccination, safe food handling, and the specific precautions used in hospitals to protect patients and staff, such as isolation, personal protective equipment, and sterilisation of instruments. In healthcare, IPC programs have been shown to prevent many avoidable infections, including those acquired in hospital. In everyday life, the same principles, led by simple measures such as hand washing and staying home when unwell, slow the transmission of colds, influenza, gastroenteritis, and more serious infections.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Infections are a leading cause of illness worldwide, and a significant proportion are preventable. In local hospitals, around one in twenty patients acquires an infection during their care, and hand hygiene compliance programs have been associated with substantial reductions in these infections, including bloodstream infections from intravenous lines and surgical site infections. In the community, respiratory and gastrointestinal infections spread most readily in households, schools, and aged-care facilities, and in seasons of peak transmission. Immunisation has eliminated or greatly reduced many infections that once caused widespread illness and death, while emerging pathogens, including new influenza strains and coronaviruses, continue to test IPC systems.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Infections spread when a pathogen, such as a virus, bacterium, or fungus, passes from a source to a susceptible person through a route of transmission. Respiratory infections spread through droplets and aerosols from coughing, sneezing, and talking. Gastrointestinal infections spread through the faecal-oral route, via contaminated hands, food, or water. Some infections spread through blood and body fluids, through skin contact, or via contaminated objects and surfaces. Pathogens can survive on surfaces for hours to days, which is why hand hygiene and cleaning matter. In hospitals, patients with wounds, catheters, or weakened immunity are especially vulnerable. IPC breaks the chain of transmission at one or more points: source, route, or susceptible host.

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Prevention practices in daily life: hand washing, covering coughs and sneezes, and staying home when unwell
    \item Healthcare precautions: gloves, gowns, masks, and eye protection for certain tasks
    \item Isolation of patients with contagious infections in hospitals
    \item Cleaning and disinfection of surfaces, equipment, and instruments
    \item Sterilisation of surgical instruments
    \item Safe handling and disposal of sharps and clinical waste
    \item Immunisation of individuals and populations
    \item Outbreak investigation and response in hospitals, aged care, and the community
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Infection prevention strategies are chosen according to how a specific infection spreads.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Contact transmission:} spread by direct or indirect contact, requiring hand hygiene, gloves, gowns, and cleaning
    \item \textbf{Droplet transmission:} spread by larger respiratory droplets, requiring masks and distancing
    \item \textbf{Airborne transmission:} spread by tiny particles that hang in the air, requiring fit-tested masks and negative-pressure rooms
    \item \textbf{Blood-borne transmission:} hepatitis B, hepatitis C, and HIV, prevented by safe injection and sharps practices
    \item \textbf{Faecal-oral transmission:} gastroenteritis and hepatitis A, prevented by hand hygiene and food safety
    \item \textbf{Vector-borne transmission:} mosquito-borne disease, prevented by insect avoidance and control
    \item \textbf{Healthcare-associated versus community-acquired infection:} different settings and control strategies
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Individuals should seek care for infections that are severe, persistent, or affecting vulnerable people, and should follow public health advice during outbreaks and pandemics. Anyone with a contagious illness should avoid contact with others, especially with babies, older people, pregnant women, and people with weakened immunity. Health professionals and facilities follow national IPC guidelines and reporting requirements for notifiable diseases.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Difficulty breathing, chest pain, or signs of severe infection such as confusion or collapse
    \item A rash that does not fade under pressure in an unwell person
    \item Fever with stiff neck, sensitivity to light, or a severe headache, which may indicate meningitis
    \item Anaphylaxis or severe allergic reactions to any medication
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Audits and surveillance:} monitoring of infection rates, hand hygiene compliance, and cleaning standards
    \item \textbf{Microbiological testing:} cultures and molecular tests to identify pathogens and antibiotic resistance
    \item \textbf{Outbreak investigation:} case finding, contact tracing, and environmental testing
    \item \textbf{Risk assessment:} identification of vulnerable patients and high-risk procedures
    \item \textbf{Environmental monitoring:} checks of sterilisation equipment, water systems, and air handling
    \item \textbf{Reporting:} notification of notifiable diseases to public health authorities
    \item \textbf{Education and training:} assessment of staff competency in IPC practices
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hand hygiene:} washing with soap and water or using alcohol-based hand rub at key moments
    \item \textbf{Respiratory etiquette:} covering coughs and sneezes, using tissues, and wearing masks when advised
    \item \textbf{Personal protective equipment:} gloves, gowns, masks, and eye protection selected for the task
    \item \textbf{Isolation precautions:} single rooms and dedicated equipment for contagious patients
    \item \textbf{Cleaning and disinfection:} routine and terminal cleaning of environments and equipment
    \item \textbf{Aseptic technique:} sterile practices for procedures, injections, and wound care
    \item \textbf{Antimicrobial stewardship:} using antibiotics only when needed to slow resistance
    \item \textbf{Vaccination:} protecting individuals and communities, including staff and vulnerable groups
    \item \textbf{Outbreak response:} rapid identification, isolation, and control measures
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Healthcare-associated infections: surgical site infections, bloodstream infections, and urinary infections
    \item Antibiotic resistance from overuse of antimicrobials
    \item Outbreaks and their effects in hospitals, aged care, schools, and workplaces
    \item Complications of infection itself, including sepsis and death
    \item Economic costs of illness, quarantine, and lost productivity
    \item Anxiety and stigma associated with contagious disease
    \item Failure of sterilisation or cleaning, with serious consequences for patients
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
When IPC practices are applied consistently, most transmission of infection is preventable. Hand hygiene alone reduces healthcare-associated infections substantially, and hospital programs that combine hand hygiene, cleaning, isolation, and antimicrobial stewardship achieve sustained reductions. Community measures, led by vaccination and hygiene, have eliminated or controlled many once-common diseases. Even for emerging infections, rapid and well-executed IPC responses dramatically reduce spread. The gains depend on continued education, resourcing, and commitment at individual, institutional, and population levels.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water, especially after the toilet and before eating
    \item Use alcohol-based hand rub when soap is unavailable
    \item Cover coughs and sneezes with a tissue or the elbow
    \item Stay home when unwell to avoid spreading infection
    \item Keep up to date with recommended vaccinations
    \item Follow food safety practices: cook, chill, clean, and avoid cross-contamination
    \item Handle sharps safely and never share needles
    \item Clean frequently touched surfaces regularly
    \item Follow healthcare and public health advice during outbreaks
    \item Take antibiotics exactly as prescribed, and never share them
\end{itemize}
