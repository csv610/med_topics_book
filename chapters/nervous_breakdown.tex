\chapter{Nervous breakdown}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
A nervous breakdown (often referred to clinically as an acute mental health crisis or acute stress reaction) is not a formal medical diagnosis but a colloquial term describing a phase of intense, acute mental distress. During this period, an individual is temporarily unable to function in daily life due to overwhelming psychological stress. It typically manifests when life's demands exceed the person's cognitive and emotional coping mechanisms, serving as a critical indicator of underlying psychiatric conditions such as major depressive disorder, anxiety disorders, or acute stress disorder. Recognising this state is vital, as it represents a tipping point where immediate support and intervention can prevent progression to severe, long-term psychiatric morbidity.

\section*{Epidemiology}
While not tracked as a distinct clinical entity in epidemiological databases, crises described as nervous breakdowns are highly prevalent globally and within Australia. Mental health crises affect individuals across all demographic groups, with a higher incidence observed in young adults, women, and individuals facing chronic socioeconomic stress, relationship breakdowns, or major life transitions. In Australia, approximately one in five individuals experiences a mental health condition in any given year, and acute stress reactions or crises account for a significant proportion of emergency department presentations, general practice consultations, and helpline calls. 

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The aetiology of a nervous breakdown is multi-factorial, representing a maladaptive stress response where cumulative stressors overwhelm neurological and psychological resilience. The physiological basis lies in the dysregulation of the body's stress response systems.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Chronic environmental stress:} Prolonged exposure to workplace stress, financial hardship, caregiving responsibilities, or interpersonal conflict.
    \item \textbf{Acute traumatic events:} Sudden, severe stressors such as bereavement, physical trauma, natural disasters, or relationship dissolution.
    \item \textbf{Genetic predisposition:} A family history of mood or anxiety disorders, which can lower the threshold for stress tolerance.
    \item \textbf{Dysregulation of the HPA axis:} Chronic activation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis, leading to persistent cortisol release, neurochemical imbalances (particularly serotonin, noradrenaline, and dopamine), and impaired neuroplasticity.
    \item \textbf{Inadequate coping mechanisms:} A lack of social support networks, maladaptive cognitive patterns, or avoidance behaviours.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
The clinical presentation of a nervous breakdown is characterised by emotional, cognitive, and physical symptoms that severely impair an individual's ability to navigate daily responsibilities.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Emotional symptoms:} Extreme anxiety, frequent panic attacks, persistent low mood, tearfulness, and feelings of hopelessness or emotional numbness.
    \item \textbf{Cognitive impairment:} Inability to concentrate, memory issues, brain fog, and severe indecisiveness.
    \item \textbf{Physical exhaustion:} Profound fatigue, insomnia or hypersomnia, muscle tension, headaches, and unexplained somatic complaints.
    \item \textbf{Social and occupational withdrawal:} Isolation from friends and family, neglecting hygiene, and avoiding work or school commitments.
    \item \textbf{Autonomic hyperarousal:} Palpitations, sweating, tremors, and gastrointestinal disturbances such as nausea or diarrhoea.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
It is critical to differentiate a nervous breakdown from defined psychiatric disorders and organic medical conditions that present with similar symptoms.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Major depressive disorder:} Characterised by persistent anhedonia, low mood for at least two weeks, and specific diagnostic criteria, rather than a transient situational crisis.
    \item \textbf{Panic disorder:} Recurrent, unexpected panic attacks without a clear external trigger, whereas a breakdown is usually linked to cumulative stressors.
    \item \textbf{Thyroid disorders:} Hyperthyroidism or hypothyroidism can mimic anxiety, depression, and severe fatigue, requiring thyroid function testing.
    \item \textbf{Adrenal insufficiency:} Chronic fatigue, weight loss, and stress intolerance due to cortisol deficiency (Addison's disease).
    \item \textbf{Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD):} Specifically tied to exposure to a traumatic event, presenting with intrusive memories, avoidance, and hyperarousal.
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
An individual should seek medical care if they feel unable to manage daily responsibilities, experience persistent distress, or feel overwhelmed by anxiety or low mood.
\hfill \break
\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Thoughts of suicide, self-harm, or harming others.
    \item Severe panic attacks accompanied by chest pain, palpitations, or shortness of breath.
    \item Acute psychosis, including auditory or visual hallucinations, and delusions.
    \item Total inability to care for oneself or perform basic daily tasks.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Diagnosis involves a comprehensive clinical and psychiatric evaluation, alongside laboratory tests to exclude organic causes.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clinical psychiatric interview:} Comprehensive assessment of symptoms, stressors, coping mechanisms, and personal/family psychiatric history.
    \item \textbf{Standardised screening tools:} Questionnaires such as the Kessler Psychological Distress Scale (K10), Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9), or Generalised Anxiety Disorder Assessment (GAD-7) to quantify distress.
    \item \textbf{Thyroid function tests:} To exclude thyroid dysfunction causing mood disturbances.
    \item \textbf{Full blood count and biochemistry:} To rule out anaemia, electrolyte imbalances, or nutritional deficiencies contributing to fatigue.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management requires a multi-faceted approach focusing on acute stabilisation, psychological support, and lifestyle modification.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Psychotherapy:} Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) and acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT) to build coping mechanisms and reframe cognitive distortions.
    \item \textbf{Pharmacotherapy:} Short-term use of anxiolytics (e.g.\ benzodiazepines) for acute crisis, or long-term antidepressants (e.g.\ SSRIs) for underlying depressive or anxiety disorders.
    \item \textbf{Crisis intervention:} Immediate counselling, helpline support, and developing a structured safety plan.
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle modifications:} Prioritising sleep hygiene, balanced nutrition, and regular moderate physical activity.
    \item \textbf{Stress management:} Mindfulness, meditation, and structured time off from work or stressful environments.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Development of chronic depressive or anxiety disorders.
    \item Increased risk of self-harm or suicidal behaviour.
    \item Cardiovascular strain due to chronic activation of the sympathetic nervous system.
    \item Relationship breakdown, loss of employment, and social isolation.
    \item Substance abuse as a maladaptive coping mechanism.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
With appropriate intervention, the prognosis for recovery from a nervous breakdown is generally excellent. Most individuals regain their baseline level of functioning once stressors are managed and adaptive coping strategies are implemented. The timeline varies from weeks to months, depending on the severity of the triggers, the strength of the patient's support network, and access to professional mental health care.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Practice regular stress-reduction techniques such as mindfulness, deep breathing, or yoga.
    \item Establish and maintain a strong social support network.
    \item Set healthy boundaries in personal and professional life to prevent burnout.
    \item Ensure adequate sleep, a balanced diet, and regular exercise.
    \item Seek professional help early when stress levels begin to feel unmanageable.
\end{itemize}
