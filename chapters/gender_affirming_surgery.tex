\chapter{Gender-Affirming Surgery}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Gender-affirming surgery is surgery that helps a transgender or gender-diverse person's body align with their gender identity. It is one component of gender-affirming care, which also includes psychological support, social affirmation, and hormone therapy, and it is accessed voluntarily by people who choose it after assessment and discussion. The surgeries include chest surgery (top surgery), genital surgery (bottom surgery), facial surgery, and other procedures, and they are generally performed after the person has been living in their affirmed gender and has usually been on hormone therapy. Surgery is safe and effective when provided by experienced teams, and it is associated with high satisfaction and significant improvements in quality of life. This chapter explains the types of surgery, the assessment process, and what to consider.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Around 1--2% of the population identifies as transgender or gender diverse, and a smaller proportion seeks gender-affirming surgery. Demand for these services has risen substantially in Australia in recent years, and public hospital programs and private providers offer the surgeries. Chest surgery is the most commonly requested procedure. Studies consistently report high satisfaction after gender-affirming surgery, with most people saying they would undergo it again, and it is associated with reduced gender dysphoria and improved mental health. Access, waiting times, and cost remain significant barriers.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Gender dysphoria:} the distress that can arise when a person's gender identity differs from the sex recorded at birth; surgery treats this by aligning the body with the identity
    \item \textbf{Assessment:} a multidisciplinary process with psychologists, psychiatrists, and surgeons, ensuring informed consent and readiness
    \item \textbf{The WPATH Standards of Care:} international guidelines require two assessments by mental health professionals for genital surgery, and one for chest surgery, with hormone therapy usually established first
    \item \textbf{Chest surgery (top surgery):} masculinising chest reconstruction (removing breast tissue) or feminising augmentation (implants)
    \item \textbf{Genital surgery (bottom surgery):} vaginoplasty (construction of a vagina) for transfeminine people, and phalloplasty or metoidioplasty (construction of a penis) for transmasculine people
    \item \textbf{Other surgeries:} facial feminisation, voice surgery, and hysterectomy or orchidectomy, chosen individually
    \item \textbf{Hormone therapy first:} oestrogen or testosterone is usually taken for 12+ months before surgery, and some people need no surgery at all
    \item \textbf{Outcomes:} surgery is irreversible and life-changing, which is why careful assessment precedes it
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
The features are those of the assessment and the procedures.
\begin{itemize}
    \item A comprehensive assessment with mental health professionals and surgeons
    \item Typically 12 months or more of living in the affirmed gender and hormone therapy before genital surgery
    \item Chest surgery as a day case or short stay, with a few weeks of recovery
    \item Genital surgery as major surgery, with a hospital stay and several months of recovery
    \item High satisfaction and relief of gender dysphoria in the majority of people
    \item The need for lifelong follow-up and care
    \item Complications are possible, as with any surgery, and are discussed before consent
    \item Continued psychological and peer support during and after the process
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Considerations in the surgical assessment include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Whether surgery is needed at all; many transgender people do not have surgery
    \item Which procedures match the person's needs and goals
    \item The readiness assessment, which confirms the diagnosis and capacity for consent
    \item Mental health conditions, which are treated first because they affect decision-making and outcomes
    \item Medical fitness for anaesthesia and surgery
    \item The distinction between surgical and non-surgical options, including hormones alone
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
People considering gender-affirming surgery should seek care through gender clinics, GPs with experience in transgender health, and specialist services, and the assessment process begins long before any surgery.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe pain, bleeding, fever, or signs of infection after surgery
    \item Chest pain or breathlessness, which may indicate a blood clot
    \item Wound problems, including opening or severe swelling
    \item Suicidal thoughts or severe distress, which warrant urgent support
    \item Any emergency symptoms
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Psychological assessment:} confirming the diagnosis of gender dysphoria and readiness for surgery
    \item \textbf{Medical assessment:} general health, hormone therapy status, and anaesthetic fitness
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} hormone levels and routine pre-operative tests
    \item \textbf{Surgical consultation:} discussion of procedures, risks, benefits, and expectations
    \item \textbf{Documentation:} the assessments required by the Standards of Care are recorded in the consent process
    \item \textbf{Post-operative review:} follow-up for healing, complications, and outcomes
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multidisciplinary care:} a team including a GP, psychologist or psychiatrist, endocrinologist, and surgeon
    \item \textbf{Hormone therapy:} established before surgery, with ongoing management
    \item \textbf{Chest surgery:} masculinising or feminising procedures, with counselling about outcomes
    \item \textbf{Genital surgery:} vaginoplasty, phalloplasty, or metoidioplasty, with detailed discussion of recovery and lifelong care
    \item \textbf{Other procedures:} facial surgery, voice therapy, and others as chosen
    \item \textbf{Post-operative care:} wound care, pain relief, pelvic floor and dilation care where relevant, and follow-up
    \item \textbf{Psychological support:} during the process and after, including peer support groups
    \item \textbf{Lifelong care:} continuing hormone therapy and health care with the GP
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Surgical complications: bleeding, infection, wound problems, and anaesthetic risks
    \item Specific procedure risks: loss of sensation, stricture or narrowing, and need for revision surgery
    \item The need for further procedures to achieve the desired result
    \item The psychological impact of a major life change, which is usually positive with support
    \item Barriers to access, cost, and long waiting times
    \item Regret, which is rare with proper assessment but possible
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Gender-affirming surgery has excellent outcomes for appropriately selected people. Studies show very high satisfaction, substantial relief of gender dysphoria, and improvements in mental health, quality of life, and social functioning, with regret rates of only 1--2%. Recovery from chest surgery is quick, while genital surgery has a longer recovery and requires lifelong follow-up. The key to good outcomes is the thorough assessment process, realistic expectations, and comprehensive aftercare. For people who choose it, gender-affirming surgery is life-changing in a profoundly positive way.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Start with a GP experienced in transgender health, who can coordinate care
    \item Engage in the full assessment process; it exists to protect the person's wellbeing
    \item Maintain hormone therapy and general health before surgery
    \item Stop smoking well before surgery, as it delays healing
    \item Follow post-operative instructions carefully, including wound care and activity limits
    \item Attend all follow-up appointments
    \item Access psychological support and peer networks during the process
    \item Seek urgent care for any surgical complication
\end{itemize}
