\chapter{Pityriasis Versicolor}
\index{Pityriasis Versicolor}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. Skin colour changes have several causes and may need examination.}

\section*{What it is}
Pityriasis versicolor, also called tinea versicolor, is an overgrowth of a yeast that normally lives on the skin. It causes patches that may be paler, darker, pink, or brown, often on the chest, back, shoulders, neck, or upper arms. It is not caused by poor hygiene and is not usually contagious.

\section*{Symptoms and diagnosis}
Patches may be flat, slightly scaly, and mildly itchy, especially in warm or humid conditions. A clinician or pharmacist may recognise the pattern; a skin scraping or other test can help when it is uncertain. Vitiligo, eczema, psoriasis, and other infections can look similar.

\section*{Treatment and outlook}
Antifungal shampoo or cream is commonly used as directed. Extensive or persistent disease may need prescription tablets. The yeast can be cleared before the skin returns to its usual colour, which may take weeks or months. Recurrence is common, and preventive treatment may be advised.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Arrange review for widespread, recurrent, painful, inflamed, or uncertain skin changes, or if treatment does not work. Seek urgent help for rapidly spreading redness, severe pain, facial swelling, fever, or blistering.
