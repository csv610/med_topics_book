\noindent\textit{Educational reference; always seek professional advice for individual care.}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Tramadol is a prescription opioid analgesic used for some moderate or severe pain when other options are unsuitable. It acts at opioid receptors and also affects serotonin and noradrenaline signalling. These effects can relieve pain but can also cause sedation, dependence, seizures, and serotonin toxicity. Legal status, formulations, and approved uses vary by country.

\section*{Safe Use}
Take tramadol only as prescribed and do not take extra doses for breakthrough pain without advice. Do not share it. Avoid alcohol, recreational sedatives, and driving or machinery until you know how it affects you. Extended-release tablets must not be crushed or chewed. Tell the prescriber about kidney or liver disease, breathing problems, pregnancy, a history of seizures, or previous substance-use problems.

\section*{Side Effects and Interactions}
Common effects include nausea, constipation, dizziness, sleepiness, and headache. Tramadol can interact with antidepressants and other serotonergic medicines, some migraine medicines, monoamine oxidase inhibitors, sedatives, and other opioids. Combining medicines can cause serotonin toxicity, excessive sedation, breathing suppression, or seizures. Ask a pharmacist to check every prescription, over-the-counter medicine, and supplement.

\section*{Dependence and Withdrawal}
Regular use can cause tolerance, dependence, and opioid-use disorder. Do not stop suddenly after regular use without a tapering plan, because withdrawal may cause anxiety, sweating, diarrhoea, pain, insomnia, and other symptoms. Dependence is a physiological response and is not the same as a moral failing.

\section*{When to Seek Urgent Help}
\textbf{Contact local emergency services} for slow or difficult breathing, inability to wake the person, blue lips, seizure, collapse, or suspected overdose. Serotonin toxicity may cause agitation, fever, sweating, diarrhoea, tremor, muscle rigidity, or rapidly changing blood pressure and heart rate; seek urgent care. Do not make a person vomit. Follow local poison-centre or emergency advice about naloxone and rescue breathing.

\section*{Alternatives and Follow-up}
For many chronic pain conditions, exercise therapy, psychological approaches, physiotherapy, and non-opioid medicines may offer safer long-term strategies. Review pain, function, dose, side effects, and ongoing need regularly. Store tramadol securely and return unused medicine through a local medicine-disposal service.
