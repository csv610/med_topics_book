\chapter{Osteoporosis treatment}
\index{Osteoporosis treatment}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; treatment must be individualized by a clinician.}

\section*{Goals of treatment}
Osteoporosis treatment aims to reduce the risk of fractures, preserve mobility, and maintain independence. The plan depends on bone density, previous and likely future fractures, age, other illnesses, kidney function, medicines, and personal preferences.

\section*{Assessment before treatment}
Assessment may include a DXA scan, fracture-risk calculation, blood tests, review of medicines and falls, and investigation for conditions that weaken bone. A fracture after a minor injury can be an important sign even if a scan is not in the osteoporosis range.

\section*{Treatment options}
Treatment may combine weight-bearing and resistance exercise, balance training, fall prevention, adequate nutrition, and medicines. Depending on risk, medicines may include antiresorptive or bone-forming treatments. Each has specific benefits, precautions, duration, and follow-up requirements; do not start, stop, or interrupt treatment without advice.

\section*{Living safely}
Use suitable footwear and aids, improve lighting and household safety, review medicines that increase falls, and address vision or balance problems. Calcium and vitamin D supplements should be based on diet, blood results, risk, and clinician advice rather than taken in excess.

\section*{When to seek urgent care}
Seek urgent help after a fall with severe pain, inability to bear weight, suspected hip or spine fracture, sudden severe back pain, new weakness or numbness, or loss of bladder or bowel control.
