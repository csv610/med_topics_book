\chapter{Orthodontic Treatments}
\index{Orthodontic Treatments}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. An orthodontist or dentist should recommend treatment after an individual examination.}

\section*{What it is}
Orthodontic treatment moves teeth and sometimes guides jaw growth to improve bite, function, oral hygiene, or appearance. Treatment may use removable appliances, fixed braces, clear aligners, expanders, retainers, or other devices. Suitability depends on age, tooth and gum health, growth, and the treatment goal.

\section*{Before treatment}
The clinician may examine the teeth and gums, take photographs, X-rays, scans, or impressions, and discuss options, duration, costs, benefits, limitations, and risks. Tooth decay and gum disease usually need attention before appliances are fitted. Ask how emergencies, lost appliances, and follow-up will be handled.

\section*{During treatment}
Keep appliances clean and follow instructions about wear. Brush carefully with fluoride toothpaste and clean between teeth; frequent sugary food and drinks increase decay risk. Some pressure or soreness after an adjustment is common. Avoid foods or habits that damage the appliance and attend planned reviews.

\section*{After treatment}
Teeth can move after active treatment, so retainers are commonly needed. Wear and replace them as instructed and maintain routine dental care. Results depend on growth, bone and gum health, cooperation, and long-term retention.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Contact the orthodontic team for a loose or broken appliance, wire cutting the cheek, persistent severe pain, swelling, or signs of infection. Seek emergency care for facial injury with uncontrolled bleeding, difficulty breathing or swallowing, or rapidly increasing swelling.
