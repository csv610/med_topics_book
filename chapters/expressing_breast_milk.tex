\chapter{Expressing Breast Milk}
\index{Expressing Breast Milk}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Expressing breast milk means removing milk from the breast by hand or with a pump, so that a mother can store milk for later feeds, leave her baby with someone else, relieve engorgement, or establish milk supply when the baby cannot feed directly. Expressing is a useful skill that supports breastfeeding, whether the mother is returning to work, needs to be away, or simply wants to share feeding with her partner. This chapter explains the methods of expressing, how to store milk safely, and how to manage common challenges.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Many local mothers express breast milk at some point, and the practice has increased with the return to work and the availability of effective breast pumps. Expressing allows mothers to continue breastfeeding while separated from their baby, and it helps maintain supply for babies who are premature or unwell. Safe storage and handling are important so that the milk remains safe and nutritious.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Breast milk is produced on a supply-and-demand basis.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Milk production:} the more the breast is drained, the more milk is produced
    \item \textbf{The let-down reflex:} the hormone oxytocin releases milk from the breast; it is triggered by the baby, by pumping, and by relaxation
    \item \textbf{Why express:} to feed the baby when separated, to relieve fullness, to build a store, or to stimulate supply
    \item \textbf{Hand expression:} using the hands to press milk out, useful in the early days and for relieving fullness
    \item \textbf{Pumps:} manual or electric pumps remove milk efficiently; double pumps express both breasts at once
    \item \textbf{Milk storage:} expressed milk keeps safely for a limited time at room temperature, in the fridge, and in the freezer
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Expressing is done for many reasons.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Building a supply for when the mother returns to work
    \item Leaving milk for a partner or carer
    \item Relieving engorgement
    \item Feeding a baby who cannot latch or is unwell
    \item Maintaining supply while the baby is in special care
    \item Increasing supply through extra expression
    \item Donating milk to milk banks
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Expressing complements, rather than replaces, direct feeding.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Direct breastfeeding is generally preferred when possible, as it is simplest and regulates supply
    \item Bottle feeding with expressed milk is an option when the mother is away
    \item Formula is an alternative for some families
    \item The choice depends on the family's circumstances and goals
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Mothers should seek help from a midwife, lactation consultant, or the local Breastfeeding Association if expressing is painful, if the baby is not gaining weight, if supply is low, or if there are concerns about the baby's feeding.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Fever with a red, hot, painful breast, which may indicate mastitis
    \item A baby who is very lethargic, not feeding, or showing signs of dehydration
    \item Severe breast pain or an abscess
    \item Any emergency involving the mother or baby
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Expressing effectively involves technique.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Preparation:} wash hands, and have clean equipment ready
    \item \textbf{Hand expression:} cup the breast, press back towards the chest, then forward rhythmically
    \item \textbf{Pumping:} use a properly fitted flange and a comfortable suction level
    \item \textbf{Frequency:} express often enough to maintain supply, matching the baby's feeds
    \item \textbf{Time:} expressing can take 10 to 20 minutes per session
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Expressing is managed with good technique and storage.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Methods:} hand, manual pump, or electric pump, alone or combined
    \item \textbf{Cleanliness:} wash and sterilise pump parts according to instructions
    \item \textbf{Storage:} breast milk in the fridge keeps for up to 72 hours (or per current guidance) and in the freezer for months
    \item \textbf{Containers:} use clean, sealable containers labelled with the date
    \item \textbf{Thawing:} thaw in the fridge or warm water, never in the microwave
    \item \textbf{Using stored milk:} warm gently and use within the recommended time
    \item \textbf{Support:} lactation consultants and the local Breastfeeding Association provide help
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Engorgement and blocked ducts if expressing is infrequent or ineffective
    \item Mastitis from incomplete drainage or infection
    \item Sore nipples from a poorly fitted pump
    \item Reduced supply if the breast is not drained regularly
    \item Wasted milk from unsafe storage
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Most mothers express successfully with a little practice and support. Expressing allows breastfeeding to continue through separation, work, and illness, and it is a reliable way to feed babies who cannot feed directly. With safe storage practices, expressed milk is a nutritious and safe food for the baby.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Start expressing only once breastfeeding is established, unless advised otherwise
    \item Express regularly to match the baby's feeding pattern
    \item Relax and use warmth before expressing to help the let-down
    \item Use a well-fitted pump and comfortable settings
    \item Label and date all stored milk
    \item Follow storage and thawing guidelines
    \item Seek support early for any difficulties
\end{itemize}
