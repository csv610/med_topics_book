\chapter{Falls}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
A fall is an event in which a person unintentionally comes to rest on the ground or a lower level. Falls are common at all ages but are particularly serious in older people, for whom they are a leading cause of injury, hospital admission, loss of independence, and death. Many falls are preventable with attention to risk factors such as muscle weakness, balance problems, medicines, and home hazards. This chapter explains why falls happen, how they are assessed, and how to prevent them.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Falls affect about one in three people over 65 each year, and a large proportion of these people fall more than once. Falls are the leading cause of injury-related hospitalisation and death in older Australians, and hip fractures, head injuries, and fear of falling all follow falls. The rate of falls rises with age and in residential care. Falls also affect younger people, particularly in the workplace and in sport, but the consequences in older people are the most serious.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Falls result from the interaction of personal and environmental factors.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Muscle weakness and balance:} age-related loss of strength and balance increases fall risk
    \item \textbf{Chronic conditions:} stroke, Parkinson's disease, arthritis, diabetes, and dementia
    \item \textbf{Vision:} poor vision, cataracts, and glasses with the wrong prescription
    \item \textbf{Medicines:} sedatives, blood pressure medicines, and multiple medicines
    \item \textbf{Feet and footwear:} foot problems and unsuitable shoes
    \item \textbf{Environmental hazards:} loose rugs, poor lighting, cords, and wet or uneven surfaces
    \item \textbf{Acute illness:} infections, low blood sugar, and dizziness can cause sudden falls
    \item \textbf{Fear of falling:} the fear itself reduces activity, worsening weakness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Falls are often the result of several factors, and their effects can be significant.
\begin{itemize}
    \item The fall itself, which may be unwitnessed
    \item Bruising, cuts, and fractures, especially of the hip, wrist, and spine
    \item Head injury, which can be serious, particularly in people on blood thinners
    \item Difficulty getting up and lying on the floor for a long time
    \item Fear of falling and reduced confidence
    \item Reduced activity and independence after a fall
    \item In some falls: a preceding symptom such as dizziness, palpitations, or loss of consciousness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
The cause of a fall should be investigated.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Simple slips and trips on hazards
    \item Dizziness and balance disorders
    \item Fainting from heart rhythm problems, low blood pressure, or dehydration
    \item Seizures and other neurological events
    \item Medication effects
    \item Acute illness, including infection
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
After any significant fall, seek medical assessment, particularly for older people, for head strikes, for people on blood thinners, or when the fall was associated with a loss of consciousness. Recurrent falls always warrant review.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A fall with severe pain or a suspected fracture
    \item A fall with a head injury, especially with confusion or drowsiness
    \item An inability to get up or to weight-bear
    \item A fall with chest pain, breathlessness, or loss of consciousness
    \item Signs of stroke or a heart attack
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
After a fall, the cause and the injuries are assessed.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Assessment of injuries:} examination, and X-rays or imaging for suspected fractures
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} for anaemia, infection, blood sugar, and other causes
    \item \textbf{Medication review:} identifying medicines that increase fall risk
    \item \textbf{Blood pressure:} checking for a drop on standing
    \item \textbf{ECG:} for heart rhythm problems
    \item \textbf{Balance and mobility assessment:} by a physiotherapist
    \item \textbf{Vision check:} for glasses and eye disease
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management addresses the injuries and the underlying risks.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Treatment of injuries:} fractures, wounds, and head injuries are treated appropriately
    \item \textbf{Falls risk assessment:} a structured review of risk factors
    \item \textbf{Exercise:} balance and strength programs are the most effective prevention
    \item \textbf{Medication adjustment:} reducing or changing medicines that increase risk
    \item \textbf{Vision care:} updating glasses and treating eye disease
    \item \textbf{Home safety:} removing hazards, improving lighting, and installing supports
    \item \textbf{Footwear and foot care:} well-fitted shoes and treatment of foot problems
    \item \textbf{Assistance devices:} walking aids and personal alarms
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Hip, wrist, and spine fractures
    \item Head injury and bleeding in the brain
    \item Loss of confidence and reduced activity
    \item Loss of independence and admission to residential care
    \item Lying on the floor for long periods, with dehydration and pressure injuries
    \item Death, particularly in older people with fractures
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Many falls and their injuries are preventable. Exercise programs that improve strength and balance reduce falls by a significant margin, and attention to medicines, vision, and the home makes further gains. After a fall, most people recover fully with appropriate treatment and rehabilitation, and a structured falls prevention plan greatly reduces the risk of another fall.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Take part in a balance and strength exercise program
    \item Review medicines with a doctor or pharmacist regularly
    \item Have eyes checked regularly and wear the correct glasses
    \item Remove loose rugs, cords, and clutter
    \item Improve lighting, including night lights
    \item Wear well-fitted, non-slip shoes
    \item Ask for a falls risk assessment after any fall
\end{itemize}
