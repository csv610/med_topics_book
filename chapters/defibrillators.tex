\chapter{Defibrillators}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
A defibrillator is a device that delivers a controlled electric shock to the heart to restore a normal rhythm in a person whose heart has stopped beating effectively. Defibrillators are used in two main settings: emergency care for cardiac arrest, using automated external defibrillators (AEDs), and ongoing protection for people at high risk of dangerous heart rhythms, using implantable cardioverter-defibrillators (ICDs). This chapter explains how defibrillators work, when they are used, and what to do when a person collapses with a cardiac arrest.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest is a major public health problem, affecting around 20,000 Australians each year, and survival is under 10\% overall. The chance of survival falls by about 10\% for every minute without defibrillation. Bystander CPR and early use of an AED can double or triple the chance of survival. AEDs are now located in many public places, workplaces, schools, and sporting venues across Australia. ICDs are implanted in thousands of Australians each year to prevent sudden cardiac death from conditions such as severe heart failure and inherited arrhythmia syndromes.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cardiac arrest rhythms:} most sudden cardiac arrests are caused by ventricular fibrillation or pulseless ventricular tachycardia, in which the heart's electrical activity is chaotic and the heart cannot pump blood
    \item \textbf{Electrical shock:} defibrillation delivers a strong, brief current that momentarily stops all electrical activity, allowing the heart's natural pacemaker to re-establish a normal rhythm
    \item \textbf{Causes of arrest:} heart attack, heart failure, cardiomyopathy, inherited arrhythmia syndromes, and electrical accidents
    \item \textbf{Timing:} defibrillation is most effective in the first minutes; without it, the heart deteriorates to a rhythm that cannot be shocked
    \item \textbf{ICD function:} an implanted device continuously monitors the heart and automatically delivers a shock or overdrive pacing if a dangerous rhythm occurs
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden collapse, unresponsiveness, and absent or abnormal breathing (cardiac arrest)
    \item No pulse and no signs of life
    \item Seizure-like movements in the first seconds of arrest
    \item In people with an ICD: the device may deliver a shock, which can feel like a thump to the chest
    \item People at risk may have prior symptoms such as fainting, palpitations, chest pain, or a known heart condition
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Fainting (syncope) or a seizure, which can mimic cardiac arrest but are not necessarily fatal
    \item Stroke, which causes collapse without cardiac arrest
    \item Severe choking or airway obstruction
    \item Severe hypoglycaemia, which causes collapse and altered consciousness
    \item Drug overdose, which can suppress breathing and heart function
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Anyone who collapses and is unresponsive with abnormal breathing requires immediate emergency action: call for help, start CPR, and use an AED as soon as it is available. People with fainting, palpitations, or a family history of sudden cardiac death should be assessed by a doctor, as these can be warning signs of arrhythmia risk.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Any person who is unresponsive and not breathing normally (possible cardiac arrest)
    \item Chest pain with collapse, severe breathlessness, or a very fast or irregular heartbeat
    \item Repeated fainting episodes with palpitations or a family history of sudden death
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{ECG (electrocardiogram):} records the heart's electrical activity and identifies arrhythmia syndromes, heart attack, and other abnormalities
    \item \textbf{Echocardiogram:} ultrasound of the heart to assess pumping function and valve problems
    \item \textbf{Exercise stress testing:} identifies rhythm problems provoked by exertion
    \item \textbf{Holter or event monitoring:} prolonged recording to capture intermittent arrhythmias
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} troponin for heart attack and screening for electrolyte disturbances or genetic conditions
    \item \textbf{Genetic testing:} for inherited arrhythmia syndromes in selected families
    \item \textbf{Electrophysiology studies:} specialist testing of the heart's electrical pathways
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Emergency defibrillation:} AEDs are designed for use by the public; they analyse the rhythm and instruct when to deliver a shock, making them safe for untrained bystanders
    \item \textbf{CPR:} continuous chest compressions before and between shocks maintain blood flow to the brain
    \item \textbf{Implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD):} for people at high risk of sudden cardiac death; the device detects and treats dangerous rhythms automatically
    \item \textbf{Medication:} antiarrhythmic drugs and treatments for heart failure or coronary disease to reduce arrhythmia risk
    \item \textbf{Cardiac resynchronisation:} specialised pacemaker function combined with ICD in some people with heart failure
    \item \textbf{Treating the cause:} angioplasty or bypass for coronary disease, surgery for valve problems, and lifestyle change
    \item \textbf{Cardiac rehabilitation:} structured exercise and education programmes after an arrest or device implantation
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Death or brain injury if defibrillation is delayed
    \item ICD complications: infection, lead problems, inappropriate shocks, or device malfunction
    \item Anxiety and psychological distress in cardiac arrest survivors and ICD recipients
    \item Complications of the underlying heart condition, including recurrent arrhythmias
    \item Physical injury from falls during collapse
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Immediate defibrillation within the first few minutes of cardiac arrest produces survival rates above 50\%, whereas survival falls sharply with each minute of delay. Bystander CPR and public-access defibrillation save thousands of lives each year. For people with ICDs, the device effectively prevents most deaths from dangerous arrhythmias, and recipients can return to normal, active lives with appropriate follow-up. Ongoing management of the underlying heart condition further improves long-term outcomes.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Learn CPR and how to use an AED; courses are widely available through St John Ambulance and other providers
    \item Know where AEDs are located in your workplace, school, and community
    \item Seek medical review for fainting, palpitations, or chest pain, and follow up on family history of sudden death
    \item Maintain heart-healthy habits: exercise, a balanced diet, not smoking, and managing blood pressure and cholesterol
    \item Attend regular follow-up for ICD checks and take prescribed heart medicines
    \item If you see someone collapse, call 000, start CPR, and send someone to fetch the nearest AED
\end{itemize}
