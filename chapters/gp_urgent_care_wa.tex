\chapter{GP Urgent Care (WA)}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
GP urgent care clinics in Western Australia provide timely, bulk-billed medical care for urgent but non-life-threatening conditions that would otherwise be treated in a hospital emergency department. They are designed to take pressure off emergency departments by treating conditions such as minor infections, minor injuries, rashes, and other problems that need same-day attention but are not emergencies. GP urgent care clinics are staffed by GPs and nurses, are open extended hours including evenings and weekends, and do not require an appointment in many cases. They are part of a broader national initiative to improve access to urgent primary care across Australia.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Emergency departments in Australia are under growing pressure, with many presentations being for conditions that could be managed in primary care. Studies suggest that 10--40\% of emergency department visits could be treated in urgent care or general practice settings. In Western Australia, GP urgent care clinics have been established in metropolitan and selected regional locations to address this. The clinics target people who need urgent care but do not have an immediately life-threatening condition, and their use has been associated with reduced waiting times for those who would otherwise attend emergency departments.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
This chapter concerns a health service rather than a disease. The conditions treated at GP urgent care clinics include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Minor infections such as urinary tract infections, ear infections, and skin infections
    \item Minor injuries including cuts, sprains, minor burns, and suspected minor fractures
    \item Rashes and skin conditions, including allergic reactions
    \item Fevers, coughs, colds, and respiratory infections
    \item Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, and mild dehydration
    \item Minor eye problems such as conjunctivitis
    \item Worsening of chronic conditions that needs same-day review
    \item Prescriptions, referrals, and sick certificates for urgent issues
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
GP urgent care is appropriate when a condition is urgent but not an emergency.

\begin{itemize}
    \item The condition needs care today but is not life-threatening
    \item Symptoms are new, worsening, or causing significant concern
    \item The person cannot get a timely appointment with their regular GP
    \item Care is needed outside normal GP hours
    \item The condition is unlikely to need hospital admission
    \item The person is generally well apart from the presenting problem
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Choosing the right service matters: not everything urgent belongs in urgent care.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Emergency conditions:} chest pain, stroke symptoms, severe breathing difficulty, major trauma, heavy bleeding, and loss of consciousness need emergency departments (call 000)
    \item \textbf{Routine care:} ongoing management of chronic conditions, health checks, and non-urgent problems are best managed by the regular GP
    \item \textbf{Children:} babies under three months with fever, and children who are very unwell, may need emergency care rather than urgent care
    \item \textbf{Pregnancy:} bleeding, severe pain, or reduced fetal movements in pregnancy need urgent obstetric or emergency review
    \item \textbf{Mental health crises:} may need emergency care or crisis services
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Use a GP urgent care clinic for conditions that need same-day attention but are not emergencies. Call your regular GP first if possible, as they know your history.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chest pain, pressure, or tightness
    \item Sudden weakness, facial drooping, or difficulty speaking (possible stroke)
    \item Severe breathing difficulty or choking
    \item Severe bleeding or major trauma
    \item Fits or seizures
    \item Loss of consciousness or collapse
    \item Any sudden, severe symptom that worries you
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
GP urgent care clinics provide the same range of assessment and treatment as a general practice, with some on-site facilities.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Consultation with a GP:} history, examination, and diagnosis
    \item \textbf{Basic investigations:} urine tests, blood tests, ECG, and some imaging may be available or arranged
    \item \textbf{Minor procedures:} wound care, suturing, removal of foreign bodies, and drainage of abscesses
    \item \textbf{Treatment:} prescriptions, dressings, splints, and referrals
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} a summary is sent to the patient's regular GP so care continues
    \item \textbf{Referral:} people who need emergency or inpatient care are referred or transferred to hospital
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Getting the most from GP urgent care involves using it appropriately and following up.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Check opening hours and location:} urgent care clinics have extended hours, and some require bookings while others accept walk-ins
    \item \textbf{Bulk billing:} most services are bulk-billed, so there is no out-of-pocket cost for Medicare cardholders
    \item \textbf{Bring information:} Medicare card, medicines list, and any relevant test results
    \item \textbf{See your regular GP for follow-up:} the clinic sends a summary, and ongoing care continues with your usual GP
    \item \textbf{Use it instead of emergency for the right conditions:} this reduces emergency waiting times for those who need them most
    \item \textbf{Know when to escalate:} if symptoms worsen, or new serious symptoms appear, seek emergency care
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
Common problems with urgent care services relate to misuse or misunderstanding:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Attending with an emergency condition that needs hospital care, delaying definitive treatment
    \item Using urgent care for routine care, fragmenting continuity with the regular GP
    \item Missing follow-up with the usual GP after an urgent care visit
    \item Not bringing medicines information, leading to prescribing errors
    \item Unnecessary cost if the service is not bulk-billed for the individual
    \item Delays when demand is high
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
GP urgent care clinics improve access to timely primary care and reduce pressure on hospital emergency departments. Most conditions treated at these clinics resolve with appropriate treatment, and patients generally have good outcomes with early care. The model works best when the community uses it for its intended purpose, leaving emergency departments for true emergencies and maintaining ongoing care with regular GPs for chronic conditions.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Keep the details of your nearest GP urgent care clinic handy
    \item Maintain a relationship with a regular GP for routine and chronic care
    \item Keep an up-to-date list of your medicines and allergies
    \item Call healthdirect (1800 022 222) for advice on where to seek care
    \item Use the emergency department for emergencies, not for convenience
    \item Follow up with your regular GP after an urgent care visit
    \item Know the warning signs that require emergency care
    \item Seek care early for conditions that are worsening rather than waiting
\end{itemize}
