\chapter{Diabetic Microvascular Complications}
\index{Diabetic Microvascular Complications}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Diabetic microvascular complications are damage to the small blood vessels caused by chronically high blood glucose levels in people with diabetes. They affect three main sites: the eyes (diabetic retinopathy), the kidneys (diabetic nephropathy), and the nerves (diabetic neuropathy). Microvascular complications develop gradually over many years and are the major cause of blindness, kidney failure, and lower-limb amputation in adults. They are largely preventable with good glucose control, blood pressure management, and regular screening, and their progression can be slowed even after they develop.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Microvascular complications are common in people with long-standing diabetes. Diabetic retinopathy affects around one-third of people with diabetes, and it is the leading cause of preventable blindness in working-age adults. Diabetic kidney disease affects around 20--40\% of people with diabetes and is the leading cause of end-stage kidney disease requiring dialysis in many countries. Diabetic neuropathy affects about half of people with diabetes over time and is a major cause of foot ulcers and amputations. The risk of all three rises with duration of diabetes and level of glucose control.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{High blood glucose:} chronic hyperglycaemia damages the lining of small blood vessels through several biochemical pathways
    \item \textbf{Advanced glycation end products:} glucose binds to proteins, impairing their function
    \item \textbf{Oxidative stress:} high glucose increases damaging free radicals
    \item \textbf{Retinopathy:} retinal blood vessels weaken, leak, and grow abnormally, damaging the retina
    \item \textbf{Nephropathy:} glomerular damage causes protein to leak into the urine and kidney function to decline
    \item \textbf{Neuropathy:} nerve damage from reduced blood supply and direct glucose toxicity causes loss of sensation, pain, and autonomic problems
    \item \textbf{Blood pressure and smoking:} both worsen microvascular damage
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Eyes:} early retinopathy has no symptoms; later there is blurred vision, floaters, and eventually sight loss
    \item \textbf{Kidneys:} early nephropathy has no symptoms; later there is swelling, fatigue, and high blood pressure as kidney function fails
    \item \textbf{Nerves:} numbness, tingling, burning, or pain in the feet and hands; loss of sensation increases injury risk
    \item \textbf{Autonomic neuropathy:} dizziness on standing, digestive problems, bladder and sexual dysfunction
    \item \textbf{Feet:} loss of feeling, ulcers, and infection that can lead to amputation
    \item \textbf{General:} complications often coexist, so a person with retinopathy should also be checked for kidney and nerve damage
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Other causes of vision loss, such as cataracts, glaucoma, and age-related macular degeneration
    \item Other causes of kidney disease, including hypertension, glomerulonephritis, and polycystic kidney disease
    \item Other causes of neuropathy, including vitamin B12 deficiency, alcohol, and spinal disorders
    \item Peripheral vascular disease, which causes leg pain and poor healing
    \item Other causes of foot ulcers, such as venous disease and trauma
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
People with diabetes should attend recommended screening for eyes, kidneys, and feet at least annually, even if they feel well, because early complications cause no symptoms. Seek prompt medical care for any change in vision, swelling of the legs, foot injuries, or loss of sensation.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden loss of vision or flashes and floaters
    \item A foot ulcer with spreading redness, fever, or signs of infection
    \item Chest pain, breathlessness, or swelling, which may indicate related heart or kidney problems
    \item Severe dizziness or collapse
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Eye examination:} dilated retinal photography or examination by an optometrist or ophthalmologist
    \item \textbf{Kidney tests:} urine albumin to creatinine ratio and blood tests of kidney function
    \item \textbf{Nerve assessment:} examination of sensation, reflexes, and foot pulses
    \item \textbf{Blood pressure and glucose control:} HbA1c and blood pressure monitoring
    \item \textbf{Foot examination:} inspection for ulcers, deformity, and sensation loss at every diabetes review
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Glucose control:} maintaining HbA1c in the target range is the most important measure
    \item \textbf{Blood pressure control:} strict blood pressure lowering protects the kidneys and eyes
    \item \textbf{Kidney-protective medicines:} ACE inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers are used even at normal blood pressure in kidney disease
    \item \textbf{Eye treatment:} laser therapy, injections into the eye, and vitrectomy for advanced retinopathy
    \item \textbf{Foot care:} daily inspection, appropriate footwear, and prompt treatment of any injury
    \item \textbf{Pain management:} medicines for neuropathic pain such as pregabalin, gabapentin, or antidepressants
    \item \textbf{Kidney failure treatment:} dialysis or kidney transplant for end-stage disease
    \item \textbf{Smoking cessation and lifestyle:} smoking cessation and healthy living slow progression
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Blindness from advanced retinopathy
    \item End-stage kidney disease requiring dialysis or transplant
    \item Foot ulcers, infection, and amputation
    \item Chronic neuropathic pain
    \item Autonomic dysfunction affecting the heart, gut, and bladder
    \item Increased risk of cardiovascular disease and premature death
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The progression of microvascular complications is substantially slowed by good glucose control, blood pressure management, and early treatment. Many people with diabetes never develop significant complications, and those who do can often be treated effectively to preserve vision, kidney function, and limbs. The outlook has improved markedly with screening programmes and modern treatments. Regular screening and early intervention are the keys to preventing disability from diabetic complications.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Keep blood glucose in the target range with diet, activity, and medicines
    \item Keep blood pressure and cholesterol controlled
    \item Attend annual eye, kidney, and foot screening
    \item Examine your feet daily and report any injury promptly
    \item Do not smoke, and limit alcohol
    \item Maintain a healthy weight and stay active
    \item Take prescribed medicines, including kidney-protective agents, as directed
    \item Seek early medical advice for any new symptoms
\end{itemize}
