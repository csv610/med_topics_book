\chapter{Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)}
\index{Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; seek prompt professional advice for individual care.}

\section*{What it is}
Pelvic inflammatory disease is infection and inflammation of the upper reproductive tract, including the uterus, fallopian tubes, and ovaries. It is often related to sexually transmitted infections, but other bacteria can also contribute. Early treatment reduces the risk of lasting damage.

\section*{Symptoms}
Symptoms may include lower-abdominal or pelvic pain, pain during sex, unusual vaginal discharge, bleeding between periods or after sex, pain when passing urine, fever, chills, nausea, or vomiting. Some people have mild or no symptoms.

\section*{Assessment and treatment}
Assessment may include pregnancy testing, pelvic examination, STI tests, urine or blood tests, and ultrasound when complications are suspected. Treatment uses prescribed antibiotics aimed at the likely organisms and should start promptly when PID is suspected; do not wait for every test result. Finish the course and ensure recent partners are assessed and treated as advised.

\section*{Sex and follow-up}
Avoid sex until treatment is completed, symptoms have settled, and partners have been treated according to clinical advice. Repeat testing may be recommended. Untreated or repeated PID can cause chronic pelvic pain, infertility, scarring, or ectopic pregnancy.

\section*{When to seek urgent care}
Seek emergency help for severe or worsening pelvic pain, fainting, heavy bleeding, high fever, repeated vomiting, pregnancy with pelvic pain, or a rapidly worsening illness. Arrange prompt review for new pelvic pain, discharge, bleeding, or STI exposure.
