\chapter{Compartment syndrome}

\section*{Overview}
Compartment syndrome is an infection caused by a microorganism such as a virus, bacterium, fungus, or parasite. Infections can spread between people, through contaminated food or water, through insect or animal bites, or by contact with the environment. Many infections are mild and resolve on their own, while others can be serious, especially in young babies, older people, pregnant women, and people with weakened immune systems.

\section*{Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Fever, chills, or sweats
    \item Tiredness and a general feeling of being unwell
    \item Aches and pains in muscles or joints
    \item Headache
    \item Cough, sore throat, or a runny nose
    \item Nausea, vomiting, or diarrhoea
    \item A rash, swelling, or redness at the site of infection
\end{itemize}

\section*{Causes}
Infections are caused by:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Viruses,} such as those that cause the common cold, influenza, COVID-19, and many childhood illnesses
    \item \textbf{Bacteria,} which can cause throat, skin, chest, urinary, and gut infections
    \item \textbf{Fungi,} which can affect the skin, nails, mouth, or vagina
    \item \textbf{Parasites,} spread for example by insects, contaminated water, or undercooked food
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Medical history and physical examination
    \item Blood, urine, throat, or swab tests to identify the organism
    \item Imaging such as a chest X-ray for chest infections
    \item Stool tests for gut infections
    \item Specific tests such as PCR for certain viruses
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Rest and plenty of fluids for most viral infections
    \item Antibiotics for bacterial infections, used only when needed and as prescribed
    \item Antiviral medicines for selected viral infections
    \item Antifungal medicines for fungal infections
    \item Anti-parasitic medicines for parasitic infections
    \item Medicines such as paracetamol or ibuprofen to reduce fever and relieve pain
    \item Hospital care for severe infections, including fluids, oxygen, or intravenous treatment
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prevention}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Hand washing with soap and water, or alcohol-based sanitiser
    \item Covering coughs and sneezes and disposing of tissues carefully
    \item Keeping up to date with recommended vaccinations
    \item Safe food handling, thorough cooking, and clean water
    \item Using insect repellent and protective clothing in risk areas
    \item Staying home when unwell to avoid spreading infection
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Most mild infections improve within a few days. Seek medical care if symptoms are severe, last longer than expected, or are getting worse. \textbf{Call 000 or go to an emergency department} for:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Difficulty breathing or chest pain
    \item Confusion, drowsiness, or a stiff neck with a rash
    \item A fever that does not settle, especially in babies, older people, or during pregnancy
    \item Persistent vomiting or signs of dehydration
    \item A rapidly spreading skin redness or a wound that becomes hot and swollen
\end{itemize}
