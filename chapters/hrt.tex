\chapter{HRT}
\index{HRT}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
HRT, or hormone replacement therapy (also called menopausal hormone therapy), is treatment with oestrogen, with or without a progestogen, to relieve menopausal symptoms and prevent the bone loss caused by falling oestrogen levels. It is the most effective treatment for hot flushes, night sweats, and the genitourinary symptoms of menopause. Oestrogen is used alone in women who have had a hysterectomy, and combined with progestogen in women with a uterus to protect the lining. Modern HRT uses the lowest effective dose for the shortest appropriate time, with treatment individualised after discussion of risks and benefits.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Around three-quarters of women experience menopausal symptoms, and up to a quarter have symptoms severe enough to affect daily life. In many countries, fewer than one in five eligible women use HRT, partly due to concerns from older research that overstated risks. For most healthy women under 60 who start treatment near menopause, the benefits outweigh the risks. HRT use has been increasing in recent years as evidence has clarified which women benefit most, and newer delivery routes have improved safety.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Menopause:} ovarian oestrogen production falls, causing vasomotor symptoms, sleep disturbance, vaginal dryness, and accelerated bone loss
    \item \textbf{Oestrogen:} replaces the missing hormone, stabilising temperature control, improving vaginal health, and preserving bone
    \item \textbf{Progestogen:} added for women with a uterus to prevent thickening of the uterine lining
    \item \textbf{Delivery:} tablets, patches, gels, and vaginal preparations
    \item \textbf{Pathophysiology:} restoring oestrogen reverses many of the physiological changes of menopause
    \item \textbf{Risks:} small increases in venous thrombosis, stroke, and breast cancer in some women, depending on age, route, and duration
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item HRT is considered for troublesome hot flushes, night sweats, and sleep disturbance
    \item Vaginal oestrogen treats dryness, discomfort, and recurrent urinary infections
    \item HRT helps prevent post-menopausal osteoporosis and fractures
    \item Symptoms improve within weeks of starting
    \item Treatment is reviewed regularly and tapered when symptoms settle
    \item It is not recommended solely for heart protection
    \item It is generally avoided in women with a history of breast cancer or blood clots
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Menopausal symptoms can overlap with thyroid disease, depression, and anaemia
    \item Not all women need HRT; non-hormonal options and lifestyle measures help some
    \item The choice to use HRT depends on age, time since menopause, and personal risk factors
    \item Uterine bleeding on HRT requires review
    \item Individualised risk assessment distinguishes who benefits most
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Women with troublesome menopausal symptoms should discuss HRT with their doctor. Any bleeding after menopause, or new symptoms while on HRT, requires review.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chest pain, sudden breathlessness, or a swollen painful leg (possible blood clot)
    \item Severe headache, visual disturbance, or signs of stroke
    \item Sudden heavy vaginal bleeding
    \item Collapse or fainting
    \item Breast changes with other concerning symptoms
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Symptom assessment:} the nature and impact of menopausal symptoms
    \item \textbf{Risk assessment:} blood pressure, weight, clot and breast cancer history, and cardiovascular risk
    \item \textbf{Bone density:} where osteoporosis risk is elevated
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} FSH may help in younger women or unclear diagnosis
    \item \textbf{Regular review:} annual assessment of symptoms and ongoing need
    \item \textbf{Breast screening:} routine mammography continues as recommended
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Systemic HRT:} oestrogen with or without progestogen, via tablet, patch, or gel
    \item \textbf{Vaginal oestrogen:} low-dose treatment for genitourinary symptoms
    \item \textbf{Dosing:} the lowest effective dose, adjusted to response
    \item \textbf{Duration:} usually 2--5 years for symptoms, longer for bone protection in some women
    \item \textbf{Tapering:} gradual reduction when stopping
    \item \textbf{Alternatives:} some antidepressants and cognitive behavioural therapy help women who cannot use HRT
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle:} exercise, calcium, vitamin D, and a healthy weight support menopausal health
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Side effects: breast tenderness, nausea, bloating, and breakthrough bleeding early on
    \item Small increased risk of venous thromboembolism and stroke, mainly with tablets in older women
    \item Small increased risk of breast cancer with prolonged combined use
    \item Gallbladder disease with tablets
    \item Risks are lower with patches and gels than tablets for clotting
    \item Vaginal bleeding requires review
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
For most healthy women who start HRT near the menopause, the benefits outweigh the risks: symptoms resolve, quality of life improves, and fracture risk falls. Absolute risks are small in women under 60 and decline after stopping. Women who cannot or choose not to use HRT have effective alternatives. Individualised decision-making, regular review, and attention to overall health give the best outcomes.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Discuss symptoms and options with your doctor
    \item Ask about the risks and benefits for your age and health
    \item Use the lowest effective dose and review regularly
    \item Report any unusual bleeding promptly
    \item Maintain activity, calcium, and vitamin D for bone health
    \item Manage cardiovascular risk: no smoking, healthy diet, and blood pressure control
    \item Consider non-hormonal options if HRT is not suitable
    \item Seek support from menopause information services
\end{itemize}
