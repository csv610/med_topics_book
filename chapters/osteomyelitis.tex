\chapter{Osteomyelitis}

\section*{Overview}
Osteomyelitis is an infection of the bone, usually caused by bacteria (most commonly Staphylococcus aureus) or fungi. It can occur through bloodstream spread, direct inoculation (trauma, surgery), or contiguous spread from adjacent infected tissue. Acute osteomyelitis presents rapidly; chronic osteomyelitis persists or recurs despite treatment.

\section*{Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Bone pain
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Swelling and warmth over affected area
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Fever and chills
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Fatigue
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Drainage from affected area (chronic cases)
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Reduced function of affected limb
\end{itemize}

\section*{Causes}
Causes include bacterial infection (Staphylococcus aureus most common), hematogenous spread, direct inoculation from trauma or surgery, and contiguous spread from soft tissue infections. Risk factors include diabetes, peripheral vascular disease, IV drug use, and recent surgery.

\section*{Diagnosis}
Diagnosis involves blood tests (elevated WBC, ESR, CRP), blood cultures, and imaging (X-ray, MRI, CT, or bone scan). Bone biopsy with culture confirms the organism and guides antibiotic therapy.

\section*{Treatment}
Treatment requires prolonged antibiotics (typically 4-6 weeks) based on culture results. Surgical debridement may be necessary to remove infected bone. Hyperbaric oxygen therapy may assist in refractory cases.

\section*{Prevention}
Prevent osteomyelitis through proper wound care, infection control, managing diabetes and vascular disease, and prompt treatment of soft tissue infections.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek immediate medical attention for unexplained bone pain, fever with localized bone tenderness, or non-healing wounds near bones, especially in diabetic patients.
