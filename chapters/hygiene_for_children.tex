\chapter{Hygiene for Children}
\index{Hygiene for Children}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Hygiene for children means the daily habits that keep them clean and reduce the spread of infection: hand washing, bathing, tooth brushing, and good toilet habits. Teaching children these habits early protects them from common infections, supports healthy teeth and skin, and builds skills that last a lifetime. Parents and caregivers play the key role, modelling hygiene and turning routines into positive habits. Good hygiene also protects others, including babies, grandparents, and people with weakened immunity.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Infectious diseases are the most common illnesses of childhood, and many are spread by hands and surfaces. Hand washing reduces the risk of gastroenteritis and respiratory infections substantially. Tooth decay affects about a quarter of children, most of it preventable with brushing, fluoride, and diet. Head lice, worms, and skin infections are common in school-age children. Establishing hygiene habits in early childhood is one of the most effective ways to reduce illness across the whole community.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hand hygiene:} washing with soap and water removes germs picked up from surfaces, toys, and other people
    \item \textbf{Pathophysiology of spread:} viruses and bacteria pass from hands to mouth, nose, or eyes, causing respiratory and gastrointestinal infection
    \item \textbf{Dental hygiene:} brushing with fluoride toothpaste removes plaque and prevents tooth decay
    \item \textbf{Bathing:} keeps skin clean and prevents rashes, fungal infections, and infestations
    \item \textbf{Toilet hygiene:} wiping front to back and hand washing after toileting prevent infections
    \item \textbf{Nail and hair care:} short nails and clean hair reduce germ and louse spread
    \item \textbf{Key times to wash:} after toileting, before eating, after play, and after coughing or sneezing
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Hand washing before meals and after the toilet is the single most important habit
    \item Tooth brushing twice daily with fluoride toothpaste from the first tooth
    \item Regular baths or showers, more often with activity and hot weather
    \item Clean clothes, especially underwear and socks
    \item Short, clean fingernails
    \item Supervision of brushing until children can do it well, around age 8
    \item Good habits are modelled by parents and caregivers
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Hygiene habits vary by age, from nappy changes in babies to independent routines in older children
    \item Excessive hygiene is not helpful; normal play with dirt is fine
    \item Recurrent infections may indicate hygiene gaps or underlying conditions
    \item Skin conditions such as eczema need moisturising, not just bathing
    \item Children with disabilities may need adapted routines
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor for frequent infections, tooth pain, persistent skin problems, or concerns about a child's development or toileting. Medical care is needed for dehydration, high fever, or any serious illness.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item High fever with lethargy, a rash that does not fade, or breathing difficulty
    \item A baby who is floppy or not feeding
    \item Severe dehydration
    \item Head injury or serious injury
    \item Any life-threatening emergency
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Routine assessment:} child health checks monitor growth and development
    \item \textbf{Dental checks:} regular visits to the dentist from age 1--2
    \item \textbf{Assessment of recurrent infections:} review hygiene, immunisation, and any underlying issues
    \item \textbf{Skin assessment:} for eczema, infections, and infestations
    \item \textbf{Toilet training support:} advice when difficulties arise
    \item \textbf{Community and school programs:} reinforce hygiene education
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hand washing:} teach 20 seconds of washing with soap, before meals and after the toilet
    \item \textbf{Tooth care:} brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste and visit the dentist regularly
    \item \textbf{Bathing:} daily or every second day, with gentle soap and attention to skin creases
    \item \textbf{Toilet training:} respond to readiness, with positive reinforcement
    \item \textbf{Hair and nails:} regular washing and trimming
    \item \textbf{Illness hygiene:} teach coughing into the elbow and staying home when unwell
    \item \textbf{Immunisation:} protects against vaccine-preventable diseases
    \item \textbf{Modelling:} children learn habits from watching parents
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Frequent gastroenteritis and respiratory infections
    \item Tooth decay, gum disease, and dental pain
    \item Skin infections, fungal rashes, and infestations
    \item Spread of infection to vulnerable family members
    \item Absences from school and childcare
    \item Poor self-esteem from hygiene-related teasing
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Children who learn good hygiene habits have fewer infections, healthier teeth, and better attendance at school and childcare. Habits established in childhood persist into adulthood, supporting lifelong health. Tooth decay and most common infections are largely preventable with hygiene, diet, and immunisation. The benefits extend to the whole community through reduced spread of infection.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Establish hand-washing routines early and make them fun
    \item Brush teeth twice daily and attend dental checks
    \item Bathe regularly and change into clean clothes daily
    \item Keep nails short and hair clean
    \item Keep immunisations up to date
    \item Stay home when unwell and teach good coughing etiquette
    \item Clean toys and surfaces regularly, especially when illness occurs
    \item Model good hygiene yourself
\end{itemize}
