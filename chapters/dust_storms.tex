\chapter{Dust Storms}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Dust storms are events in which strong winds lift large amounts of dust and soil particles into the air, reducing visibility and spreading fine particles over vast areas. They occur naturally in arid and semi-arid regions and can be worsened by drought, land clearing, and poor land management. In Australia, dust storms are most common in inland areas but can carry dust hundreds of kilometres to coastal cities. The health effects of dust storms arise from the inhalation of fine particles, which can irritate the airways and aggravate conditions such as asthma, chronic lung disease, and heart disease.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Dust storms affect many regions of Australia, particularly the interior, and occasionally blanket major cities, as happened in the significant dust storm of September 2009. Exposure to elevated particle levels during dust storms has been associated with increases in hospital admissions for asthma, respiratory illness, and cardiovascular disease. People living in rural areas and those with pre-existing respiratory or cardiac conditions are at greatest risk, along with children and older people.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Dust storms occur when wind speed and surface conditions allow soil particles to become airborne.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Wind and soil:} strong winds lift loose, dry topsoil in areas of low vegetation cover
    \item \textbf{Drought and land management:} prolonged dry periods and practices that disturb the soil increase the supply of dust
    \item \textbf{Particle composition:} dust contains soil minerals, and may also carry pollen, fungi, bacteria, and chemical residues
    \item \textbf{Particle size:} fine particles (PM10 and smaller) are inhaled deep into the lungs, where they cause irritation and inflammation
    \item \textbf{Health effects:} inhaled particles trigger bronchospasm, airway inflammation, and stress on the heart and circulation
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Symptoms are most prominent in people with existing respiratory or cardiac conditions, but healthy people can also be affected.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Cough, wheezing, chest tightness, and shortness of breath
    \item Nasal congestion, sneezing, and throat irritation
    \item Itchy, red, or watery eyes
    \item Worsening of asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease
    \item Chest pain or palpitations in people with heart disease
    \item Headaches and fatigue in some people
    \item Skin irritation from settled dust
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
The effects of dust exposure can resemble other respiratory problems.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Asthma attacks triggered by other allergens or irritants
    \item Respiratory infections such as colds, influenza, and bronchitis
    \item Allergic rhinitis from pollen or mould
    \item Cardiac conditions presenting with breathlessness or chest pain
    \item Heat-related illness, which may occur during the hot, dry conditions associated with dust storms
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
People with asthma, chronic lung disease, or heart disease should follow their action plans during dust storms and seek medical advice if symptoms worsen despite treatment.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe difficulty breathing that does not improve with reliever medication
    \item Wheezing or breathlessness that prevents speaking full sentences
    \item Chest pain, palpitations, or collapse
    \item Fainting or severe headache during the storm, especially in heat
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Most people do not need formal investigation, and care focuses on symptom control.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History:} symptom onset during the dust event and any underlying conditions
    \item \textbf{Examination:} assessment of breathing, oxygen levels, and peak flow in people with asthma
    \item \textbf{Monitoring:} air quality alerts and particle monitoring inform risk during dust storms
    \item \textbf{Further tests:} chest X-rays or blood tests are only needed for people with severe or persistent symptoms
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management centres on reducing exposure and treating symptoms.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Stay indoors:} keep windows and doors closed during the storm, and use recirculated air in air conditioning
    \item \textbf{Avoid outdoor activity:} particularly strenuous exercise, until air quality improves
    \item \textbf{Respiratory treatment:} people with asthma should use their preventers as prescribed and relievers for symptoms
    \item \textbf{Medical review:} seek care if symptoms persist or worsen after the storm
    \item \textbf{Cleaning up:} damp-wipe surfaces rather than sweeping to avoid re-suspending dust
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Asthma attacks and hospital admission
    \item Exacerbation of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease
    \item Increased risk of cardiac events in vulnerable people
    \item Eye irritation and infection from dust particles
    \item Respiratory infection following heavy exposure
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
For most people, the health effects of a dust storm are temporary and settle within a few days as air quality improves. People with chronic respiratory or cardiac disease may take longer to recover and should seek medical review. Repeated exposure over years, particularly in occupations involving dust, carries greater long-term risk, so ongoing protection is important.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Monitor air quality alerts and dust storm warnings
    \item Stay indoors with windows and doors closed during dust events
    \item Wear a P2/N95 mask if going outside is unavoidable
    \item Keep reliever and preventer medications available and follow your asthma action plan
    \item Protect eyes with wrap-around glasses or goggles during clean-up
    \item Damp-wipe surfaces after the storm instead of sweeping
    \item Discuss a plan with your doctor if you have lung or heart disease
\end{itemize}
