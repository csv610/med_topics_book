\chapter{Blood and Blood Vessels}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Blood and blood vessels together make up the circulatory system. Blood is the fluid that carries oxygen, nutrients, hormones, immune cells, and clotting factors around the body, while blood vessels -- arteries, veins, and capillaries -- are the network of tubes that transport it. The heart pumps the blood, and the whole system delivers to every organ what it needs and removes the waste it produces. Disease can affect the blood itself, as in anaemia and leukaemia, or the vessels, as in atherosclerosis, aneurysms, and varicose veins, and disease of the vessels is the basis of most heart attacks and strokes.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Diseases of the blood and blood vessels are the leading cause of death worldwide. Heart attack, stroke, and peripheral artery disease, driven largely by atherosclerosis, kill more people each year than any other group of conditions. High blood pressure affects roughly one in three adults and is the single largest contributor to global disease burden. Anaemia is the most common blood disorder, affecting around a quarter of the world's population, particularly women and children. The burden of vascular disease rises with age, and risk is increased by smoking, diabetes, high cholesterol, obesity, and physical inactivity.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The blood and blood vessels are affected by several distinct processes, often coexisting:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Atherosclerosis:} fatty plaques build up inside arteries, narrowing them and raising the risk of blockage by clots, causing heart attack, stroke, or limb ischaemia
    \item \textbf{High blood pressure:} sustained pressure damages the lining of arteries and the organs they supply
    \item \textbf{Blood disorders:} anaemia (too few red cells), abnormal white-cell production (leukaemia), and clotting-factor deficiencies (haemophilia)
    \item \textbf{Clots and emboli:} thrombosis forming within a vessel, or a clot travelling to block a vessel elsewhere
    \item \textbf{Weak vessel walls:} aneurysms balloon outwards and may rupture
    \item \textbf{Vein disease:} varicose veins and chronic venous insufficiency result from damaged vein valves
    \item \textbf{Inflammation:} vasculitis, in which the immune system attacks the vessel walls
    \item \textbf{Inherited disorders:} sickle cell disease, thalassaemia, haemophilia, and inherited thrombophilias
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Symptoms depend on which part of the system is affected:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Heart:} chest pain or pressure, breathlessness, and palpitations
    \item \textbf{Brain:} sudden weakness, numbness, speech difficulty, or facial drooping (stroke)
    \item \textbf{Limbs:} cramping leg pain on walking, cold feet, or a pale, painful limb
    \item \textbf{Anaemia:} fatigue, pale skin, shortness of breath, and dizziness on exertion
    \item \textbf{Clotting problems:} easy bruising, prolonged bleeding, or a swollen, painful leg from a deep vein thrombosis
    \item \textbf{Vein disease:} aching, heavy legs with prominent, twisted veins
    \item \textbf{Aneurysm:} frequently silent, or a deep, persistent pain in the chest, abdomen, or back
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Symptoms arising from the blood and vessels overlap with many other conditions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Chest pain: angina, heart attack, pulmonary embolism, gastro-oesophageal reflux, and musculoskeletal pain
    \item Leg pain on walking: peripheral artery disease, spinal stenosis, or muscle strain
    \item Fatigue: anaemia, thyroid disease, depression, or chronic illness
    \item A swollen calf: deep vein thrombosis, muscle tear, or a Baker's cyst
    \item Leg ulcers: venous ulcers versus arterial ulcers versus diabetic foot ulcers
    \item Palpitations: arrhythmias, anxiety, or thyroid overactivity
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor for persistent fatigue, chest discomfort on exertion, leg pain on walking, new or easy bruising, or a swollen, painful limb.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chest pain or pressure, especially with breathlessness, sweating, or nausea
    \item Sudden weakness, numbness, facial drooping, or difficulty speaking (possible stroke)
    \item Sudden severe breathlessness, or coughing up blood
    \item A swollen, painful, hot leg that may be a deep vein thrombosis
    \item A sudden, tearing pain in the chest, abdomen, or back (possible aortic aneurysm)
    \item Severe bleeding that will not stop
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Investigation depends on the suspected disorder, and a core set of tests applies to most presentations:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Blood pressure measurement:} repeated readings to detect high or low pressure
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} full blood count, iron studies, cholesterol and lipid profile, blood glucose, and kidney function
    \item \textbf{Electrocardiogram (ECG):} recording of the heart's electrical activity
    \item \textbf{Doppler ultrasound:} examination of blood flow in arteries and veins
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} CT or MRI angiography to map the vessels in detail
    \item \textbf{Exercise testing and cardiac imaging:} for suspected coronary artery disease
    \item \textbf{Specialist tests:} bone marrow biopsy and coagulation studies for blood disorders
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management spans lifestyle change, medication, and procedures, and is tailored to the specific condition:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle:} a healthy diet, regular exercise, weight management, and stopping smoking form the foundation for all vascular disease
    \item \textbf{Medication:} blood pressure and cholesterol-lowering drugs, antiplatelet agents, anticoagulants, and iron or vitamin supplements for anaemia
    \item \textbf{Procedures:} angioplasty and stenting to open narrowed arteries
    \item \textbf{Surgery:} coronary and peripheral bypass grafting, aneurysm repair, and treatment of varicose veins
    \item \textbf{Blood products:} transfusion for severe anaemia and clotting-factor replacement for bleeding disorders
    \item \textbf{Specialist care:} haematologists, cardiologists, and vascular surgeons manage complex cases
    \item \textbf{Ongoing monitoring:} regular review of blood pressure, diabetes, cholesterol, and anticoagulation
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Heart attack, stroke, and peripheral artery disease from atherosclerosis
    \item Rupture of an aortic or other aneurysm, a life-threatening emergency
    \item Venous ulcers, leg swelling, and skin changes from chronic venous disease
    \item Deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism
    \item Severe anaemia straining the heart and leading to heart failure
    \item Bleeding complications of anticoagulant treatment
    \item Reduced mobility, disability, and reduced quality of life
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Many diseases of the blood and vessels are chronic but highly controllable. With modern treatment, people with high blood pressure, raised cholesterol, and even established arterial disease can live long, active lives, and stopping smoking dramatically improves the outlook. Anaemia is usually fully correctable once its cause is identified. Outcomes after a heart attack or stroke depend on how quickly treatment begins and on long-term risk-factor control. Regular review and adherence to treatment are the most important determinants of a good outcome.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Do not smoke, and avoid exposure to second-hand smoke
    \item Eat a diet rich in vegetables, fruit, whole grains, and fish, and low in salt, saturated fat, and sugar
    \item Exercise for at least 30 minutes on most days
    \item Maintain a healthy weight and limit alcohol
    \item Know your blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood sugar numbers
    \item Keep blood pressure, diabetes, and cholesterol under treatment if they are raised
    \item Take prescribed medicines as directed and attend follow-up reviews
    \item Seek urgent care for new chest pain, stroke symptoms, or a swollen, painful leg
\end{itemize}
