\chapter{Adenoids}
\index{Adenoids}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
The adenoids are a small pad of tissue at the very back of the nose, where the nose joins the throat. They are part of the immune system and help the body fight infection, particularly in young children, when the immune system is still learning to respond to the many germs encountered early in life.

The adenoids cannot be seen from the mouth without special instruments. They are largest in early childhood, gradually shrink from around the age of 7, and usually become very small by the teenage years. For most people they cause no problems at all and are an important part of normal childhood immunity.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Almost all children have adenoids that are active and, at times, enlarged. They are a normal part of childhood immunity and grow in response to the infections children encounter.

Problems with enlarged adenoids, such as noisy breathing, mouth breathing, snoring, glue ear, and repeated ear infections, are most common between the ages of about 3 and 7, when the adenoids are proportionally largest relative to the airway. By adulthood they rarely cause symptoms.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The adenoids enlarge when they are exposed to infections, much as lymph nodes swell during a sore throat. In most children they return to normal size, but in some they stay large.

Enlarged adenoids can block the back of the nose, making it hard to breathe through the nose, and can block the eustachian tube, which drains fluid from the middle ear. They can also act as a reservoir for bacteria, contributing to repeated ear infections and, occasionally, to infections of the tonsils or sinuses. When the eustachian tube is blocked, fluid collects behind the eardrum (glue ear), reducing hearing and, in younger children, slowing speech development.

\section*{Clinical Features}
Enlarged adenoids can cause:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Nasal obstruction:} mouth breathing and a constantly stuffy nose
    \item \textbf{Snoring:} loud snoring and restless sleep
    \item \textbf{Breathing pauses:} pauses in breathing during sleep (obstructive sleep apnoea)
    \item \textbf{Voice change:} a nasal or muffled voice
    \item \textbf{Ear problems:} recurrent middle-ear infections or glue ear
    \item \textbf{Hearing and speech:} reduced hearing and, in younger children, delayed speech
    \item \textbf{Frequent infections:} recurring colds, a runny nose, or cough
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Symptoms of enlarged adenoids overlap with other common conditions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Allergic rhinitis (hay fever)
    \item Recurrent colds and viral infections
    \item Enlarged tonsils
    \item Chronic sinusitis
    \item Nasal polyps or a deviated septum
    \item A foreign body in the nose (in young children)
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor if a child persistently breathes through the mouth, snores heavily, or has concerns about hearing, speech, or frequent ear infections. Early assessment allows simple treatments to be tried and identifies the small number of children who need specialist review or surgery.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Difficulty breathing or gasping during sleep
    \item Turning blue or stopping breathing during sleep
    \item Unusual drowsiness, confusion, or loss of consciousness
    \item Any life-threatening breathing emergency
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History:} symptoms of nasal blockage, sleep disturbance, hearing problems, and ear infections
    \item \textbf{Examination:} looking at the nose, throat, and eardrums
    \item \textbf{Nasendoscopy:} a small flexible camera used to see the adenoids directly
    \item \textbf{Lateral X-ray of the neck:} may be used to judge adenoid size
    \item \textbf{Hearing tests:} to check for glue ear or hearing loss
    \item \textbf{Sleep study:} if obstructive sleep apnoea is suspected
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Most of the time enlarged adenoids need no treatment, because they shrink on their own as the child grows. When symptoms are troublesome:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Watchful waiting:} regular review while the child grows
    \item \textbf{Nasal steroid sprays:} may help reduce adenoid size and nasal blockage
    \item \textbf{Treating allergies:} antihistamines and avoiding triggers
    \item \textbf{Treating ear problems:} antibiotics for active infection, and grommets for glue ear
    \item \textbf{Adenoidectomy:} surgical removal when blockage, sleep apnoea, or ear problems are significant
    \item \textbf{Good hygiene:} hand washing to reduce the spread of infection
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
Most children with enlarged adenoids do not develop complications, but untreated problems can include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Glue ear, with hearing loss and effects on speech and learning
    \item Obstructive sleep apnoea, with poor sleep, behaviour, and growth
    \item Recurrent ear infections and, rarely, mastoiditis
    \item Changes in facial appearance and dental alignment with long-standing mouth breathing
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
For most children, adenoid problems resolve with time as the tissue naturally shrinks. When symptoms are more severe, treatment, including surgery, is very effective.

Once the adenoids are removed or shrink, mouth breathing, snoring, and ear problems usually improve, and any effects on hearing, speech, and behaviour can recover, especially when treated early. The overall outlook for children with enlarged adenoids is excellent.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Practise good hand hygiene to reduce childhood infections
    \item Avoid tobacco smoke around children
    \item Treat allergic rhinitis and colds early
    \item Keep a child with a stuffy nose well hydrated
    \item Raise the head of the bed slightly if snoring is troublesome
    \item See a doctor early about persistent mouth breathing, snoring, or hearing concerns
\end{itemize}
