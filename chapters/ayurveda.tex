\chapter{Ayurveda}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Ayurveda is a traditional system of medicine that developed in India more than 3,000 years ago. The word means "the science of life". It treats health as a balance between body, mind and spirit, and it emphasises preventing illness through diet, lifestyle, herbal remedies, yoga and meditation rather than treating disease in isolation.

A core idea of Ayurveda is that every person has a unique constitution made up of three energies, or doshas, called Vata, Pitta and Kapha. Health depends on keeping these doshas balanced, and illness is understood as an imbalance. Ayurvedic treatment is highly individualised: two people with the same complaint may receive quite different advice. It is used worldwide today, often as a complementary therapy alongside conventional medicine.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Ayurveda is one of the world's oldest and most widely practised traditional medicine systems. It is a mainstream form of healthcare in India, where it is used by a large share of the population, and it is also practised in Nepal, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh. In Western countries, interest in Ayurveda has grown alongside the general popularity of complementary and integrative medicine.

People typically turn to Ayurveda for chronic problems such as digestive complaints, joint pain, skin conditions, stress, sleep difficulties and general wellbeing. Many users combine Ayurvedic treatments with conventional medical care. Regulation and training standards vary widely between countries, and in many places practitioners are not formally registered.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Ayurveda explains health and disease in its own terms, which differ from the disease models of Western medicine.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The three doshas:} Vata is linked to movement and the nervous system, Pitta to digestion and metabolism, and Kapha to structure, fluids and immunity
    \item \textbf{Imbalance:} disease is thought to arise when a dosha becomes excessive, deficient or blocked
    \item \textbf{Causes of imbalance:} unsuitable diet, irregular routines, stress, poor sleep, seasonal changes and environmental factors
    \item \textbf{Digestive fire:} weak or disturbed digestion is seen as a root of many illnesses, producing toxins that clog the body's channels
    \item \textbf{Constitution:} the inborn balance of doshas (Prakriti) is said to influence vulnerability to particular conditions
\end{itemize}

Ayurvedic treatments aim to restore balance through diet, cleansing, herbal preparations and lifestyle. These concepts are not measured or explained in biomedical terms, and scientific evidence for many Ayurvedic theories is limited.

\section*{Clinical Features}
Because Ayurveda is a system of healthcare rather than a single disease, its "clinical features" are the patterns of imbalance it recognises and the way assessment is tailored to the individual.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Assessment is individualised through questioning, and examination of the pulse, tongue, eyes, skin and nails
    \item A person is described by their dominant dosha and any doshas that are out of balance
    \item Common complaints people seek Ayurvedic care for include indigestion and bloating, joint and muscle aches, skin problems, fatigue, anxiety and poor sleep
    \item Ayurvedic advice for the same condition can differ between people, depending on their constitution
    \item Severe or acute illness is generally outside the scope of self-treatment in this system
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Because Ayurvedic symptoms are non-specific, it is important that serious medical conditions are not overlooked.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Chest pain, breathlessness or collapse must be assessed for heart disease, not treated as an imbalance
    \item Unexplained weight loss, fevers or lumps may indicate cancer or infection and need medical investigation
    \item Persistent abdominal pain or bleeding could be an ulcer, gallstone disease or bowel disease
    \item Joint pain may be inflammatory arthritis rather than a dosha imbalance
    \item Fatigue may be due to anaemia, thyroid disease or diabetes
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a conventional doctor for any symptom that is severe, sudden, persistent or worsening, or for any new lump, unexplained weight loss or bleeding. Ayurvedic care can complement medical treatment but should not replace it, and it is not appropriate as the first response to emergencies.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chest pain, difficulty breathing or fainting
    \item Sudden weakness, numbness or difficulty speaking
    \item Severe abdominal pain, vomiting of blood or black stools
    \item High fever with confusion or a stiff neck
    \item Any sudden, severe symptom that worries you
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
There are no standard laboratory tests that confirm an Ayurvedic diagnosis. Assessment is based on the practitioner's evaluation, and modern medicine uses its own tests to investigate symptoms.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Ayurvedic assessment:} constitution and dosha evaluation by a trained practitioner
    \item \textbf{Conventional investigations:} blood tests, imaging and other tests arranged by a doctor to find medical causes of symptoms
    \item \textbf{Checking for interactions:} review of prescription medicines, because some Ayurvedic herbs can interact with them
    \item \textbf{Product testing:} laboratory testing of herbal preparations where contamination is suspected
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Ayurveda offers a broad set of treatments, and many are used safely as lifestyle and self-care measures.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dietary advice:} individualised food plans based on dosha, season and digestive strength
    \item \textbf{Herbal remedies:} preparations such as turmeric, ashwagandha and triphala, taken as directed
    \item \textbf{Yoga and meditation:} postures, breathing exercises and relaxation techniques
    \item \textbf{Massage and oil therapies:} including warm oil body massage
    \item \textbf{Panchakarma:} a structured cleansing programme involving procedures such as purgation and enemas, undertaken under supervision
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle routines:} regular meals, sleep and daily habits matched to the seasons
    \item \textbf{Integrative care:} best used alongside, and after assessment by, conventional medical care
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Herb--drug interactions, which can change the effect of prescription medicines
    \item Contamination of some herbal products with heavy metals (lead, mercury, arsenic) or other substances
    \item Allergic reactions or side effects from herbs and oils
    \item Risks from vigorous cleansing procedures if performed unsafely or in vulnerable people
    \item Delay in diagnosing serious illness if Ayurvedic care replaces medical assessment
    \item Financial cost, since most Ayurvedic treatment is not covered by health funds
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Ayurveda can be a helpful complement to conventional medicine, especially for chronic problems, stress-related symptoms and general wellbeing. Some individual herbs and practices, such as certain herbal preparations, yoga and meditation, have supporting evidence from research studies, while many others do not. Outcomes depend heavily on the condition, the skill of the practitioner and the quality of the products used. For serious or life-threatening disease, Ayurveda alone is not reliable, and it should not delay effective medical treatment.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
Ayurveda emphasises prevention, and many of its general recommendations overlap with mainstream health advice.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Eat regular, balanced meals based on whole foods and seasonal produce
    \item Go to bed and wake at consistent times
    \item Exercise regularly, including gentle practices such as yoga and walking
    \item Manage stress with breathing exercises, meditation or time in nature
    \item Stay well hydrated and limit alcohol and processed foods
    \item Choose an Ayurvedic practitioner carefully and ask about their training
    \item Tell your doctor about every herb and supplement you take
    \item Continue to attend medical check-ups, screening and prescribed treatments
\end{itemize}
