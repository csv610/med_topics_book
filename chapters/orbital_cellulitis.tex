\chapter{Orbital cellulitis}
\index{Orbital cellulitis}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; seek urgent professional advice.}

\section*{What it is}
Orbital cellulitis is an infection in the deeper tissues within the eye socket. It often follows sinus infection but may also follow injury, surgery, or spread from nearby infection. It can threaten sight and can spread to the brain or bloodstream.

\section*{Warning signs}
Symptoms may include fever, feeling very unwell, eyelid swelling and redness, pain around the eye, pain or restriction when moving the eye, double vision, reduced vision, colour-vision change, or a bulging eye. Swelling around the eyelid without painful eye movement may be a more superficial infection, but only an examination can distinguish these reliably.

\section*{Diagnosis}
This is an emergency assessment. Clinicians examine vision, pupils, eye movements, the face, and neurological function. Blood tests and urgent CT or MRI of the orbits and sinuses may be needed, depending on the examination and local specialist advice.

\section*{Treatment}
Treatment usually requires hospital admission, urgent ophthalmology and ear-nose-throat review, and intravenous antibiotics. Drainage or surgery may be needed for an abscess, severe sinus disease, or failure to improve. Do not delay assessment while trying topical or leftover antibiotics at home.

\section*{When to seek emergency care}
Get emergency help immediately for painful or restricted eye movement, reduced or double vision, a bulging eye, severe headache, confusion, vomiting, marked fever, rapidly worsening swelling, or severe illness. These signs require urgent specialist assessment.
