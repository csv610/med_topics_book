\section*{Definition and Overview}

Symptoms are subjective experiences (e.g.\ pain, nausea); signs are objective findings (e.g.\ fever, rash). Neither a symptom nor a sign is a diagnosis in itself; rather, each is a clue that guides differential diagnosis and investigation.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Symptoms are the most common reason people seek medical care. Pain, fatigue, cough, and digestive complaints are among the most frequently reported. Most symptoms are transient and benign, but a minority signal serious disease. The challenge in clinical practice is distinguishing self-limiting from progressive conditions using history, examination, and targeted investigation.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Infection:} viral, bacterial, or other pathogen triggering inflammation and immune response
    \item \textbf{Inflammation:} immune-mediated tissue damage (allergic, autoimmune, or reactive)
    \item \textbf{Mechanical:} injury, obstruction, compression, or overuse
    \item \textbf{Metabolic/endocrine:} electrolyte disturbance, thyroid dysfunction, diabetes
    \item \textbf{Neurological:} nerve compression, central or peripheral neuropathy
    \item \textbf{Psychogenic:} stress, anxiety, depression manifesting as physical symptoms
    \item \textbf{Neoplastic:} tumour causing direct or paraneoplastic symptoms
    \item \textbf{Iatrogenic:} medication side effects or post-procedural changes
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History:} a detailed description of the symptom -- onset, character, duration, progression, triggers, associated features, and prior episodes
    \item \textbf{Examination:} systematic physical examination of the relevant system and general examination for systemic signs
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} full blood count, CRP, biochemistry, and organ-specific markers
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} X-ray, ultrasound, CT, or MRI depending on the suspected cause
    \item \textbf{Specialised tests:} endoscopy, biopsy, nerve conduction studies, or cardiac stress testing as indicated
\end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Lifestyle measures:} rest, hydration, dietary modification, or activity adjustment
    \item \textbf{Monitoring:} review to ensure the symptom is resolving and to detect any new features
    \item \textbf{Specialist referral:} if the cause is unclear, symptoms persist despite treatment, or specialist investigation is needed

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Most symptoms resolve within days to weeks as the underlying cause is treated or self-resolves. Persistent or recurrent symptoms require thorough evaluation to exclude serious disease. Functional symptoms (those without an identified organic cause) can be challenging to manage and may benefit from a multidisciplinary approach including psychological support.
