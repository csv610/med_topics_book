\chapter{Diarrhoea Causes}
\index{Diarrhoea Causes}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
The causes of diarrhoea are many, ranging from short-lived infections to chronic conditions of the gut. Infectious causes, including viruses, bacteria, and parasites, account for most acute episodes, while chronic diarrhoea is more often due to conditions such as irritable bowel syndrome, inflammatory bowel disease, coeliac disease, and food intolerances. Medicines, surgery, and other medical conditions can also cause diarrhoea. Identifying the cause matters because treatment and prevention differ. This chapter explains the common causes of diarrhoea and how they are distinguished.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Acute diarrhoea is most often caused by viral gastroenteritis, and most people experience it once or more each year. Bacterial causes are less common but are important because of foodborne outbreaks. Chronic diarrhoea affects a smaller proportion of people but can significantly impair quality of life; irritable bowel syndrome is the most common cause, while inflammatory bowel disease and coeliac disease are less common but important. Travellers' diarrhoea affects many people travelling to developing countries.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Viruses:} rotavirus, norovirus, and adenovirus cause most acute gastroenteritis
    \item \textbf{Bacteria:} salmonella, campylobacter, shigella, E. coli, and others from contaminated food or water
    \item \textbf{Parasites:} giardia, cryptosporidium, and amoeba, often from contaminated water
    \item \textbf{Food toxins:} pre-formed toxins cause rapid-onset vomiting and diarrhoea
    \item \textbf{Irritable bowel syndrome:} altered gut motility and sensitivity without inflammation
    \item \textbf{Inflammatory bowel disease:} Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis cause chronic inflammation
    \item \textbf{Coeliac disease:} immune reaction to gluten damages the small intestine
    \item \textbf{Food intolerances:} lactose intolerance and others cause fermentative diarrhoea
    \item \textbf{Medicines:} antibiotics, laxatives, metformin, and others
    \item \textbf{Other causes:} malabsorption, pancreatic disease, thyroid disease, and bowel surgery
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Acute diarrhoea: sudden onset, often with vomiting, fever, and cramps, lasting days
    \item Viral gastroenteritis: watery diarrhoea with vomiting, often in outbreaks
    \item Bacterial diarrhoea: may include blood, mucus, high fever, and severe cramps
    \item Chronic diarrhoea: loose stools for more than four weeks
    \item Irritable bowel syndrome: alternating diarrhoea and constipation with bloating, without weight loss or blood
    \item Inflammatory bowel disease: blood, mucus, urgency, weight loss, and fatigue
    \item Coeliac disease: diarrhoea with bloating, fatigue, and nutrient deficiency
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Acute versus chronic diarrhoea, which have different causes
    \item Infectious versus non-infectious causes
    \item Irritable bowel syndrome versus inflammatory bowel disease versus coeliac disease
    \item Malabsorption versus secretory diarrhoea
    \item Medication-related versus disease-related diarrhoea
    \item Loose stools from constipation with overflow versus true diarrhoea
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor if diarrhoea persists beyond a few days, is chronic, contains blood, or is accompanied by weight loss, fever, or dehydration. Anyone with recurrent or severe symptoms should be assessed to identify the cause.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe dehydration with confusion, drowsiness, or no urine
    \item Bloody diarrhoea with severe abdominal pain or high fever
    \item A baby or child who is listless or seriously unwell
    \item Inability to keep down any fluids
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History:} duration, pattern, associated symptoms, foods, travel, medicines, and family history
    \item \textbf{Stool tests:} microscopy, culture, and testing for parasites and toxins
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} coeliac antibodies, inflammatory markers, thyroid function, and vitamin levels
    \item \textbf{Endoscopy and biopsy:} for inflammatory bowel disease and coeliac disease
    \item \textbf{Breath tests:} for lactose intolerance and bacterial overgrowth
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} CT or MRI in selected cases
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Acute infectious diarrhoea:} fluids, rest, and time; antibiotics only when indicated
    \item \textbf{Irritable bowel syndrome:} diet, fibre, stress management, and medications for symptoms
    \item \textbf{Inflammatory bowel disease:} specialist treatment with anti-inflammatory medicines and immune therapy
    \item \textbf{Coeliac disease:} strict gluten-free diet
    \item \textbf{Food intolerances:} identifying and avoiding trigger foods
    \item \textbf{Medication review:} changing or stopping medicines that cause diarrhoea
    \item \textbf{Nutritional support:} supplements for deficiencies and dietitian advice
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Dehydration and electrolyte disturbance
    \item Malnutrition and weight loss in chronic diarrhoea
    \item Nutrient deficiencies, including iron, B12, and vitamin D
    \item Complications of underlying diseases, such as strictures in inflammatory bowel disease
    \item Reduced quality of life and work absence
    \item Spread of infectious causes to others
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Acute infectious diarrhoea resolves on its own in the vast majority of people within a few days. Chronic diarrhoea improves with accurate diagnosis and appropriate treatment of the underlying cause. Irritable bowel syndrome is manageable long-term, and coeliac disease and inflammatory bowel disease respond well to treatment. Identifying the cause is the key to a good outcome.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Practise good hand hygiene and food safety
    \item Drink safe water, especially when travelling
    \item Ensure infants receive rotavirus vaccination
    \item Keep a symptom and food diary if chronic diarrhoea is a problem
    \item Discuss medicines that may cause diarrhoea with a doctor
    \item Seek medical assessment for persistent symptoms
    \item Follow dietary advice for diagnosed conditions
\end{itemize}
