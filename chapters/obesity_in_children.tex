\section*{Definition and Overview}
Obesity in children is a health matter affecting infants, children, or adolescents. Children are not merely small adults; their physiology, pharmacology, psychology, and developmental needs differ fundamentally from those of adults. Many childhood conditions are self-limiting, but some require prompt diagnosis and management. Assessment must always consider the developmental stage and the family/social context.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Children under 5 years account for a disproportionate burden of global morbidity and mortality, primarily from infectious diseases. In developed countries, chronic conditions (asthma, diabetes, obesity, mental health disorders) are increasingly common. Congenital anomalies remain a leading cause of infant mortality. Injury is the leading cause of death in children over 1 year in high-income countries. Immunisation has dramatically reduced the burden of vaccine-preventable diseases.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Infections:} viral upper respiratory infections, otitis media, gastroenteritis, bronchiolitis, croup
    \item \textbf{Developmental:} teething, colic, temper tantrums, pubertal changes
    \item \textbf{Genetic/congenital:} inherited disorders, chromosomal abnormalities, structural anomalies
    \item \textbf{Allergic/atopic:} eczema, food allergy, asthma, allergic rhinitis
    \item \textbf{Behavioural/mental health:} ADHD, autism spectrum disorder, anxiety, depression in older children
    \item \textbf{Injury:} falls, poisoning, burns, road trauma
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Fever -- the most common presentation
    \item Lethargy, irritability, or reduced feeding/fluid intake
    \item Respiratory: tachypnoea, wheeze, stridor, chest recession
    \item Gastrointestinal: vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain
    \item Rash -- viral exanthems, allergic, or meningococcal
    \item Altered behaviour: drowsiness, floppiness, or unresponsiveness
    \item Seizures or abnormal movements
    \item Failure to thrive or delayed developmental milestones
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Viral vs.\ bacterial infection (e.g.\ viral vs.\ bacterial tonsillitis; viral vs.\ bacterial gastroenteritis)
    \item Serious bacterial infection (meningitis, sepsis, pyelonephritis) vs.\ self-limiting viral illness
    \item Asthma vs.\ bronchiolitis vs.\ pneumonia
    \item Colic vs.\ organic disease in infants
    \item Behavioural vs.\ psychiatric disorder in adolescents
    \item Accidental vs.\ non-accidental injury
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek urgent medical attention for any child who is drowsy, floppy, breathing rapidly or with effort, very pale, has a non-blanching rash, has a fit, or is unable to keep fluids down.

\textbf{Call 000 if the child looks seriously ill or you are worried.}

See a GP for:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Fever in a baby under 3 months old
    \item Symptoms not improving after 48 hours
    \item Ongoing concerns about feeding, growth, or development
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History:} from parent/carer, including onset, progression, feeding, urine output, and behaviour change
    \item \textbf{Examination:} vital signs (temperature, respiratory rate, heart rate, capillary refill), observation of interaction and alertness
    \item \textbf{Growth assessment:} weight, height/length, and head circumference plotted on growth charts
    \item \textbf{Developmental assessment:} milestones and school performance
    \item \textbf{Investigations:} urine dipstick/culture, throat swab, blood tests, or imaging as indicated
    \item \textbf{Paediatric early warning scores (PEWS):} to identify deterioration
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Supportive care:} fluids, rest, antipyretics (paracetamol or ibuprofen at weight-appropriate dose)
    \item \textbf{Specific therapy:} antibiotics for bacterial infection, inhalers for asthma, insulin for diabetes
    \item \textbf{Fluid management:} oral rehydration solution for gastroenteritis; IV fluids for severe dehydration
    \item \textbf{Family-centred care:} involve parents in decision-making and care
    \item \textbf{Multidisciplinary approach:} paediatricians, nurses, allied health, and school liaison
    \item \textbf{Hospital admission:} for severe illness, dehydration, or conditions requiring specialist care
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Dehydration and electrolyte disturbance (especially with gastroenteritis)
    \item Sepsis and meningitis -- can be rapidly fatal in young children
    \item Respiratory failure (e.g.\ severe bronchiolitis, asthma)
    \item Developmental delay or disability
    \item Long-term impact of chronic illness on growth and psychosocial development
    \item Impact on family (financial, psychological, social)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Most childhood illnesses are self-limiting and resolve without sequelae. Children recover rapidly from most infections. Chronic conditions such as asthma and diabetes require ongoing management but most children lead full, active lives. Early intervention for developmental and mental health concerns improves long-term outcomes. Mortality is highest in the first year of life, particularly in the neonatal period.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Keep vaccinations up to date on the National Immunisation Program schedule
    \item Encourage regular hand washing and healthy eating
    \item Use age-appropriate car seats, helmets, and safety equipment
    \item Create a safe home environment: stair gates, pool fencing, medication lock-up
    \item Supervise young children at all times near water, roads, and heights
    \item Support emotional and mental wellbeing through family and school
    \item Attend regular growth and developmental checks
\end{itemize}
