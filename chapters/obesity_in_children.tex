\section*{Definition and Overview}
Childhood obesity is excess body fat that may affect health and wellbeing. It is assessed using age- and sex-specific growth charts and clinical context rather than adult body-mass-index cut-offs alone. Weight is influenced by biology, family, environment, sleep, medicines, and social conditions.

\section*{Assessment}
Assessment includes growth trajectory, diet, activity, sleep, medicines, family history, mental health, physical examination, and social context. Blood tests or specialist assessment are used when clinically indicated, not as a substitute for a supportive history.

\section*{Health Effects}
Possible effects include breathing and sleep problems, joint pain, insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, fatty liver disease, high blood pressure, and stigma or distress. A child may have obesity without immediate complications.

\section*{Management}
Use respectful, family-based goals focused on health, activity, sleep, nutrition, and wellbeing. Avoid shame and restrictive diets without professional support. A GP or paediatric team can coordinate dietetics, psychology, exercise, and treatment of complications.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek review for rapid unexplained weight change, breathing or sleep symptoms, excessive thirst or urination, pain, reduced mobility, or emotional distress. Contact your local emergency services for a life-threatening emergency.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
Offer regular meals, water as the usual drink, varied foods, enjoyable activity, adequate sleep, and limited sedentary screen time. Support the whole family and discuss concerns with a clinician.
\section*{Safety-netting}
Seek urgent care for breathing difficulty, blue or very pale appearance, seizure, reduced consciousness, severe dehydration, serious injury, or safeguarding concerns.
