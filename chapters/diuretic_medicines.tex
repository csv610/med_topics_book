\chapter{Diuretic Medicines}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Diuretic medicines, commonly called fluid tablets or water pills, increase the amount of salt and water the kidneys remove from the body as urine. They are used to treat high blood pressure, heart failure, and conditions causing fluid retention, such as kidney and liver disease. There are several types of diuretics, including thiazides, loop diuretics, and potassium-sparing diuretics, which work at different parts of the kidney. Diuretics are effective and widely used, but they require monitoring because they can disturb fluid, electrolyte, and kidney balance. This chapter explains the types of diuretics, their uses, and their risks.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Diuretics are among the most prescribed medicines in Australia. Thiazide diuretics are a first-line treatment for high blood pressure, which affects around one in three Australian adults. Loop diuretics are essential in heart failure, which affects hundreds of thousands of Australians, and are commonly used in kidney disease. Diuretic use increases with age, and older people are at greater risk of side effects such as dehydration and electrolyte disturbance.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mechanism:} diuretics act on the kidneys to increase excretion of sodium and water
    \item \textbf{Thiazides:} work in the distal tubule; used mainly for high blood pressure
    \item \textbf{Loop diuretics:} work in the loop of Henle; the most powerful, used for heart failure and significant fluid retention
    \item \textbf{Potassium-sparing diuretics:} retain potassium; used with other diuretics or alone in some conditions
    \item \textbf{Effects:} reduced blood volume lowers blood pressure and relieves swelling and breathlessness
    \item \textbf{Electrolyte effects:} diuretics can lower potassium (thiazides and loops) or raise it (potassium-sparing)
    \item \textbf{Kidney effects:} diuretics can affect kidney function, especially at high doses or with dehydration
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Increased urination, especially shortly after taking the medicine
    \item Reduction in swelling of the legs, feet, and abdomen
    \item Relief of breathlessness in heart failure
    \item Lowering of blood pressure
    \item Side effects: dizziness, dehydration, muscle cramps, and electrolyte disturbance
    \item The dose is adjusted by the doctor based on symptoms, blood pressure, and blood tests
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Different types of diuretics, chosen according to the condition and kidney function
    \item Diuretics versus other blood pressure medicines, which may be used alone or together
    \item The cause of fluid retention, which determines whether diuretics are appropriate
    \item Diuretic use versus stopping them, which requires medical guidance
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
People taking diuretics should have regular reviews of blood pressure, kidney function, and electrolytes. Seek medical advice for dizziness, weakness, cramps, or reduced urine output, and never stop diuretics suddenly without medical guidance.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden severe breathlessness, chest pain, or palpitations
    \item Confusion, severe weakness, or fainting
    \item Very low urine output or signs of severe dehydration
    \item Irregular heartbeat, which may indicate electrolyte disturbance
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} kidney function and electrolytes are checked before and during treatment
    \item \textbf{Blood pressure monitoring:} regular measurement to guide dosing
    \item \textbf{Assessment of fluid status:} weight, swelling, and breathing symptoms
    \item \textbf{Medication review:} checking for interactions, including with pain relievers and other blood pressure medicines
    \item \textbf{Monitoring frequency:} more frequent testing when starting or changing doses
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Thiazides:} once-daily dosing for high blood pressure, usually in the morning
    \item \textbf{Loop diuretics:} dosing tailored to fluid balance, sometimes multiple times daily
    \item \textbf{Potassium-sparing diuretics:} combined with other diuretics or used alone
    \item \textbf{Diet:} salt reduction supports the effect of diuretics
    \item \textbf{Electrolyte management:} potassium supplements or dietary advice when needed
    \item \textbf{Fluid balance:} monitoring of weight and swelling, with the doctor adjusting doses
    \item \textbf{Avoiding dehydration:} adequate fluids, with care in hot weather and illness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Dehydration and dizziness, increasing fall risk in older people
    \item Low potassium causing weakness, cramps, and heart rhythm problems
    \item High potassium with potassium-sparing diuretics, which can be dangerous
    \item Worsening kidney function
    \item High uric acid and gout
    \item Impaired glucose tolerance and increased cholesterol in some people
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Diuretics are highly effective: they control blood pressure, relieve the symptoms of heart failure, and prevent hospital admissions. With regular monitoring of blood tests and blood pressure, side effects are usually manageable. Most people take diuretics long-term and remain well controlled. The balance of benefit and risk is best when doses are individualised and reviewed regularly.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Take diuretics at the same time each day, usually in the morning
    \item Attend regular blood tests and reviews
    \item Weigh yourself regularly if you have heart failure, and report sudden gains
    \item Reduce salt in the diet
    \item Stay hydrated but do not overdrink
    \item Avoid medicines such as ibuprofen unless advised, as they can reduce diuretic effect and harm the kidneys
    \item Report dizziness, weakness, or palpitations promptly
\end{itemize}
