\chapter{Osteoarthritis Medication}

\section*{Overview}
Medications for osteoarthritis aim to reduce pain, improve function, and slow disease progression. Treatment is typically stepwise, beginning with topical agents and progressing to oral medications as needed.

\section*{Causes}
Indicated for symptomatic osteoarthritis when lifestyle modifications are insufficient.

\section*{Diagnosis}
Prescribing decisions are based on OA severity, affected joints, patient comorbidities, and medication tolerance.

\section*{Treatment}
First-line includes topical NSAIDs (diclofenac gel) and oral acetaminophen. Oral NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) are effective but require monitoring for GI and cardiovascular side effects. Intra-articular corticosteroid injections provide temporary relief. Duloxetine may help chronic musculoskeletal pain. Recent guidelines consider hyaluronic acid injections less effective.

\section*{Prevention}
Prevent medication side effects through appropriate dosing, monitoring, gastroprotective agents when indicated, and regular assessment of treatment benefit.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Report signs of GI bleeding, kidney problems, allergic reactions, or inadequate pain relief to your healthcare provider.
