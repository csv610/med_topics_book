\chapter{Anal Care}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Anal care is the everyday attention given to keeping the anus and the skin around it clean, healthy, and free of irritation. The anal area is delicate and easily becomes sore, itchy, or infected if it is over-washed, under-washed, or irritated by wiping, moisture, or hygiene products.

Good anal care is an important part of preventing and managing common conditions such as haemorrhoids, anal fissures, itching, and skin irritation. It matters for everyone who experiences bowel problems, for people after anal surgery, and during pregnancy. It is also a key part of recovery for people living with a stoma or recovering from anal procedures.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Anal symptoms are very common. Itching around the anus (pruritus ani) affects a large proportion of people at some point in their lives, and haemorrhoids are among the most frequent reasons for visiting a general practitioner. Anal irritation and fissures are more common in people who strain with constipation, have frequent or loose stools, or are pregnant.

The problem affects men and women of all ages. Simple improvements in anal hygiene and toilet habits prevent or improve many cases, and much of the work of good anal care is educational: breaking the cycle of irritation, scratching, and further irritation.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Most anal skin problems are caused by irritation, moisture, and friction rather than by serious underlying disease:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Moisture:} sweat or dampness from not drying properly softens the skin and makes it sore
    \item \textbf{Irritants:} perfumed soaps, wet wipes, and harsh toilet paper strip the natural protective oils
    \item \textbf{Friction:} vigorous wiping, scratching, or tight clothing damages the delicate skin
    \item \textbf{Bowel motions:} frequent loose stools or constipation with straining irritate the anal area
    \item \textbf{Underlying conditions:} haemorrhoids, fissures, fungal infection, and skin conditions such as eczema or psoriasis
    \item \textbf{Hair and sweat glands:} trapped hair and blocked glands can cause painful boils or abscesses
\end{itemize}
In most cases a cycle develops in which irritation causes scratching, which causes more irritation. This cycle can be broken with consistent good habits.

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Itching, soreness, or burning around the anus
    \item Redness, rawness, or chafing of the skin
    \item A persistent feeling of moisture or dampness
    \item Small cracks or splits in the skin
    \item Light bleeding on wiping
    \item Discomfort when sitting, wiping, or passing stool
    \item A lump or skin tag if haemorrhoids or tags are present
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Symptoms related to poor anal hygiene can be confused with specific conditions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Haemorrhoids (piles)
    \item Anal fissure
    \item Perianal abscess or fistula
    \item Fungal infection, such as thrush or ringworm
    \item Eczema, psoriasis, or contact dermatitis
    \item Threadworms, especially in children
    \item Inflammatory bowel disease, including Crohn's disease
    \item Rarely, pre-cancerous or cancerous changes in the anal skin
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor if anal symptoms persist despite good self-care, or if you have:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Bleeding from the back passage, especially if persistent or heavy
    \item A painful lump or swelling that is getting worse
    \item Persistent itching that interferes with sleep or daily life
    \item Pain or discharge, which may suggest infection
    \item Unexplained weight loss, fever, or night sweats
    \item Symptoms that do not settle within a few weeks
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Heavy bleeding from the back passage
    \item Sudden severe anal or abdominal pain
    \item A sudden inability to pass urine or stool
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History:} questions about bowel habit, diet, hygiene products, and medications
    \item \textbf{Examination:} careful inspection of the skin around the anus
    \item \textbf{Digital rectal examination:} feeling inside the anal canal for lumps, tenderness, or narrowing
    \item \textbf{Proctoscopy or anoscopy:} direct viewing of the anal canal and lower rectum
    \item \textbf{Skin swabs or scrapings:} when fungal or bacterial infection is suspected
    \item \textbf{Blood or stool tests:} when bleeding or underlying bowel disease needs investigation
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Keep it clean and dry:} wash the area with plain warm water and dry gently by patting
    \item \textbf{Avoid irritants:} stop perfumed soaps, wet wipes, and aggressive wiping; use soft, unscented toilet paper or rinse with water
    \item \textbf{Soothe the skin:} barrier creams or a small amount of plain moisturiser where advised
    \item \textbf{Treat the underlying cause:} antifungal creams for fungal infection, treatment of haemorrhoids or fissures, and management of skin conditions
    \item \textbf{Adjust the diet:} enough fluid and fibre to keep stools soft and regular
    \item \textbf{Wear loose clothing:} cotton underwear and loose trousers keep the area ventilated
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} review to ensure symptoms settle, and arrange further tests if they do not
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Worsening itching and soreness from the scratch--irritation cycle
    \item Skin breakdown, infection, and bleeding
    \item Anal fissures developing from hard stools and straining
    \item Local infection, including boils or abscesses
    \item Delay in diagnosing an underlying condition such as haemorrhoids, bowel disease, or cancer
    \item Psychological effects, including embarrassment, disturbed sleep, and reduced quality of life
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
With consistent good hygiene and removal of irritants, most anal skin irritation settles within days to weeks. Itching usually improves once the skin is kept clean and dry and the irritation cycle is broken. Where an underlying condition such as haemorrhoids, a fissure, or a skin disorder is present, the outlook depends on treating that condition. Most people who follow simple anal care advice remain free of troublesome symptoms, and symptoms that persist despite good self-care are usually easy to investigate and treat.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Wash the anal area gently with plain water and pat dry rather than rubbing
    \item Avoid perfumed soaps, bath products, and wet wipes in the anal area
    \item Use soft, white, unscented toilet paper, or rinse with water after a bowel motion
    \item Avoid straining on the toilet and do not sit for long periods
    \item Keep stools soft with plenty of fluid and fibre, and treat constipation early
    \item Wear cotton underwear and loose clothing, and change out of damp clothing promptly
    \item Avoid scratching; keep nails short and treat itching promptly
    \item See a doctor if symptoms last longer than a few weeks or if you notice bleeding
\end{itemize}
