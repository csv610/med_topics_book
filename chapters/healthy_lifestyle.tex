\chapter{Healthy Lifestyle}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
A healthy lifestyle is a pattern of daily behaviours that promotes physical, mental, and social wellbeing and reduces the risk of chronic disease. Its core pillars are a balanced diet, regular physical activity, adequate sleep, avoidance of tobacco and excess alcohol, healthy stress management, and positive social connection. A healthy lifestyle is not about perfection or deprivation; it is about sustainable habits that are maintained over a lifetime. Because lifestyle factors underpin most of the leading causes of illness and death, they are the single most powerful tool individuals have to protect their health.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Non-communicable diseases linked to lifestyle, including heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes, many cancers, and chronic lung disease, account for the majority of deaths in Australia. Around one-third of Australian adults do not get enough physical activity, fewer than one in ten eat the recommended five serves of vegetables daily, and a significant proportion drink alcohol at levels that exceed the national guidelines. Smoking rates have fallen steadily but remain elevated in disadvantaged groups. These statistics are encouraging in one sense: because lifestyle diseases are so prevalent, even modest population-wide improvements in behaviour have the potential to prevent an enormous burden of illness.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Diet:} a diet high in processed foods, sugar, salt, and saturated fat promotes obesity, insulin resistance, hypertension, and atherosclerosis
    \item \textbf{Physical inactivity:} muscle loss, reduced cardiorespiratory fitness, and impaired glucose handling increase chronic disease risk
    \item \textbf{Tobacco:} smoking damages blood vessels, lungs, and DNA, driving cancer, heart disease, and COPD
    \item \textbf{Alcohol:} excess intake raises blood pressure, damages the liver, and increases the risk of several cancers
    \item \textbf{Poor sleep:} chronic sleep deprivation disrupts hormone balance, appetite, immunity, and mood
    \item \textbf{Chronic stress:} sustained stress hormones raise blood pressure, blood sugar, and inflammation
    \item \textbf{Social isolation:} loneliness is associated with increased cardiovascular risk and earlier death
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item People living a healthy lifestyle typically have normal blood pressure, blood sugar, and cholesterol
    \item Healthy body weight and body fat distribution, with a healthy waist circumference
    \item Good energy levels, mood stability, and quality sleep
    \item Strong fitness, flexibility, and functional independence
    \item Absence of the early warning signs of chronic disease, such as fatigue, breathlessness, or chest discomfort
    \item A healthy lifestyle also reduces symptoms in people already living with chronic conditions
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The concept of a healthy lifestyle should be distinguished from fad diets and extremes, which can be harmful
    \item Exercise and healthy eating are sometimes pursued to excess, leading to disordered eating or overtraining
    \item Persistent fatigue or low mood despite a healthy lifestyle should prompt assessment for medical causes
    \item Wellness products and supplements marketed as lifestyle "fixes" are often unsupported by evidence
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Most people do not need a doctor to start living healthily, but a GP can help with screening for risk factors such as blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood sugar, and can refer to dietitians, exercise physiologists, and psychologists where helpful.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chest pain or breathlessness during activity
    \item Fainting, collapse, or palpitations with dizziness
    \item Signs of stroke: sudden facial drooping, arm weakness, or difficulty speaking
    \item Severe injury sustained during exercise or sport
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle review:} assessment of diet, activity, sleep, alcohol, smoking, and stress
    \item \textbf{Physical measures:} weight, body mass index, waist circumference, and blood pressure
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} cholesterol profile, blood sugar (fasting glucose or HbA1c), and liver function as part of routine health screening
    \item \textbf{Fitness assessment:} may be useful for people starting a new exercise program, especially those with existing conditions
    \item \textbf{Regular health checks:} interval screening according to age, sex, and risk factors
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Nutrition:} follow the Australian Dietary Guidelines, with plenty of vegetables, fruit, wholegrains, lean protein, and healthy fats
    \item \textbf{Physical activity:} aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate or 75 minutes of vigorous activity weekly, plus muscle-strengthening exercise twice weekly
    \item \textbf{Smoking cessation:} quit smoking with the support of nicotine replacement, medication, and Quitline
    \item \textbf{Alcohol:} stay within national guidelines of no more than ten standard drinks weekly and no more than four in one day
    \item \textbf{Sleep:} prioritise 7--9 hours nightly with a consistent routine
    \item \textbf{Stress management:} mindfulness, relaxation, and time in nature
    \item \textbf{Social connection:} maintain relationships, community involvement, and activities that bring meaning
    \item \textbf{Small sustainable changes:} set realistic goals and build habits gradually rather than overhauling everything at once
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Unhealthy lifestyles contribute to obesity, type 2 diabetes, and metabolic syndrome
    \item Atherosclerosis leads to heart attack, stroke, and peripheral artery disease
    \item Tobacco and alcohol increase the risk of many cancers
    \item Chronic lung disease, liver disease, and kidney disease are strongly lifestyle-related
    \item Mental health conditions, including depression and anxiety, are more common with unhealthy lifestyles
    \item Reduced quality of life, disability, and premature death
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The benefits of a healthy lifestyle begin almost immediately and accumulate over time. Blood pressure and cholesterol improve within weeks of dietary change and exercise, and the risk of heart attack, stroke, diabetes, and many cancers falls substantially within a few years of quitting smoking and becoming active. Even people who adopt healthier habits later in life gain significant benefit. Long-term adherence is the key to outcomes: sustainable, moderate changes maintained for years achieve more than intensive short-term efforts.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Eat a varied diet rich in plant foods and limit processed foods, sugar, and salt
    \item Be active every day, and build incidental activity into daily routines
    \item Quit smoking and avoid second-hand smoke
    \item Drink alcohol only within recommended limits, if at all
    \item Aim for regular, refreshing sleep
    \item Manage stress with healthy coping strategies rather than food, alcohol, or smoking
    \item Stay socially connected and seek help early for mental health concerns
    \item Attend routine health checks and screenings appropriate to your age and risk
\end{itemize}
