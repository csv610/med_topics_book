\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; seek professional advice for individual care.}

\section*{Common symptoms and causes}
Penis problems may include pain, itching, rash, lumps, sores, ulcers, swelling, discharge, bleeding, difficulty passing urine, erection changes, or a change in shape. Causes include irritation, balanitis, fungal infection, sexually transmitted infection, skin disease, injury, medication effects, priapism, or rarely cancer. Normal anatomical variations can also be mistaken for disease.

\section*{Assessment}
A clinician or sexual-health service may examine the skin, foreskin, urethra, testicles, and groin and ask about symptoms, medicines, sexual exposure, and general health. Urine, blood, or swab tests may be used. Do not try to diagnose a lump or ulcer from pictures alone, and avoid sex until an infection has been assessed if symptoms or exposure suggest an STI.

\section*{Treatment and self-care}
Treatment depends on the cause and may include gentle washing with water, avoiding irritants, prescribed antifungal or other medicines, STI treatment and partner notification, or specialist care. Do not force a tight foreskin, apply unprescribed steroid or antibiotic creams, or use home remedies on an ulcer.

\section*{When to seek urgent care}
Seek emergency help for an erection lasting several hours, inability to pass urine, a foreskin trapped behind the glans, severe injury, rapidly increasing swelling, heavy bleeding, or severe pain. Arrange prompt medical or sexual-health review for a persistent lump, sore, ulcer, discharge, rash, or change in appearance.
