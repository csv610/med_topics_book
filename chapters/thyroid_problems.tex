\section*{Definition and Overview}
``Thyroid problems'' is an umbrella term people use for any condition that affects how the thyroid gland looks or works. In practice this includes hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism, thyroiditis, goitre, nodules, thyroid eye disease, and thyroid cancer, as well as medicine-related and pregnancy-related thyroid issues. Symptoms are often non-specific---tiredness, weight change, mood shift, heart racing, or a neck lump---so blood tests and, when needed, ultrasound are essential. Most thyroid problems are manageable in general practice with endocrinology support for complex, pregnant, or surgical cases. This chapter is a patient-oriented map of common problems, what they feel like, how they are sorted out, and which situations are urgent.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Thyroid problems are among the commonest endocrine issues in local primary care. Women are affected more often than men. Autoimmune hypothyroidism leads chronic disease lists; Graves disease drives many thyrotoxic presentations in younger adults; multinodular goitre and toxic nodules increase with age. Subclinical TSH abnormalities appear frequently on screening and need individualised decisions rather than automatic lifelong treatment. Thyroid nodules are found in a large fraction of people who have neck ultrasound for any reason, yet thyroid cancer remains much less common than benign nodules. Postpartum thyroiditis occurs in a notable minority of pregnancies. Amiodarone and lithium users form special high-surveillance groups.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Too little hormone:} gland destruction (Hashimoto), iodine problems, pituitary failure, or treatment effects after surgery or radioiodine
    \item \textbf{Too much hormone:} stimulating antibodies (Graves), autonomous nodules, inflammatory leak (thyroiditis), or excess tablets
    \item \textbf{Structural change:} colloid nodules, cysts, hyperplasia, and neoplasms alter neck contour without always changing hormone levels
    \item \textbf{Immune attack on orbital tissues:} in Graves orbitopathy, shared autoimmunity inflames extraocular muscles and fat
    \item \textbf{Drug interference:} amiodarone (iodine load and direct toxicity), lithium, interferons, checkpoint inhibitors
    \item \textbf{Transient postpartum immune rebound:} thyroiditis after delivery
    \item \textbf{Feedback confusion in illness:} non-thyroidal illness syndrome complicates hospital TFT interpretation
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Slowing down (hypothyroid):} fatigue, weight gain, coldness, constipation, dry skin, hoarse voice, low mood, heavy periods, slow pulse
    \item \textbf{Speeding up (hyperthyroid):} heat intolerance, weight loss, tremor, anxiety, loose stools, palpitations, light periods, muscle weakness
    \item \textbf{Neck changes:} smooth goitre, lumpy multinodular gland, solitary nodule, tenderness
    \item \textbf{Eye symptoms:} irritation, prominence, double vision in Graves disease
    \item \textbf{Skin and hair:} loss of outer eyebrows, onycholysis, pretibial myxoedema (uncommon)
    \item \textbf{Heart presentations:} new atrial fibrillation may be the first clue to thyrotoxicosis in older adults
    \item \textbf{Incidental blood-test findings:} abnormal TSH on routine checks without clear symptoms
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle fatigue, sleep apnoea, depression, and iron deficiency versus hypothyroidism}
    \item \textbf{Primary anxiety, caffeine excess, and arrhythmia versus hyperthyroidism}
    \item \textbf{Menopause transition overlapping both under- and overactive symptom lists}
    \item \textbf{Thyroiditis versus Graves:} pain, uptake scan, and TRAb help
    \item \textbf{Malignant versus benign nodule:} ultrasound pattern and FNA, not size alone
    \item \textbf{Central hypothyroidism mimicking ``normal TSH'' traps:} always pair TSH with free T4 when pituitary disease is possible
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Book a GP appointment for lasting unexplained metabolic symptoms, neck swelling, or eye changes. Ask for earlier review if you are pregnant, planning pregnancy, or taking amiodarone, lithium, or cancer immunotherapy.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chest pain, severe breathlessness, fainting, or very fast irregular heartbeat
    \item Confusion with extreme underactive or overactive features
    \item Rapidly progressive eye pain, vision loss, or corneal exposure in thyroid eye disease
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{TSH with free T4:} cornerstone panel; add free T3 if hyperthyroidism suspected
    \item \textbf{Antibody tests:} TPOAbs, TRAb as directed
    \item \textbf{Ultrasound $\pm$ FNA:} structure and cancer risk triage
    \item \textbf{Uptake scanning:} cause of thyrotoxicosis
    \item \textbf{ECG and bone density:} selected complications screening
    \item \textbf{Pituitary testing:} when pattern suggests central disease
    \item \textbf{Serial tests:} avoid diagnosing permanent disease on a single mild abnormality during acute illness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hypothyroidism:} levothyroxine titration; education on consistent dosing
    \item \textbf{Hyperthyroidism:} carbimazole or PTU, beta-blockers, then definitive radioiodine or surgery if needed
    \item \textbf{Thyroiditis:} supportive care; temporary replacement sometimes; avoid unnecessary antithyroid drugs when uptake is low
    \item \textbf{Nodules:} observe, repeat ultrasound, or refer for surgery based on results
    \item \textbf{Eye disease:} lubricating drops, selenium in mild selected cases per specialist advice, smoking cessation, ophthalmology co-management, urgent care for sight threat
    \item \textbf{Pregnancy:} preconception optimisation; early pregnancy test access; dose adjustments
    \item \textbf{Cancer:} multidisciplinary surgery and adjuvant pathways
    \item \textbf{Medicine review:} never stop cardiac drugs like amiodarone yourself---coordinate specialists
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
Untreated major thyroid hormone imbalance harms the heart, bones, fertility, pregnancy outcomes, and mental health. Antithyroid drugs rarely cause agranulocytosis. Surgery risks voice and calcium problems. Delayed cancer diagnosis, though uncommon relative to nodule prevalence, worsens stage. Thyroid storm and myxoedema coma remain medical emergencies.

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Most thyroid problems have an excellent outlook with monitoring and standard therapies. People on stable levothyroxine live normal lives. Graves disease may remit after antithyroid courses or is cured with definitive therapy at the cost of replacement tablets. Benign nodules often need only surveillance. Low-risk thyroid cancers have high survival. Engagement with follow-up blood tests is the main determinant of day-to-day success.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Keep a personal record of TSH results and medicine doses
    \item Take levothyroxine on an empty stomach; space calcium and iron supplements
    \item Maintain adequate but not excessive dietary iodine
    \item Stop smoking to protect thyroid eyes and general health
    \item Plan pregnancy with a thyroid check if you have known disease
    \item Learn sick-day rules if you have been taught dose adjustments
    \item Report fever and sore throat urgently while on carbimazole or PTU
\end{itemize}
