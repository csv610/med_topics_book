\chapter{Infants}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Infancy is the period from birth to the first birthday, a time of extraordinary growth and development. This chapter provides an overview of the normal milestones, feeding, sleep, growth, and development of infants, together with common problems, preventive care, and the signs that should prompt medical attention. Understanding what is normal in infancy helps parents and carers respond confidently to the many minor symptoms and behaviours of the first year, while recognising when a baby needs assessment. Immunisation, health checks, and responsive parenting provide the foundations for lifelong health.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Infant mortality in Australia has fallen dramatically over the past century and is now among the lowest in the world, at around three to four deaths per thousand live births. Most infant deaths occur in the neonatal period, related to prematurity, congenital conditions, or birth complications. Common health problems of infancy include respiratory infections, gastroenteritis, ear infections, and skin conditions such as nappy rash and eczema. Breastfeeding initiation rates are high, although exclusive breastfeeding to six months remains below recommended levels. Immunisation coverage for infants in Australia is very high, protecting against serious childhood infections.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Normal infant development is driven by rapid growth of the brain and body. The brain doubles in size in the first year, and the developing nervous system drives the acquisition of motor, language, and social skills. Breast milk or infant formula provides nutrition, and the infant gut and immune system mature progressively. The immature immune system leaves infants vulnerable to infections, while the small airways and developing lungs make respiratory infections more significant. Common conditions arise from these normal developmental processes: reflux and colic from an immature digestive system, teething discomfort, and infections from exposure combined with immature immunity. Safe sleep practices address the leading modifiable risk for sudden unexpected death in infancy.

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Rapid weight gain and linear growth, tracked on growth charts
    \item Motor milestones: head control, rolling, sitting, crawling, and beginning to stand and walk
    \item Social and language milestones: smiling, babbling, and responding to their name
    \item Feeding: exclusive breastfeeding or formula for the first six months, then introduction of solids
    \item Sleep patterns that consolidate gradually, with night waking still normal in the first year
    \item Common minor symptoms: spitting up, colic, teething, nappy rash, and colds
    \item Responses to the world through crying, eye contact, and early attachment behaviours
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Common infant symptoms can signal a range of conditions, and assessment distinguishes minor from serious problems.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Fever:} usually viral, but bacterial infection, urinary tract infection, or meningitis must be considered, especially under three months
    \item \textbf{Vomiting:} reflux or gastroenteritis, but bowel obstruction or metabolic disease in severe cases
    \item \textbf{Diarrhoea:} viral gastroenteritis most often, but rarely serious dehydration
    \item \textbf{Crying and colic:} normal in many infants, but hunger, illness, or rarely abuse
    \item \textbf{Rashes:} nappy rash, eczema, or viral rashes, but meningococcal disease in a febrile ill child
    \item \textbf{Cough:} viral infection, bronchiolitis, or whooping cough
    \item \textbf{Poor weight gain:} feeding problems, reflux, infection, or metabolic disease
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Parents should seek medical advice whenever a baby seems unusually unwell, is difficult to settle, is feeding poorly, has a fever, or when they are worried. All infants need scheduled health checks: newborn screening, growth and development checks, and the immunisation schedule from two months of age. Trusted instincts matter: if something feels wrong with a baby, assessment is always appropriate.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A baby who is floppy, very difficult to wake, or unresponsive
    \item Difficulty breathing, fast breathing, or blue lips
    \item A fever in a baby under three months of age
    \item Seizures or convulsions
    \item A rash that does not fade when pressed, which may indicate meningococcal disease
    \item Signs of severe dehydration: sunken fontanelle, dry mouth, no wet nappies, or lethargy
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Growth assessment:} regular weight, length, and head circumference plotted on growth charts
    \item \textbf{Developmental checks:} structured reviews of milestones at key ages
    \item \textbf{Hearing and vision screening:} newborn hearing screening and later vision checks
    \item \textbf{Newborn screening:} heel-prick blood test for serious genetic and metabolic conditions
    \item \textbf{Physical examination:} at each health check, including the hips, heart, and genitals
    \item \textbf{Assessment of illness:} temperature, hydration status, breathing, and examination for specific conditions
    \item \textbf{Investigations as indicated:} urine tests, blood tests, or imaging when specific illness is suspected
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Feeding:} responsive breastfeeding or formula feeding, and the gradual introduction of iron-rich solids from around six months
    \item \textbf{Immunisation:} the funded national schedule from two months of age
    \item \textbf{Safe sleep:} back to sleep, a firm cot with no loose bedding, and a smoke-free environment
    \item \textbf{Skin care:} simple nappy care, moisturising for eczema, and sun protection
    \item \textbf{Managing minor illness:} fluids, paracetamol or ibuprofen as advised for fever, and rest
    \item \textbf{Support for parents:} maternal and child health services, family support, and help for postnatal depression
    \item \textbf{Early intervention:} referral for developmental concerns as soon as they are noticed
    \item \textbf{Car safety:} approved rear-facing car seats and never leaving an infant unattended in a vehicle
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe dehydration from gastroenteritis
    \item Serious bacterial infections, including sepsis and meningitis
    \item Bronchiolitis and pneumonia in vulnerable infants
    \item Injuries from falls, burns, choking, or car crashes
    \item Effects of neglect or abuse
    \item Sudden unexpected death in infancy, the risk of which is reduced by safe sleep practices
    \item Delayed development when concerns are not recognised and acted upon
    \item Untreated postnatal depression in parents, affecting attachment and care
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The great majority of infants are healthy and thrive, and with modern care, outcomes are excellent. Premature and medically fragile infants have improved dramatically in survival and long-term health. Early identification of problems, through screening and health checks, allows interventions that prevent or limit disability. The first year sets the foundation for attachment, learning, and health, and responsive caregiving combined with preventive health care is the strongest predictor of good outcomes.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Breastfeed where possible, and hold the baby during bottle feeds
    \item Follow the national immunisation schedule
    \item Always place the baby on their back to sleep, in a safe cot
    \item Keep the home smoke-free
    \item Use an approved car seat fitted correctly and rear-facing
    \item Attend all scheduled health and developmental checks
    \item Protect the skin from sun, and use safe bathing practices
    \item Support parental wellbeing and seek help for postnatal depression
    \item Learn infant first aid and know the emergency signs
\end{itemize}
