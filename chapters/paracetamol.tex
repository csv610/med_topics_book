\chapter{Paracetamol}
\index{Paracetamol}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; check the product information and seek professional advice for individual care.}

\section*{What it is used for}
Paracetamol is a non-opioid medicine used to relieve pain and reduce fever. Products differ in strength and formulation. The safe amount depends on the product, age, body weight, liver health, alcohol use, and other medicines.

\section*{Using it safely}
Follow the package or prescription instructions and leave the stated interval between doses. Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest time. Check cold, flu, combination, and prescription products because more than one product may contain paracetamol. Ask a pharmacist or clinician before use if the person has liver disease, weighs less than expected for their age, drinks heavily, or takes several medicines.

\section*{Overdose}
Taking more than directed can cause serious liver injury, even when there are no early symptoms. Nausea, vomiting, sweating, abdominal discomfort, confusion, or yellowing of the skin may occur, but symptoms cannot reliably show how serious an overdose is.

\section*{When to seek urgent help}
Contact a poison-information service or emergency department immediately after taking more than the label or prescription allows, taking doses too close together, or taking several paracetamol-containing products. Do not wait for symptoms and do not take extra doses to compensate for a missed dose. Bring the packaging and a list of other medicines if seeking care.

\section*{Treatment and follow-up}
Clinicians establish the amount and timing taken and may perform blood tests. Hospital treatment can include the antidote acetylcysteine when indicated. Take further doses only after receiving clear advice.
