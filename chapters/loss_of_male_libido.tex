\chapter{Loss of male libido}
\index{Loss of male libido}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Loss of male libido, clinically referred to as hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD) or low libido in men, is a persistent or recurrent deficiency or absence of sexual fantasies and desire for sexual activity. Sexual desire is a complex physiological and psychological phenomenon driven by a delicate balance of hormones, brain chemistry, psychological factors, and interpersonal relationships. While sexual desire naturally fluctuates throughout a man's life, a prolonged or severe loss of libido that causes personal distress or relationship difficulties is a clinical concern. It is important to distinguish low libido (lack of interest in sex) from erectile dysfunction (difficulty achieving or maintaining an erection), although the two conditions frequently coexist.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Low libido is a common but frequently underreported concern among men. Studies estimate that between 15\% and 25\% of men experience a significant loss of sexual desire at some point in their lives. The prevalence increases steadily with age, largely correlating with age-related declines in serum testosterone levels and a higher burden of chronic medical conditions. Low libido can affect men of all age groups, and its impact is closely associated with psychological distress and reduced quality of life.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
The causes of low male libido are multifactorial, categorized into hormonal, physical, psychological, and lifestyle factors:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hormonal factors:} testosterone deficiency (hypogonadism) is the primary hormonal cause. High levels of prolactin (hyperprolactinaemia) or thyroid dysfunction (hypothyroidism) also suppress libido.
    \item \textbf{Psychological factors:} chronic stress, anxiety, depression, body image concerns, and unresolved relationship conflicts are powerful suppressors of sexual desire.
    \item \textbf{Physical conditions:} chronic illnesses such as cardiovascular disease, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, obesity, and obstructive sleep apnoea.
    \item \textbf{Medications:} SSRI antidepressants, anti-androgen treatments (for prostate cancer), beta-blockers, and certain pain medications (opioids) significantly reduce libido.
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle and substance use:} excessive alcohol consumption, recreational drug use, smoking, sleep deprivation, and lack of exercise.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
The clinical presentation is characterised by a decrease in sexual interest and related behaviours:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Reduced or absent interest in sexual activity, including intercourse, masturbation, or intimacy
    \item Lack of sexual thoughts, fantasies, or dreams
    \item Personal distress, frustration, low self-esteem, or anxiety related to the lack of desire
    \item Relationship strain or conflict with partners due to mismatched sexual desire
    \item Accompanying features of underlying causes (e.g., fatigue, muscle loss, and mood changes in hypogonadism; depressed mood and anhedonia in depression)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Erectile dysfunction (ED):} the inability to obtain or maintain an erection, though the interest in sex remains intact.
    \item \textbf{Major depressive disorder:} where low libido is part of general anhedonia (loss of pleasure in all activities) and low mood.
    \item \textbf{Premature or delayed ejaculation:} which can cause performance anxiety and lead to secondary avoidance of sex, simulating low desire.
    \item \textbf{Normal physiological variation:} temporary fluctuations in desire due to stress, fatigue, or life changes, without causing significant clinical distress.
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Men should consult a general practitioner or a urologist if they experience a persistent decline in sexual desire that causes distress, affects their relationship, or is accompanied by other physical symptoms (like extreme fatigue, unexplained muscle loss, or mood changes).

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe, sudden chest pain or shortness of breath (especially if symptoms occur during physical activity or are related to undiagnosed cardiovascular disease)
    \item Sudden severe headache, confusion, or unilateral weakness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clinical history:} detailed discussion about onset, duration, relationship dynamics, psychological symptoms, and medication use.
    \item \textbf{Hormone assays:} early morning blood tests for total and free testosterone, sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG), prolactin, luteinising hormone (LH), and thyroid function tests (TSH).
    \item \textbf{General blood tests:} fasting glucose and HbA1c (to screen for diabetes), lipid profile, and renal function.
    \item \textbf{Psychological assessment:} screening for depression, anxiety, and chronic stress.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Treatment is tailored to the underlying cause:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Testosterone replacement therapy (TRT):} indicated for men with clinically confirmed hypogonadism (consistently low testosterone levels accompanied by symptoms).
    \item \textbf{Medication review:} adjusting or switching medications (e.g., changing to an antidepressant with fewer sexual side effects, like bupropion).
    \item \textbf{Psychological therapy:} cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), sex therapy, or relationship counselling to address stress, anxiety, or relationship issues.
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle modifications:} regular physical exercise (which naturally boosts testosterone and improves body image), weight management, reducing alcohol intake, and improving sleep hygiene.
    \item \textbf{Treating chronic conditions:} optimizing management of diabetes, hypertension, or sleep apnoea.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe relationship distress, separation, or divorce
    \item Depression, anxiety, and profound loss of self-confidence
    \item Reduced quality of life and social isolation
    \item In hypogonadism: osteoporosis, muscle wasting, and increased cardiovascular risk if left untreated
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The prognosis for recovery of male libido is generally good, particularly when a specific underlying cause (such as hypogonadism, medication side effects, or relationship conflict) is identified and treated. Hormone therapy and psychological counseling yield significant improvements in desire and relationship satisfaction. In cases where low libido is related to chronic, irreversible conditions, management focuses on adaptation, communication, and maximizing intimacy through non-penetrative means.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Maintain a healthy weight and engage in regular exercise, including resistance training
    \item Ensure adequate, high-quality sleep (7--8 hours per night) to support normal hormone production
    \item Limit alcohol consumption and avoid recreational drugs or tobacco
    \item Practice stress-reduction techniques such as mindfulness, yoga, or hobbies
    \item Maintain open, honest communication with your partner about sexual needs, preferences, and feelings
\end{itemize}
