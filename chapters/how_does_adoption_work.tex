\chapter{How Does Adoption Work}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Adoption is a legal process by which a child's legal parentage is transferred from their birth parents to adoptive parents, giving the adoptive parents full legal rights and responsibilities. In Australia, adoption is managed by state and territory governments and is subject to strict legal requirements designed to protect the child's welfare. Adoption can occur through domestic arrangements, including adoption of children in out-of-home care, and through intercountry adoption. Open adoption, with ongoing contact between birth and adoptive families, is now common. The process is complex, and prospective parents should seek thorough information and legal advice.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Adoption numbers in Australia have declined substantially over recent decades, with around 200--300 adoptions completed nationally each year. Most domestic adoptions are of children from out-of-home care, while intercountry adoptions have fallen as countries have restricted programs. Many more children live permanently with relatives or foster carers through care arrangements that do not involve adoption. Demand from prospective adoptive parents far exceeds the number of children available, and waiting times are often long.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Legal framework:} adoption is governed by state and territory legislation and, for intercountry adoption, international agreements
    \item \textbf{Domestic adoption:} includes adoption of children from out-of-home care and, less commonly, babies placed for adoption at birth
    \item \textbf{Intercountry adoption:} Australian residents adopt children from overseas countries under the Hague Convention
    \item \textbf{Open adoption:} ongoing contact arrangements between birth and adoptive families, now common in Australia
    \item \textbf{The process:} application, assessment, matching, placement, and a court order finalising the adoption
    \item \textbf{Consent:} birth parents must give informed consent, usually after counselling
    \item \textbf{Best interests:} the child's welfare is the paramount consideration
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Prospective parents apply through a state or territory adoption authority
    \item Applicants undergo a detailed assessment of suitability
    \item Intercountry applicants meet the requirements of both Australian and overseas authorities
    \item Children in out-of-home care may become legally free for adoption
    \item Placement is followed by a period of supervision before finalisation
    \item Birth parents receive counselling and support
    \item Adopted adults can access information about their origins
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Fostering and kinship care are alternatives to adoption that do not transfer legal parenthood
    \item Surrogacy arrangements are a separate legal pathway
    \item Permanent care orders provide stability without adoption
    \item The best option depends on the child's circumstances and the family's goals
    \item Adoption authorities provide information to help families choose
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Adoption itself is a legal and social process, but health is important throughout: prospective parents may need medical and psychological assessment, and adopted children need health checks and support for any special needs.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Any medical emergency in a child in your care
    \item Signs of serious illness, injury, or abuse
    \item Mental health crises in birth or adoptive parents
    \item Situations where a child's safety is at immediate risk
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Information sessions:} adoption authorities hold sessions explaining the process
    \item \textbf{Assessment:} home studies and interviews assess applicants' suitability
    \item \textbf{Medical and police checks:} required for applicants
    \item \textbf{Child assessment:} health and developmental assessment of children being adopted
    \item \textbf{Legal advice:} independent legal advice for birth parents
    \item \textbf{Post-adoption services:} counselling and support are available
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Application:} submitted to the relevant state or territory authority
    \item \textbf{Assessment:} includes interviews, home visits, and preparation training
    \item \textbf{Matching:} children are matched with families by the authority
    \item \textbf{Placement and supervision:} the child lives with the family while the adoption is supervised
    \item \textbf{Court finalisation:} the adoption is made legal by court order
    \item \textbf{Post-adoption support:} counselling, support groups, and information access
    \item \textbf{Intercountry process:} additional requirements and coordination with overseas authorities
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Long waiting times and uncertainty for applicants
    \item The emotional complexity for birth parents and adopted children
    \item Attachment and identity issues for adopted children
    \item Health and developmental needs of adopted children, especially from out-of-home care
    \item Financial costs, particularly for intercountry adoption
    \item Disruption of placements in a minority of cases
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Adoption provides stable, loving homes for children who cannot live with their birth families, and outcomes for adopted children are generally positive, particularly with early placement and good support. Open adoption allows children to maintain connections with their birth families. The process is lengthy and emotionally demanding, but most adoptive families report high satisfaction. Post-adoption support services help families and adopted people manage identity, contact, and adjustment issues throughout life.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Attend information sessions and seek independent advice before applying
    \item Prepare emotionally and practically for the process
    \item Learn about attachment and the needs of adopted children
    \item Use post-adoption counselling and support services
    \item Maintain open and honest communication with the child about adoption
    \item Seek help early for behavioural or emotional concerns
    \item Connect with adoptive family support networks
    \item Understand the rights of adopted adults to access information
\end{itemize}
