\chapter{Pleurisy}
\index{Pleurisy}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; seek professional advice for chest pain.}

\section*{What it is}
Pleurisy is inflammation of the pleura, the thin layers around the lungs and inside the chest wall. It can occur with a viral illness, pneumonia, autoimmune disease, injury, or other conditions. Similar sharp chest pain can also be caused by a blood clot in the lung or a heart problem.

\section*{Symptoms and assessment}
The usual symptom is sharp pain that worsens with breathing, coughing, sneezing, or movement and may spread to the shoulder or back. Assessment includes examination and may involve blood tests, chest X-ray, ultrasound, or CT to identify the cause and exclude serious problems.

\section*{Treatment}
Treatment depends on the cause. Rest and suitable pain relief may help uncomplicated cases; antibiotics are used only when a bacterial infection is diagnosed or strongly suspected. Hospital treatment may be needed for pneumonia, pulmonary embolism, tuberculosis, or another serious cause.

\section*{When to seek emergency care}
Seek emergency help for chest pain lasting more than a few minutes, pain spreading to the arm, back, neck, or jaw, difficulty breathing, fainting, a very fast heartbeat, or coughing up more than small streaks of blood. Arrange prompt review for persistent sharp chest pain, regular breathlessness, or blood-streaked phlegm.
