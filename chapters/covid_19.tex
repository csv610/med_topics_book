\chapter{COVID-19}
\index{COVID-19}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. Current testing, vaccination, isolation, and treatment recommendations change over time and vary by country.}

\section*{What it is}
COVID-19 is an illness caused by infection with SARS-CoV-2. It can range from no symptoms or a mild respiratory illness to severe pneumonia, blood-clotting problems, organ failure, or death. Risk is higher with older age, some medical conditions, pregnancy, and reduced immunity, but severe illness can occur in anyone.

\section*{Symptoms}
Possible symptoms include fever, cough, sore throat, runny nose, shortness of breath, fatigue, headache, muscle aches, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, or altered taste or smell. Symptoms overlap with other respiratory infections, so symptoms alone cannot establish the diagnosis.

\section*{Testing and treatment}
Testing may use a rapid antigen test or a molecular test, depending on local guidance and the clinical situation. People at higher risk may benefit from early antiviral treatment, which must be prescribed promptly and checked for interactions. Antibiotics do not treat COVID-19 unless a bacterial infection is also present. Follow current public-health advice about vaccination and reducing transmission.

\section*{Self-care and reducing spread}
Rest, drink enough to avoid dehydration, and use symptom relief medicines only as directed. Stay away from others while unwell and improve ventilation when possible. Follow local advice about masking, testing, work or school, and contact with people at higher risk.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek emergency care for severe breathing difficulty, persistent chest pain or pressure, blue or grey lips or skin, new confusion, inability to wake or stay awake, severe dehydration, or rapidly worsening illness. Contact a clinician early if you are at increased risk of severe disease, symptoms are worsening, or symptoms persist beyond expected recovery.

\section*{After recovery}
Most people improve over days to weeks, but tiredness, breathlessness, reduced concentration, or other symptoms can persist. Seek assessment for ongoing or disabling symptoms. Return to activity gradually and follow advice for any underlying condition.
