\section*{Definition and Overview}
Temazepam is a medicine from the benzodiazepine family, a group of drugs that calm the brain and nervous system. It is prescribed in many countries mainly for the short-term treatment of insomnia, helping people fall asleep and stay asleep, and occasionally for anxiety before minor medical or dental procedures. It works by enhancing the effect of a natural brain chemical called GABA, which slows nerve activity and produces a calming, sedating effect. Temazepam is a Schedule 4 prescription medicine in many countries, meaning it is available only on a doctor's prescription and should be dispensed by a pharmacist. It is a controlled drug in some states, because of its potential for dependence and misuse, and pharmacy and prescribing rules are strict. The medicine is well tolerated by many people for short courses, but it carries real risks that every user should understand: tolerance develops quickly, meaning the same dose stops working after a week or two; dependence can develop within a few weeks of regular use; and stopping suddenly after regular use can cause withdrawal symptoms and rebound insomnia. Because of these risks, temazepam is intended for short-term use, usually a few days to a couple of weeks, and should be combined with good sleep habits and, where possible, with treatment of the underlying cause of the insomnia. This chapter explains what temazepam is, how it is used safely, what side effects and interactions to watch for, and the important rules around stopping the medicine.

\section*{Uses and Indications}
The primary use of temazepam in many countries is the short-term treatment of insomnia, particularly the kind where a person has trouble getting to sleep, waking through the night, or waking too early. Guidelines recommend it for no more than a few weeks, and often for only a few nights, because the benefit is greatest early on and the risks rise with continued use. It may also be used:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Insomnia:} as a short-term aid when sleep problems are causing significant daytime distress, and after simple measures such as better sleep hygiene have been tried
    \item \textbf{Pre-procedure sedation:} a small dose taken the night before or before a minor procedure, under dental or medical guidance, to reduce anxiety and help the patient rest
    \item \textbf{Acute stress and anxiety:} occasionally prescribed for brief, severe anxiety, though other medicines and psychological treatments are usually preferred for ongoing anxiety
    \item \textbf{Travelling across time zones:} sometimes used on medical advice for jet lag when an overnight flight must be followed by important daytime commitments, but this use is controversial and should not be self-directed
\end{itemize}

Temazepam is not intended for regular nightly use, for self-treatment, for use with alcohol, or for driving, and it is not a treatment for the underlying causes of insomnia, such as anxiety, depression, pain, or poor sleep habits. A doctor should review the medicine regularly, and the dose and course should be as small and short as possible.

\section*{How Temazepam Works}
Temazepam belongs to the benzodiazepine class, and its effects follow directly from its chemistry. Benzodiazepines attach to a specific site on receptors for GABA, the brain's main inhibitory neurotransmitter, and they make the GABA that the brain already produces more effective. The result is a general dampening of nerve signals: the brain becomes less excitable, muscle tension falls, anxiety is reduced, and sleep is promoted. These are the same mechanisms that give benzodiazepines their risks: over-dampening the brain causes drowsiness, impaired thinking, slowed reactions, and, at high doses, loss of consciousness and slowed breathing. Temazepam is a short-to-intermediate acting benzodiazepine: it takes effect within about half an hour to an hour, which suits falling asleep, and its effects last for several hours, which suits staying asleep, but it is short enough that most of it has cleared by the morning. Unlike some benzodiazepines, it has a relatively simple metabolic pathway and no active metabolites, which is one reason it is a common choice for sleep. The brain adapts to the drug quickly: within days the same dose produces less effect (tolerance), and the brain's own sleep systems partly stop trying, which is why stopping the drug suddenly can cause rebound insomnia and withdrawal.

\section*{Dosing and Administration}
Temazepam comes as capsules and tablets, and the usual adult dose for insomnia is 10 to 20 mg taken about half an hour before bedtime. The dose is kept as low as possible, and older people are generally started at half the usual dose, because they are more sensitive to the sedative and balance effects and are at higher risk of falls and confusion. Key rules include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Take the dose just before bed:} the medicine acts within thirty to sixty minutes, and taking it too early increases the risk of daytime drowsiness
    \item \textbf{Allow a full night's sleep:} at least seven to eight hours should be planned, because residual effects the next morning impair driving and concentration
    \item \textbf{Short courses only:} usually three to fourteen nights, and never without medical supervision once past a couple of weeks
    \item \textbf{Never combine with alcohol:} alcohol multiplies the sedative effects and can cause dangerous respiratory depression
    \item \textbf{Avoid in pregnancy and breastfeeding:} temazepam crosses the placenta and enters breast milk; use should be discussed with a doctor
    \item \textbf{Caution in liver or lung disease:} clearance may be reduced, and chronic respiratory disease makes breathing depression more dangerous
    \item \textbf{Don't drive after use:} the next-morning effects mean driving or operating machinery should be avoided until it is clear how the medicine affects you
\end{itemize}

\section*{Side Effects}
Most people tolerate short courses well, but side effects are common and important to know.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Drowsiness and sedation:} the intended effect can spill into the next day, causing sleepiness, fatigue, and reduced concentration
    \item \textbf{Impaired memory and concentration:} especially with higher doses; some people experience poor recall for events after taking the dose
    \item \textbf{Dizziness and unsteadiness:} particularly on standing or at night when getting up to use the bathroom; the fall risk is a major concern in older people
    \item \textbf{Confusion and paradoxical reactions:} a minority, especially older people, become agitated, restless, or confused rather than calm; this is a sign to stop and seek advice
    \item \textbf{Nausea and headache:} occasional early side effects that usually settle
    \item \textbf{Motor impairment:} slowed reaction time and reduced coordination, which is why driving and operating machinery are unsafe while affected
\end{itemize}

\section*{Risks and Cautions}
The most important risks of temazepam relate not to the immediate dose but to what happens with continued use.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tolerance:} the brain adapts within days to weeks, so the same dose stops working; this drives people to increase the dose, which increases all the other risks
    \item \textbf{Dependence:} physical and psychological dependence can develop after just a few weeks of regular use, and is common with long-term use
    \item \textbf{Withdrawal:} stopping suddenly can cause rebound insomnia, anxiety, agitation, sweating, tremor, and, in severe cases, seizures; withdrawal should always be supervised
    \item \textbf{Falls and fractures:} the combination of sedation, dizziness, and night-time waking makes falls much more likely, particularly in the elderly
    \item \textbf{Oversedation and respiratory depression:} especially with alcohol, other sedatives, or in people with lung disease; severe drowsiness or slowed breathing is an emergency
    \item \textbf{Misuse and diversion:} temazepam has street value and is sometimes taken with alcohol or other drugs to intensify effects; this is dangerous and can be fatal
    \item \textbf{Rebound insomnia:} when the drug is stopped, the original sleep problem can return worse than before for a few nights
\end{itemize}

\section*{Interactions}
Temazepam interacts with many substances, and the most important message is to tell the doctor or pharmacist about everything being taken, including over-the-counter medicines and herbal products.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Alcohol:} the most dangerous interaction, causing severe sedation, impaired breathing, and risk of overdose
    \item \textbf{Other sedatives and opioids:} sleeping tablets, antihistamines, pain relievers, and medicines for anxiety or mental health add to the sedation and can cause dangerous respiratory depression
    \item \textbf{Central nervous system depressants:} muscle relaxants and anticonvulsants increase drowsiness and unsteadiness
    \item \textbf{Some antidepressants and antipsychotics:} may increase the sedative effect and require a dose adjustment
    \item \textbf{Medicines that alter liver enzymes:} some antiseizure medicines and certain antibiotics can change how quickly temazepam is cleared
\end{itemize}

A pharmacist is the best first point of contact for a medicine-interaction check, and the interaction list above is why no one should combine temazepam with other sedatives without professional advice.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Anyone taking temazepam should be reviewed by their doctor if the medicine is not working, if it is needed beyond a couple of weeks, or if they want to stop after regular use.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe drowsiness that is difficult to wake, or breathing that is slow, shallow, or stops
    \item Collapse, confusion, or a seizure
    \item A suspected overdose, especially if alcohol or other medicines were also taken
    \item Signs of a severe allergic reaction, such as swelling of the face or lips, or difficulty breathing
\end{itemize}

Seek prompt medical advice for persistent daytime drowsiness, falls or near-falls, new confusion, or any wish to change or stop the medicine. Never stop temazepam suddenly after regular use without a plan, because withdrawal can be unpleasant and, in severe cases, dangerous.

\section*{Management of Dependence and Withdrawal}
When temazepam has been used regularly for weeks or longer, it should be stopped under medical supervision, not abruptly. The doctor will usually arrange a gradual reduction in dose, called a taper, over weeks or months. The slower the taper, the fewer and milder the withdrawal symptoms. Symptoms of withdrawal include anxiety, restlessness, irritability, sweating, tremor, palpitations, difficulty sleeping, and vivid dreams, and rebound insomnia is expected for the first week or two after the last dose. In most people, gradually reducing the dose, combined with good sleep hygiene and psychological support for the underlying problem, allows the medicine to be stopped successfully. For people who have been on benzodiazepines for years, referral to a specialist withdrawal program or a GP with experience in deprescribing can make the process much more comfortable, and cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia is a proven alternative that many people use to replace the drug with a durable skill.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Treat insomnia at its source first: address stress, anxiety, pain, alcohol, caffeine, and poor sleep habits before reaching for a tablet
    \item Use the smallest effective dose for the shortest time, exactly as prescribed, and never borrow or share temazepam
    \item Keep the medicine out of reach of children, and never keep more than needed or carry it without a label
    \item Never drive or operate machinery after taking the dose until you know how you are affected the next day
    \item Avoid alcohol and other sedatives while taking the medicine
    \item Keep a single current medication list and review it with a GP or pharmacist regularly
    \item Discuss stopping the medicine with a doctor rather than stopping suddenly, and consider a sleep program or psychological support as the alternative
    \item Return unused or unwanted temazepam to a pharmacy for safe disposal, and never sell or give it away
\end{itemize}
