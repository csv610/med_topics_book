\chapter{Tumours (cancer)}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Tumours (cancer) is a neoplastic disease characterised by the uncontrolled proliferation of abnormal cells that have the capacity to invade surrounding tissues and metastasise to distant sites via the blood and lymphatic systems. It represents a heterogeneous group of disorders that can arise in virtually any organ or tissue. The behaviour, natural history, and response to therapy vary considerably depending on histological type, grade, stage at presentation, and host factors.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Cancer is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide. One in two men and one in three women will be diagnosed with some form of cancer in their lifetime. Incidence rises steeply with age, with the majority of new diagnoses occurring in people over 60 years. Geographic and ethnic variation is substantial, reflecting differences in environmental exposures, screening practices, and genetic susceptibility. Modifiable risk factors (tobacco, alcohol, obesity, infection) account for a significant proportion of cases.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Cancer arises from the accumulation of somatic (and occasionally inherited) genetic alterations that disrupt normal cellular regulatory pathways. Key mechanisms include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Activation of oncogenes:} mutations that promote uncontrolled cell division (e.g.\ RAS, MYC, HER2)
    \item \textbf{Inactivation of tumour suppressor genes:} loss of genes that normally restrain growth (e.g.\ TP53, RB1, APC)
    \item \textbf{Evasion of apoptosis:} cancer cells resist programmed cell death
    \item \textbf{Angiogenesis:} tumours induce new blood vessel formation to sustain growth
    \item \textbf{Tissue invasion and metastasis:} altered cell adhesion and motility enable spread to distant organs
    \item \textbf{Immune evasion:} tumours develop mechanisms to avoid detection and destruction by the immune system
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A new or changing lump, thickening, or mass
    \item Unexplained weight loss, anorexia, or fatigue
    \item Persistent pain not explained by other causes
    \item Changes in bowel or bladder habits
    \item Unusual bleeding or discharge from any body opening
    \item A sore or ulcer that does not heal
    \item Persistent cough, hoarseness, or dysphagia
    \item Changes in the size, shape, or colour of a skin lesion or mole
    \item Persistent fever or night sweats of unknown cause
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Benign tumours (e.g.\ lipomas, fibroadenomas, adenomatous polyps)
    \item Infectious processes (e.g.\ abscess, granulomatous disease, lymphadenitis)
    \item Inflammatory or autoimmune conditions (e.g.\ sarcoidosis, vasculitis)
    \item Cysts and developmental anomalies
    \item Reactive lymph node hyperplasia
    \item Other primary or metastatic malignancies
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek prompt medical assessment for any persistent unexplained symptom, particularly:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A new or enlarging lump
    \item Unexplained weight loss or fatigue
    \item Unusual bleeding from any site
    \item A changing mole or non-healing skin lesion
    \item Persistent cough, dysphagia, or altered bowel habits
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Call 000} for acute emergencies related to cancer or its treatment, such as severe chest pain, difficulty breathing, neutropenic sepsis, spinal cord compression, or hypercalcaemia.

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History and examination:} symptom assessment, risk factors, family history, systemic enquiry
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} full blood count, liver and renal function, tumour markers where appropriate
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} X-ray, ultrasound, CT, MRI, PET-CT for localisation and staging
    \item \textbf{Biopsy:} histopathological examination is the gold standard for definitive diagnosis
    \item \textbf{Endoscopy:} direct visualisation and sampling of hollow organ lesions (e.g.\ colonoscopy, bronchoscopy)
    \item \textbf{Molecular and genetic testing:} to guide targeted therapy (e.g.\ HER2, EGFR, BRCA status)
    \item \textbf{Staging:} TNM classification to determine disease extent and guide treatment
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Cancer management is coordinated by a multidisciplinary team (MDT) and depends on type, stage, molecular profile, and patient fitness. Modalities include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Surgery:} definitive excision for localised disease; also for diagnosis, staging, and palliation
    \item \textbf{Radiotherapy:} ionising radiation to destroy tumour cells or control local disease
    \item \textbf{Chemotherapy:} cytotoxic drugs that target rapidly dividing cells, used alone or in combination
    \item \textbf{Targeted therapy:} drugs that act on specific molecular targets (e.g.\ tyrosine kinase inhibitors, monoclonal antibodies)
    \item \textbf{Immunotherapy:} agents that enhance the immune response against cancer cells (e.g.\ checkpoint inhibitors, CAR-T cells)
    \item \textbf{Hormone therapy:} for hormone-sensitive tumours (e.g.\ breast, prostate)
    \item \textbf{Supportive and palliative care:} symptom control, psychological support, and quality-of-life management at all stages
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Local: pain, obstruction, compression of adjacent structures, bleeding, infection
    \item Metastatic: organ failure (liver, lung, brain, bone), pathological fractures, spinal cord compression
    \item Paraneoplastic: syndromes not directly caused by the tumour mass (e.g.\ hypercalcaemia, Cushing syndrome, neuropathies)
    \item Treatment-related: myelosuppression, nausea, fatigue, cardiotoxicity, neuropathy, secondary malignancies, infertility
    \item Psychological: anxiety, depression, body-image disturbance, existential distress
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Prognosis depends on cancer type, histological grade, stage at diagnosis, molecular markers, performance status, and response to treatment. Early-stage localised cancers are frequently curable with surgery alone, while advanced or metastatic disease is often incurable but increasingly manageable with systemic therapies. Five-year survival varies widely, from over 95\% for some early-stage skin cancers to under 10\% for advanced pancreatic cancer. Survivorship care addresses long-term physical and psychological needs.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Do not smoke; avoid second-hand smoke
    \item Maintain a healthy weight and engage in regular physical activity
    \item Eat a balanced diet rich in vegetables, fruit, and whole grains
    \item Limit or avoid alcohol consumption
    \item Protect skin from UV radiation; avoid solariums
    \item Participate in recommended cancer screening programs (e.g.\ bowel, breast, cervical)
    \item Vaccinate against cancer-causing infections (HPV, hepatitis B)
    \item Follow occupational health and safety guidance to minimise carcinogen exposure
\end{itemize}
