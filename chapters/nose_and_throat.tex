\chapter{Nose and throat}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational draft; seek professional advice for individual care.}

\section*{Definition and scope}
Nose and throat is a symptom or sign rather than a diagnosis. Its meaning depends on onset, severity, associated findings, age, pregnancy status, medicines, and underlying conditions.

\section*{Contributors and risk}
Possible causes range from self-limited irritation or infection to inflammatory, structural, neurological, metabolic, or medication-related disease.

\section*{Presentation}
Describe onset, progression, triggers, severity, location, associated symptoms, exposures, and what makes it better or worse.

\section*{Assessment}
A clinician may examine the relevant system and order targeted tests based on red flags and risk factors.

\section*{Management}
Treatment is directed at the cause and may include supportive measures, medication review, rehabilitation, or referral.

\section*{When to seek urgent care}
Seek urgent care for sudden severe symptoms, chest pain, breathing difficulty, new weakness or confusion, fainting, heavy bleeding, or rapid worsening.

\section*{Prevention and follow-up}
Follow prescribed treatment, avoid triggers, maintain hydration, and seek assessment for persistent unexplained symptoms.

\section*{Sources and limitations}
This concise educational overview should be checked against current local clinical guidance and adapted to the individual.
