\index{Paramedics}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. Emergency services and scopes of practice differ by location.}

\section*{What paramedics do}
Paramedics are trained prehospital clinicians who assess and treat people during emergencies, illness, injury, and transport. They may provide first aid, resuscitation, airway and breathing support, pain relief, medicines, monitoring, wound care, and advice about the safest destination. Their work is guided by local protocols, medical oversight, equipment, and level of training.

\section*{When to call emergency services}
Call your local emergency number for severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse, major bleeding, stroke symptoms, seizure, serious injury, poisoning, severe allergic reaction, or a person who is unconscious or rapidly deteriorating. Give the location, what happened, the person’s condition, and any immediate hazards. Follow the dispatcher’s instructions.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Use emergency services for any immediate danger or rapidly worsening illness. For less urgent concerns after an ambulance assessment, follow the discharge advice or contact a local healthcare service.

\section*{What happens on arrival}
The crew makes the scene safe, performs a rapid assessment, treats immediately dangerous problems, and decides whether transport or another service is needed. They may ask about medical history, medicines, allergies, and the timeline. Handover information is shared with the receiving team to support continuity of care.

\section*{After an emergency}
Ask what treatment was given, what warning signs to watch for, and where to obtain follow-up. Do not move a seriously injured person unless there is immediate danger, and do not drive yourself when symptoms could worsen or urgent treatment may be needed.
