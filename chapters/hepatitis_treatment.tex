\chapter{Hepatitis Treatment}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Hepatitis treatment depends on the cause and type of the infection. Acute viral hepatitis A and E require only supportive care, while chronic hepatitis B is controlled with long-term antiviral therapy and chronic hepatitis C is cured with a short course of direct-acting antiviral tablets. Non-viral hepatitis, such as alcoholic hepatitis and fatty liver disease, is treated by removing the cause and supporting the liver. Because untreated hepatitis can progress to cirrhosis and liver cancer, timely diagnosis and treatment are central to modern liver care.

\section*{Epidemiology}
In Australia, around 200,000 people live with chronic hepatitis B and 100,000 with chronic hepatitis C. Treatment rates have risen sharply since direct-acting antivirals became available, with tens of thousands of Australians cured of hepatitis C. Hepatitis B treatment controls the virus in most people but is not yet curative. Alcoholic and metabolic (fatty) liver disease are increasingly common and are managed largely by lifestyle change. Vaccination has made hepatitis A and B largely preventable diseases.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hepatitis A and E:} acute, self-limiting infections requiring supportive care only
    \item \textbf{Hepatitis B:} treated with antivirals that suppress viral replication; the immune response and duration of infection determine who needs treatment
    \item \textbf{Hepatitis C:} cured with direct-acting antivirals that block viral proteins essential for replication
    \item \textbf{Hepatitis D:} treated alongside hepatitis B
    \item \textbf{Alcoholic hepatitis:} abstinence, nutrition, and corticosteroids in severe cases
    \item \textbf{Autoimmune hepatitis:} immunosuppression with prednisolone and azathioprine
    \item \textbf{Fatty liver disease:} weight loss, exercise, and control of diabetes and lipids
    \item \textbf{Medication-induced liver injury:} stopping the offending drug
    \item \textbf{Pathophysiology:} treatment aims to stop viral replication or remove the cause, allowing the liver to heal and preventing progression to cirrhosis and cancer
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Treatment is guided by blood tests showing viral activity and liver inflammation
    \item Chronic hepatitis B: treatment when viral load and liver enzymes are elevated or fibrosis is present
    \item Hepatitis C: treatment is offered to nearly everyone with active infection
    \item Response is measured by viral tests during and after therapy
    \item Cure is confirmed when the hepatitis C virus is undetectable 12 weeks after treatment
    \item People with cirrhosis continue cancer surveillance after treatment
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The cause of hepatitis must be confirmed before treatment is chosen
    \item Acute and chronic viral hepatitis are distinguished by serology
    \item Alcohol, medication, autoimmune, and metabolic causes are diagnosed by history and specific tests
    \item Liver stiffness measurement identifies those with significant fibrosis who need closer follow-up
    \item Treatment decisions differ greatly between causes, so accurate diagnosis is essential
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Anyone diagnosed with hepatitis should be referred for specialist or GP-supervised care. People with risk factors should be tested so treatment can start early, before liver damage develops.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Confusion, drowsiness, or personality change with liver disease
    \item Vomiting blood or passing black stools
    \item Severe abdominal pain or swelling
    \item Sudden severe jaundice with bleeding or easy bruising
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Viral markers:} hepatitis B surface antigen, viral DNA, and hepatitis C RNA
    \item \textbf{Liver function tests:} monitor inflammation and response
    \item \textbf{Fibrosis assessment:} liver stiffness measurement, ultrasound, and blood-based fibrosis scores
    \item \textbf{Surveillance imaging:} six-monthly ultrasound for liver cancer in at-risk patients
    \item \textbf{Drug interaction review:} important for hepatitis C treatment
    \item \textbf{End-of-treatment and cure testing:} confirm the outcome
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hepatitis A and E:} rest, fluids, nutrition, and avoidance of alcohol and paracetamol
    \item \textbf{Chronic hepatitis B:} tenofovir or entecavir taken long term to suppress the virus; many patients require no treatment but need monitoring
    \item \textbf{Hepatitis C:} 8--12 weeks of pangenotypic direct-acting antivirals, curing over 95\%
    \item \textbf{Hepatitis D:} antiviral and immune therapies with hepatitis B treatment
    \item \textbf{Alcoholic hepatitis:} abstinence, nutrition, and corticosteroids in selected severe cases
    \item \textbf{Autoimmune hepatitis:} long-term immunosuppression
    \item \textbf{Fatty liver:} lifestyle programs and metabolic risk management
    \item \textbf{Advanced disease:} management of cirrhosis complications and transplantation
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Progression to cirrhosis and liver failure if untreated
    \item Hepatocellular carcinoma
    \item Drug side effects and interactions
    \item Relapse or reinfection after treatment, particularly in people who continue injecting drugs
    \item Treatment failure in a small minority of hepatitis C cases
    \item Complications of immunosuppression in autoimmune hepatitis
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Treatment outcomes for hepatitis have never been better. Hepatitis C is curable in over 95\% of people, and cured patients can expect a normal life expectancy. Hepatitis B is controlled long term with antivirals, and monitoring prevents most serious complications. Alcoholic hepatitis and fatty liver disease improve substantially when the cause is removed. Early diagnosis and treatment, before significant scarring develops, give the best outcomes, which is why testing at-risk people matters so much.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Take all hepatitis medication exactly as prescribed and complete the course
    \item Attend follow-up and monitoring appointments
    \item Avoid alcohol entirely while the liver is inflamed or damaged
    \item Maintain a healthy weight and diet
    \item Protect the liver by getting vaccinated against hepatitis A and B
    \item Use safe injecting practices and sterile equipment to prevent reinfection
    \item Attend cancer surveillance if you have cirrhosis
    \item Keep a record of your hepatitis tests and treatment
\end{itemize}
