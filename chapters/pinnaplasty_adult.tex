\chapter{Adult Pinnaplasty}
\index{Adult Pinnaplasty}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. Ear-reshaping surgery should be discussed with a suitably qualified, regulated surgeon.}

\section*{What it is}
Pinnaplasty, or otoplasty, reshapes the cartilage of a prominent or asymmetrical outer ear and positions it closer to the head. In adults it is usually elective and may be performed with local or general anaesthesia, depending on the procedure and the person.

\section*{Before surgery}
The surgeon should discuss the reason for surgery, expected result, alternatives, scars, cost, anaesthesia, recovery, and the possibility of asymmetry or further surgery. Ask who provides aftercare and how complications are managed. Tell the team about medicines, allergies, smoking, bleeding problems, and previous ear surgery.

\section*{Recovery and risks}
A dressing or headband may protect the ears while they heal. Pain, swelling, bruising, numbness, and temporary tenderness can occur. Risks include infection, bleeding or haematoma, pressure injury, visible scars, altered sensation, recurrence, asymmetry, and dissatisfaction with the result. Follow instructions about keeping dressings dry, sleeping, exercise, and contact sports.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Contact the surgical team promptly for increasing pain or pressure, a rapidly enlarging swelling, fever, spreading redness, pus, wound separation, or a displaced dressing. Seek urgent care for severe bleeding, breathing difficulty, collapse, or rapidly worsening illness.
