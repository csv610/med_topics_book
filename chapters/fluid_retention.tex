\chapter{Fluid Retention}
\index{Fluid Retention}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Fluid retention, also called oedema or water retention, is the build-up of excess fluid in the body's tissues, most often noticeable as swelling of the feet, ankles, and legs, and sometimes of the hands, face, or abdomen. It occurs when fluid leaks from the blood vessels into the tissues faster than the lymphatic system drains it away. Mild fluid retention is common and often harmless, linked to heat, standing for long periods, salty food, hormonal changes, or pregnancy. But it can also be a sign of more serious conditions, including heart, kidney, or liver disease, and it should be assessed when persistent, severe, or accompanied by other symptoms. This chapter explains the causes, when it matters, and how it is treated.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Fluid retention is very common. Most people experience mild ankle swelling at some point, particularly after long flights or standing, in hot weather, during pregnancy, and around the menstrual cycle. Significant oedema is common in chronic heart failure, which affects around 2--3\% of adults and is much more common in older people, and in chronic kidney disease, liver cirrhosis, and venous insufficiency, all of which are widespread. Fluid retention is also a frequent side effect of medicines, including some blood pressure drugs, steroids, and anti-inflammatory medicines. The condition is more common with age and in people with chronic disease.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mechanism:} fluid moves between blood vessels and tissues; retention occurs when pressure inside vessels rises, when blood proteins (albumin) fall, when the vessel walls leak, or when the kidneys and lymphatic system cannot keep up
    \item \textbf{Heart failure:} a weakened heart cannot pump effectively, so blood backs up and fluid accumulates in the lungs and legs
    \item \textbf{Kidney disease:} failing kidneys retain salt and water, causing swelling, often first noticed in the ankles and around the eyes
    \item \textbf{Liver disease:} cirrhosis raises pressure in the portal vein and lowers albumin, causing leg swelling and abdominal fluid (ascites)
    \item \textbf{Venous insufficiency:} damaged leg veins fail to return blood to the heart, pooling fluid in the lower legs
    \item \textbf{Medicines:} calcium-channel blockers, steroids, NSAIDs, and some diabetes medicines cause fluid retention
    \item \textbf{Hormonal changes:} the menstrual cycle and pregnancy alter fluid balance
    \item \textbf{Lymphatic obstruction:} after surgery or radiotherapy, or from infection, lymph drainage fails
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle:} high salt intake, heat, prolonged standing, and inactivity worsen retention
    \item \textbf{More serious causes:} deep vein thrombosis (one leg), and severe protein loss from the gut or kidney
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Swelling of the feet, ankles, and lower legs, which pits with pressure (the skin stays indented)
    \item Swelling that is worse at the end of the day and improves overnight with the legs raised
    \item Tight shoes, rings, or watches that feel suddenly tight
    \item Puffiness of the hands, face, or around the eyes in the morning
    \item Rapid weight gain over days to weeks, reflecting fluid accumulation
    \item Abdominal swelling and distension with ascites
    \item Shortness of breath, especially lying flat, which may indicate fluid on the lungs
    \item Swelling of only one leg, which may indicate a deep vein thrombosis
    \item Skin changes: tight, shiny skin and, in chronic cases, discolouration and ulceration
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
The causes range from benign to serious.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Heart failure, with breathlessness and weight gain
    \item Chronic kidney disease
    \item Liver cirrhosis with ascites
    \item Venous insufficiency, the common benign-ish cause in older adults
    \item Deep vein thrombosis, when one leg is swollen and painful
    \item Medication side effects
    \item Pregnancy, premenstrual swelling, and hot weather
    \item Lymphoedema and hypothyroidism
    \item Severe malnutrition or protein-losing conditions
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
New, persistent, or worsening swelling should be assessed by a doctor, especially when accompanied by breathlessness, weight gain, or a change in urine output, or when it affects only one leg.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden shortness of breath, breathlessness at rest, or lying flat
    \item Chest pain or pressure
    \item A sudden, painful, swollen leg, which may indicate a deep vein thrombosis
    \item Confusion, reduced urine output, or collapse
    \item Swelling of the face or throat with difficulty breathing (severe allergy)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History and examination:} distribution of swelling, associated symptoms, medicines, and chronic conditions; examination of the heart, lungs, abdomen, and legs
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} kidney function, albumin, liver function, thyroid function, and brain natriuretic peptide (BNP) for heart failure
    \item \textbf{Urine tests:} protein and blood, to assess kidney causes
    \item \textbf{Chest X-ray:} for fluid on the lungs
    \item \textbf{Echocardiogram:} to assess heart function where heart failure is suspected
    \item \textbf{Ultrasound of the legs:} for suspected deep vein thrombosis
    \item \textbf{Abdominal ultrasound:} for ascites and liver disease
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Treat the underlying cause:} heart, kidney, liver, and venous conditions each have specific treatments
    \item \textbf{Diuretics (fluid tablets):} increase urine output to remove excess fluid; used under medical supervision with monitoring of kidney function and electrolytes
    \item \textbf{Salt restriction:} reducing dietary salt decreases fluid retention
    \item \textbf{Leg elevation:} raising the legs above heart level helps drain fluid in venous insufficiency
    \item \textbf{Compression stockings:} support the veins and reduce lower-leg swelling
    \item \textbf{Exercise and movement:} regular activity and avoiding long periods of standing improve circulation
    \item \textbf{Medication review:} changing or stopping medicines that cause retention
    \item \textbf{Weight monitoring:} daily weighing helps detect fluid accumulation early
    \item \textbf{Pregnancy-related swelling:} rest, elevation, and review for pre-eclampsia, especially if accompanied by headache or high blood pressure
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Pulmonary oedema, with breathlessness and respiratory distress in heart failure
    \item Skin breakdown and ulcers from chronic leg swelling
    \item Reduced mobility and discomfort
    \item Complications of the underlying disease, including kidney failure and liver failure
    \item Side effects of diuretics, including dehydration and electrolyte disturbances
    \item Deep vein thrombosis risk when immobile
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The outlook depends on the cause. Benign causes of fluid retention, such as heat, hormones, and prolonged standing, resolve with elevation, movement, and salt reduction. Venous insufficiency is well managed with compression and activity. Fluid retention from heart, kidney, or liver disease is controlled with treatment of the underlying condition and diuretics, though it tends to return and needs ongoing management. The most important step is an accurate diagnosis, since treating the wrong cause wastes time and lets the real one progress.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Reduce salt in the diet, including processed and takeaway foods
    \item Elevate the legs regularly, especially after long periods of standing or sitting
    \item Move and walk regularly, and avoid sitting or standing for hours
    \item Wear compression stockings if advised for venous insufficiency
    \item Monitor weight daily if heart, kidney, or liver disease is present
    \item Review medicines with a doctor if swelling follows a new prescription
    \item Report breathlessness, weight gain, or one-sided leg swelling promptly
    \item In pregnancy, attend antenatal care and report sudden swelling with headache
\end{itemize}
