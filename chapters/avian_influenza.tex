\chapter{Avian Influenza}
\index{Avian Influenza}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Avian influenza, or bird flu, is infection with an influenza virus that mainly circulates among birds. Human infection is uncommon and usually follows close contact with infected or dead birds, poultry, other infected animals, or contaminated environments. Different virus subtypes behave differently, and local public-health advice changes as outbreaks occur.

\section*{Clinical Features}
Human illness may cause fever, cough, sore throat, muscle aches, tiredness, eye inflammation, or gastrointestinal symptoms. Some infections are mild, but severe pneumonia, respiratory failure, encephalitis, and death can occur. Symptoms after a relevant animal exposure should be reported promptly because early testing and public-health action are important.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Contact a healthcare service promptly if you develop fever, respiratory symptoms, conjunctivitis, or unexplained illness after contact with potentially infected animals or after visiting an outbreak area. Contact local emergency services for severe breathing difficulty, blue lips, confusion, collapse, seizures, or rapidly worsening illness. Follow local public-health instructions about isolation and testing.

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Assessment includes the illness history, animal or environmental exposure, travel, examination, and testing of respiratory or other samples. Testing should be arranged through the local public-health or clinical system; routine influenza tests may not identify every avian subtype.

\section*{Management}
Treatment depends on the virus, severity, and local guidance. A clinician may prescribe an antiviral such as oseltamivir and provide supportive care; severe disease may require hospital treatment and respiratory support. Antibiotics do not treat influenza viruses, although they may be used for a secondary bacterial infection. Do not handle or consume sick or dead animals without appropriate protective advice.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Avoid unprotected contact with sick or dead birds and other animals during outbreaks.
    \item Follow food-safety advice; thoroughly cook poultry, eggs, and other animal products.
    \item Wash hands after contact with animals, carcasses, raw meat, or contaminated surfaces.
    \item Use appropriate protective equipment and report unusual animal deaths to local authorities.
    \item Follow current vaccination, monitoring, testing, and isolation advice from public-health authorities.
\end{itemize}
