\chapter{Oral Sex and Mouth Care}
\index{Oral Sex and Mouth Care}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. Sexual-health services can provide confidential, non-judgemental advice and testing.}

\section*{Oral sex and infection risk}
Some sexually transmitted infections (STIs), including gonorrhoea, chlamydia, syphilis, herpes, and HPV, can pass between the mouth, throat, genitals, or anus during oral sex. Many infections cause no symptoms. HIV transmission through oral sex is much less likely than through vaginal or anal sex, but other infections remain possible.

\section*{Reducing risk}
Use a condom for oral sex on a penis and a dental dam or cut-open condom for oral sex on a vulva or anus. Avoid contact when there are sores, bleeding gums, genital lesions, or a known untreated infection. Do not brush or floss immediately before oral sex if it causes gum bleeding. Ask about STI testing and vaccines such as HPV and hepatitis A or B where appropriate.

\section*{Mouth care}
Brush teeth twice daily with fluoride toothpaste, clean between teeth, and attend dental checks. Seek advice for persistent ulcers, bleeding, swelling, tooth pain, difficulty swallowing, or a lump. Mouthwash does not replace brushing and does not reliably prevent STIs.

\section*{Testing and treatment}
Tell the clinician which body sites were exposed because throat, genital, and rectal tests may differ. Do not self-treat with leftover antibiotics. Partners may need testing or treatment, and sexual activity may need to pause until advised.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek urgent help for difficulty breathing or swallowing, rapidly increasing mouth or throat swelling, uncontrolled bleeding, severe dehydration, or a severe allergic reaction. Arrange assessment for persistent mouth sores, discharge, genital sores, pain, or possible STI exposure.
