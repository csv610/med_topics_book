\chapter{Questions to Ask Your Doctor}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Asking questions at medical appointments helps patients understand their health, treatment options, and what to expect, and it supports shared decision-making with their doctor. Many people leave appointments unsure about their diagnosis or treatment because they did not ask, felt rushed, or were overwhelmed. Preparing questions in advance makes appointments more effective and helps people take an active role in their care. This chapter provides practical guidance on what to ask about symptoms, tests, diagnoses, and treatments, and how to get the most from medical appointments.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Health literacy, the ability to understand and use health information, is low in around 40--60\% of Australian adults, and many people struggle to understand medical terms, treatment options, and instructions. Patients who are actively involved in decisions report better understanding, satisfaction, and health outcomes. Short appointment times and the complexity of medical information make preparation important. Encouraging questions is now a recognised part of quality health care in Australia.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Understanding the diagnosis:} what the condition is, how it is caused, and what it means
    \item \textbf{Understanding tests:} why a test is needed, what it involves, and what the results mean
    \item \textbf{Treatment options:} the benefits, risks, and alternatives of each option
    \item \textbf{Medicines:} what they do, how to take them, and possible side effects
    \item \textbf{What to expect:} the course of the illness, recovery, and follow-up
    \item \textbf{When to seek help:} warning signs and what to do if things get worse
    \item \textbf{Shared decision-making:} the doctor and patient together choose the best approach
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Preparing questions in advance helps appointments stay on track
    \item Key questions include: What is causing my symptoms? Is it serious? What tests do I need?
    \item For treatments: What are the options? What are the benefits and risks? What happens if I do nothing?
    \item For medicines: How do I take this? What side effects should I watch for? How long will I need it?
    \item For results: What does this mean for me? Do I need follow-up?
    \item Bringing a list, taking notes, and bringing a support person improve understanding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Questions about symptoms versus diagnosis versus treatment, which differ at each stage
    \item Urgent concerns that need immediate answers versus ongoing questions
    \item Questions for a GP versus questions for specialists and other health professionals
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Ask questions at every appointment, and seek medical care promptly for concerning symptoms. If you do not understand your diagnosis, treatment, or follow-up, ask your doctor to explain again, or ask to see a written plan.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Emergency symptoms that need urgent care, including chest pain, difficulty breathing, and severe bleeding
    \item Any urgent health concern
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Prepare before the appointment:} write down symptoms, medicines, and questions
    \item \textbf{Prioritise questions:} ask the most important ones first
    \item \textbf{During the appointment:} take notes or ask someone to help
    \item \textbf{After the appointment:} review what was discussed and clarify anything unclear
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} ask when to return and what to watch for
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Ask about the diagnosis:} the name, cause, and implications
    \item \textbf{Ask about tests:} the reason, risks, preparation, and how results will be communicated
    \item \textbf{Ask about treatment:} options, benefits, risks, costs, and the consequences of no treatment
    \item \textbf{Ask about medicines:} purpose, dose, timing, side effects, and interactions
    \item \textbf{Ask about self-care:} what you can do at home
    \item \textbf{Ask about follow-up:} when to return and what symptoms warrant urgent care
    \item \textbf{Use decision aids:} evidence-based tools help weigh treatment options
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Misunderstanding the diagnosis or treatment plan
    \item Not taking medicines correctly
    \item Delayed care when warning signs are not understood
    \item Anxiety from uncertainty
    \item Unnecessary or unwanted treatment when options are not discussed
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Patients who ask questions and participate in decisions report better understanding, adherence, and outcomes. Most doctors welcome questions and are happy to explain in plain language. There is no such thing as a silly question, and asking for clarification is a sign of good self-management. Preparing for appointments improves the quality of care people receive.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Write down your questions before appointments
    \item Bring a list of medicines and allergies
    \item Bring a support person to help listen and take notes
    \item Ask for plain-language explanations and written information
    \item Repeat back what you understood to check
    \item Ask for an interpreter if language is a barrier
    \item Follow up by phone or a further appointment if questions remain
\end{itemize}
