\chapter{Pethidine}
\index{Pethidine}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; use only under qualified medical supervision.}

\section*{What it is}
Pethidine, also called meperidine, is an opioid analgesic used in some settings for short-term severe pain or other specific indications. It can cause dependence, sedation, and dangerous breathing suppression, and its use is limited in many settings because of adverse effects and interactions.

\section*{Safe use}
Use only the prescribed dose and route. Do not combine it with alcohol, sedatives, sleeping medicines, or other opioids unless a clinician specifically directs this. Tell the prescriber about breathing disease, kidney or liver problems, pregnancy, seizure history, mental-health conditions, and all medicines, including antidepressants.

\section*{Possible effects}
Drowsiness, nausea, constipation, dizziness, confusion, low blood pressure, and slowed breathing can occur. Repeated use can cause tolerance and dependence. Older adults, children, and people with breathing or kidney problems may be at higher risk.

\section*{When to seek emergency care}
Seek emergency help for slow, shallow, or stopped breathing, long pauses between breaths, extreme sleepiness, inability to wake, blue or grey lips, collapse, seizure, or suspected overdose. Keep naloxone available where prescribed and do not leave an affected person alone.

\section*{Stopping and follow-up}
Do not stop long-term use abruptly without a plan; withdrawal and return of pain may occur. The prescriber should review ongoing need, dose, interactions, constipation, driving safety, and access to overdose prevention.
