\chapter{Peyronie's Disease}
\index{Peyronie's Disease}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. A urologist can discuss diagnosis and treatment options confidentially.}

\section*{What it is}
Peyronie's disease is scar tissue, or a plaque, in the fibrous covering of the penis. It can cause a new bend or narrowing during erection, pain, a palpable lump, shortening, or difficulty with sexual intercourse. It is not a sexually transmitted infection and is not cancer.

\section*{Assessment}
The clinician will ask when the change began, whether it is progressing, and how it affects erections and sex. Examination and, sometimes, photographs or ultrasound help assess the plaque and blood flow. Other causes of curvature and erectile difficulty may need consideration.

\section*{Treatment}
Mild, stable symptoms may need reassurance and observation. Treatment can include pain relief, support for erectile dysfunction, traction or other devices under specialist instruction, injections for selected plaques, or surgery after the condition has stabilised. Do not attempt to break or straighten the plaque yourself, and be cautious with unregulated supplements or injections.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Arrange review for new or worsening curvature, pain, a lump, shortening, or difficulty with erections. Seek urgent care for an erection lasting more than 4 hours, severe injury, sudden swelling, or inability to urinate.
