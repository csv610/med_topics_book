\chapter{Pneumonia}
\index{Pneumonia}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Pneumonia is an acute infection of the lung parenchyma (alveoli and interstitial tissue) causing inflammation and consolidation. It is classified by setting of acquisition: community-acquired pneumonia (CAP), hospital-acquired pneumonia (HAP, $\ge$48 h after admission), ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP), and healthcare-associated pneumonia (HCAP). It is also classified by pathogen (typical vs atypical bacteria, viruses, fungi) and severity. Pneumonia is a leading cause of infectious morbidity and mortality worldwide. In many countries, it accounts for ~50,000 hospitalisations and ~4,000 deaths annually, predominantly in the elderly and those with comorbidities.

\section*{Epidemiology}
CAP incidence: 5--11/1,000 adults/year; increases sharply with age (>65: 30--50/1,000). Leading cause of sepsis and infectious death. Seasonal peaks in winter (influenza, RSV). Indigenous people have 3--5x higher hospitalisation rates and earlier onset. Risk factors: age >65, chronic lung disease (COPD, bronchiectasis), cardiovascular disease, diabetes, immunosuppression, smoking, alcohol excess, malnutrition, dementia, aspiration risk, institutionalisation. Vaccination (pneumococcal, influenza, COVID-19, RSV) has reduced but not eliminated burden.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pathogens by setting:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item CAP typical: \textit{Streptococcus pneumoniae} (most common), \textit{Haemophilus influenzae}, \textit{Moraxella catarrhalis}, \textit{Staphylococcus aureus}, Gram-negatives (in COPD/severe)
        \item CAP atypical: \textit{Mycoplasma pneumoniae}, \textit{Chlamydia pneumoniae}, \textit{Legionella pneumophila}, \textit{Coxiella burnetii} (Q fever)
        \item Viral: influenza A/B, RSV, SARS-CoV-2, parainfluenza, hMPV, adenovirus; often co-infection
        \item HAP/VAP: \textit{Pseudomonas aeruginosa}, MRSA, \textit{Acinetobacter}, ESBL-producing Enterobacteriaceae, \textit{Stenotrophomonas}
        \item Aspiration: anaerobes (\textit{Peptostreptococcus}, \textit{Prevotella}, \textit{Fusobacterium}) + aerobes
        \item Immunocompromised: \textit{Pneumocystis jirovecii}, fungi (\textit{Aspergillus}, \textit{Candida}, endemic mycoses), CMV, \textit{Nocardia}, TB
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pathogenesis:} microaspiration of oropharyngeal flora $\to$ alveolar macrophage activation $\to$ cytokine release (TNF-$\alpha$, IL-1, IL-6, IL-8) $\to$ neutrophil recruitment $\to$ alveolar exudate (consolidation) $\to$ impaired gas exchange; systemic inflammatory response $\to$ sepsis syndrome
    \item \textbf{Host defence:} mucociliary clearance, cough reflex, alveolar macrophages, surfactant proteins, neutrophils, complement, immunoglobulins; impaired by age, smoking, COPD, alcohol, diabetes, medications
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Typical CAP:} acute onset fever, rigors, productive cough (purulent/rust-coloured sputum), pleuritic chest pain, dyspnoea, tachycardia, tachypnoea
    \item \textbf{Atypical CAP:} subacute, dry cough, headache, myalgia, extrapulmonary features (Legionella: GI, neuro, hyponatraemia; Mycoplasma: rash, haemolysis, neuro)
    \item \textbf{Elderly/immunocompromised:} may lack fever; confusion, falls, functional decline, anorexia, hypothermia
    \item \textbf{Signs:} fever, tachycardia, tachypnoea ($>$24), hypotension (sepsis), bronchial breathing, crackles, dullness to percussion, increased vocal fremitus; pleural rub if effusion
    \item \textbf{Severe:} confusion (AMT $<$8), SpO$_2$ $<$90\%, RR $\ge$30, SBP $<$90, multilobar involvement, effusion
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Acute bronchitis:} cough dominant, no focal signs, no consolidation on imaging, usually viral
    \item \textbf{COPD exacerbation:} increased dyspnoea, sputum volume/purulence; may overlap with pneumonia
    \item \textbf{Pulmonary embolism:} pleuritic pain, haemoptysis, hypoxia, tachycardia; Wells score, D-dimer, CTPA
    \item \textbf{Acute heart failure:} orthopnoea, PND, bilateral crackles, raised JVP, cardiomegaly, BNP elevated; CXR: vascular congestion, Kerley B lines
    \item \textbf{Interstitial lung disease:} subacute dyspnoea, dry cough, bilateral fine crackles; HRCT pattern
    \item \textbf{Malignancy:} weight loss, haemoptysis, persistent consolidation unresponsive to antibiotics
    \item \textbf{Vasculitis:} haemoptysis, renal impairment, systemic features; ANCA, biopsy
    \item \textbf{Aspiration pneumonitis:} chemical injury, sudden onset post-aspiration; may superinfect
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Any adult with fever, cough, and dyspnoea, especially if elderly or comorbid. Urgent review for: RR $\ge$30, SpO$_2$ $<$92\%, confusion, hypotension, inability to take oral fluids/medications, comorbidities (COPD, heart failure, immunosuppression).

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe respiratory distress (accessory muscle use, inability to speak)
    \item Haemodynamic instability (SBP $<$90, cool peripheries, altered consciousness)
    \item Cyanosis, SpO$_2$ $<$85\% on room air
    \item Acute confusion or reduced GCS
    \item Haemoptysis $>$100 mL
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Chest X-ray (CXR):} gold standard for CAP; lobar consolidation, interstitial infiltrates, effusion, cavitation; may lag clinical picture (24--48 h); repeat if not improving
    \item \textbf{Severity scores:} CURB-65 (Confusion, Urea $>$7, RR $\ge$30, BP $<$90/60, Age $\ge$65) -- 0--1 outpatient, 2 consider admission, 3--5 ICU; SMART-COP, PSI (Pneumonia Severity Index) for finer stratification
    \item \textbf{Bloods:} FBC (neutrophilia/lymphopenia), CRP (trend), UEC (AKI, hyponatraemia), LFTs, glucose, coagulation, ABG/VBG (hypoxaemia, hypercapnia, acidosis), lactate (sepsis)
    \item \textbf{Microbiology (before antibiotics if possible, but don't delay):} blood cultures (2 sets), sputum Gram stain/culture, urinary pneumococcal/Legionella antigens, nasopharyngeal PCR panel (viruses, atypicals), serology (Mycoplasma, Chlamydia, Coxiella, Legionella)
    \item \textbf{CT chest:} if CXR equivocal, complications suspected (abscess, empyema), underlying malignancy, immunocompromised
    \item \textbf{Pleural fluid:} if effusion $>$1 cm on decubitus; pH, glucose, LDH, Gram stain, culture, cytology; Light's criteria for exudate; pH $<$7.2 $\to$ drainage
    \item \textbf{Bronchoscopy:} immunocompromised, non-resolving, suspected TB/fungal/malignancy
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Site of care:} CURB-65 0--1: outpatient; 2: admit general ward; 3--5: HDU/ICU; clinical judgement overrides scores
    \item \textbf{Oxygen:} target SpO$_2$ 92--96\% (88--92\% if COPD/hypercapnic risk); high-flow nasal cannula (HFNC) or NIV for hypercapnic respiratory failure
    \item \textbf{Fluid resuscitation:} balanced crystalloids for sepsis; avoid overload
    \item \textbf{Empiric antibiotics (start within 1--4 h of presentation):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Outpatient (mild CAP):} amoxicillin 1 g TDS (or doxycycline 100 mg BD if penicillin allergy) $\times$ 5 days; add macrolide (azithromycin 500 mg daily) if atypical suspected or CURB-65 1
        \item \textbf{Inpatient non-ICU (moderate CAP):} ceftriaxone 2 g IV daily + azithromycin 500 mg IV/PO daily $\times$ 5 days; or amoxicillin-clavulanate + azithromycin; penicillin allergy: respiratory fluoroquinolone (moxifloxacin 400 mg daily)
        \item \textbf{ICU (severe CAP):} ceftriaxone 2 g IV BD + azithromycin 500 mg IV daily; if Pseudomonas risk (bronchiectasis, recent antibiotics, steroids): piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5 g IV Q6H or meropenem 1 g IV Q8H + azithromycin OR ciprofloxacin; if MRSA risk (post-influenza, prior MRSA): add vancomycin/linezolid
        \item \textbf{Aspiration:} amoxicillin-clavulanate or piperacillin-tazobactam; clindamycin if anaerobic concern
        \item \textbf{Immunocompromised:} broad coverage per local guidelines; PJP prophylaxis (TMP-SMX), antifungal if prolonged neutropenia
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{De-escalation:} narrow based on microbiology at 48--72 h; switch to oral when clinically stable (afebrile 24 h, improving WBC, tolerating oral)
    \item \textbf{Duration:} 5 days for most CAP (evidence-based); 7--10 days if severe, bacteremia, empyema, Legionella, Staphylococcus; procalcitonin-guided discontinuation
    \item \textbf{Adjuncts:} corticosteroids (dexamethasone 6 mg daily $\times$ 5 days) for severe CAP with high inflammatory response (RECOVERY, CAPE-COD trials); avoid routine in non-severe; antiviral (oseltamivir) if influenza; IVIG not routine
    \item \textbf{Complications:} parapneumonic effusion/empyema $\to$ chest drain + fibrinolytics (tPA/DNase) or VATS; lung abscess $\to$ prolonged antibiotics, drainage; necrotising pneumonia $\to$ surgery
    \item \textbf{Vaccination:} pneumococcal (PCV20 or PCV15+PPSV23), influenza annually, COVID-19 boosters, RSV (older adults); post-pneumonia: review vaccination status
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Parapneumonic effusion / empyema:} 20--40\% CAP; organised empyema requires surgery
    \item \textbf{Lung abscess:} cavitation, anaerobes/Staph; prolonged antibiotics
    \item \textbf{Necrotising pneumonia:} rare, severe, often \textit{S. aureus} (PVL+), \textit{S. pneumoniae} serotype 3
    \item \textbf{Sepsis/septic shock:} multi-organ failure; leading cause of death
    \item \textbf{Respiratory failure:} hypoxaemic (ARDS) or hypercapnic
    \item \textbf{Metastatic infection:} meningitis, endocarditis, septic arthritis, brain abscess (rare)
    \item \textbf{Long-term:} reduced exercise capacity, recurrent pneumonia, accelerated functional decline in elderly
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Outpatient CAP mortality <1\%; inpatient 5--15\%; ICU 20--30\%. Elderly and comorbid have higher mortality and prolonged recovery (months to baseline). Early appropriate antibiotics, severity assessment, and supportive care improve outcomes. 30-day readmission ~10--15\%. Quality of life may not return to baseline in frail elderly. Vaccination and smoking cessation are key preventive strategies.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Vaccination:} PCV20 (single dose) or PCV15 + PPSV23 (12 months later) for $\ge$65 or $<$65 with risk factors; annual influenza; COVID-19 boosters; RSV vaccine (Arexvy/Abrysvo) for $\ge$60 with comorbidities
    \item \textbf{Smoking cessation:} single most effective intervention
    \item \textbf{Alcohol moderation}
    \item \textbf{Oral hygiene:} reduces aspiration pneumonia risk (especially nursing homes)
    \item \textbf{Swallowing assessment:} if dysphagia/stroke/dementia; texture modification, positioning
    \item \textbf{Hand hygiene, mask in crowds during respiratory season}
    \item \textbf{Manage comorbidities:} COPD optimisation, diabetes control, heart failure management
    \item \textbf{Complete antibiotic course; follow-up CXR at 6 weeks if smoker, >50, or persistent symptoms (exclude malignancy)}
    \item \textbf{Gradual return to activity; pulmonary rehab if deconditioned}
\end{itemize}
