\chapter{Stroke}
\index{Stroke}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Stroke is an acute neurological deficit caused by interruption of blood flow to the brain (ischaemic stroke) or bleeding from a cerebral vessel (haemorrhagic stroke). It is a medical emergency where rapid assessment can preserve brain tissue. A transient ischaemic attack (TIA) causes temporary stroke-like symptoms and also requires urgent assessment, even when symptoms resolve. Stroke is a leading cause of death and adult disability worldwide.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Stroke risk and outcomes vary by age, sex, genetics, blood pressure, diabetes, tobacco exposure, air pollution, access to care, and other social and environmental factors. Population rates and access to reperfusion treatment differ substantially between countries and regions. Many strokes can be prevented or their risk reduced through control of blood pressure and other modifiable risk factors.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Ischaemic stroke (TOAST classification):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Large artery atherosclerosis (carotid, intracranial): plaque rupture $\to$ artery-to-artery embolism or in situ occlusion
        \item Cardioembolism: atrial fibrillation (most common), mechanical valves, LV thrombus post-MI, cardiomyopathy, endocarditis, PFO
        \item Small vessel disease (lacunar): lipohyalinosis, microatheroma $\to$ occlusion of penetrating arteries (<1.5 cm infarcts)
        \item Other determined: dissection, vasculitis, hypercoagulable states, moyamoya, radiation
        \item Undetermined (cryptogenic): ~30\%; ESUS (embolic stroke of undetermined source) subset
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Haemorrhagic stroke:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Intracerebral haemorrhage (ICH): hypertensive small vessel disease (basal ganglia, thalamus, pons, cerebellum), cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA, lobar, elderly), anticoagulant-related, vascular malformations (AVM, cavernoma), tumours, amyloid angiopathy
        \item Subarachnoid haemorrhage (SAH): aneurysmal rupture (85\%), perimesencephalic (non-aneurysmal), other vascular malformations
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Ischaemic cascade:} energy failure $\to$ loss of ion gradients $\to$ cytotoxic oedema $\to$ glutamate excitotoxicity $\to$ calcium influx $\to$ free radicals, inflammation, apoptosis $\to$ infarction; penumbra (salvageable tissue) surrounds core
    \item \textbf{ICH pathophysiology:} primary injury (mass effect, mechanical disruption) + secondary injury (haemoglobin/iron toxicity, inflammation, perihaematomal oedema, expanded over 24--72 h)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sudden onset focal deficit:} FAST (Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to contact local emergency services)
    \item \textbf{Anterior circulation (carotid/MCA/ACA):} contralateral hemiparesis/hemianaesthesia (face+arm > leg), homonymous hemianopia, aphasia (dominant hemisphere), neglect (non-dominant), gaze preference
    \item \textbf{Posterior circulation (vertebrobasilar/PCA):} vertigo, diplopia, dysarthria, dysphagia, ataxia, crossed signs (ipsilateral cranial nerve + contralateral body), bilateral visual loss (PCA), locked-in syndrome (pons)
    \item \textbf{Lacunar syndromes:} pure motor hemiparesis, pure sensory stroke, ataxic hemiparesis, sensorimotor stroke, dysarthria-clumsy hand
    \item \textbf{ICH:} headache, vomiting, reduced consciousness, severe hypertension, focal signs; lobar (CAA) vs deep (hypertensive)
    \item \textbf{SAH:} thunderclap headache (worst ever), meningism, photophobia, reduced consciousness, focal signs if aneurysm compresses nerve (III, VI)
    \item \textbf{TIA:} identical symptoms but resolve completely; ABCD2 score for short-term stroke risk
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Stroke mimics (up to 20--30\% of ``code stroke'' activations):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Seizure / post-ictal Todd's paresis
        \item Migraine with aura (hemiplegic migraine)
        \item Hypoglycaemia
        \item Functional neurological disorder (conversion)
        \item Brain tumour (subacute progression)
        \item Multiple sclerosis (younger, relapsing-remitting)
        \item Peripheral vertigo (vestibular neuritis, BPPV)
        \item Syncope with brief confusion
        \item Metabolic encephalopathy (hyponatraemia, hepatic, uraemic)
        \item Medication effects (sedatives, anticholinergics)
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Imaging (CT/MRI) distinguishes true stroke from mimics}
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
\textbf{Contact your local emergency services immediately for any sudden focal neurological deficit}. Do not wait. Do not drive. Ambulance enables pre-hospital notification, direct transport to stroke-capable hospital, and time-critical treatment.

\textbf{Time-critical treatments:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item IV thrombolysis (alteplase / tenecteplase): within 4.5 h (up to 9 h with imaging selection)
    \item Endovascular thrombectomy (EVT): within 6 h (up to 24 h with perfusion imaging selection)
    \item ICH: urgent neurosurgical assessment, BP control, reversal of anticoagulation
    \item SAH: urgent CT angiography / DSA, aneurysm securing (coiling/clipping)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pre-hospital:} FAST, Cincinnati Prehospital Stroke Scale, LAMS (EVT triage); pre-notification to stroke centre
    \item \textbf{Emergency CT brain (non-contrast):} within 10 min of arrival; exclude haemorrhage, early ischaemic signs (ASPECTS), hyperdense vessel sign; CT angiography (arch to vertex) + CT perfusion for EVT selection
    \item \textbf{MRI brain (DWI/FLAIR/GRE/SWI):} gold standard; DWI positive within minutes; FLAIR-DWI mismatch for wake-up/unknown onset; GRE/SWI for microbleeds, CAA
    \item \textbf{Vascular imaging:} CTA / MRA / DSA for large vessel occlusion, dissection, aneurysm, stenosis
    \item \textbf{Cardiac workup:} ECG (AF), troponin, prolonged cardiac monitoring (Holter 24--72 h, implantable loop recorder for cryptogenic), TTE/TOE (cardioembolic source, PFO)
    \item \textbf{Bloods:} FBC, coagulation (INR, aPTT, fibrinogen), glucose, lipids, HbA1c, renal, LFTs, ESR/CRP, autoimmune screen if young/unexplained
    \item \textbf{Carotid imaging:} duplex ultrasound for symptomatic carotid stenosis (NASCET criteria)
    \item \textbf{Stroke unit admission:} multidisciplinary care (neurology, nursing, PT, OT, Speech, SW, pharmacy, dietetics) -- reduces death/disability by ~20\%
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hyperacute (Emergency Department):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item IV alteplase 0.9 mg/kg (max 90 mg) over 60 min (10\% bolus) -- within 4.5 h, no major contraindications
        \item Tenecteplase may be used in selected patients according to current local stroke protocols and eligibility criteria
        \item EVT (stent retriever $\pm$ aspiration): large vessel occlusion (ICA, M1, M2, basilar); imaging selection (ASPECTS $\ge$6, core <70 mL, mismatch) extends window to 24 h
        \item BP management: thrombolysis target <185/110 pre-treatment, <180/105 post; EVT <180/105; ICH target SBP <140 (INTERACT2); SAH target SBP <160 pre-secure
        \item Antiplatelet: aspirin 150--300 mg loading then 100 mg daily (after 24 h if thrombolysed); dual (aspirin + clopidogrel) for minor stroke/TIA $\times$ 21 days (CHANCE, POINT)
        \item Anticoagulation: DO NOT start acutely for cardioembolic; delay 1--14 days by infarct size (ENRICH, ELAN trials); DOACs preferred over warfarin for AF
        \item ICH: urgent reversal (idarucizumab for dabigatran, andexanet alfa for factor Xa inhibitors, 4F-PCC + vitamin D for warfarin); BP control; avoid platelet transfusion unless antiplatelet-related (PATCH trial); neurosurgical evacuation for cerebellar >3 cm, lobar >30 mL with decline, hydrocephalus (EVD)
        \item SAH: nimodipine 60 mg Q4H $\times$ 21 days (vasospasm prophylaxis); aneurysm securing (coiling preferred); EVD for hydrocephalus; treat vasospasm (hemodynamic augmentation, angioplasty, IA vasodilators)
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Acute stroke unit care:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Physiological monitoring: glucose (avoid hyper/hypoglycaemia), temperature (treat fever), oxygenation (target SpO$_2$ $\ge$94\%), hydration, early mobilisation (AVERT protocol: 24 h), dysphagia screen before oral intake
        \item VTE prophylaxis: mechanical (IPC) from admission; pharmacological (LMWH) after 24--48 h if no haemorrhage / low bleed risk
        \item Nutrition: NG tube if dysphagia >7 days; PEG if prolonged
        \item Secondary prevention initiation before discharge
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Secondary prevention (ischaemic stroke/TIA):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Antiplatelet: aspirin 100 mg or clopidogrel 75 mg or aspirin/dipyridamole MR; not DAPT long-term (except minor stroke/TIA $\times$ 21 days)
        \item Anticoagulation for AF: DOAC (apixaban, rivaroxaban, dabigatran, edoxaban) -- start per timing above
        \item Statin: high-intensity (atorvastatin 80 mg or rosuvastatin 40 mg) -- all ischaemic stroke/TIA (SPARCL)
        \item BP target: <130/80 (all); agent per comorbidity (ACEi/ARB, thiazide, CCB)
        \item Diabetes: SGLT2i/GLP-1 RA for cardiorenal benefit
        \item Carotid endarterectomy / stenting: symptomatic 50--99\% stenosis (NASCET) within 2 weeks; asymptomatic 60--99\% selective
        \item PFO closure: age 18--60, cryptogenic stroke, no other cause, RoPE score high; shared decision
        \item Lifestyle: Mediterranean diet, exercise, smoking cessation, alcohol moderation, weight management
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rehabilitation:} early, intensive, task-specific rehabilitation may include physiotherapy, occupational therapy, speech and language therapy, neuropsychology, nursing, social support, and community rehabilitation after discharge; access depends on local services
    \item \textbf{Palliative care:} early integration for severe stroke; goals-of-care discussions; advance care planning
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Early neurological:} malignant MCA infarction (space-occupying oedema, days 2--5) $\to$ decompressive hemicraniectomy; haemorrhagic transformation (HI1/2, PH1/2); recurrent stroke; cerebral oedema; seizures
    \item \textbf{Medical:} aspiration pneumonia (dysphagia), UTI, DVT/PE, cardiac arrhythmias (AF, QT prolongation), neurogenic stress cardiomyopathy, hyponatraemia (SIADH/CSW), pressure injuries, falls
    \item \textbf{Late:} post-stroke depression (30\%), anxiety, cognitive impairment/dementia (30\% at 1 year), spasticity, shoulder pain, central post-stroke pain, fatigue, epilepsy (5--10\%), sexual dysfunction
    \item \textbf{ICH-specific:} haematoma expansion (first 24 h), perihaematomal oedema, intraventricular extension, hydrocephalus
    \item \textbf{SAH-specific:} delayed cerebral ischaemia (vasospasm, days 3--14), rebleeding (pre-securing), hydrocephalus, hyponatraemia (CSW/SIADH), neurogenic stunned myocardium
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Functional outcome at 90 days (mRS) is standard. With reperfusion therapies, ~45--50\% achieve functional independence (mRS 0--2) after large vessel occlusion stroke. Recovery is most rapid in first 3 months but continues for years. Age, stroke severity (NIHSS), infarct volume, reperfusion success, and comorbidities predict outcome. TIA carries 10--20\% 90-day stroke risk without treatment; <2\% with urgent management. Life expectancy reduced; recurrent stroke risk ~10\%/year. Quality of life depends on disability, cognition, mood, and social support.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Know FAST:} Face, Arm, Speech, Time -- contact local emergency services immediately
    \item \textbf{Primary prevention:} use a validated cardiovascular-risk assessment appropriate to the person's country and clinical context, then treat modifiable risk factors
    \item \textbf{BP control:} <130/80; home monitoring; adherence
    \item \textbf{AF detection:} opportunistic screening (pulse, ECG) $\ge$65; treat with DOAC
    \item \textbf{Lipids:} high-intensity statin if CVD; LDL <1.4 mmol/L
    \item \textbf{Diabetes:} HbA1c $\le$7\%; SGLT2i/GLP-1 RA
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle:} Mediterranean diet; 150 min/week exercise; no smoking; alcohol $\le$10 std drinks/week; weight management
    \item \textbf{Carotid disease:} screen if bruit or risk factors; intervene if symptomatic
    \item \textbf{Medication adherence:} antiplatelet/anticoagulant/statin/BP meds -- lifelong
    \item \textbf{Rehabilitation engagement:} continue exercises; community programs
    \item \textbf{Mental health:} screen/treat depression/anxiety; peer support (Stroke Foundation, EnableMe)
    \item \textbf{Advance care planning:} discuss preferences; substitute decision-maker
\end{itemize}
