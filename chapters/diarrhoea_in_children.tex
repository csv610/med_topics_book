\chapter{Diarrhoea in Children}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Diarrhoea in children is the passage of loose or watery stools more often than usual, usually caused by viral infection of the gut. Gastroenteritis is very common in childhood, and most children recover within a few days with fluids and rest. The main risk in children is dehydration, because their bodies are small and lose fluid quickly, so the focus of treatment is replacing fluids early. Severe dehydration is a medical emergency. Vaccination, hygiene, and good food safety help prevent diarrhoea in children. This chapter explains the causes, signs, and treatment of diarrhoea in children.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Gastroenteritis is one of the most common illnesses of childhood. Children under five typically have several episodes a year, and rotavirus was historically the leading cause before vaccination. Since rotavirus vaccination was introduced in Australia, hospitalisations for rotavirus gastroenteritis have fallen dramatically. Norovirus and other viruses now cause many episodes. Most children with diarrhoea are managed at home, but gastroenteritis remains a common reason for children to present to emergency departments, and a small proportion need hospital care for dehydration.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Viruses:} rotavirus, norovirus, and adenovirus cause most cases
    \item \textbf{Bacteria:} salmonella, campylobacter, shigella, and E. coli from contaminated food or contact
    \item \textbf{Parasites:} giardia and cryptosporidium, especially in childcare settings
    \item \textbf{Medicines:} antibiotics can cause diarrhoea in children
    \item \textbf{Other causes:} food allergy, toddler's diarrhoea, and other gut conditions
    \item \textbf{Risk of dehydration:} children lose fluid quickly and cannot always express thirst
    \item \textbf{Mechanism:} infection reduces the gut's absorption of water, causing watery stools and fluid loss
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Loose, watery stools, often with vomiting, fever, and abdominal cramps
    \item Reduced wet nappies or infrequent urination
    \item Dry mouth and lips, no tears when crying, and sunken eyes
    \item A sunken soft spot (fontanelle) in babies
    \item Irritability, listlessness, or unusual drowsiness
    \item Poor feeding and reduced appetite
    \item Most children recover within 3--5 days
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Viral gastroenteritis versus bacterial infection, which may need different care
    \item Appendicitis, which can cause vomiting, pain, and loose stools
    \item Urinary tract infection, which can cause fever and poor feeding
    \item Food allergy or intolerance
    \item Intussusception, a rare bowel emergency with severe intermittent pain
    \item Other causes of dehydration and illness
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor if a child with diarrhoea shows signs of dehydration, is unable to drink, has blood in the stool, or is getting worse. Babies under six months and children with chronic illness need earlier review.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A baby or child who is listless, floppy, difficult to wake, or very drowsy
    \item No wet nappies for six hours or more in a baby
    \item Sunken eyes or fontanelle with signs of severe dehydration
    \item Vomiting that prevents keeping down any fluids
    \item Blood in the stool with severe abdominal pain or high fever
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clinical assessment:} hydration is assessed from appearance, nappies, tears, and alertness
    \item \textbf{Weight:} acute weight loss reflects fluid loss
    \item \textbf{Stool tests:} rarely needed, but used for persistent, bloody, or outbreak-related diarrhoea
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} electrolytes in significant dehydration
    \item \textbf{Review of feeding:} ensuring adequate intake
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Oral rehydration solution:} the main treatment; given in small frequent sips after each loose stool
    \item \textbf{Continue feeding:} breastfed babies continue breastfeeding; formula-fed babies continue formula, with extra fluid
    \item \textbf{Bland foods:} toddlers can eat foods such as bread, rice, pasta, and bananas as tolerated
    \item \textbf{Avoid:} sugary drinks, juices, and soft drinks, which worsen diarrhoea
    \item \textbf{Medicines:} antidiarrhoeal medicines are not recommended in children
    \item \textbf{Probiotics:} may shorten some episodes, but are not essential
    \item \textbf{Hospital care:} intravenous fluids for children who cannot drink or are severely dehydrated
    \item \textbf{Hygiene:} careful hand washing to prevent spread within the family
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Dehydration, the most serious complication
    \item Electrolyte disturbance
    \item Weight loss and temporary growth faltering
    \item Spread of infection to siblings and other family members
    \item Secondary lactose intolerance after severe gastroenteritis
    \item Rare complications such as haemolytic uraemic syndrome from E. coli
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The vast majority of children with diarrhoea recover fully within a few days with fluids and normal feeding. Severe dehydration responds quickly to treatment, and death from gastroenteritis is very rare in Australia. Rotavirus vaccination has greatly reduced the most serious infections. With early fluid replacement and appropriate care, children recover without long-term effects.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Ensure children receive rotavirus vaccination on schedule
    \item Practise thorough hand washing, especially after nappy changes
    \item Teach children to wash hands before eating and after the toilet
    \item Practise safe food handling and cooking
    \item Give oral rehydration solution early in any episode of diarrhoea
    \item Keep children home while infectious
    \item Seek medical advice early for babies and young children
\end{itemize}
