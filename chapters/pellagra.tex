\chapter{Pellagra}
\index{Pellagra}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. Suspected nutritional deficiency should be assessed rather than treated with high-dose supplements without advice.}

\section*{What it is}
Pellagra is illness caused by inadequate niacin (vitamin B3) or tryptophan, or by an inability to use these nutrients. It is uncommon where diets are varied but can occur with severe malnutrition, malabsorption, alcohol dependence, some illnesses, or certain medicines.

\section*{Features}
Classical features include dermatitis, diarrhoea, and neurological or mental changes. The rash may be symmetrical and worse with sunlight; the tongue and mouth can be sore. Weakness, poor appetite, confusion, irritability, memory problems, or unsteady movement may occur. Untreated severe disease can be life-threatening.

\section*{Diagnosis and treatment}
Diagnosis is based on symptoms, diet and medical history, examination, and tests for other causes; there is no single routine test that excludes every case. Treatment usually involves prescribed nicotinamide or niacin, correction of the underlying cause, and broader nutritional support. Clinicians may check for other vitamin deficiencies, anaemia, liver disease, or malabsorption.

\section*{Prevention and outlook}
Eat a varied diet containing protein and fortified or naturally nutrient-rich foods when possible. Treat alcohol dependence, gastrointestinal disease, or other causes with appropriate support. Skin and neurological symptoms may improve with treatment, but delayed care can leave lasting neurological problems.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek urgent help for confusion, severe weakness, fainting, inability to eat or drink, severe diarrhoea with dehydration, seizures, or rapidly worsening neurological symptoms. Arrange assessment for a persistent sun-sensitive rash, chronic diarrhoea, mouth soreness, or unexplained weight loss.
