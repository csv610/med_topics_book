\chapter{Consumer rights}

\section*{Overview}
Consumer rights concerns the way health services, support services, or community resources are organised and used. Knowing what is available and how to access it helps people get the right care at the right time.

\section*{Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Confusion about which service to use for a problem
    \item Difficulty getting an appointment, transport, or cost
    \item Feeling that needs are not being met by current care
\end{itemize}

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Complex systems and many types of providers
    \item Costs, distance, or limited availability
    \item Language, cultural, or communication barriers
    \item Not knowing what is available or how to ask
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Identifying what help is needed and roughly which service fits
    \item Checking eligibility for programs and benefits
    \item Writing down current providers, medicines, and needs
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Start with a GP, who can guide further care
    \item Use nurse-led, pharmacist, or after-hours services for less urgent needs
    \item Explore community services, peer groups, and carer support
    \item Plan ahead with advance care plans and known contacts
    \item Use emergency services for genuine emergencies
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prevention}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Find and keep a regular GP
    \item Keep a list of medicines, allergies, and contacts
    \item Ask about bulk-billing and concession options early
    \item Build support networks before a crisis
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Call 000 for emergencies
    \item Contact the service directly for non-urgent questions
    \item Ask a GP or community worker for help navigating services
\end{itemize}
