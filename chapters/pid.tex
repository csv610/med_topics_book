\chapter{Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID)}
\index{Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID)}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. Pelvic pain or possible infection needs prompt clinical assessment.}

\section*{What it is}
Pelvic inflammatory disease is infection or inflammation of the upper female reproductive tract, including the uterus, fallopian tubes, and ovaries. Sexually transmitted infections such as chlamydia and gonorrhoea are common causes, but other bacteria can contribute. PID can be mild or have no obvious symptoms.

\section*{Symptoms}
Possible symptoms include lower-abdominal or pelvic pain, pain during sex, unusual vaginal discharge, bleeding between periods or after sex, pain when urinating, fever, nausea, or vomiting. These symptoms can also be caused by ectopic pregnancy, appendicitis, ovarian conditions, urinary infection, or other problems.

\section*{Diagnosis and treatment}
There is no single test that rules PID in or out. Assessment may include a pregnancy test, examination, STI testing, urine or blood tests, and ultrasound when needed. Clinicians may start broad-spectrum antibiotics when PID is suspected rather than wait for every result. Take the full course, do not share antibiotics, and return for review if symptoms are not clearly improving within 2--3 days or become worse. Hospital treatment may be needed for pregnancy, severe illness or vomiting, an abscess, inability to take oral treatment, or uncertainty about a surgical emergency.

\section*{Partners and prevention}
Avoid sex until treatment is complete, symptoms have settled, and recent sex partners have been assessed and treated as advised. Partners may need STI testing and treatment even without symptoms. Use condoms consistently, consider appropriate STI testing, and avoid douching. An intrauterine device usually does not need removal solely because PID is diagnosed, but discuss this with the treating clinician.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek emergency help for severe or one-sided pain, fainting, heavy bleeding, high fever, confusion, repeated vomiting, or possible pregnancy with pain or bleeding.

\section*{Complications and urgent care}
Delayed or repeated PID can cause scarring, chronic pelvic pain, infertility, ectopic pregnancy, or a tubo-ovarian abscess. Seek emergency help for severe or one-sided pain, fainting, heavy bleeding, high fever, confusion, repeated vomiting, or possible pregnancy with pain or bleeding.
