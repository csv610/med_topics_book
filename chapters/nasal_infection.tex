\chapter{Nasal infection}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational draft; seek professional advice for individual care.}

\section*{Definition and scope}
Nasal infection is an infection-related topic requiring identification of the affected site, likely organism, severity, and transmission risk.

\section*{Contributors and risk}
Potential causes include bacteria, viruses, fungi, or parasites; exposure, travel, immune status, wounds, and resistance patterns influence risk.

\section*{Presentation}
Record onset, fever, pain, rash, discharge, breathing or neurological symptoms, exposures, vaccination, immune status, and contacts.

\section*{Assessment}
Assessment may include examination, cultures or molecular tests, blood tests, and imaging when indicated; not every infection needs antibiotics.

\section*{Management}
Care may include fluids, rest, isolation or notification advice, wound care, targeted antimicrobials, and complication treatment.

\section*{When to seek urgent care}
Seek urgent care for sepsis features, breathing difficulty, confusion, severe dehydration, meningitis symptoms, or rapid deterioration.

\section*{Prevention and follow-up}
Use vaccination, hand hygiene, safer sex, food and water safety, protective equipment, and appropriate contact precautions.

\section*{Sources and limitations}
This concise educational overview should be checked against current local clinical guidance and adapted to the individual.
