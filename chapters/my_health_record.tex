\section*{Definition and Overview}
An electronic health record is a secure digital summary of a person's key health information. Depending on the jurisdiction, it may be created from insurance or public-health records and supplemented by documents uploaded by general practitioners, specialists, hospitals, pathologists, pharmacists, and the patient. It may include medicines, allergies, immunisations, pathology and imaging reports, discharge summaries, and advance care planning documents. Its aims are to give clinicians timely access to relevant information, improve safety, reduce duplicate testing, and support coordinated care. Participation may be opt-in or opt-out; access should be controlled by the person and authorised representatives.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Electronic-record systems are increasingly used worldwide, but their coverage, design, privacy rules, and ability to exchange information vary. People should ask which organisations can view their record, how to correct errors, how to withdraw or restrict access, and what happens if they move between health systems.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Purpose:} consolidate a person's health information in one secure, nationally accessible digital record to support safer, more efficient care
    \item \textbf{Data sources:} public health insurance and public medicines subsidy claims automatically populate the record; clinicians upload clinical documents (discharge summaries, referrals, pathology, imaging, care plans)
    \item \textbf{Mechanism:} a national infrastructure stores and links health documents associated with a unique Individual Healthcare Identifier (IHI) under secure access controls
    \item \textbf{Access controls:} individuals (and nominated representatives, e.g., carers or children's guardians) can view and manage access; emergency access is permitted for urgent care
    \item \textbf{Not a replacement:} it supplements, rather than replaces, clinical records held by individual providers and does not hold all of a patient's information
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Medicines:} current and past prescribed medications (from public medicines subsidy and pharmacy uploads), helping prevent adverse interactions and polypharmacy
    \item \textbf{Allergies and adverse reactions:} recorded allergies and adverse drug reactions to inform prescribing
    \item \textbf{Immunisation history:} local Immunisation Register (AIR) data, useful for catch-up vaccination and travel
    \item \textbf{Pathology and imaging:} results of blood tests, scans, and other investigations shared by providers
    \item \textbf{Hospital records:} discharge summaries, letters, and some procedure reports
    \item \textbf{Advance care planning:} advance care directives, enduring guardianship documents, and other end-of-life preferences
    \item \textbf{Nominated representatives:} nominated people (e.g., a partner or adult child) can access the record to help manage care
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Not a clinical diagnosis; the "differential" here concerns record features relevant to interpretation:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Gaps in the record: documents may not reflect care provided outside connected systems or before the record was created
    \item Outdated or duplicate entries that may mislead if not checked against current clinician-held records
    \item Medications discontinued but still appearing if not updated (medication lists may lag actual prescribing)
    \item The record reflects uploads by providers and may be incomplete for services not connected to the system
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Individuals do not "seek care" for the record itself. However, they should review their record regularly and correct errors with their provider.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Contact a healthcare provider to correct inaccurate information (e.g., wrong allergy or medication) in the record
    \item Use the My Health Record app or public health insurance online account to view and manage the record, nominations, and access
    \item Report suspected unauthorised access through the ADHA (privacy and security concerns)
\end{itemize}
For an emergency, standard emergency services apply; emergency access to the record can help clinicians during urgent care.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:} acute medical emergencies as per standard advice; the record is an adjunct, not a substitute for emergency care.

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Access:} via the My Health Record website/app or myGov, using public health insurance online services
    \item \textbf{Review of content:} confirm medications, allergies, immunisations, pathology, imaging, and shared summaries are correct
    \item \textbf{Nomination management:} add or remove nominated representatives and set access controls
    \item \textbf{Privacy settings:} choose which documents to share and with whom; restrict access where desired
    \item \textbf{Correction process:} request amendments to inaccurate documents through the relevant provider or the ADHA
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Keeping it updated:} ensure providers (GP, specialists, hospital, pharmacy) are aware of the record and upload relevant documents
    \item \textbf{Medication accuracy:} review medicines at each visit and flag errors to the prescriber/pharmacist
    \item \textbf{Advance care planning:} upload advance care directives and enduring guardianship documents so they are accessible in an emergency
    \item \textbf{Privacy management:} use access controls, remove records of sensitive documents, and manage nominations
    \item \textbf{Opt-out/withdraw:} individuals can remove their record through the ADHA if they no longer wish to participate
    \item \textbf{Seeking help:} technical support via the My Health Record helpline for access or usability issues
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Privacy and security concerns if sensitive health information is inappropriately shared
    \item Incomplete or inaccurate records leading to clinical errors (e.g., wrong medication reconciliation)
    \item Over-reliance on a possibly outdated record where it does not reflect current care
    \item Difficulty controlling access if an individual is unsure of their privacy settings
    \item Digital exclusion for people who cannot easily access or manage an online record
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
When accurate and up to date, My Health Record can improve medication safety, reduce duplicate tests, and support coordinated care, particularly across transitions (hospital to home, referrals). There is evidence of benefit in reconciliation of medications and reduction of avoidable harm. Overall outcomes depend on the completeness of uploaded data and how actively clinicians use the record. For the vast majority of people, the system operates safely, and individuals retain control over access and content.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Review the record periodically (e.g., at least annually) to ensure information is correct
    \item Keep medications and allergy information current with your prescriber and pharmacist
    \item Update nominated representatives as your circumstances change
    \item Ensure advance care planning documents are included if relevant
    \item Learn how privacy settings and access controls work through the ADHA website
    \item Contact the My Health Record helpdesk for any access or technical problems
\end{itemize}
