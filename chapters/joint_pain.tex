\chapter{Joint Pain}
\index{Joint Pain}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Joint pain, or arthralgia, is discomfort, aching, or soreness in a joint, and it is one of the most common symptoms for which people seek medical care. It can arise from many causes, including osteoarthritis, inflammatory arthritis, gout, injury, infection, overuse, and referred pain from other structures. The pattern of the pain, the joints involved, and associated symptoms guide the diagnosis. Most joint pain responds to self-care with activity, weight control, and pain relief, but persistent, severe, or inflammatory pain warrants assessment, and a hot, swollen joint with fever is an emergency.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Joint pain affects a large proportion of the population, particularly with age: around half of people over 65 report joint pain, and osteoarthritis affects over two million people. The knee, hip, hand, and spine are the most commonly affected. Joint pain is a leading cause of disability, work absence, and reduced quality of life. Gout, rheumatoid arthritis, and other inflammatory conditions are less common but important causes, and sport and work-related injuries are common in younger people.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Joint pain arises from the structures within and around the joint: the cartilage, bone, lining (synovium), ligaments, tendons, and bursae. In osteoarthritis, cartilage wears away and bone changes cause use-related pain. In inflammatory arthritis, the immune system inflames the lining, causing swelling and pain at rest and in the morning. Gout causes intense pain from uric acid crystals. Injury tears or strains ligaments and tendons, and overuse inflames tendons and bursae. Pain can also be referred from other sites, such as the hip causing knee pain, or from the spine causing leg pain. Understanding the mechanism guides treatment.

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Aching or sharp pain in the joint, which may be worse with use or at rest
    \item Morning stiffness, which is brief in osteoarthritis and prolonged in inflammatory arthritis
    \item Swelling, warmth, redness, or tenderness over the joint
    \item Reduced range of movement and stiffness
    \item Difficulty with daily activities such as walking, stairs, and gripping
    \item Sudden severe pain in a single joint, as in gout or injury
    \item Fever and a hot, swollen joint, which may indicate infection
    \item Pain that is worse after activity or at the end of the day
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
The cause of joint pain is identified through history, examination, and tests.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Osteoarthritis:} gradual, use-related pain in weight-bearing and hand joints
    \item \textbf{Inflammatory arthritis:} rheumatoid, psoriatic, and related conditions
    \item \textbf{Gout and pseudogout:} sudden flares of severe pain
    \item \textbf{Septic arthritis:} a hot, swollen joint with fever, an emergency
    \item \textbf{Injury and overuse:} sprains, strains, tendonitis, and bursitis
    \item \textbf{Referred pain:} from the hip, spine, or other structures
    \item \textbf{Fibromyalgia:} widespread pain including joints, without inflammation
    \item \textbf{Viral and other systemic illness:} transient joint pains
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Persistent, worsening, or disabling joint pain should be assessed, and joint pain with swelling, redness, fever, or significant morning stiffness warrants early review. Pain after injury, or pain that stops you using a joint, should also be assessed. A hot, swollen joint with fever is a medical emergency.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A hot, swollen, red joint with fever, which may indicate septic arthritis
    \item Severe pain after injury with deformity, inability to move the joint, or loss of feeling
    \item Sudden severe chest pain or breathlessness, which may indicate a blood clot
    \item Collapse, confusion, or severe illness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History and examination:} which joints, the pattern of pain, and aggravating factors
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} inflammatory markers, rheumatoid factor, and uric acid
    \item \textbf{X-rays:} for osteoarthritis, fracture, and joint damage
    \item \textbf{Ultrasound and MRI:} for soft tissue and early inflammatory changes
    \item \textbf{Joint aspiration:} for gout, infection, and inflammatory fluid
    \item \textbf{Assessment of function:} the effect of pain on daily life
    \item \textbf{Referral:} to rheumatology, orthopaedics, or physiotherapy as indicated
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Exercise and activity:} the foundation of management for most joint pain
    \item \textbf{Weight management:} reducing load on weight-bearing joints
    \item \textbf{Pain relief:} paracetamol, topical or oral anti-inflammatories
    \item \textbf{Physiotherapy:} strengthening, stretching, and joint protection
    \item \textbf{Heat and cold:} applied to soothe pain and stiffness
    \item \textbf{Specific treatment:} disease-modifying medicines for inflammatory arthritis, urate-lowering for gout
    \item \textbf{Joint injections:} corticosteroids for persistent inflammation
    \item \textbf{Surgery:} joint replacement for advanced osteoarthritis
    \item \textbf{Self-management:} pacing, education, and support programs
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chronic pain and reduced mobility
    \item Loss of independence and work capacity
    \item Falls and fractures from impaired mobility
    \item Joint damage from untreated inflammatory disease
    \item Depression and anxiety with chronic pain
    \item Complications of medicines and surgery
    \item Serious outcomes from missed septic arthritis
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Most joint pain is manageable, and many people improve with exercise, weight control, and simple measures. Osteoarthritis progresses slowly in most people, and joint replacement is highly successful when needed. Inflammatory arthritis responds to modern medicines, and gout is controllable. The outlook is best when pain is assessed early, the cause is identified, and management is matched to it.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Maintain a healthy weight
    \item Stay active with low-impact and strengthening exercise
    \item Protect joints with good technique and appropriate equipment
    \item Manage pain with heat, cold, and simple medicines
    \item Pace activities and take regular breaks
    \item Use a walking aid if it reduces pain
    \item See a doctor for persistent, inflammatory, or severe pain
    \item Seek urgent care for a hot, swollen joint
    \item Join a self-management program for ongoing joint problems
\end{itemize}
