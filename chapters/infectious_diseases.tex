\chapter{Infectious Diseases}
\index{Infectious Diseases}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Infectious diseases are illnesses caused by microorganisms: viruses, bacteria, fungi, parasites, and prions. They range from the common cold to life-threatening conditions such as sepsis, and they spread by a variety of routes, including respiratory droplets, contaminated food and water, blood and body fluids, insect bites, and direct contact. This chapter provides an overview of how infections develop and spread, the major categories of infectious disease, common presentations, and the principles of prevention, diagnosis, and treatment. Vaccination, hygiene, safe food handling, and appropriate use of antimicrobials remain the cornerstones of controlling infectious disease at individual and population levels.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Infectious diseases remain a leading cause of illness and death worldwide, particularly in children and in lower-income countries, where respiratory infection, diarrhoeal disease, malaria, tuberculosis, and HIV exact their greatest toll. In many countries, vaccination has eliminated or greatly reduced many infections, including measles, polio, and smallpox, and childhood immunisation coverage is high. At the same time, new and re-emerging threats continue to appear: pandemic influenza, coronaviruses, antimicrobial-resistant bacteria, and infections linked to travel and climate change. Antibiotic resistance, driven by the overuse of antibiotics, is one of the most serious global health threats, complicating the treatment of common infections.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Infection occurs when a pathogen enters the body, evades its defences, and multiplies. Viruses hijack host cells to reproduce; bacteria multiply independently and can produce toxins; fungi and parasites cause a range of diseases from skin infection to systemic illness. The immune system responds with inflammation, fever, and specific defences, which usually clear the infection but can themselves cause symptoms and tissue damage. Whether exposure leads to disease depends on the dose and virulence of the pathogen and the susceptibility of the host, which is increased by age, pregnancy, chronic illness, malnutrition, and immune suppression. Understanding the chain of transmission, from source to susceptible host, is the basis for prevention and control.

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Fever, chills, and sweats are the hallmarks of most infections
    \item Respiratory symptoms: cough, sore throat, runny nose, and breathlessness
    \item Gastrointestinal symptoms: diarrhoea, vomiting, and abdominal pain
    \item Skin manifestations: rashes, blisters, ulcers, and cellulitis
    \item Urinary symptoms: burning, frequency, and loin pain
    \item General features: fatigue, muscle aches, headache, and loss of appetite
    \item Serious features: confusion, drowsiness, rapid breathing, low blood pressure, and reduced urine output, which may indicate sepsis
    \item Specific syndromes reflect the site and organism, from meningitis to hepatitis
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
The same symptoms can be produced by different pathogens and by non-infectious conditions.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Viral versus bacterial infection:} often indistinguishable clinically, guiding antibiotic decisions
    \item \textbf{Respiratory infections:} cold viruses, influenza, COVID-19, and pneumonia
    \item \textbf{Gastrointestinal infections:} viral gastroenteritis, food poisoning, and parasitic infection
    \item \textbf{Skin and soft tissue infections:} impetigo, cellulitis, and abscesses
    \item \textbf{Systemic infections:} sepsis, meningitis, and endocarditis
    \item \textbf{Tropical and travel-related disease:} malaria, dengue, and others after travel
    \item \textbf{Non-infectious mimics:} autoimmune disease, inflammatory conditions, cancer, and drug reactions
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Most common infections resolve with rest, fluids, and simple medicines, but medical care is needed for severe or persistent symptoms, for infections in vulnerable people, and for certain warning features. Babies, older people, pregnant women, and people with weakened immunity warrant earlier assessment. Public health advice should be followed during outbreaks and for notifiable diseases.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Difficulty breathing, severe chest pain, or confusion
    \item A rash that does not fade when pressed, which may indicate meningococcal disease
    \item Fever with a stiff neck, severe headache, or sensitivity to light
    \item Seizures or convulsions
    \item Signs of sepsis: fever with rapid breathing, mottled skin, drowsiness, or low urine output
    \item Anaphylaxis or severe allergic reactions
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Clinical assessment:} history of symptoms, exposure, travel, and examination
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} full blood count, inflammatory markers such as C-reactive protein, and blood cultures in serious illness
    \item \textbf{Microbiological culture:} from blood, urine, stool, throat, or wounds
    \item \textbf{Molecular tests:} PCR for viruses and other pathogens
    \item \textbf{Antigen and antibody tests:} rapid tests and serology for specific infections
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} chest X-ray for pneumonia, ultrasound or CT for abscesses and collections
    \item \textbf{Lumbar puncture:} cerebrospinal fluid analysis for meningitis
    \item \textbf{Sensitivity testing:} to guide antibiotic choice and detect resistance
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Antibiotics:} for bacterial infections, chosen according to the likely organism and local resistance patterns
    \item \textbf{Antivirals:} for influenza, herpes, HIV, hepatitis, and other viral infections
    \item \textbf{Antifungals and antiparasitics:} for fungal and parasitic disease
    \item \textbf{Supportive care:} fluids, fever control, rest, and oxygen or hospital care when needed
    \item \textbf{Surgical drainage:} for abscesses and infected collections
    \item \textbf{Immunisation:} prevention of infection through routine and outbreak vaccination
    \item \textbf{Antimicrobial stewardship:} using antibiotics only when needed, at the right dose and duration
    \item \textbf{Infection control:} hygiene, isolation, and contact tracing to prevent spread
    \item \textbf{Public health action:} surveillance, notification, and outbreak response
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sepsis and septic shock, with organ failure and death
    \item Spread of infection to other sites, such as pneumonia after influenza
    \item Abscess formation and tissue destruction
    \item Chronic infection, such as chronic hepatitis or tuberculosis
    \item Post-infectious conditions, including reactive arthritis and post-viral fatigue
    \item Antimicrobial resistance, making infections harder to treat
    \item Complications of severe illness: dehydration, respiratory failure, and long-term disability
    \item Economic and social effects of illness, outbreaks, and quarantine
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The outcome of an infectious disease depends on the pathogen, the host, and the speed of treatment. Most common infections resolve fully within days to weeks, even without specific treatment. Antibiotics cure most bacterial infections when resistance is not a factor, and antivirals markedly improve outcomes for HIV, hepatitis C, and other viral diseases. Severe infections such as sepsis and meningitis carry significant mortality and require urgent hospital care. Vaccination has transformed the prognosis of many once-feared diseases, and continued prevention, diagnosis, and treatment advances keep improving outcomes.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Keep immunisations up to date for yourself and your family
    \item Wash hands regularly with soap and water or use hand sanitiser
    \item Cover coughs and sneezes, and stay home when unwell
    \item Practise food safety: cook thoroughly, refrigerate promptly, and avoid cross-contamination
    \item Use condoms and practise safe injecting to prevent blood-borne and sexually transmissible infections
    \item Prevent insect bites when travelling and in mosquito-prone areas
    \item Take antibiotics exactly as prescribed and never share them
    \item Support immunity with sleep, nutrition, physical activity, and stress management
    \item Follow public health advice during outbreaks and for notifiable diseases
\end{itemize}
