\chapter{Healthcare Services: Finding the Right One}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Finding the right healthcare service means choosing the service that best meets your needs for the right level of care, at the right time, and at an appropriate cost. The Australian health system offers a wide range of services, from GPs and pharmacies for everyday care, to allied health, hospitals, and specialist services. Choosing well involves knowing where to go for different problems: a pharmacist for minor ailments, a GP for assessment and ongoing care, urgent care or emergency departments for urgent problems, and specialists for complex conditions. Understanding the options, costs, and how to access services helps people get timely, appropriate, and affordable care.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Most Australians use multiple health services each year. GPs are the most common point of care, and community pharmacies are the most visited services. Emergency departments receive around 9 million visits a year in Australia, and a significant proportion of these are for conditions that could have been managed in primary care. Healthdirect Australia's helpline and website receive millions of contacts a year, guiding people to the right service. Choosing the wrong service leads to delays, higher costs, and unnecessary pressure on emergency departments.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
This chapter concerns health system navigation rather than a disease. The main service types include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{GPs:} the first point of contact for most health problems, ongoing care, and coordination
    \item \textbf{Community pharmacies:} advice and treatment for minor ailments, medicines, and vaccinations
    \item \textbf{Allied health:} physiotherapy, psychology, dietetics, and other services, often with GP referral
    \item \textbf{Dental services:} public and private dental care
    \item \textbf{Urgent care clinics:} same-day care for urgent but non-life-threatening problems
    \item \textbf{Emergency departments:} for serious, urgent, or life-threatening conditions
    \item \textbf{Hospitals:} inpatient care, surgery, and specialised treatment
    \item \textbf{Community health services:} nursing, mental health, and support services
    \item \textbf{Telehealth:} phone and video consultations
    \item \textbf{Healthdirect:} the national helpline that triages symptoms and directs people to services
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Choosing the right service depends on the nature of the problem.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Minor, self-limiting ailments: home care or a pharmacist
    \item New or persistent symptoms: a GP
    \item Chronic conditions: a regular GP with care plans and allied health support
    \item Urgent but non-life-threatening problems: an urgent care clinic or GP
    \item Life-threatening emergencies: call 000 or go to an emergency department
    \item Mental health concerns: a GP, psychologist, or crisis services
    \item Women's and children's health: specialised services and clinics
    \item After-hours care: after-hours GP services and telehealth
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Choosing the right service is a form of triage.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Self-care versus professional care for minor ailments
    \item Pharmacist versus GP for symptoms needing assessment
    \item GP versus urgent care versus emergency department
    \item Public versus private care, including cost and waiting time
    \item In-person versus telehealth
    \item Generalist versus specialist care
    \item Hospital versus community care
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek care early for new, persistent, or worsening symptoms rather than waiting. For non-urgent problems, see your GP. For urgent but not life-threatening problems, consider an urgent care clinic or after-hours GP. For serious symptoms, do not delay.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, or symptoms of stroke
    \item Severe bleeding, major trauma, or collapse
    \item Loss of consciousness or a seizure
    \item Any sudden, severe symptom that worries you
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Finding the right service involves planning and knowing your options.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Establish a regular GP:} most care is best coordinated through a GP
    \item \textbf{Know your local services:} locate pharmacies, clinics, urgent care, and hospitals near you
    \item \textbf{Use Healthdirect (1800 022 222):} free advice on where to seek care
    \item \textbf{Check opening hours:} for after-hours needs
    \item \textbf{Check costs:} ask about bulk billing and out-of-pocket costs
    \item \textbf{Consider telehealth:} for minor concerns and follow-up
    \item \textbf{Know the emergency number:} 000 for emergencies
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Using services well improves outcomes and reduces costs.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Start with your GP:} for assessment, diagnosis, and coordination
    \item \textbf{Use the pharmacist:} for minor ailments and medicine advice
    \item \textbf{Use urgent care for urgent problems:} this relieves pressure on emergency departments
    \item \textbf{Use emergency care for emergencies:} do not delay for serious symptoms
    \item \textbf{Follow referrals:} attend specialist and allied health appointments
    \item \textbf{Use care plans:} Medicare-funded plans for chronic conditions
    \item \textbf{Use telehealth wisely:} for convenience and follow-up
    \item \textbf{Keep a summary:} of your conditions, medicines, and test results to share with services
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Delayed care when the wrong service is chosen or care is postponed
    \item Unnecessary emergency department visits for primary care problems
    \item Higher costs from using the wrong service
    \item Fragmented care across multiple services without coordination
    \item Missed serious conditions when symptoms are dismissed as minor
    \item Confusion about the range of services and how to access them
    \item Long waits when the wrong service type is used
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
People who use the right service for the right problem get faster, cheaper, and more appropriate care. A strong GP relationship improves coordination and outcomes, especially for chronic conditions. Healthdirect and urgent care clinics reduce avoidable emergency visits, and telehealth improves access. Understanding the health system is a skill that pays off in better health, lower costs, and less frustration.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Establish a regular GP and attend annual health checks
    \item Use the pharmacist for minor ailments
    \item Call Healthdirect (1800 022 222) for guidance on where to seek care
    \item Know the location and hours of your nearest urgent care and emergency services
    \item Keep a list of your medicines, conditions, and allergies
    \item Use telehealth for follow-up and minor concerns
    \item Attend referrals and follow-up appointments
    \item Call 000 or go to an emergency department for serious symptoms
    \item Maintain healthy habits to prevent the need for services
\end{itemize}
