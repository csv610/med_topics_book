\chapter{Paying for Diagnostic Testing}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Paying for diagnostic testing refers to the costs involved in having medical investigations such as blood tests, imaging, and other tests, and how these costs are covered in Australia. Most diagnostic tests ordered by a doctor attract Medicare rebates, which reduce the out-of-pocket cost, but there may be gaps between the fee charged and the rebate. Public hospitals provide many tests free of charge, and some tests are covered by private health insurance or through government programs. Understanding the costs before having a test helps people make informed decisions. This chapter explains how diagnostic tests are funded in Australia and how to manage the costs.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Most Australians have diagnostic tests each year, from blood tests to imaging such as X-rays, CT, MRI, and ultrasound. The Medicare Benefits Schedule (MBS) lists the tests for which Medicare rebates are available and sets the rebate amounts. Around 30--40\% of pathology and imaging services involve out-of-pocket costs, depending on the provider and the type of test. Access to affordable testing is important because cost can be a barrier to diagnosis and monitoring, particularly for people on low incomes.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Medicare:} the national public health insurance scheme provides rebates for many diagnostic tests ordered by a doctor
    \item \textbf{Bulk billing:} when a provider accepts the Medicare rebate as full payment, the patient pays nothing
    \item \textbf{Gap payments:} when a provider charges more than the rebate, the patient pays the difference
    \item \textbf{Public hospitals:} diagnostic tests for public patients are generally provided at no charge
    \item \textbf{Private health insurance:} may cover some gap costs for tests and scans, depending on the policy
    \item \textbf{Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme (PBS):} covers medicines, not most diagnostic tests
    \item \textbf{Government programs:} free or subsidised screening programs exist for conditions such as bowel, breast, and cervical cancer
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A doctor orders a test and provides a referral or request form
    \item The test is performed at a pathology collection centre, imaging practice, or hospital
    \item The patient may be asked to pay at the time of the test, and to claim the Medicare rebate afterwards
    \item Costs vary between providers, regions, and types of tests
    \item Some tests require prior approval or an authority from Medicare
    \item Patients can ask for a written estimate of costs before agreeing to a test
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Bulk-billed versus privately billed testing, which affects the out-of-pocket cost
    \item Medicare-subsidised tests versus tests not listed on the MBS, which are not rebated
    \item Tests covered by private health insurance versus those not covered
    \item Public hospital testing versus private provider testing
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Discuss costs with your doctor or the testing provider before having a test if cost is a concern. Ask whether the provider bulk bills, what the gap payment will be, and whether cheaper options are available. Concession card holders and people in hardship should ask about reduced fees and public options.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Emergency situations where immediate testing is required as part of urgent care, which is not a cost consideration in emergencies
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Ask for a referral:} most diagnostic tests require a doctor's referral or request form
    \item \textbf{Check the Medicare Benefits Schedule:} shows which tests attract rebates and the rebate amounts
    \item \textbf{Compare providers:} fees and bulk-billing status vary between practices
    \item \textbf{Ask for an estimate:} providers can usually give a written estimate before the test
    \item \textbf{Check private health cover:} some policies cover gap payments for imaging
    \item \textbf{Consider public options:} public hospitals and community services may offer lower-cost testing
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Choose a bulk-billing provider:} eliminates the out-of-pocket cost for the listed rebate amount
    \item \textbf{Claim the Medicare rebate:} available online or through the Medicare app, or at a Medicare service centre
    \item \textbf{Use concession entitlements:} health care card and pensioner concession card holders may have lower costs
    \item \textbf{Discuss alternatives:} a doctor can advise whether a cheaper test is clinically appropriate
    \item \textbf{Plan for costs:} ask about payment plans or public options for expensive tests
    \item \textbf{Use screening programs:} free national programs for bowel, breast, and cervical screening
    \item \textbf{Private health insurance:} check the policy for out-of-hospital diagnostic cover
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Delayed diagnosis or monitoring when cost prevents testing
    \item Financial stress from unexpected out-of-pocket costs
    \item Avoidance of recommended tests due to cost concerns
    \item Variation in access to testing between regions and income groups
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Understanding the costs of diagnostic testing allows people to plan and access the tests they need. Bulk-billing providers and public services make testing affordable for most Australians, and Medicare rebates reduce the cost of most tests ordered by a doctor. Being informed about fees and entitlements improves access and reduces financial stress, supporting earlier diagnosis and better health outcomes.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Ask your doctor about the cost of any recommended test
    \item Ask whether the provider bulk bills before booking
    \item Claim Medicare rebates promptly through the app or online
    \item Keep receipts for private health insurance claims
    \item Use free government screening programs when eligible
    \item Discuss financial concerns openly with your doctor
    \item Check concession entitlements if you hold a health care card
\end{itemize}
