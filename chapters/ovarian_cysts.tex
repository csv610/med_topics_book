\chapter{Ovarian cysts}
\index{Ovarian cysts}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; seek professional advice for individual care.}

\section*{What they are}
An ovarian cyst is a fluid-filled sac or a mass containing other tissue in or on an ovary. Cysts are common, especially during the reproductive years, and many are benign and disappear without treatment. The approach differs after menopause, in pregnancy, and when a cyst has concerning features.

\section*{Symptoms}
Many cysts cause no symptoms and are found during an examination or scan for another reason. Possible symptoms include pelvic pain, pressure, bloating, pain during sex, or changes related to the menstrual cycle. Sudden severe one-sided pain may occur if a cyst twists the ovary or ruptures, sometimes with nausea or vomiting.

\section*{Assessment}
Assessment may include the history, pregnancy testing when relevant, examination, and ultrasound. The size, appearance, symptoms, age, menopausal status, and change over time guide follow-up. Blood tests are used selectively; a result such as CA-125 cannot by itself diagnose or exclude ovarian cancer.

\section*{Management}
Many simple cysts are monitored with repeat imaging. Treatment may include symptom relief, management of an underlying condition, or surgery if a cyst is large, persistent, painful, complicated, or concerning for cancer. Surgical choices consider ovarian function, fertility goals, age, and the appearance of the cyst.

\section*{When to seek urgent care}
Seek emergency help for sudden severe pelvic or abdominal pain, fainting, marked dizziness, heavy bleeding, fever with worsening pain, persistent vomiting, or a swollen abdomen with rapid deterioration. Arrange medical review for persistent pain, abnormal bleeding, or symptoms after menopause.
