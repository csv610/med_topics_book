\chapter{Heart and Cardiovascular Conditions}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Heart and cardiovascular conditions are disorders of the heart and the network of blood vessels that supply every organ in the body. They include coronary artery disease, heart failure, arrhythmias, valvular heart disease, hypertension, stroke, and diseases of the peripheral arteries. Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death in Australia and worldwide. Most cardiovascular conditions develop silently over decades through the process of atherosclerosis, making prevention, early detection, and risk factor control central to reducing their enormous health burden.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Cardiovascular disease accounts for around one in four deaths in Australia and is the single largest cause of death among women. An estimated one in five Australian adults has high blood pressure, and a similar proportion has elevated cholesterol. Coronary heart disease affects roughly 580,000 Australians, and around 55,000 people are hospitalised with heart failure each year. Stroke affects more than 27,000 Australians annually. While death rates from heart disease have fallen substantially over recent decades thanks to better prevention and treatment, the burden remains high and is set to grow with the ageing population.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Atherosclerosis:} cholesterol-rich plaques build up in artery walls, narrowing vessels and restricting blood flow
    \item \textbf{Modifiable risk factors:} smoking, high blood pressure, high cholesterol, diabetes, physical inactivity, poor diet, obesity, and excess alcohol
    \item \textbf{Non-modifiable factors:} age, male sex, and family history
    \item \textbf{Coronary artery disease:} narrowing of the arteries supplying the heart muscle, causing angina and heart attack
    \item \textbf{Heart failure:} the heart muscle weakens or stiffens and cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs
    \item \textbf{Arrhythmias:} disturbances of the heart's electrical system, such as atrial fibrillation
    \item \textbf{Valvular disease:} valves fail to open or close properly, straining the heart
    \item \textbf{Stroke and peripheral disease:} atherosclerosis affecting arteries to the brain and limbs
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chest pain or discomfort (angina) on exertion, relieved by rest
    \item Breathlessness on activity or at rest
    \item Palpitations, a racing, fluttering, or irregular heartbeat
    \item Fatigue, swelling of the ankles and legs, and weight gain in heart failure
    \item Dizziness, fainting, or blackouts
    \item Leg pain on walking (claudication) from peripheral artery disease
    \item High blood pressure is often completely silent until complications occur
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chest pain can arise from the lungs, oesophagus, stomach, muscles, or anxiety, not just the heart
    \item Breathlessness may reflect lung disease, anaemia, or deconditioning
    \item Palpitations can be benign, such as ectopic beats, or serious, such as ventricular arrhythmias
    \item Dizziness and fainting have many causes, including low blood pressure and ear problems
    \item Ankle swelling may be due to varicose veins, kidney disease, or medication
    \item The evaluation must always exclude dangerous cardiac causes first
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor for new or worsening chest discomfort, breathlessness, palpitations, dizziness, or swelling, or for a routine cardiovascular risk assessment, especially from middle age onwards or with risk factors.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Chest pain, pressure, heaviness, or tightness lasting more than a few minutes, or with sweating, nausea, or breathlessness
    \item Pain spreading to the arm, neck, jaw, or back
    \item Sudden weakness, numbness, facial drooping, or difficulty speaking (stroke)
    \item Fainting, collapse, or palpitations with dizziness and shortness of breath
    \item Severe breathlessness at rest, especially with a cold sweat
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Risk assessment:} blood pressure, cholesterol, blood sugar, and calculated cardiovascular risk scores
    \item \textbf{Electrocardiogram (ECG):} records the heart's electrical activity at rest
    \item \textbf{Exercise stress testing:} detects reduced blood supply to the heart during exertion
    \item \textbf{Echocardiogram:} ultrasound imaging of heart structure and function
    \item \textbf{Holter monitoring:} continuous ECG recording over days to capture intermittent arrhythmias
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} cardiac enzymes in acute events, plus lipids, glucose, and kidney function
    \item \textbf{Coronary angiography and CT:} detailed imaging of the coronary arteries and heart
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Lifestyle:} smoking cessation, healthy eating, regular activity, weight loss, and limited alcohol
    \item \textbf{Blood pressure and cholesterol medicines:} reduce stroke, heart attack, and death risk
    \item \textbf{Antiplatelet and anticoagulant therapy:} prevent blood clots
    \item \textbf{Angina treatment:} medications that relax vessels and reduce the heart's workload
    \item \textbf{Heart failure therapy:} diuretics, ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, and newer agents tailored to the individual
    \item \textbf{Arrhythmia management:} rate and rhythm control, cardioversion, and ablation
    \item \textbf{Procedures:} angioplasty and stenting, bypass surgery, valve repair or replacement, and pacemakers
    \item \textbf{Cardiac rehabilitation:} supervised exercise and education after events and procedures
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Heart attack and sudden cardiac death
    \item Stroke and transient ischaemic attack
    \item Progressive heart failure with fluid retention and hospitalisation
    \item Arrhythmias, including atrial fibrillation and life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias
    \item Kidney disease from hypertension and reduced cardiac output
    \item Peripheral artery disease with limb pain, poor healing, and amputation risk
    \item Reduced quality of life, disability, and premature death
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The outlook for cardiovascular disease has improved dramatically: with modern treatment, most people with coronary disease, heart failure, or atrial fibrillation live long, active lives. Prognosis depends strongly on the specific condition, its severity, and adherence to treatment. The greatest gains come from prevention: controlling blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood sugar and quitting smoking reduce cardiovascular risk by half or more. People with established disease benefit from comprehensive secondary prevention, and cardiac rehabilitation improves survival and quality of life.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Know your blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood sugar numbers
    \item Quit smoking and avoid second-hand smoke
    \item Eat a heart-healthy diet rich in vegetables, fruit, wholegrains, and healthy fats
    \item Be physically active most days, building up gradually
    \item Maintain a healthy weight and waist circumference
    \item Limit alcohol to within national guidelines
    \item Take prescribed medicines exactly as directed and attend follow-up
    \item Learn the warning signs of heart attack and stroke and act fast
\end{itemize}
