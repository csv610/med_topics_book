\section*{Definition and Overview}
Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) is a group of practices that developed over centuries in China and neighbouring regions. It may include acupuncture, herbal medicines, moxibustion, cupping, massage, dietary practices, tai chi, and qigong. Classical TCM concepts such as qi, yin and yang, and organ networks differ from biomedical anatomy. Evidence and safety vary greatly between individual practices and products.

\section*{Potential Uses and Evidence}
Some people use acupuncture or mind--body practices alongside conventional care for pain, nausea, or wellbeing. Research has found benefit for selected conditions, but results are not uniform and treatment effects may be modest. Evidence for many complex herbal preparations is limited by variation in ingredients, dose, quality, and study design. A traditional explanation should not delay assessment of a new or serious symptom.

\section*{Risks and Interactions}
Herbal products can interact with anticoagulants, diabetes medicines, antidepressants, immunosuppressants, and many other drugs. Products may be contaminated, adulterated, incorrectly labelled, or contain heavy metals. Acupuncture and cupping can cause bleeding, burns, infection, scarring, or organ injury if performed unsafely. Avoid needling through infected skin and tell the practitioner about pregnancy, bleeding disorders, immune suppression, and implanted devices.

\section*{Choosing Safer Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Use a practitioner whose training, registration, and infection-control standards can be verified locally
    \item Tell every health professional about herbs, supplements, acupuncture, and other complementary treatments
    \item Prefer products with independent quality testing where available; do not assume ``natural'' means safe
    \item Do not replace urgent or proven treatment for cancer, infection, diabetes, or other serious disease
    \item Seek advice before use in pregnancy, childhood, older age, kidney or liver disease, or immune suppression
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek medical assessment for persistent or worsening symptoms rather than relying on a traditional diagnosis alone. \textbf{Contact local emergency services} for breathing difficulty, collapse, severe allergic reaction, chest pain, sudden neurological symptoms, heavy bleeding, or suspected poisoning.

\section*{Outlook}
TCM may be a culturally meaningful complement to evidence-based care when used transparently and safely. Its benefits and risks should be assessed practice by practice, with shared decisions based on the person's goals and medical circumstances.
