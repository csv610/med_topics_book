\chapter{Correcting a squint (child)}

\section*{Overview}
Correcting a squint (child) is a health matter that concerns babies, children, or teenagers. Children are not simply small adults; their bodies, minds, and needs change as they grow. Many childhood conditions are mild and improve with simple care, but some need prompt assessment by a doctor.

\section*{Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Fever
    \item Trouble feeding, drinking, or waking as usual
    \item Crying that cannot be settled
    \item Vomiting or diarrhoea
    \item A rash, especially one that does not fade when pressed
    \item Breathing faster than usual, or working harder to breathe
    \item Drowsiness, floppiness, or being less responsive than usual
    \item A fit or seizure
\end{itemize}

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Common childhood infections such as colds, ear infections, and gastroenteritis
    \item Normal developmental stages such as teething and tantrums
    \item Allergies or asthma
    \item Minor injuries as children learn to move and explore
    \item Mental and emotional changes during adolescence
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item History from the parent or carer, including how the child is behaving
    \item Observation and examination of the child
    \item Temperature, breathing rate, and other vital signs
    \item Tests such as throat swabs, urine samples, or blood tests if needed
    \item Growth and developmental checks
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Simple care such as fluids, rest, and paracetamol or ibuprofen at the right dose for the child's weight
    \item Specific medicines such as antibiotics, asthma inhalers, or allergy treatments
    \item Keeping the child comfortable and watching closely for changes
    \item Treatment plans developed with the family and school where needed
    \item Hospital care for more serious illness or injury
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prevention}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Keep vaccinations up to date on the National Immunisation Program schedule
    \item Encourage regular hand washing and healthy eating
    \item Use car seats, helmets, and safety equipment
    \item Create a safe home and supervise young children
    \item Support emotional and mental wellbeing through family and school
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek urgent medical attention for a baby or child who is very drowsy, floppy, breathing rapidly or with effort, very pale, has a non-blanching rash, has a fit, or is unable to keep fluids down. \textbf{Call 000} if the child looks seriously ill or you are worried. Otherwise see a GP for:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Fever in a baby under three months old
    \item Symptoms that are not improving after a few days
    \item Ongoing concerns about feeding, growth, or development
\end{itemize}
