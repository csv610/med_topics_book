\chapter{Holiday Health}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Holiday health covers the steps needed to stay healthy and safe while travelling and during holiday periods. It includes pre-travel preparation such as vaccinations, medications, and travel insurance, as well as managing health on the road: food and water safety, sun and heat protection, insect precautions, and care of chronic conditions. Good planning prevents most holiday health problems, from traveller's diarrhoea to blood clots on long flights. Being prepared also means knowing how to access health care in Australia and overseas, and what to do in an emergency.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Australians take millions of overseas trips each year, and up to half of travellers experience some health problem while away, most commonly diarrhoea, respiratory infections, and injuries. Around 1--5\% of travellers require medical care during or after travel, and a small proportion need hospitalisation. Common travel-related illnesses include traveller's diarrhoea, malaria, dengue, and infections acquired abroad that appear after return. Falls, road accidents, and drowning are leading causes of holiday injury and death. Much of this is preventable with sensible preparation.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Infections:} contaminated food and water cause traveller's diarrhoea; insect bites transmit malaria, dengue, and other diseases
    \item \textbf{Sun and heat:} ultraviolet exposure causes sunburn and skin cancer risk; heat causes dehydration and heat illness
    \item \textbf{Injuries:} road traffic accidents, falls, water accidents, and alcohol-related injury
    \item \textbf{Altitude and diving:} specific environments carry particular health risks
    \item \textbf{Chronic disease:} disrupted routines, missed medications, and poor access to care worsen chronic conditions
    \item \textbf{Blood clots:} prolonged immobility on flights increases the risk of deep vein thrombosis
    \item \textbf{Pathophysiology:} each hazard acts through distinct mechanisms, but all are reduced by planning and prevention
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Traveller's diarrhoea: frequent loose stools, cramps, nausea, and dehydration
    \item Respiratory infections: cough, fever, and sore throat
    \item Sunburn, heat exhaustion, and heat stroke
    \item Insect-borne fevers: fever, headache, and muscle aches after exposure
    \item Injuries: fractures, sprains, and wounds from accidents
    \item Exacerbations of asthma, diabetes, and heart disease
    \item DVT: calf pain and swelling after long flights
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Fever after travel may be malaria, dengue, typhoid, or many other infections, and requires prompt assessment
    \item Diarrhoea may be infectious or due to food intolerance
    \item Respiratory symptoms may be viral, bacterial, or related to altitude or pollution
    \item Post-travel illness should always be evaluated with travel history in mind
    \item A thorough history of destinations, exposures, and timing guides diagnosis
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor 6--8 weeks before travel for vaccinations and advice, and visit a travel clinic if travelling to higher-risk destinations. Seek medical care for any significant illness during or after travel, especially fever.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Fever with confusion, severe headache, or rash after travel (possible malaria or meningitis)
    \item Difficulty breathing or chest pain
    \item Severe dehydration with inability to keep down fluids
    \item Severe injuries, suspected fractures, or head injury
    \item Signs of heat stroke or collapse
    \item Swollen, painful calf with breathlessness (possible pulmonary embolism)
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pre-travel consultation:} immunisation status, destination risks, and medication planning
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} for fever after travel, including malaria films and serology
    \item \textbf{Stool tests:} for persistent diarrhoea
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} for injuries and suspected blood clots
    \item \textbf{Post-travel assessment:} a detailed itinerary and exposure history
    \item \textbf{Travel insurance review:} understanding cover before departure
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pre-travel preparation:} vaccinations, malaria prevention, and a travel health kit
    \item \textbf{Food and water safety:} safe water, thoroughly cooked food, and good hand hygiene
    \item \textbf{Insect protection:} repellents, protective clothing, and bed nets in risk areas
    \item \textbf{Sun protection:} sunscreen, hats, and shade, especially in Australia and the tropics
    \item \textbf{Chronic disease care:} enough medication for the trip, a health summary, and a plan for care abroad
    \item \textbf{Blood clot prevention:} regular movement, hydration, and compression stockings on long flights
    \item \textbf{Travel insurance:} comprehensive cover, including evacuation
    \item \textbf{Emergency planning:} knowing local emergency numbers and the location of health services
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe infections acquired abroad, including malaria, dengue, and typhoid
    \item Dehydration from diarrhoea, especially in children
    \item Heat stroke and sun-related skin damage
    \item Injuries and accidents, including road and water accidents
    \item Blood clots after long flights
    \item Exacerbation of chronic disease with poor access to care
    \item Financial and logistical problems from uninsured illness or injury
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Most holiday health problems are minor and resolve quickly with simple treatment, and nearly all are preventable with preparation. Travellers who plan vaccinations, practise food and water safety, protect against insects and sun, and manage chronic conditions have a low risk of serious illness. When illness does occur, prompt access to care gives good outcomes. The key is to plan before leaving, act early when unwell, and ensure appropriate insurance for the unexpected.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Visit a doctor or travel clinic 6--8 weeks before travel
    \item Carry a travel health kit: medications, rehydration salts, sunscreen, and repellent
    \item Drink safe water and eat safely prepared food
    \item Use insect repellent and sleep under nets in risk areas
    \item Protect against sun and stay hydrated in heat
    \item Take regular breaks and move on long flights
    \item Pack enough medication and a doctor's letter for chronic conditions
    \item Take out comprehensive travel insurance
\end{itemize}
