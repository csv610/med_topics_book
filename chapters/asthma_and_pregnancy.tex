\chapter{Asthma and Pregnancy}
\index{Asthma and Pregnancy}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Asthma and pregnancy concerns the management of asthma during pregnancy and the way the two conditions interact. Asthma does not need to prevent a healthy pregnancy: with good control and regular medical review, most women with asthma have a normal pregnancy and a healthy baby. The central principle is to keep asthma well controlled throughout pregnancy, because poorly controlled asthma and acute attacks pose a far greater risk to both mother and baby than the medicines used to prevent them. Continuing proven asthma treatment is safer than stopping it.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Asthma affects around one in ten to one in thirteen pregnant women in many countries, making it one of the most common medical conditions in pregnancy. The effect of pregnancy on asthma varies: roughly one third of women improve, one third remain the same, and one third worsen during pregnancy. Women with severe asthma before pregnancy are more likely to worsen, whereas those with few symptoms often remain stable. Deterioration is most common in the second and third trimesters, and acute attacks can occur in labour and after birth.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hormonal changes:} pregnancy hormones can influence airway inflammation and bronchial smooth muscle tone
    \item \textbf{Mechanical changes:} the enlarging uterus elevates the diaphragm, but this usually does not seriously worsen asthma
    \item \textbf{Immune changes:} pregnancy alters immune responses, which can modulate allergic and inflammatory activity in either direction
    \item \textbf{Gastro-oesophageal reflux:} common in pregnancy, can irritate the airways and worsen symptoms
    \item \textbf{Triggers continue:} the same allergens, tobacco smoke, viral infections, and irritants that trigger asthma outside pregnancy remain active
    \item \textbf{Undertreatment:} some women reduce or stop their asthma medicines through concern about the baby, leading to loss of control and increased risk of attacks
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Wheeze, cough, chest tightness, and breathlessness, as with asthma outside pregnancy
    \item Symptoms may be better, worse, or unchanged compared with before pregnancy
    \item Difficulty sleeping, or waking at night with cough or wheeze
    \item A need to use the reliever inhaler more often than usual
    \item Breathlessness is partly a normal feature of late pregnancy and must be distinguished from true asthma symptoms
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The normal breathlessness of advanced pregnancy
    \item Pulmonary embolism, which causes sudden breathlessness and pleuritic chest pain
    \item Pneumonia or bronchitis
    \item Anaemia causing fatigue, breathlessness, and pallor
    \item Anxiety or hyperventilation
    \item Cardiac problems that can arise in pregnancy, such as peripartum cardiomyopathy
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Attend regular antenatal and asthma reviews, and see a doctor promptly if symptoms are worsening, night waking is increasing, or reliever use is rising. Tell your obstetrician, midwife, and general practitioner that you have asthma, and keep them informed about your level of control.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for an asthma emergency in pregnancy:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe breathlessness or an inability to speak in full sentences
    \item Reliever medicine not helping or not lasting
    \item Lips or face turning blue
    \item Reduced or absent baby movements
    \item Exhaustion, confusion, or drowsiness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History:} previous asthma severity, current medicines, symptom pattern, trigger exposure, and any history of attacks
    \item \textbf{Lung function:} spirometry and peak flow measurement are safe in pregnancy and confirm the diagnosis and level of control
    \item \textbf{Peak flow diary:} home monitoring to track variability and to detect worsening early
    \item \textbf{Examination:} auscultation of the chest for wheeze and assessment of breathing effort
    \item \textbf{Routine antenatal monitoring:} blood pressure and fetal growth checks, because severe uncontrolled asthma can affect the baby
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Continue usual medicines:} inhaled preventers and relievers are considered safe in pregnancy and should be used exactly as prescribed
    \item \textbf{Optimise control before and during pregnancy:} the aim is no daytime symptoms, no night waking, and no attacks
    \item \textbf{Trigger avoidance:} avoid tobacco smoke, allergens, and airway irritants
    \item \textbf{Acute attacks:} treated promptly with repeated reliever doses and oxygen under medical care; a short course of oral corticosteroids may be required and is safe when clinically indicated
    \item \textbf{Birth planning:} continue asthma medicines during labour, and the asthma management plan should not alter the mode of delivery
    \item \textbf{Baby checks:} monitoring of fetal growth during pregnancy, with observation of the baby after birth, particularly if control was poor
    \item \textbf{After the birth:} asthma control usually returns to its pre-pregnancy pattern; keep taking asthma medicines while breastfeeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Maternal:} asthma attacks, which are more common when control is poor, and a somewhat increased risk of pre-eclampsia in some studies
    \item \textbf{Fetal:} poor growth, low birth weight, and preterm birth, which are largely linked to poorly controlled asthma and attacks rather than to the medicines
    \item Reduced oxygen delivery to the baby during severe attacks
    \item Anxiety about medicines or about the effect of asthma on the baby
    \item A postnatal flare of symptoms as hormone levels change
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The great majority of women with well-controlled asthma have a normal pregnancy and a healthy baby. Many women find their asthma unchanged or improved during pregnancy, and control usually returns to its previous pattern after the birth. The principal risk factors for complications are poor control and severe attacks, both of which are largely preventable with regular review and adherence to treatment. With appropriate care, asthma should not interfere with pregnancy, delivery, or breastfeeding.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Have an asthma review before pregnancy, or as early in pregnancy as possible
    \item Use preventer inhalers daily as prescribed and keep an up-to-date written asthma action plan
    \item Practise correct inhaler technique with your doctor or pharmacist
    \item Avoid smoking and second-hand smoke
    \item Receive the influenza vaccination, which is recommended and safe in pregnancy
    \item Manage reflux, avoid known triggers, and keep physically active
    \item Attend all antenatal appointments and report any worsening symptoms early
    \item If breastfeeding, continue asthma medicines as prescribed, because they are safe
\end{itemize}
