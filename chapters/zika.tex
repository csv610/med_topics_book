\chapter{Zika Virus}

\section{Overview}
Zika virus is a mosquito-borne flavivirus primarily transmitted by Aedes species mosquitoes. First identified in Uganda in 1947, Zika gained global attention during a large outbreak in Brazil in 2015. The virus is usually mild but can cause serious birth defects when pregnant women are infected, particularly microcephaly and other brain abnormalities in developing fetuses.

\section{Symptoms}
Most infections (80\%) are asymptomatic. When symptoms occur, they typically include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Low-grade fever
    \item Maculopapular rash
    \item Joint pain (polyarthralgia), especially in hands and feet
    \item Conjunctivitis (pink eye)
    \item Muscle pain
    \item Headache
    \item Postauricular lymphadenopathy
\end{itemize}

Symptoms last 2-7 days and are usually mild. Complications include Guillain-Barr\'e syndrome and congenital Zika syndrome.

\section{Causes}
Zika virus is transmitted by:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Bite from infected Aedes aegypti or Aedes albopictus mosquitoes
    \item Mother-to-fetus during pregnancy
    \item Sexual transmission from an infected partner
    \item Blood transfusion (rare)
\end{itemize}

The virus belongs to the Flaviviridae family and is closely related to dengue, yellow fever, and West Nile viruses.

\section{Diagnosis}
Diagnosis requires:
\begin{itemize}
    \item RT-PCR testing of blood (positive within first week)
    \item IgM antibody testing (may cross-react with dengue)
    \item Serology with plaque reduction neutralization tests
    \item Urine testing for viral RNA
\end{itemize}

Pregnant women with exposure should be tested regardless of symptoms. Fetal ultrasound monitoring is recommended for confirmed infections during pregnancy.

\section{Treatment}
There is no specific antiviral treatment for Zika:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Rest and hydration
    \item Acetaminophen for fever and pain
    \item Avoid aspirin and NSAIDs until dengue is ruled out
    \item Supportive care for complications
\end{itemize}

Pregnant women require enhanced fetal monitoring. Physical therapy may help with Guillain-Barr\'e syndrome.

\section{Prevention}
Prevention focuses on mosquito bite avoidance:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Use EPA-registered insect repellents
    \item Wear long-sleeved shirts and long pants
    \item Use air conditioning or window screens
    \item Eliminate standing water where mosquitoes breed
    \item Practice safe sex or abstain if partner has been exposed
    \item Pregnant women should avoid travel to Zika-affected areas
\end{itemize}

\section{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek medical attention if you:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Are pregnant and have possible Zika exposure
    \item Develop symptoms after travel to affected areas
    \item Experience severe headache, vision changes, or weakness
    \item Have a rash with fever lasting more than 2 days
\end{itemize}
