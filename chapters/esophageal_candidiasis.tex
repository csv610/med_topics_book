\chapter{Oesophageal Candidiasis}
\index{Oesophageal Candidiasis}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Oesophageal candidiasis is an infection of the oesophagus with Candida, a yeast that normally lives harmlessly in the body. It occurs when the immune system is weakened or when the normal balance of organisms is disturbed, allowing the yeast to overgrow. The infection causes pain on swallowing, difficulty swallowing, and sometimes a feeling of food sticking. It is most common in people with HIV, in people taking corticosteroids or other immunosuppressants, and in those with diabetes or using inhaled steroids for asthma. Treatment with antifungal medicines is very effective.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Oesophageal candidiasis is uncommon in healthy people and is most often seen in people with weakened immune systems. It was once a common first sign of HIV infection, and while less common now with better HIV treatment, it still affects people with advanced disease, transplant recipients, and people taking immune-suppressing medicines. It also occurs in people using inhaled corticosteroids who do not rinse their mouths, and in people with poorly controlled diabetes.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Candida species, usually Candida albicans, cause the infection when conditions allow overgrowth.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Immune suppression:} HIV, chemotherapy, and immunosuppressant medicines weaken the defences against Candida
    \item \textbf{Corticosteroids:} both inhaled and oral steroids increase risk
    \item \textbf{Diabetes:} high blood sugar favours yeast growth
    \item \textbf{Antibiotics:} broad-spectrum antibiotics kill the normal bacteria that keep Candida in check
    \item \textbf{Other factors:} malnutrition, ageing, and achlorhydria
    \item \textbf{Mechanism:} Candida invades the lining of the oesophagus, causing inflammation, white plaques, and ulcers
    \item \textbf{Presentation:} the classic finding is white patches on the oesophageal lining, seen at endoscopy
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Symptoms usually develop gradually.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Pain on swallowing, often described as burning
    \item Difficulty or a sensation of food sticking when swallowing
    \item Chest discomfort
    \item A feeling of food coming back up
    \item Weight loss in some people
    \item Thrush in the mouth, which often accompanies oesophageal candidiasis
    \item Fever is unusual unless the infection spreads
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Other conditions cause similar swallowing symptoms.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Viral oesophagitis, especially from herpes simplex or cytomegalovirus in immunocompromised people
    \item Reflux oesophagitis from acid reflux
    \item Pill oesophagitis from medicines stuck in the oesophagus
    \item Eosinophilic oesophagitis
    \item Oesophageal cancer, which causes progressive difficulty swallowing
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Anyone with pain or difficulty swallowing should see a doctor, especially people with weakened immunity, diabetes, or a history of steroid use, in whom oesophageal candidiasis is more likely.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden complete difficulty swallowing
    \item Severe chest pain
    \item Vomiting blood
    \item Fever with difficulty swallowing in an immunocompromised person
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Diagnosis is often made on the history and a trial of treatment, with endoscopy for confirmation.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History:} the symptoms and risk factors, including immunity, steroids, and diabetes
    \item \textbf{Trial of treatment:} antifungal treatment is often started on suspicion
    \item \textbf{Gastroscopy:} shows the characteristic white plaques and allows biopsy
    \item \textbf{Biopsy:} confirms Candida invasion
    \item \textbf{Investigation of risk factors:} HIV testing and diabetes checks where appropriate
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Treatment is with antifungal medicines.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Oral antifungals:} fluconazole is the usual treatment, taken for one to three weeks
    \item \textbf{Other antifungals:} for resistant infection, other medicines are used
    \item \textbf{Treating the cause:} managing diabetes, reviewing steroids, and improving immunity
    \item \textbf{HIV treatment:} antiretroviral therapy restores immunity and prevents recurrence
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} confirmation that symptoms have resolved
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Bleeding and ulceration of the oesophagus
    \item Weight loss and poor nutrition from painful swallowing
    \item Spread of the infection in severely immunocompromised people
    \item Recurrence if the underlying cause is not addressed
    \item Antifungal resistance in people with frequent treatment
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Oesophageal candidiasis responds well to antifungal treatment, and symptoms usually improve within a few days. The condition recurs if the underlying immune problem persists, which is why treating the cause is central. With modern HIV treatment and careful management of the risk factors, most people recover fully.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Rinse the mouth after using inhaled corticosteroids, and use a spacer
    \item Manage diabetes with good blood sugar control
    \item Use antibiotics only when needed
    \item Seek care early for swallowing symptoms
    \item Take the full course of antifungal treatment
    \item Attend follow-up and manage the underlying condition
\end{itemize}
