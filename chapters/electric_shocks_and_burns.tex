\chapter{Electric Shocks and Burns}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Electric shocks and electrical burns are injuries caused by electric current passing through the body. An electric shock is the effect of the current itself, which can disrupt the heart, muscles, and nerves, while electrical burns are the tissue damage caused where the current enters and exits the body. Electrical burns can look small on the surface but extend deep into the tissues beneath, which makes them deceptive and potentially serious. This chapter explains how electrical injuries occur, their unique features, and their treatment.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Electrical injuries occur in homes, workplaces, and outdoors. Children are commonly injured by exposed sockets and electrical cords, while adults are injured at work, through DIY, and in accidents involving power lines. High-voltage and lightning injuries are much rarer but far more serious. Most electrical injuries in Australia are low-voltage household shocks, and while most are minor, a proportion require hospital care for burns, heart problems, or nerve injury.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Electrical injuries are complex because the current damages the body in several ways at once.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Direct tissue damage:} current heats and destroys tissues along its path, causing burns
    \item \textbf{Entry and exit burns:} current enters and exits at points of contact, producing characteristic burn marks; dry skin creates high resistance and deep local burns
    \item \textbf{Cardiac effects:} current through the chest can disturb or stop the heart rhythm
    \item \textbf{Neurological effects:} current damages nerves, causing weakness, numbness, and pain
    \item \textbf{Muscle damage:} severe muscle injury releases muscle enzymes that can damage the kidneys
    \item \textbf{Secondary injury:} the person may be thrown, fall, or suffer other injuries
    \item \textbf{Lightning:} produces a brief, massive current that can stop the heart and cause characteristic injuries
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
The features of electrical injury vary widely.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Burns at entry and exit points, which may be small, round, or extensive
    \item Muscle pain, spasms, and weakness
    \item Numbness, tingling, and loss of feeling
    \item Palpitations, chest pain, and irregular heartbeat
    \item Confusion, headache, and memory loss
    \item Breathing difficulty
    \item Loss of consciousness or cardiac arrest in severe injuries
    \item Late complications, including cataracts and spinal cord problems after high-voltage injury
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Electrical burns can be confused with other injuries.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Thermal burns from heat and flames
    \item Chemical burns
    \item Friction burns
    \item Nerve injuries from other trauma
    \item Heart rhythm problems from other causes
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
All electrical shocks and burns should be assessed by a doctor, because internal damage may not be apparent. This is especially important for shocks involving the chest, high voltage, loss of consciousness, or pregnancy.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Unconsciousness, cardiac arrest, or difficulty breathing
    \item Chest pain or an irregular heartbeat
    \item Seizures
    \item Burns, particularly deep, extensive, or over the face, hands, or heart area
    \item Any lightning or high-voltage injury
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Assessment of electrical injury aims to find hidden damage.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History:} the voltage, pathway, and duration of contact
    \item \textbf{Examination:} burns, heart, breathing, and neurological assessment
    \item \textbf{ECG and cardiac monitoring:} essential after significant shocks
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} muscle enzymes and kidney function
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} X-rays or CT for suspected fractures or internal injury
    \item \textbf{Burn assessment:} evaluation of burn depth, which may be deeper than it appears
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Treatment addresses the whole pattern of injury.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Emergency care:} safe removal from the current, CPR if needed, and ambulance transport
    \item \textbf{Burn treatment:} cleaning, dressings, and specialist burns care for significant burns
    \item \textbf{Cardiac care:} monitoring and treatment of rhythm disturbances
    \item \textbf{Fluid and kidney support:} intravenous fluids for severe muscle damage
    \item \textbf{Surgery:} debridement of dead tissue and reconstruction for severe burns
    \item \textbf{Neurological care:} assessment and rehabilitation of nerve damage
    \item \textbf{Rehabilitation:} physiotherapy, occupational therapy, and psychological support
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Cardiac arrest and death
    \item Deep burns requiring skin grafts and reconstruction
    \item Permanent nerve damage and chronic pain
    \item Kidney failure from muscle breakdown
    \item Fractures and other injuries from falls
    \item Late effects, including cataracts and spinal injuries after high-voltage shocks
    \item Psychological trauma
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Most low-voltage electrical injuries heal fully, and burns often heal with little scarring when treated promptly. High-voltage and lightning injuries are much more serious, with significant mortality and long-term disability among survivors. Specialist burns and rehabilitation care improves outcomes substantially.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Treat all electricity with respect and follow safety instructions
    \item Use safety switches and have electrical work done by licensed tradespeople
    \item Never touch appliances with wet hands
    \item Keep power cords and appliances away from children
    \item Follow workplace electrical safety procedures
    \item Seek shelter indoors during thunderstorms
    \item Have burns reviewed by a doctor even when they seem small
\end{itemize}
