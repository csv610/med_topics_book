\chapter{COVID-19 Medication}
\index{COVID-19 Medication}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
COVID-19 medications are medicines used to treat people infected with SARS-CoV-2, the virus that causes COVID-19. Most people with COVID-19 recover at home without specific antiviral treatment, but some people at higher risk of severe illness benefit from antiviral medicines taken early in the infection. These medicines reduce the risk of hospitalisation and death when started within five days of symptoms beginning. Other COVID-19 treatments, such as corticosteroids and blood thinners, are used in hospital for people with severe disease. Vaccination remains the most important measure for preventing serious COVID-19.

\section*{Epidemiology}
COVID-19 has infected hundreds of millions of people worldwide, and although most infections are mild, a significant proportion of people, particularly older adults and those with chronic conditions, develop severe illness. Antiviral treatments became available from 2022 and are prescribed to people at high risk of severe disease, including older people, people with immune suppression, and those with significant chronic illness. Their use has contributed to lower rates of hospitalisation and death in treated groups, alongside vaccination.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
COVID-19 medication works at different stages of the illness.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Viral entry:} antivirals block the virus from replicating in the early phase of infection
    \item \textbf{Immune response:} in severe disease, the immune system's over-reaction drives lung damage
    \item \textbf{Antivirals:} nirmatrelvir--ritonavir (Paxlovid) and molnupiravir stop viral replication and are taken early in the illness
    \item \textbf{Corticosteroids:} dexamethasone and similar drugs dampen excessive inflammation in hospitalised patients
    \item \textbf{Anticoagulants:} blood thinners prevent blood clots, a serious complication of severe COVID-19
    \item \textbf{Oxygen and supportive care:} treat low oxygen levels and organ failure
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
People who may benefit from COVID-19 medication are those with symptoms who are at high risk.

\begin{itemize}
    \item A positive rapid antigen or PCR test for COVID-19
    \item Symptoms such as fever, cough, sore throat, or runny nose
    \item Age over 70, or over 50 with additional risk factors
    \item Immune suppression, such as after organ transplant, chemotherapy, or steroid treatment
    \item Chronic conditions such as heart, lung, kidney, or liver disease, or diabetes
    \item Pregnancy with additional risk factors
    \item Symptoms of a cold or flu that started within five days
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Not everyone with a positive test needs medication.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Mild infection in a healthy young person, which generally needs no treatment
    \item A positive test without symptoms, where treatment is usually not indicated
    \item Symptoms caused by another virus, such as influenza, which is treated differently
    \item Severe illness needing hospital care rather than home antiviral treatment
    \item People taking medicines that interact with antivirals, where alternatives are needed
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
If you test positive for COVID-19 and are at higher risk of severe illness, seek medical advice promptly, because antiviral treatment must begin within five days of symptoms starting. Contact a GP, a COVID-19 clinic, or Healthdirect for assessment.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Difficulty breathing, shortness of breath at rest, or chest pain
    \item Blue lips or face, or a very low oxygen level
    \item Confusion, drowsiness, or difficulty waking
    \item An inability to keep fluids down or signs of dehydration
    \item Symptoms that rapidly worsen
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Assessment determines whether medication is appropriate.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Testing:} rapid antigen or PCR test to confirm COVID-19
    \item \textbf{Risk assessment:} age, medical conditions, immune status, and pregnancy
    \item \textbf{Medicine review:} checking for interactions with existing medicines, especially blood thinners and other drugs
    \item \textbf{Kidney and liver function:} blood tests before some antivirals
    \item \textbf{Oxygen monitoring:} pulse oximetry for people at risk
    \item \textbf{Hospital assessment:} for severe symptoms or high-risk presentations
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Treatment is time-critical and tailored to risk.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Antiviral medicines:} nirmatrelvir--ritonavir or molnupiravir, started within five days of symptoms
    \item \textbf{Self-care:} rest, fluids, and paracetamol for fever and aches
    \item \textbf{Monitoring:} watching for worsening symptoms, with a plan for urgent review
    \item \textbf{Hospital treatment:} oxygen, dexamethasone, and blood thinners for severe disease
    \item \textbf{Other treatments:} some people may be eligible for antibody therapies
    \item \textbf{Isolation:} staying home and away from others while infectious
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} review by a GP or telehealth service during and after the illness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Side effects of antivirals, including nausea, diarrhoea, and a metallic taste
    \item Drug interactions between antivirals and other medicines
    \item Progression to severe illness if treatment is started too late
    \item Long COVID, with persistent symptoms after the acute infection
    \item Blood clots, pneumonia, and organ damage in severe disease
    \item Secondary bacterial infection
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Antiviral treatment substantially reduces the risk of hospitalisation and death in high-risk people when started within five days of symptoms. Most people treated with antivirals recover fully at home. For people who develop severe disease, hospital treatment with oxygen, steroids, and blood thinners has markedly improved survival compared with the early pandemic. Vaccination and early antiviral treatment together have transformed the outlook for most people with COVID-19.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Stay up to date with COVID-19 vaccination
    \item Test promptly if you develop symptoms, especially if you are at higher risk
    \item Seek assessment early if eligible for antiviral treatment; do not wait until symptoms worsen
    \item Take the full course of medicine as prescribed, even if you feel better
    \item Rest, drink fluids, and use paracetamol for fever
    \item Monitor symptoms and have a plan for urgent care
    \item Practise good hygiene and ventilation to reduce spread
\end{itemize}
