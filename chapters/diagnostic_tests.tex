\chapter{Diagnostic Tests}
\index{Diagnostic Tests}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Diagnostic tests are medical investigations used to confirm or rule out disease, assess severity, guide treatment, and monitor progress. They include blood and urine tests, imaging such as X-rays, ultrasound, CT, MRI, and scans, and specialised procedures such as biopsies and endoscopy. Diagnostic tests are a central part of modern medicine, but no test is perfect: results can be false positive or false negative, and tests are most useful when chosen for the right reason. This chapter explains the main types of diagnostic tests, how they are used, and what to expect.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Diagnostic testing is one of the most common activities in healthcare. Millions of pathology tests and imaging examinations are performed in many countries each year, and most people have at least one test annually. Blood tests are the most frequent, followed by imaging. Testing rates have grown over time with new technology, and appropriate use is an important focus of quality care, because unnecessary tests can cause harm through false alarms, further testing, and cost.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pathology tests:} analysis of blood, urine, and other body fluids, including full blood count, biochemistry, hormones, and infection markers
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} X-rays, ultrasound, CT, MRI, and nuclear medicine scans create images of the body's structures and function
    \item \textbf{Biopsy:} removal of a small sample of tissue for microscopic examination, the definitive test for many cancers
    \item \textbf{Endoscopy:} direct visualisation of internal organs using a flexible camera, such as gastroscopy and colonoscopy
    \item \textbf{Functional tests:} assessments of how organs work, such as lung function tests and heart stress tests
    \item \textbf{Screening tests:} used in healthy people to detect early disease, such as bowel, breast, and cervical screening
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Most tests require a doctor's referral and are done at pathology laboratories, imaging practices, or hospitals
    \item Blood tests involve a needle in the arm and take minutes
    \item Imaging tests are painless and take from minutes to an hour, sometimes requiring preparation such as fasting or contrast
    \item Some tests carry small risks, such as radiation exposure from CT or complications of biopsy
    \item Results are reported to the referring doctor, who explains them in context
    \item Tests are interpreted alongside symptoms, examination, and other results
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Screening versus diagnostic testing: screening detects early disease in healthy people; diagnostic tests confirm or exclude suspected disease
    \item Tests with similar purposes, where the choice depends on accuracy, cost, and availability
    \item Confirmatory versus monitoring tests, used at different stages of care
    \item Normal variations and borderline results, which require interpretation in context
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
A doctor will arrange diagnostic tests when they are clinically indicated. Ask your doctor what a test is for, what the results mean, and whether it is necessary. Seek medical care for symptoms, and let the doctor decide which tests are appropriate.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Emergency symptoms such as chest pain, difficulty breathing, severe bleeding, or sudden weakness
    \item Any urgent situation where immediate diagnosis and treatment are needed
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History and examination:} guide which tests are needed
    \item \textbf{Appropriate test selection:} the doctor chooses tests with good accuracy for the suspected condition
    \item \textbf{Preparation:} some tests require fasting, stopping medicines, or other preparation
    \item \textbf{Result interpretation:} tests are interpreted in the context of the whole picture
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} abnormal results may lead to further testing or referral
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pre-test discussion:} the doctor explains the purpose, procedure, risks, and cost of the test
    \item \textbf{Informed consent:} for invasive tests such as biopsy and endoscopy
    \item \textbf{Safe performance:} tests are performed by trained professionals using standard precautions
    \item \textbf{Result communication:} results are discussed with the patient and explained in plain language
    \item \textbf{Action on results:} treatment, monitoring, or further investigation as indicated
    \item \textbf{Record keeping:} results are kept in the health record and inform future care
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item False positive results causing anxiety and unnecessary further testing
    \item False negative results causing missed diagnosis and delay in treatment
    \item Complications of invasive tests, including bleeding, infection, and injury
    \item Radiation exposure from imaging, which is small but cumulative
    \item Over-testing and incidental findings that lead to unnecessary procedures
    \item Cost and time involved in testing
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Diagnostic tests have transformed medicine by allowing early and accurate diagnosis, and they are essential to modern care. Most tests are safe, quick, and reliable when used appropriately. The value of a test lies in whether it changes management and improves outcomes. Discussing the purpose and limitations of tests with your doctor ensures they are used to best effect.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Ask your doctor about the reason for any recommended test
    \item Follow preparation instructions, such as fasting, accurately
    \item Keep a record of your tests and results
    \item Attend recommended screening tests for your age and risk
    \item Report any symptoms or concerns to your doctor
    \item Discuss abnormal results fully before assuming the worst
    \item Maintain a healthy lifestyle to reduce the need for tests
\end{itemize}
