\chapter{Abortion: Surgical and Medical Options}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Abortion is the deliberate ending of a pregnancy before it can survive outside the uterus. This chapter focuses on induced abortion and the two principal methods available in Australia: medical and surgical. Medical abortion uses medicines to end the pregnancy, whereas surgical abortion removes the pregnancy tissue from the uterus by a procedure. Both methods are safe, effective, and well tolerated when provided by trained clinicians, and both are usually completed in the first trimester, within the first 12 weeks of pregnancy. The choice of method depends on gestational age, medical history, access to services, and personal preference, and both options should be discussed openly so that a woman can make an informed decision.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Roughly one in four to one in five pregnancies worldwide ends in induced abortion, and the great majority of these occur in the first trimester. Abortion is one of the most common gynaecological procedures performed in Australia, and both methods are widely used, with medical abortion increasingly preferred in early pregnancy. Women of all ages, backgrounds, and circumstances seek abortion. The most common situations include unplanned pregnancy, contraceptive failure, pregnancy that poses a risk to the woman's health, and the diagnosis of a serious fetal abnormality.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
An induced abortion is chosen for a wide range of personal, social, and medical reasons. The two methods act differently but share the same endpoint: ending the pregnancy and emptying the uterus so that it can return to its normal state.

In a medical abortion, mifepristone blocks the action of progesterone, the hormone that maintains the pregnancy, causing the pregnancy to detach from the uterine lining. A second medicine, misoprostol, is then taken to stimulate uterine contractions that expel the pregnancy tissue. In a surgical abortion, the cervix is gently dilated and a thin suction cannula is passed into the uterus to remove the pregnancy tissue from the lining. The method chosen is guided by gestational age, the woman's health, and local availability of services.

\section*{Clinical Features}
Abortion is a procedure rather than an illness, and most women recover quickly. The expected course is well defined.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Medical abortion:} bleeding like a heavy period with cramping, usually beginning a few hours after the second medicine and continuing for several days
    \item \textbf{Surgical abortion:} the procedure itself takes only minutes, with mild cramping and light bleeding afterwards
    \item Bleeding settles over 1--2 weeks, occasionally followed by light spotting
    \item Nausea, fatigue, and breast tenderness may persist briefly as pregnancy hormones fall
    \item The next menstrual period usually returns within 4--6 weeks
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Before and after an abortion, clinicians distinguish normal recovery from problems that require treatment.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Retained pregnancy tissue versus normal post-abortion bleeding
    \item Endometritis (infection of the uterus) versus normal cramping
    \item Ectopic pregnancy versus an early intrauterine pregnancy
    \item Molar pregnancy versus a normal early pregnancy
    \item Ongoing pregnancy after medical abortion versus a complete abortion
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
The clinic or a doctor should be contacted if bleeding is heavy, pain is severe, fever develops, or any symptom is worrying after an abortion.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Very heavy bleeding that soaks two or more pads an hour for more than two hours
    \item Severe abdominal pain, especially with fainting or collapse
    \item Shoulder-tip pain together with bleeding, which can indicate an ectopic pregnancy
    \item High fever with chills or a foul-smelling vaginal discharge, suggesting infection
    \item Passing large clots along with heavy bleeding
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Assessment before abortion confirms the pregnancy, establishes gestational age, and identifies factors that affect the choice of method.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Confirmation of pregnancy:} a urine or serum pregnancy test
    \item \textbf{Ultrasound:} confirms the pregnancy is within the uterus and determines gestational age
    \item \textbf{Blood group and type:} Rh-negative women may need anti-D immunoglobulin to protect future pregnancies
    \item \textbf{Haemoglobin check:} to identify and treat anaemia
    \item \textbf{Infection screening:} tests for chlamydia, gonorrhoea, and other sexually transmitted infections
    \item \textbf{Counselling and consent:} discussion of both methods, their risks and benefits, and what to expect during and after
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management involves selecting the method, performing it safely, and providing appropriate follow-up.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Medical abortion:} mifepristone followed 24--48 hours later by misoprostol, usually used up to about nine weeks of pregnancy, with a follow-up check to confirm completion
    \item \textbf{Surgical abortion:} suction aspiration, typically under local anaesthesia or light sedation, usually performed in the first trimester
    \item \textbf{Pain management:} paracetamol, anti-inflammatory medicines, and heat packs for cramping
    \item \textbf{Antibiotics:} sometimes administered at the time of the procedure to reduce the risk of infection
    \item \textbf{Contraception:} most women can begin effective contraception immediately, including long-acting reversible methods
    \item \textbf{Follow-up:} review to confirm completion, check recovery, and offer emotional support if needed
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
Complications are uncommon when abortion is performed safely.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Retained pregnancy tissue requiring further treatment, more common after medical abortion
    \item Prolonged or heavy bleeding
    \item Infection of the uterus or pelvis, uncommon with safe provision
    \item Ongoing pregnancy, rare but seen mostly with medical abortion
    \item Perforation of the uterus or injury to the cervix during surgery, which is very rare
    \item Emotional distress or grief in some women
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Both methods are highly effective. Medical abortion is complete in about 9 in 10 women, and surgical abortion is successful in nearly all cases. Serious complications are uncommon, and future fertility is generally not affected. Most women recover fully within days to two weeks. Emotional responses vary widely: many women report relief, while a minority experience sadness or regret and may benefit from counselling and support.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Use reliable contraception consistently to prevent unplanned pregnancy; long-acting reversible methods are the most effective
    \item Use emergency contraception promptly after unprotected sex
    \item After an abortion, rest as needed, use pads rather than tampons, and avoid sexual intercourse and swimming until bleeding has stopped
    \item Take pain relief as advised and report heavy bleeding or fever promptly
    \item Attend the follow-up appointment to confirm that the abortion is complete
    \item Seek emotional support from the clinic, a counsellor, or a trusted service if you are distressed
\end{itemize}
