\chapter{Party drugs}

\section*{Overview}
Party drugs is a medicine or substance used to prevent or treat a health condition. Medicines work in many different ways: they may kill or slow organisms, change how the body's systems work, replace something missing (such as a hormone or vitamin), or relieve symptoms. Using medicines safely means knowing what they are for, how to take them, and what side effects to watch for.

\section*{Symptoms}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Side effects vary by medicine but may include nausea, drowsiness, headache, or upset stomach
    \item Allergic reactions such as rash, swelling, or difficulty breathing
    \item Symptoms of taking too much, which can range from mild to life-threatening
    \item Withdrawal symptoms if certain medicines are stopped suddenly
\end{itemize}

\section*{Causes}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A medicine is prescribed because a doctor has judged that the benefits outweigh the risks
    \item Side effects arise from the medicine acting on healthy tissues as well as the target problem
    \item Interactions can occur with other medicines, food, alcohol, or health conditions
    \item Harm can result from taking the wrong dose, the wrong medicine, or someone else's prescription
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A review of all medicines, including prescription, over-the-counter, herbal, and recreational drugs
    \item Questions about dose, timing, and any side effects
    \item Blood tests to check levels or organ function for some medicines
    \item Monitoring for known side effects
\end{itemize}

\section*{Treatment}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Take the medicine exactly as prescribed, at the right dose and time
    \item Read the Consumer Medicines Information (CMI) leaflet for each medicine
    \item Tell the doctor about all other medicines and health problems
    \item Do not stop or change a prescribed medicine without medical advice
    \item Store medicines out of reach of children and dispose of expired medicines safely
    \item Seek advice from a pharmacist or doctor if side effects occur
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prevention}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Keep an up-to-date list of all medicines and bring it to appointments
    \item Use one pharmacy where possible so interactions can be checked
    \item Never share prescription medicines
    \item Avoid alcohol if the medicine warns against it
    \item Ask about alternatives if a medicine causes trouble
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
\textbf{Call 000} for signs of a severe reaction such as difficulty breathing, swelling of the face or throat, or collapse. Otherwise seek urgent advice if:
\begin{itemize}
    \item You think too much medicine has been taken (call the Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26 in Australia)
    \item You develop a rash, yellowing of the skin or eyes, or unusual bleeding
    \item You are concerned about side effects that are severe or persistent
    \item You are pregnant, breastfeeding, or planning pregnancy and are unsure whether a medicine is safe
\end{itemize}
