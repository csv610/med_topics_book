\chapter{STI pregnancy}
\index{STI pregnancy}
\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational draft; seek professional advice for individual care.}

\section*{Definition and scope}
STI pregnancy concerns reproductive, sexual, or pregnancy-related health. Causes may be hormonal, infectious, structural, medication-related, or pregnancy-specific.

\section*{Contributors and risk}
Consider age, cycle or pregnancy stage, sexual exposure, contraception, previous procedures, infection, medicines, and chronic disease.

\section*{Presentation}
Record timing, severity, pain, fever, discharge, urinary symptoms, pregnancy possibility, sexual-health history, dizziness, and fainting while respecting privacy and consent.

\section*{Assessment}
Assessment may include pregnancy testing, examination with consent, STI tests, blood tests, ultrasound, or specialist review.

\section*{Management}
Treatment depends on cause and may include reassurance, symptom relief, antimicrobials, hormonal treatment, procedures, or referral.

\section*{When to seek urgent care}
Seek urgent care for severe or one-sided pain, heavy bleeding, fainting, fever with pelvic pain, suspected ectopic pregnancy, testicular torsion, or acute pregnancy concerns.

\section*{Prevention and follow-up}
Use safer-sex practices, appropriate contraception, recommended screening, vaccination, and prompt review of persistent symptoms.
\section*{Safety-netting}
Seek urgent help for severe or rapidly worsening symptoms, collapse, breathing difficulty, severe pain, confusion, dehydration, uncontrolled bleeding, or any immediate danger.
section*{Sources and limitations}
This concise educational overview should be checked against current local clinical guidance and adapted to the individual.
