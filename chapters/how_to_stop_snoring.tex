\chapter{How to Stop Snoring}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Snoring is the sound made when air flows past relaxed tissues in the throat during sleep, causing them to vibrate. It is very common and often harmless, but it can disrupt the snorer's and the partner's sleep and can be a sign of obstructive sleep apnoea, a serious condition in which breathing repeatedly stops during sleep. Stopping snoring involves identifying the cause: weight loss, sleeping position, avoiding alcohol, and treating nasal congestion help many people, while mouthpieces, CPAP, and surgery are options for persistent or severe cases. A sleep assessment distinguishes simple snoring from sleep apnoea.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Around 40\% of adults snore, with higher rates in men, in people who are overweight, and with increasing age. Snoring becomes more common in women after menopause. Around one in four men and one in ten women have obstructive sleep apnoea, and snoring is its most common symptom. Many snorers are unaware of the problem, and partners often raise it first. Untreated sleep apnoea increases the risk of high blood pressure, heart disease, stroke, and daytime sleepiness-related accidents.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Narrow airway:} obesity, large tonsils, a thick neck, and anatomical features narrow the throat
    \item \textbf{Muscle relaxation:} alcohol, sedatives, and deep sleep relax the throat muscles
    \item \textbf{Sleeping position:} sleeping on the back lets the tongue and soft palate fall backwards
    \item \textbf{Nasal obstruction:} allergies, colds, and a deviated septum force mouth breathing
    \item \textbf{Pathophysiology:} airflow through a narrowed, relaxed airway becomes turbulent and vibrates the soft palate, uvula, and throat walls
    \item \textbf{Obstructive sleep apnoea:} the airway closes completely for 10 seconds or more, repeatedly, causing oxygen drops and arousal from sleep
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Loud, habitual snoring, often worse on the back and after alcohol
    \item Gasping, choking, or pauses in breathing during sleep
    \item Excessive daytime sleepiness and unrefreshing sleep
    \item Morning headache, dry mouth, and sore throat
    \item Poor concentration, irritability, and mood changes
    \item A partner's report of breathing pauses is important
    \item Children who snore may have large tonsils and behavioural problems
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Simple snoring without apnoea needs no treatment beyond lifestyle measures
    \item Obstructive sleep apnoea requires diagnosis and treatment
    \item Other sleep disorders and chronic fatigue cause daytime sleepiness
    \item Allergic rhinitis and sinusitis cause nasal obstruction
    \item A sleep study distinguishes these conditions
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor for loud snoring with witnessed pauses in breathing, excessive daytime sleepiness, or cardiovascular risk factors, and for children who snore. A referral for a sleep study may be arranged.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden severe breathlessness or chest pain
    \item Collapse, loss of consciousness, or choking
    \item Falling asleep while driving or a serious accident from sleepiness
    \item Signs of stroke or heart attack
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History:} snoring pattern, witnessed pauses, sleepiness, and risk factors
    \item \textbf{Examination:} weight, neck circumference, and upper airway anatomy
    \item \textbf{Questionnaires:} tools such as the Epworth Sleepiness Scale
    \item \textbf{Sleep study (polysomnography):} the definitive test for sleep apnoea, in-lab or at home
    \item \textbf{BLOOD pressure and cardiovascular assessment:} because sleep apnoea is linked to these conditions
    \item \textbf{ENT assessment:} for nasal and airway obstruction
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Weight loss:} the most effective long-term measure for overweight snorers
    \item \textbf{Sleep position:} side sleeping and positional aids reduce supine snoring
    \item \textbf{Alcohol and sedatives:} avoid before bed
    \item \textbf{Nasal treatment:} decongestants, allergy treatment, and nasal strips
    \item \textbf{Oral appliances:} mandibular advancement devices hold the jaw forward, effective for mild to moderate cases
    \item \textbf{CPAP:} continuous positive airway pressure, the standard treatment for sleep apnoea
    \item \textbf{Surgery:} tonsillectomy, palate procedures, or septoplasty for selected anatomical problems
    \item \textbf{Children:} treatment of tonsils and adenoids
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Relationship strain from disturbed sleep
    \item Fatigue and reduced daytime performance
    \item Obstructive sleep apnoea: high blood pressure, heart disease, stroke, and diabetes risk
    \item Motor vehicle and workplace accidents from sleepiness
    \item Mood disturbance and depression
    \item Reduced quality of life
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Simple snoring responds well to lifestyle measures, with weight loss and positional change helping most people. Sleep apnoea is highly treatable: CPAP eliminates apnoeas and restores normal sleep, and oral appliances help many people. Untreated, apnoea carries significant health risks, so assessment matters. With the right combination of measures, most snorers and their partners achieve quiet, restful nights and better health.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Maintain a healthy weight
    \item Sleep on your side rather than your back
    \item Avoid alcohol and sedatives in the evening
    \item Treat nasal congestion and allergies
    \item Establish a regular sleep routine
    \item Stop smoking, which worsens airway inflammation
    \item Ask a partner to report breathing pauses and loud snoring
    \item Seek a sleep assessment if snoring is loud, habitual, or with sleepiness
\end{itemize}
