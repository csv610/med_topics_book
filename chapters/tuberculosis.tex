\chapter{Tuberculosis}

\section{Overview}
Tuberculosis (TB) is a potentially serious infectious disease that mainly affects the lungs. Caused by the bacterium Mycobacterium tuberculosis, TB spreads through the air when people with lung tuberculosis cough, sneeze, or otherwise transmit their germs. Not everyone infected with TB bacteria becomes sick; those with dormant, inactive TB have no symptoms and cannot spread the disease. When the immune system fails to contain the infection, however, it becomes active and can spread to other parts of the body.

\section{Symptoms}
Symptoms of active tuberculosis include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Persistent cough lasting three weeks or longer
    \item Chest pain
    \item Coughing up blood or sputum
    \item Unintentional weight loss
    \item Fever and night sweats
    \item Chills and fatigue
    \item Loss of appetite
    \item Shortness of breath
\end{itemize}

Extra-pulmonary TB symptoms vary depending on the affected organ and may include lymph node swelling, back pain, kidney dysfunction, or neurological symptoms.

\section{Causes}
TB is caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis bacteria. These organisms are spread through the air from one person to another. When a person with active TB disease of the lungs or throat coughs or sneezes, they project millions of tiny droplets containing the TB bacteria into the air. Only a few of these germs are needed to cause a new infection. TB cannot be spread by shaking hands, sharing food, touching bed linens, or kissing.

Risk factors include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Weakened immune system (HIV/AIDS, cancer treatment, diabetes)
    \item Close contact with someone with active TB
    \item Living in or traveling to areas with high TB rates
    \item Substance abuse and alcoholism
    \item Living or working in congregate settings (prisons, homeless shelters)
    \item Young age or advanced age
\end{itemize}

\section{Diagnosis}
Diagnosis involves several tests:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Skin test (Mantoux tuberculin skin test):} A small amount of fluid is injected under the skin; induration is measured after 48-72 hours
    \item \textbf{Blood tests (IGRAs):} Interferon-gamma release assays measure immune response to TB bacteria
    \item \textbf{Chest X-ray:} Can reveal characteristic lung changes
    \item \textbf{Sputum tests:} Microscopic examination and culture of sputum samples
    \item \textbf{Biopsy:} Tissue sampling for suspected extrapulmonary TB
\end{itemize}

\section{Treatment}
Active TB is treated with a combination of antibiotics taken for 6-9 months:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Isoniazid
    \item Rifampin
    \item Ethambutol
    \item Pyrazinamide
\end{itemize}

Patients must complete the full course even if they feel better. Drug-resistant TB requires longer treatment with different combinations. Latent TB infection may be treated with shorter courses of single or dual antibiotics.

\section{Prevention}
Prevention strategies include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item BCG vaccination in high-prevalence countries
    \item Treating latent TB infection to prevent progression
    \item Respiratory isolation for infectious patients
    \item Proper ventilation in healthcare settings
    \item Screening high-risk populations
    \item Wearing N95 respirators in high-risk environments
\end{itemize}

\section{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek immediate medical attention if you have:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A cough lasting more than three weeks
    \item Coughing up blood
    \item Unexplained fever and night sweats with weight loss
    \item Chest pain that worsens with breathing or coughing
    \item Exposure to someone with active TB disease
\end{itemize}

Early diagnosis and treatment prevent complications and transmission to others.
