\chapter{Osteoporosis Medicines}

\section*{Overview}
Pharmacological treatments for osteoporosis aim to increase bone density, reduce bone resorption, or stimulate bone formation. The choice of medication depends on fracture risk, age, sex, and individual patient factors.

\section*{Causes}
Indicated for patients with osteoporosis (T-score $\leq$ -2.5), osteopenia with high fracture risk, or after osteoporotic fracture.

\section*{Diagnosis}
Treatment decisions are guided by DEXA scan results, FRAX fracture risk assessment, clinical fracture history, and bone turnover markers.

\section*{Treatment}
First-line treatments include oral bisphosphonates (alendronate, risedronate, ibandronate). Intravenous bisphosphonates (zoledronic acid) are alternatives. Denosumab is a monoclonal antibody given every 6 months. Anabolic agents (teriparatide, romosozumab) stimulate bone formation. Selective estrogen receptor modulators (raloxifene) may be used in postmenopausal women.

\section*{Prevention}
Prevent osteoporosis and fractures through adequate calcium and vitamin D intake, weight-bearing exercise, fall prevention, and appropriate pharmacological therapy when indicated.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Report severe bone, joint, or muscle pain; difficulty swallowing; chest pain; or jaw pain to your healthcare provider. Dental evaluation is recommended before starting bisphosphonate therapy.
