\section*{Definition and Overview}
A nursing post is a nursing position or station where nurses receive patients, perform assessment, coordinate care, document clinical information, and communicate with the wider health-care team. In some settings the term may refer to a nurse's work area rather than a separate clinical service.

\section*{Functions}
A nursing post supports patient observation, medication and treatment processes, clinical handover, escalation of deterioration, admission and discharge work, and communication with patients, carers, doctors, and allied-health professionals.

\section*{Safe Practice}
Work should follow local policies, scope-of-practice requirements, infection-prevention procedures, privacy rules, medication checks, and structured handover. Staffing, equipment, and escalation pathways should be clear.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Patients should tell a nurse about new pain, breathlessness, fever, confusion, bleeding, or worsening symptoms. Call 000 for collapse, severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, major bleeding, or other life-threatening emergencies.

\section*{Quality and Documentation}
Accurate observations, medication records, care plans, incident reports, and handover reduce communication failures. Nurses should escalate concerns promptly and seek supervision when a task exceeds their competence or authority.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
Use hand hygiene and standard precautions, maintain professional boundaries, protect confidential information, take breaks where possible, and report unsafe staffing, equipment, violence, or fatigue-related risks.
