\chapter{Gynaecologist}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
A gynaecologist is a medical specialist who cares for women's reproductive health, including the uterus, ovaries, fallopian tubes, cervix, vagina, and vulva. Gynaecologists diagnose and treat conditions such as menstrual problems, pelvic pain, endometriosis, fibroids, ovarian cysts, pelvic organ prolapse, incontinence, and gynaecological cancers, and they perform procedures including hysteroscopy, laparoscopy, and hysterectomy. Many gynaecologists also have training in obstetrics and provide care during pregnancy and childbirth. Women are usually referred to a gynaecologist by their GP when they have a condition that needs specialist assessment or treatment, and referral is often arranged promptly for concerning symptoms.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Gynaecological conditions are common and affect women across their lifespan. Around 1 in 10 women of reproductive age has endometriosis, up to 1 in 3 has heavy menstrual bleeding at some stage, and around 1 in 4 women over 40 has uterine fibroids requiring consideration. Gynaecological cancers, including cervical, ovarian, uterine, and vulvar cancers, affect thousands of Australian women each year, and cervical cancer is now largely preventable through HPV vaccination and screening. Many women will see a gynaecologist at some point, particularly for menstrual disorders, pelvic pain, fertility concerns, and menopausal management.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
This chapter concerns the role of a medical specialist. The conditions a gynaecologist manages include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Menstrual disorders:} heavy, painful, irregular, or absent periods
    \item \textbf{Pelvic pain:} including endometriosis, adenomyosis, and chronic pelvic pain syndromes
    \item \textbf{Fibroids and polyps:} benign growths in the uterus
    \item \textbf{Ovarian conditions:} cysts, polycystic ovary syndrome, and ovarian cancer
    \item \textbf{Infections:} pelvic inflammatory disease and vulvovaginal infections
    \item \textbf{Prolapse and incontinence:} pelvic organ prolapse and urinary incontinence
    \item \textbf{Gynaecological cancers:} cervical, uterine, ovarian, vulvar, and vaginal cancer
    \item \textbf{Fertility problems:} investigation and treatment of subfertility
    \item \textbf{Menopause:} management of menopausal symptoms and hormone therapy
    \item \textbf{Contraception:} including long-acting methods and sterilisation
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Women are usually referred to a gynaecologist for specific symptoms or findings.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Heavy, painful, or irregular periods
    \item Pelvic pain or pain during sex
    \item Abnormal vaginal bleeding, including bleeding after menopause
    \item A pelvic mass or abnormal finding on imaging
    \item Recurrent pelvic infections
    \item Vaginal prolapse or incontinence
    \item Difficulty conceiving
    \item Abnormal cervical screening results
    \item Menopausal symptoms that are difficult to manage
    \item Endometriosis symptoms, including painful periods and pelvic pain
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Gynaecological symptoms overlap with many other conditions.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Urinary tract conditions, including infections, kidney stones, and interstitial cystitis
    \item Gastrointestinal conditions, including irritable bowel syndrome and constipation
    \item Musculoskeletal pelvic pain
    \item Pregnancy-related conditions, including ectopic pregnancy
    \item Skin conditions of the vulva
    \item Endocrine conditions affecting menstruation, including thyroid and prolactin disorders
    \item Non-gynaecological causes of pelvic masses
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Women should see their GP for any concerning gynaecological symptoms, including abnormal bleeding, pelvic pain, or a change in periods. The GP can investigate, manage many conditions, and refer to a gynaecologist when needed. Women should attend routine cervical screening from age 25.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Very heavy vaginal bleeding, especially with faintness or dizziness
    \item Sudden severe pelvic or abdominal pain
    \item Bleeding in pregnancy with pain or collapse, which may indicate ectopic pregnancy
    \item Fever with pelvic pain, which may indicate pelvic infection
    \item Sudden shoulder-tip pain with vaginal bleeding and a positive pregnancy test
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Gynaecologists use a range of investigations to reach a diagnosis.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History and examination:} including pelvic examination and speculum examination
    \item \textbf{Pelvic ultrasound:} transvaginal and transabdominal imaging of the uterus, ovaries, and pelvis
    \item \textbf{Hysteroscopy:} visual inspection of the inside of the uterus
    \item \textbf{Laparoscopy:} keyhole surgery to inspect the pelvis, including the diagnosis of endometriosis
    \item \textbf{Biopsy:} cervical, endometrial, or vulvar sampling
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} hormone levels, tumour markers, and pregnancy testing
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} MRI and CT for complex conditions and cancer staging
    \item \textbf{Specialist referral:} to gynaecological oncology or reproductive medicine where appropriate
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Gynaecologists provide medical, surgical, and procedural treatments.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Medical treatment:} hormonal contraceptives, progesterone, hormone therapy, and medicines for pain, bleeding, and endometriosis
    \item \textbf{Minor procedures:} hysteroscopy, endometrial ablation, polypectomy, and removal of cysts
    \item \textbf{Surgery:} laparoscopy, myomectomy, hysterectomy, and surgery for prolapse and incontinence
    \item \textbf{Cancer treatment:} surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy for gynaecological cancers, coordinated with oncology teams
    \item \textbf{Fertility treatment:} investigation and medical and surgical management of subfertility
    \item \textbf{Menopause management:} hormone therapy and non-hormonal options
    \item \textbf{Colposcopy:} assessment of abnormal cervical screening results
    \item \textbf{Long-term follow-up:} monitoring of chronic conditions and post-treatment surveillance
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Delayed diagnosis of gynaecological cancer if symptoms are not investigated
    \item Chronic pain and reduced quality of life from conditions such as endometriosis
    \item Infertility from untreated pelvic infection or endometriosis
    \item Complications of surgery, including bleeding, infection, and injury to surrounding organs
    \item Side effects of hormonal treatments
    \item Effects of hysterectomy, including surgical menopause and psychological effects
    \item Recurrence of conditions such as fibroids and endometriosis after treatment
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Most gynaecological conditions respond well to modern treatment. Menstrual disorders are controlled effectively with medical or surgical treatment, endometriosis is manageable with a range of therapies, and gynaecological cancers have much better outcomes when detected early. Cervical screening and HPV vaccination have dramatically reduced cervical cancer rates. Fertility treatment helps many couples conceive, and menopausal management restores quality of life for most women. The outlook for most women under gynaecological care is excellent.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Attend cervical screening every five years from age 25
    \item Have the HPV vaccine as recommended
    \item Use contraception as needed to plan pregnancy and prevent STIs
    \item Practise safe sex to prevent infections that affect the pelvis and fertility
    \item See a doctor for abnormal bleeding, pain, or any change in periods
    \item Maintain a healthy weight, exercise, and do not smoke
    \item Discuss menopause symptoms and treatment options with your doctor
    \item Attend recommended imaging and follow-up after treatment
    \item Know the symptoms of gynaecological cancer, including postmenopausal bleeding and unusual discharge
\end{itemize}
