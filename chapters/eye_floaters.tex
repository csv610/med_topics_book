\chapter{Eye Floaters}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Eye floaters are small spots, specks, or thread-like shapes that drift across the field of vision. They are caused by clumps of gel inside the eye casting shadows on the retina. Most floaters are a normal part of ageing and are harmless, but a sudden increase in floaters, especially with flashes of light or a shadow in the vision, can signal a serious problem such as a retinal tear or detachment, which requires urgent treatment.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Floaters are extremely common and become more so with age, as the jelly inside the eye (the vitreous) shrinks and liquefies. They are more common in people who are short-sighted, after eye surgery, and after eye injury. Most people notice occasional floaters, and they are usually harmless, but retinal detachment, the serious complication, is an emergency that needs treatment within hours.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Floaters develop when the vitreous gel changes.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Normal ageing:} the vitreous gel shrinks and pulls away from the retina, and clumps of gel cast shadows, seen as floaters
    \item \textbf{Posterior vitreous detachment:} the common age-related separation of the gel from the retina, which causes new floaters and flashes
    \item \textbf{Retinal tear:} when the gel pulls away, it can tear the retina, causing a sudden shower of floaters and flashes
    \item \textbf{Retinal detachment:} fluid seeps through a tear and lifts the retina off the back of the eye, causing a curtain or shadow in the vision
    \item \textbf{Other causes:} bleeding inside the eye, inflammation, and eye injury
    \item \textbf{The shadows:} floaters move with eye movement and appear more visible against bright backgrounds
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
The features of floaters help distinguish harmless from serious.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Small spots, dots, or lines that drift in the vision
    \item Floaters that move when the eye moves and float when the eye stops
    \item Most noticeable against bright, plain backgrounds
    \item A sudden increase in the number of floaters
    \item Flashes of light, usually at the edge of the vision
    \item A shadow or curtain spreading across the vision
    \item Loss of vision in part of the eye
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Other conditions can be confused with floaters.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Migraine aura, which causes shimmering shapes and flashes
    \item Bleeding in the eye from diabetes or other causes
    \item Retinal detachment, the serious emergency
    \item Uveitis, inflammation inside the eye
    \item Corneal and lens problems affecting vision
    \item A visual field loss from stroke
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
New floaters that are occasional and stable can be mentioned at the next eye check. A sudden shower of floaters, flashes, or a shadow in the vision requires an urgent eye examination, because a retinal tear must be treated quickly.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden loss of vision
    \item A shadow or curtain spreading across the vision
    \item A sudden shower of floaters with flashes of light
    \item Sudden severe eye pain
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
A dilated eye examination is essential.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dilated fundus examination:} the pupils are dilated and the retina is examined
    \item \textbf{Ultrasound:} when the retina cannot be seen clearly
    \item \textbf{OCT:} detailed imaging of the retina
    \item \textbf{Assessment of vision:} visual field and acuity checks
    \item \textbf{Urgency:} a suspected retinal tear or detachment is seen the same day
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management depends on the findings.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Harmless floaters:} no treatment is needed; they usually settle and the brain learns to ignore them
    \item \textbf{Posterior vitreous detachment:} monitoring, with urgent review if new symptoms develop
    \item \textbf{Retinal tear:} laser treatment to seal the tear and prevent detachment
    \item \textbf{Retinal detachment:} urgent surgery to reattach the retina
    \item \textbf{Treating the cause:} management of bleeding or inflammation if these are the cause
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Retinal detachment, which causes permanent vision loss if untreated
    \item Bleeding inside the eye
    \item Persistent, distracting floaters that affect quality of life
    \item The anxiety of not knowing whether floaters are serious
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Most floaters are harmless and fade or become less noticeable over months. Retinal tears, when treated early, almost always prevent detachment, and retinal detachment surgery is successful in most cases when performed promptly. The key is seeking urgent review for sudden changes.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Know the warning signs: a sudden increase in floaters, flashes, or a shadow in the vision
    \item Seek urgent review for any sudden change
    \item Attend regular eye examinations, especially with age or short-sightedness
    \item Protect the eyes from injury
    \item Do not rub the eyes forcefully
    \item Manage diabetes and blood pressure to protect the retina
\end{itemize}
