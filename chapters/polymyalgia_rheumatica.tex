\chapter{Polymyalgia Rheumatica}
\index{Polymyalgia Rheumatica}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. Diagnosis and steroid treatment require clinical supervision.}

\section*{What it is}
Polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR) is an inflammatory condition that usually causes pain and marked morning stiffness around the shoulders, neck, and hips. It is uncommon before age 50 and is more frequent with increasing age. The cause is not fully understood.

\section*{Symptoms and diagnosis}
Stiffness often lasts longer than 45 minutes after waking and may make dressing, rising, or lifting difficult. Tiredness, low appetite, low mood, mild fever, or weight loss can occur. There is no single diagnostic test. A clinician uses the pattern of symptoms and examination, blood tests such as inflammatory markers, and tests for other causes such as rheumatoid arthritis or infection.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek urgent care for severe or rapidly worsening symptoms. PMR can occur with giant cell arteritis. Seek same-day urgent medical care for a new severe headache, scalp tenderness, jaw or tongue pain when chewing, double vision, or any loss of vision. These symptoms can threaten sight and need rapid treatment.

\section*{Treatment and monitoring}
Prednisolone is the usual initial treatment. Symptoms often improve within days, but treatment commonly continues for many months and the dose is reduced gradually under supervision. Do not stop a steroid suddenly. Long-term steroids can increase the risk of infection, osteoporosis, high blood pressure, diabetes, eye problems, and other adverse effects, so follow-up, bone protection, vaccinations, and monitoring may be advised. Other medicines may be considered when relapses or steroid-related problems occur.

\section*{Self-care and outlook}
Keep follow-up appointments and report a return of stiffness, new symptoms, or medicine adverse effects. Gentle activity and strength exercises can help maintain function when safe. Recovery varies; relapses are possible, and persistent symptoms should be reassessed rather than treated by changing the steroid dose independently.
