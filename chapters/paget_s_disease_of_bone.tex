\chapter{Paget's Disease of Bone}
\index{Paget's Disease of Bone}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. Bone pain, deformity, or unexplained fractures should be assessed.}

\section*{What it is}
Paget's disease of bone is a chronic disorder of bone remodelling. Affected bone is broken down and rebuilt too quickly, which can make it enlarged, misshapen, weaker, or more painful. It often affects only one or a few bones and may cause no symptoms.

\section*{Symptoms and complications}
Possible features include bone pain, arthritis near an affected bone, bowed legs, increased head size, headaches, hearing loss, or pressure on nerves in the skull or spine. Complications can include fractures, nerve problems, joint damage, and rarely heart strain or bone cancer.

\section*{Diagnosis}
Assessment may include examination, alkaline phosphatase and other blood tests, X-rays, and a bone scan. The pattern can help distinguish Paget's disease from arthritis, metastases, fracture, and other bone disorders. A specialist may be needed when the diagnosis is uncertain or the skull, spine, or weight-bearing bones are involved.

\section*{Treatment and self-care}
Some people need monitoring only. Bisphosphonates can reduce abnormal bone activity and pain in selected cases; kidney function, dental health, and other medicines must be considered. Surgery may be needed for a fracture, severe arthritis, deformity, or nerve compression. Maintain safe activity and adequate calcium and vitamin D according to individual advice; exercise should not overload fragile or painful bone.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek urgent care after a suspected fracture, sudden weakness or loss of sensation, new difficulty walking, severe headache with neurological symptoms, or sudden hearing loss. Arrange review for persistent bone pain, increasing deformity, or new hearing problems.
