\chapter{Pilonidal Sinus Disease}
\index{Pilonidal Sinus Disease}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. A painful or infected lump near the cleft of the buttocks needs medical assessment.}

\section*{What it is}
A pilonidal sinus is a small tunnel or opening in the skin, usually in the cleft between the buttocks near the tailbone. Hair and skin debris can become trapped and cause inflammation or an abscess. Some sinuses cause no symptoms.

\section*{Symptoms}
An infected sinus may cause a painful, swollen lump, redness, warmth, bleeding, or pus and other discharge. Pain can develop quickly and make sitting difficult. Recurrent disease may cause intermittent drainage.

\section*{Diagnosis and treatment}
Diagnosis is usually made by examining the area. An uninfected sinus may be observed. A painful abscess commonly needs an incision and drainage; antibiotics may be used when infection has spread or when clinically indicated. Recurrent or persistent disease may be treated with a planned procedure, with the choice depending on the sinus and local surgical expertise.

\section*{Self-care and prevention}
Keep the cleft clean and dry, avoid squeezing the lump, and follow wound-care instructions after drainage. Avoid shaving or hair-removal treatments unless advised, because they may irritate the skin. Reduce prolonged pressure where practical and seek advice if the problem keeps returning.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Arrange prompt care for a painful, red, swollen lump or pus, bleeding, or fever. Seek urgent help for rapidly spreading redness, severe pain, confusion, fainting, or feeling seriously unwell.
