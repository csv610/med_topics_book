\noindent\textit{This chapter is for general education. The title, registration, and services of foot-care professionals vary by country.}

\section*{What a podiatrist does}
A podiatrist or other regulated foot-care professional assesses and treats problems affecting the feet, ankles, and sometimes lower limbs. They may help with pain, corns and calluses, nail disorders, skin infections, gait, sports injuries, and foot complications of diabetes or reduced circulation.

\section*{Assessment and treatment}
Assessment may include skin and nail examination, pulses, sensation, strength, movement, footwear, walking pattern, and medical history. Treatment may include education, safe nail or skin care, footwear and orthotic advice, exercises, wound care, or referral to another clinician. Services and procedures differ by training and local regulation.

\section*{Foot care at home}
Wash and dry feet, inspect them regularly, use suitable footwear, and do not cut corns or calluses with blades. People with diabetes, poor circulation, or reduced sensation should ask their care team before self-treating a wound, blister, nail problem, or callus.

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Seek urgent care for a cold, pale, blue, or black foot; a rapidly spreading infection; severe pain; fever; a deep wound; or sudden loss of sensation or movement. Arrange review for persistent pain, recurrent wounds, unexplained swelling, or a change in walking.
