\chapter{How to Use an Inhaler Device}
\index{How to Use an Inhaler Device}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Inhaler devices deliver medication directly to the lungs, where it is needed most for conditions such as asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). There are several types of inhalers, including pressurised metered-dose inhalers (pMDIs), dry powder inhalers (DPIs), soft mist inhalers, and spacers, which are used with pMDIs to improve drug delivery. Using an inhaler correctly is essential: poor technique means less medication reaches the lungs, leading to uncontrolled symptoms even with prescribed treatment. This chapter explains how each type of inhaler is used, common mistakes, and how to get the most benefit from inhaler treatment.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Asthma affects around 2.7 million people, and COPD affects around one in 13 adults over 40. Most of these people use inhalers daily or as needed. Studies consistently show that up to 70--90\% of people use their inhaler incorrectly, and poor technique is associated with worse symptom control, more reliever use, and more hospital admissions. Pharmacists, GPs, and asthma educators can check and correct inhaler technique, and this simple intervention significantly improves outcomes.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{pMDI (puffer):} pressurised canister that delivers a fine aerosol; requires slow, coordinated inhalation
    \item \textbf{Dry powder inhaler (DPI):} breath-activated device; requires a fast, forceful inhalation
    \item \textbf{Soft mist inhaler:} slow-moving mist; requires slow, deep inhalation
    \item \textbf{Spacer:} a chamber attached to a pMDI that holds the aerosol, so coordination is less important
    \item \textbf{Pathophysiology:} the medication must reach the small airways; larger particles deposited in the mouth and throat are wasted and can cause side effects
    \item \textbf{Common errors:} not shaking the pMDI, poor coordination of press and inhale, stopping inhalation at the puff, not holding the breath, and not cleaning the device
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Preventer inhalers (usually brown or orange) are used daily to reduce inflammation
    \item Reliever inhalers (usually blue) give rapid relief of symptoms
    \item Combination inhalers contain both preventer and long-acting reliever
    \item Good technique: slow steady inhalation for pMDIs, fast deep inhalation for DPIs, and a 10-second breath hold
    \item Signs of poor technique: the medication visible in the mouth, gurgling, coughing during use, or poor symptom control
    \item Rinsing the mouth after steroid inhalers prevents oral thrush
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Different inhalers require different techniques; using the wrong technique for the device is the commonest problem
    \item A spacer should be used with pMDIs, especially in children and during acute attacks
    \item Rinse the mouth after using steroid-containing inhalers
    \item Poor symptom control may be due to incorrect technique, undertreatment, or a diagnosis that needs review
    \item Device choice is individualised by age, dexterity, and preference
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor or pharmacist if symptoms are not controlled, if you are unsure about technique, or for a regular asthma or COPD review. During an acute attack, use the reliever and follow the written action plan.

\textbf{Contact your local emergency services for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe breathlessness with an inability to speak in sentences
    \item No improvement after using the reliever
    \item Lips turning blue, confusion, or drowsiness
    \item Chest pain or collapse
    \item Any severe asthma attack not responding to treatment
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Technique review:} demonstrate your inhaler use to a health professional
    \item \textbf{Device matching:} ensure the device suits your abilities and preference
    \item \textbf{Spacer use:} pMDI users, especially children, should use a spacer
    \item \textbf{Action plan:} a written asthma or COPD action plan guides daily and emergency treatment
    \item \textbf{Lung function tests:} spirometry and peak flow monitoring measure control
    \item \textbf{Adherence review:} check that medication is taken as prescribed
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{pMDI technique:} shake, breathe out, place the mouthpiece, press once while breathing in slowly, hold the breath for 10 seconds
    \item \textbf{DPI technique:} load the dose, breathe out away from the device, inhale fast and deeply, hold the breath
    \item \textbf{Spacer technique:} attach the spacer, one press per puff, breathe in and out normally, or slow tidal breathing for young children
    \item \textbf{Cleaning:} rinse and dry pMDIs and spacers regularly
    \item \textbf{Rinsing the mouth:} after steroid inhalers, to prevent thrush and hoarseness
    \item \textbf{Regular review:} technique and treatment are rechecked at every asthma or COPD review
    \item \textbf{Education:} pharmacists and asthma educators provide practical training
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Uncontrolled asthma and COPD from poor drug delivery
    \item Oral thrush and hoarse voice from unrinsed steroid inhalers
    \item Increased reliever use and hospital admissions
    \item Wasted medication and cost
    \item Incorrect device use in acute attacks delays effective treatment
    \item Over-reliance on relievers when preventers are not used correctly
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Correct inhaler technique is one of the most effective interventions in respiratory care. People who use their devices properly achieve better symptom control, fewer attacks, less reliever use, and better quality of life. Technique checks and education improve outcomes in nearly everyone, and the effect is immediate. With the right device, correct technique, and an action plan, most people with asthma and COPD lead full, active lives.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Learn and practise the correct technique for your specific device
    \item Use a spacer with pMDIs, especially for children
    \item Ask your pharmacist or GP to watch you use your inhaler
    \item Rinse your mouth after steroid inhalers
    \item Clean inhalers and spacers regularly
    \item Keep a written action plan and use the reliever early in an attack
    \item Attend regular asthma and COPD reviews
    \item Replace inhalers before they run out
\end{itemize}
