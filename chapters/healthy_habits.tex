\chapter{Healthy Habits}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Healthy habits are the everyday routines and behaviours that protect and improve health, including healthy eating, regular physical activity, adequate sleep, not smoking, limiting alcohol, managing stress, and staying connected with others. Habits are powerful because they run automatically, without requiring willpower or decision-making each time. Building healthy habits involves starting small, linking new behaviours to existing routines, and being consistent. Over time, healthy habits prevent chronic disease, support mental wellbeing, and add years to life. Even people with chronic conditions benefit from the habits that support their management.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Lifestyle-related behaviours are the leading contributors to preventable disease in Australia. Smoking, poor diet, physical inactivity, excess alcohol, and sleep problems together account for a large proportion of deaths from heart disease, stroke, cancer, and diabetes. Around half of Australian adults have at least one chronic condition, and many of these are influenced by habits. People who maintain healthy habits live on average several years longer and spend more of those years in good health.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Habits influence health through biological and behavioural pathways.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Physical activity:} strengthens the heart, muscles, and bones, and improves metabolism and mood
    \item \textbf{Nutrition:} provides nutrients and limits harmful components of the diet
    \item \textbf{Sleep:} restores the body and brain, and supports immunity and metabolism
    \item \textbf{Smoking cessation:} reduces the risk of cancer, heart disease, and lung disease
    \item \textbf{Limited alcohol:} reduces the risk of liver disease, cancer, and injury
    \item \textbf{Stress management:} lowers stress hormones and their effects
    \item \textbf{Social connection:} supports mental health and reduces the risk of disease
    \item \textbf{Habit formation:} repetition creates neural pathways that make behaviours automatic
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Healthy habits are relevant to every aspect of health.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Eating a balanced diet most of the time
    \item Being active most days
    \item Getting adequate, quality sleep
    \item Not smoking and limiting alcohol
    \item Managing stress with relaxation and social support
    \item Attending health checks and screening
    \item Taking medicines as prescribed
    \item Limiting screen time and sedentary behaviour
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Barriers to healthy habits have many causes.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Medical conditions that limit activity, including pain and fatigue
    \item Mental health conditions, including depression, which reduce motivation
    \item Practical barriers, including time, cost, and access
    \item Social and environmental factors, including family and workplace culture
    \item Lack of knowledge about what to change
    \item Previous failed attempts affecting confidence
    \item Readiness to change, which varies between people
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor for support with changing habits, for health checks, or for any health concerns. Health professionals can provide structured programs, referral, and medicines that support change, such as nicotine replacement therapy.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item This chapter concerns behaviour rather than emergencies
    \item Any medical emergency, including chest pain, stroke symptoms, or severe illness
    \item Mental health crises, including thoughts of self-harm
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Building healthy habits starts with understanding your current behaviour.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Self-assessment:} reviewing diet, activity, sleep, smoking, and alcohol
    \item \textbf{Health checks:} blood pressure, weight, cholesterol, and blood glucose
    \item \textbf{Readiness assessment:} how ready you are to change
    \item \textbf{Goal setting:} specific, measurable, achievable goals
    \item \textbf{Professional support:} GPs, dietitians, exercise physiologists, and psychologists
    \item \textbf{Review:} tracking progress and adjusting
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Healthy habits are built with simple, evidence-based strategies.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Start small:} make one small change at a time
    \item \textbf{Anchor new habits:} link new behaviours to existing routines, such as walking after dinner
    \item \textbf{Be consistent:} repeat the behaviour until it becomes automatic
    \item \textbf{Set specific goals:} "walk 20 minutes after lunch" works better than "exercise more"
    \item \textbf{Track progress:} diaries, apps, and checklists make progress visible
    \item \textbf{Build a supportive environment:} keep healthy food visible and temptations out of reach
    \item \textbf{Enlist support:} exercise with a friend or share goals with family
    \item \textbf{Anticipate setbacks:} plan how to get back on track
    \item \textbf{Celebrate success:} acknowledge progress
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Unrealistic goals leading to giving up
    \item Trying to change too many things at once
    \item All-or-nothing thinking after a slip
    \item Lack of support or accountability
    \item Conflicting or misleading health information
    \item Health problems from unhealthy habits that persist
    \item Frustration when change is slower than expected
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Healthy habits produce compounding benefits over a lifetime. Regular activity, good nutrition, adequate sleep, and not smoking reduce the risk of heart disease, stroke, cancer, diabetes, and dementia, and improve mental health and quality of life. Habits built early are the most powerful, but change at any age produces measurable benefits. People who adopt healthy habits typically feel better within weeks and gain years of life expectancy.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Choose one small habit to build and be consistent
    \item Move your body most days
    \item Eat a balanced diet and drink water
    \item Protect your sleep with a consistent routine
    \item Do not smoke, and limit alcohol
    \item Manage stress with relaxation and connection
    \item Attend health checks and act on results
    \item Take medicines as prescribed
    \item Seek professional support for change
    \item Be patient and kind to yourself
\end{itemize}
