\chapter{Otitis externa}
\index{Otitis externa}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Educational information; seek professional advice for individual care.}

\section*{What it is}
Otitis externa is inflammation or infection of the ear canal between the outer ear and eardrum. It may follow water exposure, scratching, earbud or hearing-aid irritation, eczema, or infection. Diabetes or a weakened immune system can increase the risk of severe disease.

\section*{Symptoms}
Itching, ear pain, tenderness when the outer ear is moved, watery or pus-like discharge, swelling, flaky skin, and temporary reduced hearing are common. Severe swelling can narrow or close the canal.

\section*{Treatment and self-care}
Treatment may involve prescribed ear drops, cleaning by a clinician, a wick when the canal is very swollen, or tablets when infection has spread or risk is high. Keep the ear dry, avoid swimming while inflamed, and do not put cotton buds, fingers, or unprescribed drops into the canal. Drops must be selected carefully if the eardrum may be perforated.

\section*{When to seek urgent care}
Seek urgent help for severe or deep ear pain, fever, swelling spreading outside the ear, facial weakness, severe headache, confusion, or rapidly worsening illness. Arrange review if symptoms do not improve after treatment, recur frequently, or hearing remains reduced.
