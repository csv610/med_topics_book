\chapter{Digestive System}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
The digestive system is the group of organs that breaks down food into nutrients the body can absorb and use for energy, growth, and repair, and that removes waste. It includes the mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, liver, gallbladder, and pancreas. Digestion involves mechanical breakdown of food, chemical digestion by enzymes and acids, absorption of nutrients, and elimination of waste. The digestive system works with the nervous and hormonal systems to control appetite, digestion, and blood sugar. This chapter explains how the digestive system works and the conditions that can affect it.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Digestive disorders are extremely common and are a leading reason for GP visits and hospital admissions. Conditions include reflux, gastritis, peptic ulcers, gallstones, irritable bowel syndrome, inflammatory bowel disease, coeliac disease, and bowel cancer, which is one of the most common cancers in Australia. Many digestive conditions are linked to diet, infection, genetics, and lifestyle. Digestive health is a major component of overall health and quality of life.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mouth and oesophagus:} teeth break food down, saliva begins digestion, and the oesophagus moves food to the stomach
    \item \textbf{Stomach:} acid and enzymes digest food into a liquid mixture; the stomach lining protects itself from acid
    \item \textbf{Small intestine:} most digestion and absorption of nutrients occurs here
    \item \textbf{Large intestine:} absorbs water and salts and forms stool
    \item \textbf{Liver and gallbladder:} the liver produces bile and processes nutrients; the gallbladder stores bile
    \item \textbf{Pancreas:} produces digestive enzymes and insulin
    \item \textbf{Common problems:} reflux, ulcers, gallstones, infections, inflammation, malabsorption, and cancer
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The digestive system extracts energy and nutrients from food
    \item Gut symptoms include heartburn, indigestion, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, bloating, and altered bowel habit
    \item Healthy digestion produces regular, comfortable bowel motions
    \item The gut contains a large community of bacteria (the microbiome) that support digestion and immunity
    \item Warning symptoms: blood in the stool, unintentional weight loss, difficulty swallowing, and persistent change in bowel habit
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Upper gut disorders (reflux, ulcer, gastritis) versus lower gut disorders (irritable bowel, inflammatory bowel, bowel cancer)
    \item Organic disease versus functional disorders, where symptoms occur without structural abnormality
    \item Malabsorption (coeliac disease, pancreatic insufficiency) versus infection
    \item Gallbladder and pancreatic disease, which can mimic other gut problems
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
See a doctor for persistent or severe digestive symptoms, blood in the stool, weight loss, difficulty swallowing, or a change in bowel habit lasting more than a few weeks. These symptoms can indicate serious conditions and should not be ignored.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sudden, severe abdominal pain
    \item Vomiting blood or passing black, tarry stools
    \item Difficulty swallowing with choking
    \item High fever with abdominal pain
    \item Signs of severe dehydration or collapse
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History and examination:} assessment of symptoms, diet, medicines, and family history
    \item \textbf{Blood tests:} full blood count, liver function, coeliac antibodies, and inflammatory markers
    \item \textbf{Stool tests:} for infection, blood, and inflammation
    \item \textbf{Endoscopy:} gastroscopy and colonoscopy with biopsy
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} ultrasound, CT, and MRI
    \item \textbf{Breath tests:} for lactose intolerance and bacterial overgrowth
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Diet and lifestyle:} a balanced diet, fibre, hydration, and regular activity support digestion
    \item \textbf{Medicines:} antacids, acid suppressants, laxatives, anti-diarrhoeal agents, and treatments for specific conditions
    \item \textbf{Antibiotics:} for gut infections
    \item \textbf{Treatment of specific conditions:} gluten-free diet for coeliac disease, anti-inflammatory treatment for inflammatory bowel disease, and surgery where needed
    \item \textbf{Cancer screening:} bowel cancer screening for eligible age groups
    \item \textbf{Specialist referral:} gastroenterologist care for complex conditions
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Malnutrition and weight loss from malabsorption
    \item Bleeding, perforation, and obstruction from ulcers or other conditions
    \item Chronic pain and reduced quality of life
    \item Bowel cancer, which is common and often treatable when detected early
    \item Complications of untreated gallstones, including pancreatitis
    \item Dehydration from severe diarrhoea or vomiting
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Most digestive symptoms are due to common, manageable conditions, and the outlook is good with appropriate treatment. Functional disorders such as irritable bowel syndrome are chronic but manageable, while inflammatory bowel disease responds to modern treatment. Bowel cancer is highly curable when detected early through screening. Maintaining a healthy diet, regular screening, and seeking early medical advice give the best outcomes for digestive health.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Eat a varied, fibre-rich diet with plenty of vegetables, fruit, and wholegrains
    \item Drink adequate water and limit alcohol
    \item Stay active and maintain a healthy weight
    \item Do not smoke
    \item Practise food safety to prevent gut infections
    \item Participate in bowel cancer screening when eligible
    \item See a doctor promptly for persistent or warning symptoms
\end{itemize}
