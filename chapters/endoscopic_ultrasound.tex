\chapter{Endoscopic Ultrasound}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Endoscopic ultrasound, known as EUS, is a procedure that combines endoscopy with ultrasound imaging. A thin, flexible tube with a small ultrasound probe at its tip is passed through the mouth into the stomach and duodenum, allowing doctors to see the walls of the digestive tract and the organs and structures around them in fine detail. Endoscopic ultrasound is used to investigate and diagnose conditions of the pancreas, bile ducts, and surrounding tissues, to assess tumours, and to take tissue samples (biopsies). It is performed under sedation and is generally safe.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Endoscopic ultrasound is a specialised procedure performed in major hospitals for selected patients. It is most commonly used to investigate pancreatic and bile duct problems, to stage cancers of the oesophagus, stomach, and pancreas, and to assess suspicious findings on other scans. It is an important tool in the diagnosis and staging of pancreatic cancer and other digestive system cancers.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Endoscopic ultrasound provides images that other tests cannot easily obtain.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The procedure:} an endoscope with an ultrasound probe at its tip is passed into the upper digestive tract
    \item \textbf{Imaging:} sound waves produce detailed images of the layers of the bowel wall and nearby organs, including the pancreas, bile ducts, lymph nodes, and liver
    \item \textbf{Why it is used:} the organs behind the stomach and bowel are hard to see with ordinary ultrasound or endoscopy
    \item \textbf{Biopsy:} a fine needle can be passed through the scope to sample suspicious tissue
    \item \textbf{Staging:} the depth of tumour invasion and spread to lymph nodes can be assessed accurately
    \item \textbf{Treatment:} in some cases, drainage of cysts or injections can be performed
\end{itemize}

\section*{Clinical Features}
Endoscopic ultrasound is requested for specific indications.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Investigation of suspected pancreatic disease, including pancreatitis and tumours
    \item Assessment of bile duct stones and obstruction
    \item Staging of oesophageal, stomach, and pancreatic cancers
    \item Investigation of abnormal findings on CT or MRI
    \item Evaluation of enlarged lymph nodes in the chest or abdomen
    \item Sampling of cysts or masses for diagnosis
    \item Unexplained abdominal pain with concerning imaging
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
The procedure is one of several tools for these conditions.
\begin{itemize}
    \item CT, MRI, and standard ultrasound, which provide different information
    \item ERCP, which focuses on the bile and pancreatic ducts
    \item Standard endoscopy, which sees the lining but not the deeper layers
    \item Surgical biopsy, which is more invasive
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Endoscopic ultrasound is arranged by a specialist after other tests. Anyone with persistent abdominal pain, weight loss, jaundice, or other concerning symptoms should be assessed by a doctor.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Severe abdominal pain, especially with vomiting
    \item Black, tarry stools or vomiting blood after the procedure
    \item Fever and chills after the procedure
    \item Difficulty swallowing or breathing
    \item Signs of pancreatitis, including severe upper abdominal pain
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
Undergoing endoscopic ultrasound involves preparation.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Preparation:} fasting for several hours beforehand
    \item \textbf{Sedation:} the procedure is done with sedation, so the person is relaxed and unaware
    \item \textbf{The procedure:} the scope is passed through the mouth; it takes about 30 to 60 minutes
    \item \textbf{Biopsy:} fine-needle aspiration of any suspicious areas
    \item \textbf{Recovery:} observation for a period after the sedation
    \item \textbf{Results:} samples are sent for pathology, and images are reviewed
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
Management follows the results.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Diagnosis:} confirming or excluding the suspected condition
    \item \textbf{Staging:} guiding treatment decisions for cancer
    \item \textbf{Treatment of bile duct stones:} drainage and, in some cases, stone removal
    \item \textbf{Sampling:} tissue diagnosis before surgery or chemotherapy
    \item \textbf{Further care:} referral to surgeons, oncologists, or gastroenterologists
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Sore throat and bloating after the procedure
    \item Effects of sedation
    \item Bleeding, particularly after biopsy
    \item Pancreatitis after procedures involving the pancreas
    \item Perforation of the digestive tract, which is rare
    \item Infection after drainage procedures
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
Endoscopic ultrasound is a safe and highly accurate procedure that has improved the diagnosis and management of pancreatic, bile duct, and digestive tract conditions. It provides information that changes treatment in a significant proportion of patients, and serious complications are uncommon when the procedure is performed by experienced specialists.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Follow the fasting and preparation instructions carefully
    \item Arrange for someone to take you home after sedation
    \item Rest for the remainder of the day
    \item Report bleeding, severe pain, or fever after the procedure
    \item Attend follow-up to receive and discuss results
    \item Take prescribed medicines after the procedure
\end{itemize}
