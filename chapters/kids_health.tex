\chapter{Children's Health}

\section*{Definition and Overview}
Children's health covers the physical, emotional, and social wellbeing of children from birth through adolescence. Healthy children grow and develop on track, learn, play, and form relationships, supported by good nutrition, activity, sleep, immunisation, and preventive care. This chapter provides an overview of the foundations of children's health, including growth and development, feeding and nutrition, immunisation, common childhood illnesses, injury prevention, and the health checks that keep children well. Parents, health professionals, and communities share the role of protecting children's health, and most childhood problems are common, manageable, and preventable.

\section*{Epidemiology}
Most Australian children are healthy, and child mortality has fallen dramatically over the past century. Common health problems include respiratory infections, ear infections, gastroenteritis, asthma, eczema, and injuries. Around one in five children has a chronic condition, with asthma, allergies, and neurodevelopmental conditions such as ADHD and autism among the most common. Obesity affects around one in four children and is increasing, and mental health conditions affect one in seven. Immunisation coverage is high, protecting children against serious infections, and most children attend all their scheduled health checks.

\section*{Aetiology and Pathophysiology}
Children are not small adults: their bodies grow rapidly, their immune systems mature, their brains develop at a remarkable rate, and their health is strongly shaped by the environment. Growth is driven by nutrition, hormones, and genetics, and the first years are critical for brain development, which is influenced by attachment, stimulation, and safety. The immature immune system makes young children prone to infections, while the developing lungs and gut cause common symptoms such as wheeze and reflux. Injuries reflect children's curiosity and developing skills, and prevention depends on supervision and safe environments. The foundations laid in childhood, in nutrition, activity, sleep, and emotional security, track into lifelong health.

\section*{Clinical Features}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Growth: regular increases in height and weight, plotted on growth charts
    \item Development: motor, language, social, and thinking milestones
    \item Feeding: breastmilk or formula in infancy, then a varied family diet
    \item Sleep: patterns that develop with age
    \item Common illnesses: colds, ear infections, gastroenteritis, and fevers
    \item Chronic conditions: asthma, eczema, and allergies
    \item Behaviour and emotions: developing self-regulation with support
    \item Injuries: falls, burns, and accidents are a leading cause of harm
\end{itemize}

\section*{Differential Diagnosis}
Childhood symptoms have many causes, and assessment distinguishes the minor from the serious.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Fever:} usually viral, but infection, including serious bacterial infection, must be considered
    \item \textbf{Cough and wheeze:} viral infection, bronchiolitis, asthma, or pneumonia
    \item \textbf{Vomiting and diarrhoea:} gastroenteritis, but obstruction or other illness in severe cases
    \item \textbf{Rashes:} viral, allergic, or serious infections such as meningococcal disease
    \item \textbf{Behavioural concerns:} developmental stage, ADHD, autism, or family factors
    \item \textbf{Slow growth:} feeding problems, chronic illness, or endocrine conditions
\end{itemize}

\section*{When to Seek Medical Care}
Parents should seek medical advice for any child who seems unusually unwell, has a persistent fever, is feeding or breathing poorly, or causes concern. Scheduled care includes the immunisation schedule, growth and development checks, hearing and vision screening, and dental checks from an early age. Trusted instincts matter: if something seems wrong with a child, assessment is always appropriate.

\textbf{Call 000 (or local emergency services) for:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A child who is floppy, very difficult to wake, or unresponsive
    \item Difficulty breathing, fast breathing, or blue lips
    \item A fever in a baby under three months of age
    \item Seizures or convulsions
    \item A rash that does not fade when pressed
    \item Signs of dehydration: sunken fontanelle, no wet nappies, or lethargy
    \item Severe injury or suspected poisoning
\end{itemize}

\section*{Diagnosis and Investigations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Growth and development checks:} at key ages, plotting growth and milestones
    \item \textbf{Newborn screening:} blood tests for serious conditions
    \item \textbf{Hearing and vision screening:} early detection of problems
    \item \textbf{Immunisation:} the funded national schedule
    \item \textbf{Assessment of illness:} examination and, where needed, tests
    \item \textbf{Dental checks:} from around 12 months
    \item \textbf{Referral:} to paediatricians and allied health when needed
\end{itemize}

\section*{Management}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Nutrition:} breastmilk or formula in infancy, iron-rich solids from six months, and a healthy family diet
    \item \textbf{Physical activity:} active play every day, and less screen time
    \item \textbf{Sleep:} age-appropriate routines and safe sleep in infancy
    \item \textbf{Immunisation:} on schedule, protecting against serious disease
    \item \textbf{Managing common illness:} fluids, fever care, and knowing the warning signs
    \item \textbf{Injury prevention:} car seats, helmets, supervision, and home safety
    \item \textbf{Chronic condition care:} asthma plans, allergy management, and specialist support
    \item \textbf{Emotional support:} warm relationships, boundaries, and talking about feelings
\end{itemize}

\section*{Complications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Serious infections in vulnerable infants
    \item Injuries and their long-term effects
    \item Obesity and its health consequences
    \item Poor growth and development when concerns are missed
    \item Mental health problems and their effects on learning and life
    \item Effects of untreated chronic conditions
    \item Family stress and the effects of parental illness
\end{itemize}

\section*{Prognosis and Outcomes}
The great majority of Australian children are healthy and thrive, and the outcomes for children with chronic conditions have improved markedly with modern care. Early detection, prevention, and treatment give children the best chance of reaching their potential. The foundations of childhood, in nutrition, activity, immunisation, safety, and loving relationships, shape lifelong health, and the investments families and communities make in children's health deliver lasting returns.

\section*{Prevention and Self-Care}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Attend all scheduled health, developmental, and dental checks
    \item Keep immunisations up to date
    \item Breastfeed where possible and introduce a healthy diet
    \item Ensure active play and limit screens
    \item Protect sleep with routines and safe sleep practices
    \item Use car seats, helmets, and home safety measures
    \item Keep medicines and chemicals out of reach
    \item Seek early help for concerns about health, development, or behaviour
    \item Model and support healthy habits as a family
\end{itemize}
