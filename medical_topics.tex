% Preface
\chapter*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface}

This book is a comprehensive reference guide to 2663 medical topics related to health and medical care. It is designed for healthcare students, clinicians, and informed patients who want clear, useful information about frequently encountered conditions. Individual chapters cite their sources; where no source is shown, the chapter remains under review and should be read with caution.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{How to Use This Book}

This book is intended as a reference, not a diagnostic tool. If you are experiencing symptoms, look them up by name and read the relevant chapter to understand possible causes and what steps to take. The ``When to Seek Medical Care'' section in each chapter provides clear guidance on when professional evaluation is needed.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Important Disclaimer}

The information in this book is for educational purposes only and does not constitute medical advice. It is not a substitute for professional medical diagnosis, treatment, or advice. Always consult a qualified healthcare provider with any questions you may have about a medical condition. Never disregard professional medical advice or delay in seeking it because of something you have read in this book.

If you are experiencing a medical emergency, call your local emergency services immediately.

\section*{Glossary of Common Medical Terms}

\begin{description}
  \item[Acute] A condition or illness that begins suddenly and lasts a short time.
  \item[Benign] Not cancerous; a growth or condition that is not harmful in itself.
  \item[Biopsy] A procedure in which a small sample of tissue is removed for examination under a microscope.
  \item[Chronic] A condition that is long-lasting or recurrent rather than acute.
  \item[Comorbidity] The presence of one or more additional conditions often co-occurring with a primary condition.
  \item[Complication] A secondary problem that arises during the course of a disease or its treatment.
  \item[Contraindication] A reason why a particular treatment or drug should not be used.
  \item[Diagnosis] The identification of a disease or condition from its signs and symptoms and any test results.
  \item[Differential diagnosis] The process of distinguishing between two or more conditions with similar symptoms.
  \item[Epidemiology] The study of how diseases occur, spread, and are controlled in populations.
  \item[Etiology] The cause, set of causes, or manner of causation of a disease.
  \item[Exacerbation] A worsening or flare-up of a condition.
  \item[First-line treatment] The preferred initial therapy for a condition.
  \item[Iatrogenic] A condition caused by medical examination, diagnosis, or treatment.
  \item[Idiopathic] Relating to or denoting a disease or condition with no known cause.
  \item[Inflammation] The body's localised response to injury or infection, marked by heat, redness, swelling, and pain.
  \item[Malignant] Cancerous; a growth that can invade and spread to other parts of the body.
  \item[Metastasis] The spread of cancer from the place where it first started to another part of the body.
  \item[Morbidity] The state of being diseased, or the rate of disease in a population.
  \item[Mortality] The state of being subject to death, or the death rate in a population.
  \item[Pathogen] A microorganism that can cause disease.
  \item[Pathophysiology] The physiological processes associated with a disease or injury.
  \item[Prognosis] The likely course and outcome of a disease or condition.
  \item[Prophylaxis] Treatment given or action taken to prevent disease.
  \item[Referral] The process of being directed by one health professional to another for specialised care.
  \item[Remission] A period during which the signs and symptoms of a disease are reduced or absent.
  \item[Sign] An objective indication of disease that can be observed by a clinician.
  \item[Symptom] A subjective indication of disease that is experienced by the patient.
  \item[Syndrome] A group of symptoms that consistently occur together.
  \item[Systemic] Affecting the whole body rather than a single organ or part.
  \item[Triage] The process of prioritising patients by the urgency of their need for care.
  \item[Acute care] Short-term medical treatment, usually in a hospital, for an urgent or severe condition.
  \item[Adjuvant therapy] Additional treatment given after the primary treatment to reduce the risk of recurrence.
  \item[Aetiology] (British/Australian spelling of Etiology) the cause of a disease.
  \item[Anaemia] A condition in which the blood lacks enough healthy red blood cells to carry adequate oxygen.
  \item[Antibiotic] A medicine used to treat infections caused by bacteria.
  \item[Anticoagulant] A medicine that reduces the ability of the blood to clot.
  \item[Autoimmune disease] A condition in which the immune system mistakenly attacks the body's own tissues.
  \item[Cardiac arrest] A sudden loss of heart function in which the heart stops beating effectively.
  \item[Cardiovascular] Relating to the heart and blood vessels.
  \item[Congenital] Present from birth.
  \item[Dehydration] A state in which the body has lost more fluid than it has taken in.
  \item[Dyspnoea] Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath.
  \item[Endoscopy] A procedure using a flexible tube with a camera to view the inside of the body.
  \item[Immunisation] The process of making a person immune to an infectious disease, usually by vaccination.
  \item[Infectious disease] A disease caused by a pathogen such as a virus, bacterium, fungus, or parasite.
  \item[Oedema] Swelling caused by excess fluid trapped in body tissues.
  \item[Palliative care] Care focused on relieving symptoms and improving quality of life, not on curing the disease.
  \item[Prevalence] The proportion of a population who have a condition at a given time.
  \item[Screening] Testing for disease in people who do not yet have symptoms.
  \item[Sepsis] A life-threatening condition in which the body's response to infection damages its own tissues.
  \item[Vaccine] A preparation used to stimulate immunity against a specific disease.
\end{description}
