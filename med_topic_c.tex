\section*{Glossary of Common Medical Terms}

\begin{description}
  \item[Abscess] A localised collection of pus, usually caused by infection, that may need drainage.
  \item[Acute] A condition or illness that begins suddenly and lasts a short time.
  \item[Acute care] Short-term medical treatment, usually in a hospital, for an urgent or severe condition.
  \item[Adjuvant therapy] Additional treatment given after the primary treatment to reduce the risk of recurrence.
  \item[Aetiology] Also spelled etiology; the cause of a disease.
  \item[AIDS] Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome, the late stage of HIV infection in which the immune system is severely damaged.
  \item[Anaemia] A condition in which the blood lacks enough healthy red blood cells to carry adequate oxygen.
  \item[Anaphylaxis] A severe, potentially life-threatening allergic reaction that requires immediate emergency care.
  \item[Aneurysm] A bulge in the wall of a blood vessel that may rupture and cause serious bleeding.
  \item[Angina] Chest pain or discomfort caused by reduced blood flow to the heart muscle.
  \item[Angiography] An imaging test that uses dye and X-rays to visualise blood vessels.
  \item[Antibiotic] A medicine used to treat infections caused by bacteria.
  \item[Antibody] A protein produced by the immune system that recognises and helps neutralise specific foreign substances such as viruses and bacteria.
  \item[Anticoagulant] A medicine that reduces the ability of the blood to clot.
  \item[Antiemetic] A medicine used to prevent or treat nausea and vomiting.
  \item[Antifungal] A medicine used to treat infections caused by fungi.
  \item[Antigen] Any substance, such as a virus or bacterium, that the immune system recognises as foreign and responds to.
  \item[Antihistamine] A medicine that blocks the effects of histamine and is used to treat allergic symptoms.
  \item[Antipyretic] A medicine used to reduce fever.
  \item[Antiseptic] A substance applied to skin or wounds to prevent infection.
  \item[Antiviral] A medicine used to treat infections caused by viruses.
  \item[Anxiety] A feeling of worry or fear that is out of proportion to the situation and interferes with daily life.
  \item[Arrhythmia] An abnormal rhythm of the heartbeat.
  \item[Artery] A blood vessel that carries blood away from the heart to the rest of the body.
  \item[Arthritis] Inflammation of one or more joints, causing pain and stiffness.
  \item[Atherosclerosis] A disease in which fatty deposits build up in artery walls, narrowing them and restricting blood flow.
  \item[Autoimmune disease] A condition in which the immune system mistakenly attacks the body's own tissues.
  \item[Bacteria] Single-celled microorganisms, some of which cause disease.
  \item[Benign] Not cancerous; a growth or condition that is not harmful in itself.
  \item[Biopsy] A procedure in which a small sample of tissue is removed for examination under a microscope.
  \item[Blood pressure] The pressure of blood against the walls of the arteries as the heart pumps it around the body.
  \item[Blood test] A laboratory test performed on a sample of blood to assess health or diagnose disease.
  \item[Bronchitis] Inflammation of the larger airways of the lungs, usually causing a cough.
  \item[Capillary] The smallest type of blood vessel, where oxygen and nutrients are exchanged with tissues.
  \item[Carcinogen] A substance capable of causing cancer.
  \item[Carcinoma] A cancer that arises from the cells lining the surface of organs or skin.
  \item[Cardiac arrest] A sudden loss of heart function in which the heart stops beating effectively.
  \item[Cardiology] The branch of medicine concerned with disorders of the heart and blood vessels.
  \item[Cardiovascular] Relating to the heart and blood vessels.
  \item[Catheter] A thin flexible tube inserted into the body to deliver or remove fluids.
  \item[Chemotherapy] Treatment of disease, usually cancer, using drugs that kill or slow the growth of cells.
  \item[Chromosome] A thread-like structure in cells that carries genetic information.
  \item[Chronic] A condition that is long-lasting or recurrent rather than acute.
  \item[Clinical trial] A carefully designed research study that tests how well a treatment works in people.
  \item[Comorbidity] The presence of one or more additional conditions often co-occurring with a primary condition.
  \item[Complication] A secondary problem that arises during the course of a disease or its treatment.
  \item[Computed tomography (CT) scan] An imaging technique that uses X-rays and a computer to produce detailed cross-sectional pictures of the body.
  \item[Congenital] Present from birth.
  \item[Constipation] Difficulty passing stools, or infrequent bowel movements.
  \item[Contraindication] A reason why a particular treatment or drug should not be used.
  \item[Corticosteroid] A medicine that reduces inflammation and suppresses immune activity.
  \item[CT scan] See Computed tomography (CT) scan.
  \item[Cyst] A closed sac or pocket, often filled with fluid or semi-solid material.
  \item[Dehydration] A state in which the body has lost more fluid than it has taken in.
  \item[Dermatitis] Inflammation of the skin, typically causing redness and itching.
  \item[Dermatology] The branch of medicine concerned with the skin, hair, and nails.
  \item[Diabetes mellitus] A group of conditions in which blood glucose levels are too high because the body cannot produce or use insulin properly.
  \item[Diagnosis] The identification of a disease or condition from its signs and symptoms and any test results.
  \item[Dialysis] A treatment that filters waste products from the blood when the kidneys are no longer able to do so.
  \item[Diarrhoea] Loose, watery stools occurring more often than normal.
  \item[Differential diagnosis] The process of distinguishing between two or more conditions with similar symptoms.
  \item[Diuretic] A medicine that increases the amount of urine produced, helping remove excess fluid from the body.
  \item[DNA] Deoxyribonucleic acid, the molecule that carries an organism's genetic instructions.
  \item[Dose] The measured quantity of a medicine to be taken at one time.
  \item[Dyspnoea] Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath.
  \item[Echocardiogram] An ultrasound test that produces images of the heart to assess its structure and function.
  \item[Electrocardiogram (ECG)] A test that records the electrical activity of the heart.
  \item[Electrolyte] A mineral in the blood and body fluids (such as sodium and potassium) needed for normal cell function.
  \item[Embolism] Blockage of a blood vessel by a clot or other material carried in the blood.
  \item[Endemic] The usual, constant presence of a disease within a particular population or region.
  \item[Endocrinology] The branch of medicine concerned with hormones and the endocrine glands.
  \item[Endoscopy] A procedure using a flexible tube with a camera to view the inside of the body.
  \item[Enzyme] A protein that speeds up chemical reactions in the body.
  \item[Epidemic] A sudden increase in the number of cases of a disease above what is normally expected in a population.
  \item[Epidemiology] The study of how diseases occur, spread, and are controlled in populations.
  \item[Etiology] The cause, set of causes, or manner of causation of a disease.
  \item[Evidence-based medicine] The practice of making clinical decisions using the best available research evidence.
  \item[Exacerbation] A worsening or flare-up of a condition.
  \item[First-line treatment] The preferred initial therapy for a condition.
  \item[Gene] A unit of heredity made of DNA that carries instructions for a particular trait or function.
  \item[Generic drug] A medicine that contains the same active ingredient as a brand-name drug but is sold under its chemical name.
  \item[Genetic] Relating to genes or heredity.
  \item[Geriatrics] The branch of medicine concerned with the health of older people.
  \item[Glucose] A simple sugar that is the main energy source for the body's cells.
  \item[Graft] Healthy tissue or an organ transplanted to replace damaged tissue.
  \item[Haematology] The branch of medicine concerned with blood and blood-forming tissues.
  \item[Haemoglobin] The oxygen-carrying protein in red blood cells.
  \item[Health literacy] A person's ability to find, understand, and use health information to make decisions.
  \item[Hereditary] Passed from parent to child through genes.
  \item[Homeostasis] The body's ability to maintain a stable internal environment despite changes outside.
  \item[Hormone] A chemical messenger produced by a gland and carried in the blood to regulate body functions.
  \item[Hypertension] Persistently raised blood pressure (high blood pressure).
  \item[Hypoglycaemia] Abnormally low blood glucose levels.
  \item[Hypotension] Abnormally low blood pressure.
  \item[Iatrogenic] A condition caused by medical examination, diagnosis, or treatment.
  \item[Idiopathic] Relating to or denoting a disease or condition with no known cause.
  \item[Immunisation] The process of making a person immune to an infectious disease, usually by vaccination.
  \item[Immunity] The body's ability to resist infection and disease.
  \item[Immunosuppressant] A medicine that reduces the strength of the immune system.
  \item[Incidence] The number of new cases of a disease in a population over a given period.
  \item[Incubation period] The time between exposure to an infection and the appearance of the first symptoms.
  \item[Infarction] Death of tissue caused by interruption of its blood supply.
  \item[Infectious disease] A disease caused by a pathogen such as a virus, bacterium, fungus, or parasite.
  \item[Inflammation] The body's localised response to injury or infection, marked by heat, redness, swelling, and pain.
  \item[Informed consent] Agreement to a proposed medical treatment given after understanding its benefits, risks, and alternatives.
  \item[Insulin] A hormone produced by the pancreas that regulates blood glucose levels.
  \item[Intensive care] Specialised hospital care for patients with life-threatening conditions requiring close monitoring.
  \item[Ischaemia] Reduced blood supply to a tissue, causing a shortage of oxygen.
  \item[Jaundice] Yellowing of the skin and whites of the eyes due to a build-up of bilirubin.
  \item[Leukaemia] Cancer of the blood-forming tissues, including the bone marrow.
  \item[Lymph node] A small bean-shaped structure of the immune system that filters lymph and helps fight infection.
  \item[Lymphoma] Cancer of the lymphatic system.
  \item[Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)] An imaging technique that uses strong magnets and radio waves to produce detailed images of the body.
  \item[Malignant] Cancerous; a growth that can invade and spread to other parts of the body.
  \item[Melanoma] A serious form of skin cancer that begins in pigment-producing cells.
  \item[Metabolism] The chemical processes by which the body converts food into energy and builds or repairs tissue.
  \item[Metastasis] The spread of cancer from the place where it first started to another part of the body.
  \item[Morbidity] The state of being diseased, or the rate of disease in a population.
  \item[Mortality] The state of being subject to death, or the death rate in a population.
  \item[MRI] See Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).
  \item[Mutation] A change in the DNA sequence that may lead to altered function.
  \item[Nausea] A feeling of wanting to vomit.
  \item[Necrosis] Death of cells or tissue, often from injury or loss of blood supply.
  \item[Neoplasm] A new and abnormal growth of tissue; a tumour.
  \item[Nephrology] The branch of medicine concerned with the kidneys.
  \item[Neurology] The branch of medicine concerned with the nervous system.
  \item[Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID)] A medicine that reduces pain and inflammation.
  \item[Obstetrics] The branch of medicine concerned with pregnancy and childbirth.
  \item[Oedema] Swelling caused by excess fluid trapped in body tissues.
  \item[Oncology] The branch of medicine concerned with the diagnosis and treatment of cancer.
  \item[Ophthalmology] The branch of medicine concerned with the eyes.
  \item[Organ] A part of the body, such as the heart or liver, with a specific function.
  \item[Orthopaedics] The branch of medicine concerned with the bones, joints, and muscles.
  \item[Osteoporosis] A condition in which bones become weak and more likely to break.
  \item[Otolaryngology] The branch of medicine concerned with the ear, nose, and throat.
  \item[Outbreak] The occurrence of cases of a disease in excess of what is normally expected.
  \item[Outpatient] A patient who receives treatment at a hospital or clinic without being admitted overnight.
  \item[Paediatrics] The branch of medicine concerned with the health of children.
  \item[Palliative care] Care focused on relieving symptoms and improving quality of life, not on curing the disease.
  \item[Palpitations] An awareness of the heart beating, such as a fluttering, racing, or pounding sensation.
  \item[Pancreas] An organ that produces digestive enzymes and the hormone insulin.
  \item[Pandemic] An epidemic that spreads over a very wide area, often worldwide.
  \item[Pathogen] A microorganism that can cause disease.
  \item[Pathophysiology] The physiological processes associated with a disease or injury.
  \item[Physical therapy] Treatment using exercise and movement to improve or restore physical function.
  \item[Placebo] An inactive substance or treatment with no therapeutic effect, used as a control in clinical trials.
  \item[Plasma] The liquid part of the blood in which cells are suspended.
  \item[Polyp] A small growth, often on the lining of the bowel or uterus, that may become cancerous.
  \item[Prevalence] The proportion of a population who have a condition at a given time.
  \item[Primary care] The first point of contact for health care, usually provided by general practitioners.
  \item[Prognosis] The likely course and outcome of a disease or condition.
  \item[Prophylaxis] Treatment given or action taken to prevent disease.
  \item[Psychiatry] The branch of medicine concerned with the diagnosis and treatment of mental disorders.
  \item[Public health] The science and practice of protecting and improving the health of communities.
  \item[Quarantine] The restriction of movement of people who may have been exposed to an infectious disease.
  \item[Radiotherapy] Treatment of disease, usually cancer, using high-energy radiation.
  \item[Red blood cell] A blood cell that carries oxygen from the lungs to the tissues.
  \item[Referral] The process of being directed by one health professional to another for specialised care.
  \item[Remission] A period during which the signs and symptoms of a disease are reduced or absent.
  \item[Renal] Relating to the kidneys.
  \item[Resuscitation] Emergency procedures used to restore breathing and circulation in a person whose heart or breathing has stopped.
  \item[Rheumatology] The branch of medicine concerned with conditions affecting the joints and connective tissues.
  \item[Risk factor] Anything that increases a person's chance of developing a condition.
  \item[Screening] Testing for disease in people who do not yet have symptoms.
  \item[Sedative] A medicine that calms a person or induces sleep.
  \item[Sepsis] A life-threatening condition in which the body's response to infection damages its own tissues.
  \item[Side effect] An unintended effect of a medicine or treatment.
  \item[Sign] An objective indication of disease that can be observed by a clinician.
  \item[Specialist] A doctor who has additional training in a particular area of medicine.
  \item[Staging] The process of determining how far a cancer has spread.
  \item[Statin] A medicine used to lower blood cholesterol.
  \item[Stent] A small mesh tube placed in a narrowed passage, such as an artery, to keep it open.
  \item[Stroke] A sudden interruption of the blood supply to the brain, causing damage to brain tissue.
  \item[Surveillance] The ongoing systematic collection and analysis of health data to monitor disease.
  \item[Symptom] A subjective indication of disease that is experienced by the patient.
  \item[Syndrome] A group of symptoms that consistently occur together.
  \item[Systemic] Affecting the whole body rather than a single organ or part.
  \item[Thrombosis] Formation of a blood clot within a blood vessel.
  \item[Transplant] The surgical transfer of an organ or tissue from one person to another.
  \item[Triage] The process of prioritising patients by the urgency of their need for care.
  \item[Tumour] An abnormal mass of tissue that may be benign or malignant.
  \item[Ulcer] A break in the skin or in the lining of an organ, such as the stomach.
  \item[Ultrasound] An imaging technique that uses high-frequency sound waves to produce images of internal structures.
  \item[Vaccine] A preparation used to stimulate immunity against a specific disease.
  \item[Vector] A living organism, such as a mosquito, that transmits an infectious agent between hosts.
  \item[Vein] A blood vessel that carries blood towards the heart.
  \item[Vertigo] A sensation of spinning or movement when the person is still.
  \item[White blood cell] A blood cell that helps fight infection.
  \item[X-ray] A form of radiation used to produce images of the inside of the body.
  \item[Zoonosis] An infectious disease that can be transmitted from animals to humans.
\end{description}
